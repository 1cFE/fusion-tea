# 1costingFE v0.1.0 release — impact on the explorer, and the Option-A migration

**Date**: 2026-06-28
**Author**: Claude (working session with Reid)
**Scope**: Why the explorer's cost spine broke when `1costingfe` was pulled fresh, what we did to bring fusion-tea onto the v0.1.0 release, and the end state — including a substantive finding about how the release changes what concept 01's analyst overrides are "worth."
**Companion**: deeper migration-options analysis in `.project/research/20260628-132851_costingfe-release-migration.md`.

---

## TL;DR

- Pulling `1costingfe` up to date (it was **62 commits behind**) landed the **v0.1.0 release build**, which gated off the bundled 0D physics (`use_0d_model`) and power→geometry sizing the explorer's tokamak pipeline runs on, recalibrated every cost constant, and renamed the package.
- We took **Option A**: stay on the latest release, re-enable the gated solvers via a runtime switch in fusion-tea, fix the package rename, and **regenerate all explorer data** against the new engine.
- **Result: the explorer works end-to-end on `1costingfe@0254385`.** FR-SO1 (the headline invariant) holds for all 33 served costingfe concepts; tests are 19/20; the 5 tokamaks that hard-failed now run.
- **Headline finding**: the recalibration **dilutes what concept 01's analyst overrides are worth on the headline** — from the ~18% the epic was premised on down to **1.26%** at the 1 GWe projection (though still ~19% at the concept's native scale). This is the one remaining test failure and the one judgment call that needs a human.
- **Loose ends**: concept **27** still serves stale data (a concept-side routing bug); the FR-SO1 test's ">5% override impact" assertion is now stale.

## 1. Background — what Phase 1 (the EXPLORER-UX-V3 epic) was premised on

This context matters because the migration's most important consequence lands right on the epic's founding motivation.

The explorer shows each concept's LCOE with a slider/tornado/headline. Phase 1 of the EXPLORER-UX-V3 epic ("Provenance & Coherence") existed to fix two things:

1. **Coherence.** The slider, tornado, and headline were computed from *three different LCOE functions*. The first slider touch silently dropped the analyst's cost overrides, so a half-percent availability nudge appeared to collapse concept 01's LCOE. The epic's own words:

   > "The first slider touch silently drops the analyst's overrides, collapsing concept 01 from **155.17 → 127.53 $/MWh (−17.8%)** on a half-percent availability nudge the user didn't make."

2. **Provenance as the product.** Under known-stale numbers, the *traceability* — the analyst's override registry (account / value / source / rationale) — is the real value. Phase 1 built one `apply_analyst_overrides` toggle that drives slider, tornado, and headline together, and the toggle's job was explicitly to **teach how much the analyst's judgment is worth**:

   > "The toggle itself teaches 'what is the analyst's judgment worth here?' (**~$28/MWh on concept 01**)."

So the epic was premised on a concrete number: on concept 01, toggling the analyst's overrides moved the **headline** LCOE by **~17.8% (~$28/MWh)** — the gap between the analyst's accountable cost story (155.17) and the costing library's bare answer (127.53). That ~18% swing *was the demonstration* that the toggle and the provenance surface were worth building.

**The single override doing most of that work** is concept 01's `C220103` (Magnets / Coils): the analyst pins the ARC magnet system at **1030 M$** (a documented FOAK→NOAK derivation from Sorbom et al. 2015), against a library default that was much lower. That one entry is the epic's poster child for "the analyst's judgment is worth $28/MWh."

Keep that 1030 M$ override and the ~18% in mind — §5 is about what the release did to it.

## 2. The situation — what the 1costingFE v0.1.0 release changed

The local `1costingfe` checkout had been frozen at `b9b0a4c` (2026-06-15) and was **62 commits behind** `origin/master`. Pulling it forward to `0254385` (tag **v0.1.0**) brought in a **2026-06-18 → 06-26 release-prep series** that did four distinct things. Only one is "removal," and even that is gating, not deletion.

### 2a. Feature gating — the blocker
`src/costingfe/model.py` now carries two module-level flags, both default `False`:

```python
SIZING_FEATURES_ENABLED = False   # size_from_power, optimize_lcoe
MODELS_0D_ENABLED        = False   # use_0d_model (0D tokamak/mirror inverse)
```

`forward()` checks them and raises `NotImplementedError` (*"The bundled 0D physics models (use_0d_model) are not available in this release; supply the physics operating point as inputs."*). The solver modules stay on disk — the comment literally says *"Flip to True to re-enable."* The release narrowed the library's job to **costing a user-supplied operating point**, dropping the bundled physics inverse.

**Why it hits the explorer:** fusion-tea's tokamak concepts route their native + 1 GWe-projection forwards through `use_0d_model=True` (the R0/β_N bisection in `model_setup_helpers.py`). With the gate closed, the **5 tokamak concepts hard-fail at import**: 01, 21, 28, 29, 33. (34 and 39 route around the 0D path and survive.) Every other concept imports.

### 2b. Cost recalibration — every number moves
Independent of gating, the release re-baselined the costing:
- `47aa7a4` (06-23) — constants standardized to **2025 USD**, conductor costs promoted to `CostingConstants`.
- `a168537` (06-25) — n_mod plant-total power scaling, net/gross reference-power unification, **account recalibrations**.
- `17f9ab3` (06-16) — n_mod-from-power sizing for module-replication concepts (DPF, FRC, Zap, orbitron).

Consequence: even concepts that still import produce **different LCOE** than the stored data. Concept 24 (DPF) swung 304 → 235; concept 27 (polywell) 61 → 25.

### 2c. Package rename — breaks `uv run` repo-wide
`50ffebf` (06-25) renamed the **distribution** `costingfe` → `1costingfe` (the import package stays `costingfe`). fusion-tea's manifest still said `costingfe`, so `uv` failed metadata resolution and **no `uv run` command worked** until the manifest was updated.

### 2d. Mirror physics overhaul + paper/blog churn
The window also rewrote mirror physics substantially (energy-balance closure, tandem confinement, fluence-based core lifetime). Not a blocker; it means MFE numbers drifted on their own merits too.

fusion-tea's last data regen was **06-24** (`70090fc8`), against the 06-15 local checkout — i.e. the explorer's committed data + code encode the **pre-release** contract end-to-end. It had never run against the release build.

## 3. What we did — Option A

We chose **Option A**: keep the latest release and re-enable the gated mechanism, rather than rewrite the tokamak pipeline to supply explicit operating points (Option B) or pin to the old library (Option C). Rationale: smallest fusion-tea change, preserves the R0/β_N bisection projection method as authored, and keeps us on the public release line.

Four changes, all in fusion-tea (no edits to the library source, so they survive `1costingfe` pulls):

1. **Re-enable the gates at the costing chokepoint.** `exploration/concept_analysis/scripts/lib/model_setup_helpers.py` now flips both flags at import:
   ```python
   import costingfe.model as _cfe_model
   _cfe_model.MODELS_0D_ENABLED = True
   _cfe_model.SIZING_FEATURES_ENABLED = True
   ```
   The flags are plain module globals read at call time inside `forward()`, and every entry point (each concept's `model_setup.py`, the extractor, and the explorer server's recompute) imports this module before any forward — so one flip covers the whole pipeline.

2. **Package rename.** `pyproject.toml`: dependency and `[tool.uv.sources]` key `costingfe` → `1costingfe` (import stays `costingfe`); `uv lock` regenerated. `uv run` works again.

3. **Full data regen.** Re-ran `extract_explorer_data.py --concept <id> --skip-narrative` **per concept in isolated subprocesses** (a fresh process each) to avoid the documented jax cross-contamination that batch mode showed on concept 24 (epic Item 1-FU2). 40 of 41 concepts refreshed. The two concepts with hand-written narratives (27, 34) had them preserved and re-injected (narrative is analysis-derived, costingfe-independent).

4. **Fixture fix.** `tests/test_slider_override_semantics.py` had a `tmp_path` fixture broken since commit `efa8e885` (which moved module resolution to `base_dir.parent/concept_analysis/analyses`); the test `FileNotFoundError`'d before any costing ran. Fixed by nesting `base_dir` and symlinking the analyses tree as its sibling.

### The headline finding — the release dilutes what the analyst override is "worth"

This is the consequential result, and it lands exactly on the epic's premise (§1).

Concept 01's `C220103` magnet override is **1030 M$**, pinned at the concept's native design point (233 MWe). Measured on the new library:

| Scale | Library-bare C220103 | Analyst override | Override effect on LCOE |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Native** (233 MWe, the design point) | 508 M$ | 1030 M$ (+102%) | 176.5 → 209.9 = **+18.9%** |
| **1 GWe projection** (the explorer headline) | 939 M$ | 1030 M$ (+9.6%) | 106.23 → 107.59 = **+1.26%** |

So the analyst's judgment is **still worth ~19% at the concept's own design scale** — the epic's ~18% is intact *there*. But the explorer **headline is the 1 GWe cross-concept projection**, and at that scale the override barely moves the number (1.26%, ~$1.36/MWh). The reason is purely projection scale: when the machine is scaled up ~4×, the library's *own* bare magnet estimate grows from 508 → 939 M$ and nearly meets the analyst's pinned 1030 M$, so the override only nudges the headline.

In epic terms: the toggle was built to teach *"the analyst's judgment is worth ~$28/MWh on concept 01"* — on the **headline**, that lesson is now *~$1.36/MWh*. The provenance surface and the toggle still work; what changed is the size of the number they reveal at projection scale. The signal is large again the moment you look at the native design point.

Two ways to read it, both legitimate, and this is the human call:
- **It's correct and worth surfacing.** The recalibrated library genuinely agrees with the analyst at 1 GWe scale; the honest story is "library and analyst converge when scaled up." Update the epic's framing and the test threshold to match reality.
- **It undersells the override.** If the headline should reflect the analyst's design-scale judgment, the projection's override-scaling (pinned absolute value vs. growing machine) is the thing to revisit — the override loses its leverage precisely because it's frozen at native scale while the bare cost scales with the projection.

### The one failing test
`test_fr_so1_noop_compute_matches_stored_headline` now passes its core assertions (`applied == stored` ✅, `bare < stored` ✅) and **fails only** on:
```python
assert (stored_lcoe - bare_lcoe) / stored_lcoe > 0.05   #  1.26% > 5%  → False
```
This assertion encodes the old ~18% premise. It is **not a product regression** — it is the test asserting a magnitude that the release made untrue. Left untouched pending the §4 judgment call.
