RF accelerators (HIDIF, charge-symmetric, and SPRFD designs) and induction linacs are the two main accelerator approaches for heavy-ion inertial fusion energy drivers, each with distinct beam manipulation, brightness preservation, and cost tradeoffs — RF systems use mature technology but suffer brightness degradation from complex beam manipulations (funnelling, storage ring injection, telescoping), while induction linacs preserve brightness through simpler multi-beam architectures but face challenges in longitudinal dynamics control and switch development. Key design parameters, cost scaling (e.g., ~$0.1/W for RF power), and experimental validation milestones (ion sources, transport experiments, MBE-4, NDCX-II) are covered for both approaches.

Shared technical challenges for RF and induction heavy-ion fusion accelerators include cost reduction, beam-halo interactions with vacuum chamber walls, managing background gas pressure recovery between pulses at 10–50 Hz repetition rates, ion stripping and ionization cross-sections peaking at low energies (<100 MeV), and electron cloud (e-cloud) accumulation in magnetic quadrupoles causing emittance growth — with mitigation strategies differing between the two approaches due to their different bunch structures. The conclusions emphasize that final beam focusing to small focal spots is the most stringent constraint, and that reducing emittance growth, improving focusing systems, and cutting costs are the highest-priority research areas.
