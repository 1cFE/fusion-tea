# FFHR Blanket Heritage — NIFS Heliotron Reactor Line

**Source**: Multiple papers by Sagara et al. at NIFS (1997-2017)
**Relevance**: HESTIA is the Helical Fusion commercial evolution of the FFHR line

## Key Point: FLiBe vs Liquid Metal

The FFHR reactor line (FFHR, FFHR-d1, FFHR-c1) at NIFS historically used **FLiBe (LiF-BeF2)** molten salt as the self-cooled tritium breeder blanket. Selection was for safety reasons: low tritium inventory, low reactivity with air/water, low pressure, low MHD resistance.

However, HESTIA (Helical Fusion's commercial design) explicitly uses **"liquid metal blanket systems"** — NOT molten salt. This represents a design evolution from the FFHR heritage. The AIP paper (2023) consistently says "liquid metal" not "molten salt."

## Oroshhi-2 Platform at NIFS

The FLiNaK/LiPb twin-loop system Oroshhi-2 was constructed in 2013-2014 at NIFS as a liquid blanket collaboration platform. This suggests LiPb is one candidate liquid metal being studied.

A supercritical CO2 gas turbine (SCOT) power generator was proposed to connect to the FLiNaK test loop in Oroshhi-2 for a 20 kW(e) demonstration (Ishiyama & Tanaka, 2019).

## Structural Materials

Helical Fusion + Tohoku University (2024): Developed high-manganese alumina-formed austenitic steel with silicon addition for blanket structural material. Non-magnetic, low-activation, corrosion-resistant at high temperatures.

## Implication

The specific liquid metal composition (pure Li, LiPb, or other alloy) for HESTIA remains unconfirmed in public sources. The NIFS research infrastructure includes both FLiNaK and LiPb loops, and the heritage FFHR designs used FLiBe — but HESTIA has moved to a "liquid metal" approach which is distinct.
