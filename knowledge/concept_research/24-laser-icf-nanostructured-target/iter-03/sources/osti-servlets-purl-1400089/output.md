SUBJECT: FY17 NIF Performance Quad Campaign: Optics damage assessment and projected optics use & cost for high-energy operations 

## **1. Executive Summary** 

In the spring of FY17, a campaign was conducted using four NIF beam lines (Quad 45T) to assess laser and optics performance at increased 3 ω energy up to 2.6 MJ Full NIF Equivalent (FNE). The laser configuration and test plan for the campaign are documented in References [1-3]. The final optics were configured using the best-known mitigations to date to minimize laser damage and maximize optics performance. The data from the campaign was used to update and extend optics use and cost models which were then employed to simulate and estimate startup costs, optics use and yearly operational cost (Figure 1) for routine NIF operations up to 2.6 MJ.  Expected start up and yearly operation costs for 2.6MJ operations are $7.5M – $17M and $5.6M over the FY17 baseline, respectively. 

![](images/tmpgembg119.pdf-0003-09.png)

**Figure 1** . Projected optics exchange and replacement cost for increasing NIF operation from 1.8 MJ to 2.6 MJ with improved optics configuration compared with current NIF (FY17) configuration and operation. Optics Recycle Loop cost (Loop) is shown as the difference in cost relative to the Current NIF case. 

The campaign results and subsequent simulations suggest that it is feasible to operate all 192 beams of NIF up to the 2.6 MJ level for the pulse format tested provided that peak power limits are kept low enough to prevent intensity-driven (B-integral-induced) damage in the final optics [4]. We have further concluded that without the improved damage mitigations recently implemented in the final optics, operation of NIF would be prohibitively expensive and practically impossible at 2.6 MJ.

## **2. Optics Configuration & Preparation** 

The configuration of the Performance Quad tested in this campaign incorporated the following: 

- i. Third-generation advanced mitigation process (AMP3) treated 3 ω fused silica optics (wedged focus lens (WFL) and grating debris shields (GDS)) for increased damage resistance 

- ii. Anti-reflection coating applied to the grating surface of the grating debris shield (AR-GDS) to reduce transport loss and stray light effects 

- iii. Thin fused-silica debris shield (FSDS) added between the AR-GDS and the disposable debris shield (DDS) to protect the former from damage related to DDS-induced debris. 

- iv. 1 ω polarization rotator fabricated from deuterated KDP (DPR) to reduce transport loss 

- v. Third harmonic generators (THG) selected for high bulk damage resistance 

- vi. Transport 1 ω mirrors, second harmonic generators (SHG), and THGs laser-conditioned to 1.8 MJ full NIF equivalent (FNE) in off-line facilities, and then further laser-conditioned on-line to 2.6 MJ FNE. 

The optics were characterized (for example by full aperture optical microscopy scans) before and after the campaign to quantify on-line damage initiation and growth rates. 

## **3. Models and simulation** 

The damage initiation and growth data obtained from this campaign was used to update and extend damage models for the optics and operational models for the NIF optics recycle loop. The updated models were then used to estimate the optics use rate for several NIF shot plans with varying shot mix.  In addition to the damage mitigations described above, the models include an “extended hit-list” protocol recently implemented in the Optics Mitigation Facility (OMF). This protocol lowers the size threshold at which a damage site is mitigated from 50 to 20 µ m, thus increasing the installed lifetime of the optics between recycles. The models also employed a blocker budget of 20 blockers per quad for all cases. 

The simulation results for projected yearly optics recycle rates are summarized in Figure 2.  This analysis uses the current NIF shot plan for FY18 which is based on a planned log growth per quarter (LG/Q) of 6 with an 80% LG utilization factor. The FY18 shot plan includes forty-two 1.8 MJ shots. The stacked bar labeled “Current NIF” corresponds to the FY17 optics configuration of NIF (with none of the mitigations described above) and the FY18 shot plan. The “1.8 MJ” case includes the optic mitigations with the FY18 shot plan.  The cases labelled 2.0 MJ, 2.2 MJ, etc., include the optic mitigations with the FY18 shot plan in which the forty-two high energy shots (1.8 MJ shots) are replaced with the corresponding higher energy – for example, the case labelled 2.0 MJ replaces all the 1.8 MJ shots in the FY18 shot plan with 2.0 MJ shots. 

## **4. Startup costs** 

Relative to the current FY17 NIF optics configuration, higher energy NIF operation would require several startup costs (see Table 1) totaling from $7.5M-$17M. The startup costs includes reconfiguration of all beamlines with DPRs and installation of the first set of new FSDS optics, representing an added cost of $0.75M and $2.25M, respectively.  There would also be a one-time cost of up to $3M to replace 57 THGs with parts having sufficient bulk damage resistance for operation at the 2.6 MJ level.  In addition to the anti-reflection coating applied to the grating surface, the AR-GDSs used in this campaign were fabricated with reduced diffraction efficiency to limit the peak fluences in the 3 ω drive diagnostics to current operating levels. It is not certain at this time whether this precaution is essential, but if so ~300 AR-GDSs would need to be fabricated with a lower diffraction efficiency.

## **5. Optics Recycle and Replacement Rates** 

The optics recycle rate is the rate of in-house repair of optics via the Optics Recycle Loop. The optics replacement rate is the rate at which used optics are remanufactured by an outside vendor or discarded and replaced with a new optic. As shown in Figure 2, the optics recycle rate for repairing damaged optics in-house (which applies primarily to AR-GDS and WFL optics) is predicted to increase to approximately 2,000 optics per year at 2.6 MJ operation, or ~42 optics/week. This recycle rate is within demonstrated capacity with appropriate staffing. For reference, the current NIF FY17 shot-rate is approximately 5 LG/Quarter, and the recycle rate for AR-GDS and WFL optics is ~20 optics/week. For operations in the range of 1.8-2.2 MJ, the optics recycle rate is expected to decline relative to current levels with the implementation of the AR-GDSs, extended hit-list mitigation protocol and the installation of FSDSs.

The projected yearly optics replacement rates are summarized in Figure 3. For most optics types (AR-GDS, WFL, THG, SHG, LM) the predicted increase in optics replacement rate for 2.6 MJ operation is fairly modest: <15 optics/year compared to the current NIF case. For the DDS the increase is 13%. Once implemented, FSDSs will need to be replaced at the rate of ~130 per year at current operating levels, increasing to ~300 per year at 2.6 MJ.

3 ω silica optics (WFL and AR-GDS):  New or pre-characterized optics were installed and then exposed to first an on-line cleaning pulse and then a single high-fluence (2.6 MJ equivalent) pulse.  The optics were removed and then examined with an automated microscope with a resolution sufficient to detect damage sites larger than a few microns.  The number of exit surface damage sites observed is tabulated in Table 2. Filamentary damage is not included in this analysis. The damage measurement was repeated with new optics but with five high-fluence pulses. The number of damage sites were compared with the distribution of laser fluences each optic was exposed to; we thus determined the WFLs had AMP3 performance while the GDS are still closer to AMP 2.5 performance. Growth in size of pre-existing damage sites was measured and compared to predictions of our mature fused silica growth models 

3 ω crystal optics (THG): Bulk damage density in the THGs was estimated via a full-aperture light scatter measurement calibrated by sampling the damage density in the volume of THGs with an automated microscope. The correlation between the R/1 and ρ(φ) methods for evaluating bulk damage resistance was generalized to all DKDP crystals on NIF. The exit surfaces of THGs were characterized before and after participation in the campaign and the number of initiated damage sites cataloged; the number of those sites which were active (i.e. grew in size) during the campaign is listed in Table 2. A new rule describing the growth rate of exit surface sites as a function of whole beam fluence was developed by measuring the growth rate of the sites with the Final Optics Damage Inspection system (FODI) for several fluence levels, including 2.6 MJ equivalent shots. 

1 ω transport mirrors (LM):  New and previously used mirrors were examined before and after participation in the campaign, and the numbers of new damage initiations incurred at low and high fluence were recorded. Damage precursors that initiate at low fluence are demonstrated to have both low initiation density, low probability of growth, and low growth rate.  Damage of high-fluence precursors was shown to be eliminated by proper online conditioning.  This includes damage initiation which occurred in the center of blockers on unconditioned LM8 mirrors.  Extrinsic (particle-induced) damage was solely observed on LM4 optics (not LM8) due to the geometry of the 45T quad, i.e. up-facing vs. down-facing mirrors. This latter mechanism led to initiation and growth of several large (> ~1mm) sites, as noted in Table 2. 

![](images/tmpgembg119.pdf-0007-01.png)

Impact of disposable debris shields (DDS and FSDS): The models for debris-induced damage on the ARGDS both with and without laser cleaning were refined and verified by comparing the final state of the damage on the FSDS optics to model predictions. The DDS transmission model was verified and extended by off-line full aperture transmission measurements of the DDS optics used during the 2.6 MJ FNE shots.
