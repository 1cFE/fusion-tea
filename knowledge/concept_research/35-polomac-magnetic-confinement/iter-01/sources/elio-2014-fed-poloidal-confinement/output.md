# Revisiting the poloidal magnetic confinement

## Introduction

Among the closed magnetic confinements the poloidal configuration based on in-vessel ring coil is attractive for the high energy efficiency with plasma beta 20–30% and the similarity with natural phenomena like the earth magnetosphere. The drift of the plasma particles, owing to the curvature and the gradient of the magnetic field, is oriented about the central axis (azimuthal direction) and remains inside the plasma volume. Thus additional field components, which are mandatory in the toroidal confinements, are not required.

The poloidal magnetic confinement with a single in-vessel ring coil has been widely studied [1], [2], [3] and experimented [4], [5], [6] before the success of the Tokamak which is now leading the development of nuclear fusion for energy production. However technical and physics issues faced during the construction of present test facilities suggest to revisit old research lines and to verify if they can be improved through new design tools. The Poloidal Machine with magnetic shielded supports (PoloMac) could be the case.

## Conclusions

The poloidal magnetic confinement with in-vessel ring coil has been reconsidered with an increased field and 3D shaping using planar magnets. The upgrade comes from the permanent support of the in-vessel coil, passing through the plasma in a magnetic tunnel.

The magnetic analyses prove the possibility to bend the field lines around the support holes in the plasma.

Further analyses on MHD, confinement, stability and an outline engineering design are required to assess the possibilities envisaged
