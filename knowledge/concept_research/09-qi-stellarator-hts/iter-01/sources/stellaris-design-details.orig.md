# Stellaris Design Details (Aggregated from Multiple Sources)
**Compiled**: 2026-03-06

## Paper
- **Title**: "Stellaris: A high-field quasi-isodynamic stellarator for a prototypical fusion power plant"
- **Journal**: Fusion Engineering and Design, Volume 214, May 2025
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.fusengdes.2025.114868
- **ScienceDirect**: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0920379625300705

## Technical Specifications (from binding.energy analysis + Proxima press)

### Magnets
- **Type**: HTS (high-temperature superconducting)
- **Field strength**: Up to 20 T (modular high-field magnets)
- **Developed with**: PSI & BNET
- **Tape supplier**: Faraday Factory Japan (REBCO HTS tape, agreement signed)

### Blanket
- **Type**: WCLL (Water-Cooled Lithium-Lead)
- **Tritium breeding ratio**: 1.07
- **Note**: Proxima has stated this is a "concept, not a complete engineering design" and the WCLL option was demonstrated as viable "without suggesting this is the optimal choice"
- **Patent**: Applied for an innovative liquid-metal breeding blanket

### Structural Material
- **Primary**: EUROFER97
- **Operating temperature**: < 500°C

### Divertor
- **Type**: Island divertor geometry (heritage from W7-X)

### Operation
- 24/7 continuous operation (steady-state)
- Disruption-free design (no plasma current)

## Energy Conversion
- Not explicitly specified in available sources
- WCLL blanket is water-cooled → strongly implies steam Rankine cycle
- Analogous stellarator designs (e.g., Helios) use Rankine cycle with steam superheated to 635°C

## Heating Method
- Not explicitly specified for Stellaris
- W7-X (the reference QI stellarator) uses ECRH exclusively — 10 gyrotrons at 140 GHz, 0.6-1.0 MW each
- ECRH is the universal stellarator heating method (no plasma current for ohmic heating)

## Power Output
- Not explicitly disclosed
- "More power per unit volume than any stellarator power plant designed before"

## Commercial Deployment
- Agreement with Free State of Bavaria, RWE, and MPG to build at former Gundremmingen nuclear site
- Target: 2030s
