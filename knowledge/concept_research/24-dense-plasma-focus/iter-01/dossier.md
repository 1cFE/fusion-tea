# Dense Plasma Focus (p-B11) — LPPFusion

## Concept Overview

LPPFusion (Lawrenceville Plasma Physics Inc.) is developing "Focus Fusion" — a fusion energy approach combining the dense plasma focus (DPF) device with proton-boron-11 fuel. The DPF uses pulsed coaxial electrodes to create a dense, short-lived plasma pinch (plasmoid) with extreme ion temperatures. The company claims a path to p-B11 net energy via the quantum magnetic field effect, which theoretically reduces bremsstrahlung losses. The device is extremely compact (~3 tons, garage-sized) and targets 5 MW electric output via direct energy conversion of charged fusion products.

Founded and led by Eric J. Lerner. Current experimental device is FF-2B with beryllium electrodes. As of 2024-2025, the project has achieved record confined ion energies (>200 keV), record plasma purity (<0.2% impurities), and a fusion yield of ~0.26 J per shot (deuterium). The target for net energy demonstration is 30 kJ per shot with p-B11 fuel.

## Column Assessments

### Confinement Family
- **Value**: Other
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Schema definition; DPF explicitly listed under "Other"
- **Notes**: Dense plasma focus does not fit cleanly into MFE, IFE, MIF, or Electrostatic categories. The plasma is self-confined by its own magnetic fields generated by the current, but the confinement is extremely transient (~10 ns) and the mechanism (pinch/plasmoid formation) is distinct from standard MFE or MIF categories.

### Confinement Concept
- **Value**: Dense plasma focus
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/
- **Notes**: Exact match with schema vocabulary. LPPFusion's proprietary name is "Focus Fusion" but the underlying device is a standard dense plasma focus (DPF) geometry — two concentric cylindrical electrodes with a pulsed discharge creating a plasmoid at the end of the inner electrode.

### Fuel
- **Value**: p-B11
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/; Lerner et al. (2023) J. Fusion Energy 42:7
- **Notes**: p-B11 is the target commercial fuel. Current experiments use deuterium for development/optimization. The company plans to transition to decaborane (B₁₀H₁₄) as the boron source. All public communications emphasize p-B11 as the defining fuel choice.

### Primary Heating
- **Value**: Electromagnetic pinch (DPF)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/; Lerner et al. (2024) Frontiers in Physics
- **Notes**: Exact match with schema vocabulary. A pulse of electricity from a capacitor bank is discharged across coaxial electrodes. The resulting current sheath (up to 2.7 MA in FF-2B) runs down the electrodes and self-organizes into filaments that pinch together at the end of the anode, forming a dense plasmoid. The heating is ohmic/pinch compression — no external magnets, no lasers, no neutral beams.

### Energy Capture
- **Value**: Direct (charged particle)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/; https://www.lppfusion.com/investing-in-lppfusion/executive-summary/
- **Notes**: Two conversion channels: (1) Ion beam (~2/3 of plasmoid energy) enters a "decelerator" — a particle accelerator in reverse — that converts kinetic energy directly to electricity. (2) X-rays (remaining ~1/3) converted via photoelectric effect (patented technology). No thermal cycle. LPPFusion describes it as a "high-tech step-down transformer." The p-B11 reaction produces only charged particles (three alpha particles per reaction), making direct conversion the natural and only viable approach.

### Plasma State
- **Value**: Pinch
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/; Lerner et al. (2024) Frontiers in Physics
- **Notes**: The plasmoid is a self-compressed plasma ball formed by the pinch of current filaments. Duration is ~10 ns. Density approaches solid density (up to 10²¹ cm⁻³). Ion energies >200 keV achieved. The "Pinch" value in the schema is described as "Self-compressed plasma column maintained by its own current" — this matches the DPF plasmoid exactly, though it's a ball/toroid rather than a column.

### Magnet Type
- **Value**: Self-confined
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/; schema definition
- **Notes**: No external confinement magnets. The plasma generates its own confining magnetic field through the pinch current. The schema description for Self-confined is "Plasma generates its own confining magnetic field (Z-pinch, DPF)" — DPF is explicitly mentioned. The only "magnets" are the current-carrying electrodes and the self-generated fields in the plasmoid.

### Tritium Breeding
- **Value**: N/A (aneutronic)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/
- **Notes**: p-B11 fuel cycle does not involve tritium. The reaction is p + ¹¹B → 3α (three helium-4 nuclei). No tritium breeding needed.

### Neutron Management
- **Value**: Minimal (aneutronic)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/; Lerner et al. (2023) J. Fusion Energy 42:7
- **Notes**: The primary p-B11 reaction produces no neutrons. Side reactions (e.g., ¹¹B(p,n)¹¹C) produce a very small neutron flux — <1% of fusion energy. LPPFusion explicitly claims the device "requires no costly containment structure" and is "safe enough to place in residential neighborhoods." Thin shielding for secondary radiation would be needed but not the heavy shielding required for D-T.

### Operation Mode
- **Value**: Pulsed
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/investing-in-lppfusion/our-plan-to-net-energy/; https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/
- **Notes**: Discrete short pulses. Each plasmoid exists for ~10 ns. The capacitor bank discharges, the plasmoid forms and burns, energy is extracted, then the system recharges. This is definitively pulsed operation with very short burn events.

### Repetition Rate
- **Value**: High (>10 Hz)
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/investing-in-lppfusion/our-plan-to-net-energy/ ("pulsing up to a few hundred times a second"); Wikipedia DPF article ("maximum repetition rate will be close to 200 Hz")
- **Notes**: LPPFusion targets ~200 Hz for a commercial power plant (5 MW at ~25 kJ/pulse). This is a design target, not yet demonstrated — current experiments are single-shot. The NX2 device (Singapore) has demonstrated 16 Hz repetition for DPF devices in a different context (X-ray source), providing some physics basis. Electrode erosion at high rep rate is identified as a major engineering challenge. Medium confidence because the 200 Hz target is stated by the company but not yet demonstrated at fusion-relevant conditions.

### Driver Technology
- **Value**: Pulsed coaxial electrodes (capacitor bank, 2.7 MA)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Lerner et al. (2024) Frontiers in Physics; https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/
- **Notes**: The driver is remarkably simple: a capacitor bank discharges through coaxial cylindrical electrodes (beryllium anode, 2.8 cm radius in FF-2B). The current sheath self-organizes into the pinch. No lasers, no magnets, no neutral beams. The "hard technology bet" is achieving sufficient compression and confinement in the plasmoid for p-B11 ignition, enabled by the quantum magnetic field effect (Lerner's theoretical contribution). Device cost <$1M. The simplicity is the key differentiator.

## Additional Metadata

### Published Machine/Plant?
- **Value**: No (conceptual targets only)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/investing-in-lppfusion/our-plan-to-net-energy/
- **Notes**: LPPFusion has published conceptual targets for a 5 MW generator (~3 tons, ~30 m³, <$1M, ~200 Hz) but no detailed reactor/plant design study. The current focus is on demonstrating net energy in the lab (FF-2B). Phase 2 (power plant development) is estimated at ~$100M and 3-4 years after net energy demonstration. No published equivalent of an "ARC study" or DEMO-class design.

### Lab Experiments
- **Value**: Various DPF devices worldwide (Chile — SPEED-2, Poland — PF-1000 at IPPLM Warsaw, Singapore — NX2, plus many smaller university devices)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Wikipedia DPF article; ResearchGate publications on PF-1000, NX2, SPEED-2
- **Notes**: Dense plasma focus is a well-studied device class with dozens of implementations worldwide since the 1960s (originally developed by Filippov and Mather independently). Key large facilities: PF-1000 (1 MJ, Warsaw, neutron yields >2×10¹¹), SPEED-2 (Chile, largest in southern hemisphere), NX2 (Singapore, demonstrated 16 Hz rep rate). LPPFusion's FF-1 (2009-) and FF-2B (current, beryllium electrodes) are the only devices pursuing p-B11 fusion specifically. Most other DPF devices are used for neutron/X-ray sources or plasma physics research, not fusion energy.

### Description (enriched)
Pulsed coaxial electrodes create a dense, short-lived plasma pinch (plasmoid) with extreme ion temperatures (>200 keV demonstrated). LPPFusion claims a path to p-B11 net energy via the quantum magnetic field effect, which reduces bremsstrahlung losses in strongly magnetized plasmas. Very simple, compact device (<$1M, ~3 tons). Energy capture via direct conversion: ion beam decelerator (~2/3 energy) + x-ray photoelectric conversion (~1/3 energy). Targets 5 MW electric at ~200 Hz rep rate.

## Overall Confidence
- **Value**: medium
- **Notes**: LPPFusion has published peer-reviewed papers (J. Fusion Energy 2023, Frontiers in Physics 2024) and has real experimental results with record ion energies and plasma purity. The device physics is well-understood for DPF generally. However: (1) the quantum magnetic field effect enabling p-B11 net energy is a contested theoretical claim; (2) current fusion yield is 0.26 J vs. the 30,000 J target (a factor of ~115,000×); (3) repetition rate and energy conversion are untested engineering targets; (4) the company is very small with limited funding. Column values for the device physics and fuel are high confidence, but the commercial/power-plant parameters are company projections.

## Remaining Gaps

All 12 differentiation columns have been filled with at least medium confidence. Specific areas where confidence could be improved:

1. **Repetition Rate** (medium): The 200 Hz target is LPPFusion's claim. Independent assessment of DPF rep rate limits at fusion-relevant energies would strengthen this. The NX2 16 Hz demonstration is for a much smaller device used as an X-ray source.

2. **Energy Capture** (high for type, but conversion efficiency is unknown): LPPFusion describes the ion beam decelerator and x-ray photoelectric conversion conceptually, but no efficiency numbers or prototype test results are published. A detailed engineering study or patent filing with efficiency data would help.

3. **Driver Technology**: The capacitor bank specifications (voltage, capacitance, stored energy) for FF-2B are not fully detailed in public sources. The Frontiers paper gives current (2.7 MA) and anode radius (2.8 cm) but not input energy per shot.
