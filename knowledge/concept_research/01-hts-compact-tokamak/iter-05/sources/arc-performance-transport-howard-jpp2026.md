## Performance and transport in the ARC tokamak

N.T. Howard, P. Rodriguez-Fernandez, J. Hall, M. Muraca, A. Saltzman, A. Ho, J.C. Hillesheim, A.J. Creely, T.H. Eich, T. Body, P.B. Snyder, C. Holland

**Citation:** Journal of Plasma Physics, Vol. 92, Issue 3, June 2026. DOI: 10.1017/S0022377826101573.

**Abstract**

The research examines fusion power generation, transport properties, and confinement characteristics for the ARC tokamak, a high-field fusion power plant under development by Commonwealth Fusion Systems. The study employs multiple integrated modeling approaches to predict performance at an operational point designed for approximately 1.13 GW of fusion power generation.

## Key quantitative results

### POPCON 0-D modeling
- P_fus target: 1133 MW
- Q ≈ 51
- ⟨T_e⟩ = 11.4 keV
- ⟨n_e⟩ = 2.44 × 10²⁰ m⁻³
- H_98,y2 = 0.9 (conservative)

### Medium-fidelity modeling
- TRANSP–PORTALS: 879 MW (Q = 38.6)
- ASTRA: 895 MW (Q = 37.1)
- TORAX: 1295 MW (Q = 51.0)
- **Predicted P_fus range: 900–1300 MW**

### High-fidelity gyrokinetic modeling (CGYRO)
- P_fus prediction: 677 MW
- Lower volume-averaged densities and temperatures; reduced peaking

## Device parameters (V3A operating point)
- R = 4.62 m, a = 1.18 m, V_p = 189 m³
- B_t = 11.4 T, I_p = 12.0 MA
- q_95 = 3.7; q* = 3.0
- κ_sep = 1.8; δ_sep = 0.65
- 50 MW ICRH at ~180 MHz; 900 s flattop

## Key transport finding

Ion temperature gradient (ITG) turbulence identified as the dominant transport mechanism via linear stability and nonlinear flux analysis.

## Why this is a primary source

Provides the fusion-power prediction band (677 MW gyrokinetic – 1300 MW TORAX) underlying the ARC V3A 400 MWe net electric target, and is the canonical citation for ARC's confinement/transport assumptions in any techno-economic model.
