## Power and particle exhaust for the ARC fusion power plant

Thomas H. Eich, Thomas A.J. Body, Tom P. Looby, Sean B. Ballinger, Alexander J. Creely, Jon C. Hillesheim, Philip B. Snyder, Nathan T. Howard, Rebecca Masline, Michael R.K. Wigram, George R. Tynan

**Citation:** Journal of Plasma Physics, Vol. 92, Issue 3, June 2026. DOI: 10.1017/S002237782610155X.

**Abstract**

The authors present an integrated power and particle management strategy for the ARC fusion power plant. The study forecasts that "with this divertor design, ARC will access detachment with modest argon seeding in the divertor (~0.9%), which should have minimal impact on the core." Key findings include predicted helium core concentration below 2% and identification of the necessity to avoid type-I edge-localized modes (ELMs) for acceptable divertor performance.

## Divertor concept

ARC employs an **X-point target (XPT)** configuration:
- Up-down symmetric divertors with secondary X-points
- Long, tightly baffled outer legs
- Extended connection length maximizing flux expansion
- Reduces target electron temperature relative to conventional geometry

## Why this is a primary source

Establishes the divertor geometry (XPT), impurity seeding strategy, and exhaust handling assumptions for ARC V3A — feeds into divertor capital cost (CAS22), maintenance schedule, and the ELM-control burden on the rest of the plant.
