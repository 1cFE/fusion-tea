## Overview of the physics basis for the ARC fusion power plant

Jon C. Hillesheim, Alexander J. Creely, Thomas H. Eich, Nathan T. Howard, Nils Leuthold, Ryan Sweeney, Alexandra LeViness, Andrew O. Nelson, Leon Nichols, Roy Alexander Tinguely, Maria Usoltseva, Devon Battaglia, Thomas A.J. Body, Christopher Hansen, Nikolas C. Logan, Robert T. Mumgaard, Pablo Rodriguez-Fernandez, Philip B. Snyder, Brandon N. Sorbom, John C. Wright

_Commonwealth Fusion Systems, MIT Plasma Science and Fusion Center, Columbia University_

**Citation:** Journal of Plasma Physics, Vol. 92, Issue 3, Article E69, June 2026. DOI: 10.1017/S0022377826101706.

**Abstract**

Commonwealth Fusion Systems intends to construct ARC as a demonstration fusion power facility in Virginia by the early 2030s. The paper presents physics-basis analysis for the ARC V3A configuration, a high-field tokamak designed for commercial power generation. Key design features include a liquid-salt blanket and replaceable vacuum vessel architecture. The analysis spans empirical fusion performance projections, H-mode accessibility assessment, radio-frequency heating simulations, energetic particle physics, and full-pulse time-dependent modeling. Complementary investigations address disruption physics, divertor performance, and magnetohydrodynamic stability. The work identifies critical physics uncertainties requiring validation through the SPARC experimental program.

## Notable design choices

- **Magnets:** REBCO HTS toroidal-field coils.
- **Blanket:** FLiBe (Li₂BeF₄) liquid eutectic — tritium breeder, neutron multiplier, primary coolant.
- **Structural / armor:** vanadium with tungsten armor.
- **Vacuum vessel:** replaceable, 1–2 year lifetime.
- **Divertor:** double-null X-point target (XPT) configuration.
- **Heating:** ICRH only; no in-vessel coils; demountable poloidal-field coils.

## Fuel cycle

- Deuterium–tritium, 50:50 fuel mix.
- Tritium breeding via ⁶Li and ⁷Li in FLiBe.
- Target TBR > 1.

## Why this is a primary source

This paper is the published engineering basis for the **ARC V3A** commercial plant — CFS's Virginia 400 MWe target. It supersedes the 2015 Sorbom et al. ARC paper as the canonical ARC design point. R₀, B₀, Iₚ, P_fus, P_e,net, blanket, and divertor are all stated in this peer-reviewed reference.
