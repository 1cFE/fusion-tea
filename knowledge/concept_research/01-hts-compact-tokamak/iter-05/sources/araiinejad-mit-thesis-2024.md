## Techno-Economic Analysis of Deuterium-Tritium Magnetic Confinement Fusion Power Plants

Layla S. Araiinejad, Koroush Shirvan

_Department of Nuclear Science and Engineering, Massachusetts Institute of Technology_

**Citation (thesis):** Araiinejad, L.S., "Techno-Economic Analysis of Deuterium-Tritium Magnetic Confinement Fusion Power Plants," MIT M.S. thesis, 2024. (ProQuest accession c1e23c0ee6d140764ac57f0835e295a5.)

**Citation (journal version):** Araiinejad, L.S. and Shirvan, K., Applied Energy 2025, S0306261925012978. (Paywalled; reference for LCOE decomposition.)

## What the thesis models

- **ARAI**: a 350 MWe tokamak reactor explicitly derived from the **MIT ARC fusion concept**. ARAI is the techno-economic descendant of ARC — same magnets, blanket family, and confinement physics; sized and re-costed for grid-scale deployment.
- Overnight capital cost build-up by CAS-equivalent category.
- LCOE sensitivity to fusion-specific subsystems (HTS magnets, FLiBe inventory, replaceable vacuum vessel cycle).

## Headline results (per ProQuest abstract)

- **Reactor scale:** ARAI = 350 MWe.
- **LCOE range:** **$140–$550/MWh** (substantial uncertainty band reflecting first-of-a-kind risk and manufacturing-learning assumptions).
- **Most sensitive cost drivers identified:** fusion-specific components and manufacturing costs.

## Why this is a primary source for ARC

ARAI is the only published, peer-reviewed-track techno-economic model explicitly derived from ARC and benchmarked against the same magnet/blanket platform CFS is now building as V3A. Its LCOE band ($140–$550/MWh) is directly comparable to the 1costingFE deterministic LCOE estimate and serves as an external validation reference for ARC's cost model. Critically, it bridges the 2015 Sorbom ARC paper (physics/geometry) and the 2026 JPP V3A papers (engineering) into one cost framework.
