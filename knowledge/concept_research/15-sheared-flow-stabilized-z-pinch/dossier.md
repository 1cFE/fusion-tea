# Sheared-Flow Stabilized Z-Pinch (D-T)

**Company**: Zap Energy
**Last updated**: 2026-03-07
**Iterations completed**: 2
**Overall confidence**: high

## Summary

The sheared-flow stabilized Z-pinch drives large axial current (~650 kA to 1.5 MA) through a plasma column, creating a self-pinching magnetic field that simultaneously confines and ohmically heats the plasma to fusion conditions. Sheared axial plasma flow, pioneered by Uri Shumlak at the University of Washington, stabilizes the Z-pinch against MHD kink and sausage instabilities, extending confinement time. The concept requires no external magnets, uses pulsed power capacitor banks as the driver, and envisions a compact (~3 m tall) reactor with liquid LiPb walls serving as electrode, tritium breeder, neutron shield, and heat transfer medium. Zap Energy (founded 2017, ~$330M raised) is pursuing commercialization through a series of experimental devices (ZaP, FuZE, FuZE-3, FuZE-Q, Century).

### Confinement Concept
- **Value**: Z-pinch (sheared-flow)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works; schema vocabulary exact match
- **Notes**: Proprietary feature is sheared-flow stabilization pioneered by Uri Shumlak at UW, which stabilizes the Z-pinch against MHD kink and sausage instabilities. FuZE-3's three-electrode design enables independent control of plasma acceleration and compression — an evolution of the basic concept but still fundamentally a sheared-flow Z-pinch.

### Fuel
- **Value**: D-T
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works — "deuterium and tritium isotopes"; Engineering Paradigms paper (2023) describes D-T fusion at 190 MWt with tritium breeding
- **Notes**: D-T is the target commercial fuel. Current experiments use deuterium (FuZE devices) or hydrogen/helium (Century uses non-fusing gas). No indication of advanced fuel ambitions.

### Primary Heating
- **Value**: Ohmic (self-pinch)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works — "the higher the current sent through the plasma, the tighter it will be constricted by magnetic fields and the hotter and denser it will become"; Engineering Paradigms paper (2023); schema description match
- **Notes**: The large axial current (500 kA on Century, up to 1.5 MA on FuZE-Q) both confines the plasma (via J x B pinch force) and heats it ohmically. No separate heating system (no NBI, no RF). Compression from the pinch effect also contributes adiabatic heating.

### Energy Capture
- **Value**: Thermal (steam)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Engineering Paradigms paper (FST 2023): LiPb blanket heat extracted via steam Rankine cycle; Ben Bridger Engineering blog (2023) confirms same detail
- **Notes**: Upgraded from medium (iter-01) to high (iter-02). The Engineering Paradigms paper (Thompson, Levitt, Nelson, Shumlak, FST 2023) describes that virtually all electrical drive power and fusion power deposits in the liquid-metal wall and blanket, extracted via a steam cycle. Multiple independent summaries consistently reference steam/Rankine. No mention of sCO2 or other advanced cycles. LiPb blanket operates at temperatures compatible with conventional steam Rankine.

### Plasma State
- **Value**: Pinch
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Schema definition: "Self-compressed plasma column maintained by its own current. Characteristic of Z-pinch."
- **Notes**: Plasma filament ~50 cm long, a few mm wide, self-compressed by J x B force to pressures up to 1.6 GPa (FuZE-3, Nov 2025). Beta ~ 1 (plasma pressure equals magnetic pressure). Sheared flow extends pinch lifetime but doesn't change fundamental plasma state.

### Magnet Type
- **Value**: Self-confined
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works — "confines plasma using the magnetic field generated by a powerful electrical current in the plasma instead of using external magnets"; schema lists Z-pinch as example for Self-confined; IEEE Spectrum
- **Notes**: No external magnets of any kind. Confining B-theta field generated by axial plasma current. Eliminates superconducting magnet cost, cryogenic systems, and MHD forces on liquid metal coolant.

### Tritium Breeding
- **Value**: LiPb blanket
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Engineering Paradigms paper (2023): LiPb eutectic (17% Li, 83% Pb), TBR ~1.1 with 3 m blanket; https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works — "mix of lead and lithium in the walls will produce tritium"
- **Notes**: LiPb serves quadruple duty: outer electrode, heat transfer medium, biological shield, and tritium breeder. Lead provides neutron multiplication. Weir-wall design allows LiPb to cascade under gravity to form the first wall. TBR of 1.1 is marginal but positive. Absence of external magnets greatly simplifies liquid metal handling (no MHD drag). Note: Century demo uses liquid bismuth (not LiPb) since it's a non-DT engineering platform.

### Neutron Management
- **Value**: Integrated blanket/shield
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Engineering Paradigms paper (2023): LiPb blanket provides tritium breeding AND biological shielding; Monte Carlo simulations show neutron irradiation confined near pinch region
- **Notes**: Thick (~3 m) LiPb blanket serves as both tritium breeder and neutron shield in a single integrated system. Compact plasma geometry concentrates neutron emission. No separate shield structure in the published design.

### Operation Mode
- **Value**: Pulsed
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works — "ten plasma pulses per second"; Engineering Paradigms paper (2023); APS DPP 2025 abstract: "pulsed, quasi-steady-state, Z pinches"
- **Notes**: Each Z-pinch event is a discrete pulse lasting microseconds. The APS DPP 2025 abstract uses "pulsed, quasi-steady-state" phrasing — this refers to the plasma being quasi-steady during each pulse (sheared flow extends the pinch lifetime), not quasi-steady operation in the schema sense. At 10 Hz with microsecond-scale burns, the duty cycle is far below the 5-minute threshold for Quasi-steady. Correctly classified as Pulsed.

### Repetition Rate
- **Value**: ~10 Hz
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works — "ten plasma pulses per second"; Engineering Paradigms paper (2023)
- **Notes**: 10 Hz is the target commercial repetition rate. Century currently operates at 0.2 Hz (one shot every 5 seconds, up from 0.1 Hz at commissioning). The path from 0.2 Hz to 10 Hz is a major engineering challenge requiring power supply scaling from ~39 kW to ~10 MW average input power.

### Driver Technology
- **Value**: Pulsed power (sheared-flow Z-pinch)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: FuZE-Q specs: ~1 MJ capacitor bank, up to 1.5 MA, passive pulse-forming networks (PFNs); https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works; Century paper (FST 2025)
- **Notes**: Capacitor bank discharging through pulse-forming networks delivers shaped current waveforms (fast rise, long flattop). Target currents: 650 kA-1.5 MA. Wall-plug to plasma efficiency ~70%. FuZE-3 introduced two separate capacitor banks with a three-electrode design enabling independent control of acceleration and compression. Century integrates repetitive pulsed power, high-duty-cycle cathodes, and liquid metal walls.
