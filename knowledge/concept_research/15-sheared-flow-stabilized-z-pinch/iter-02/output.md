- **Citation**: Engineering Paradigms paper (FST 2023): "LiPb to steam cycle" — heat deposited in liquid metal wall/blanket is extracted via steam Rankine cycle; [Ben Bridger Engineering blog](https://benbridgerengineering.com/2023/08/29/zap-energy/) confirms same detail
- **Notes**: **Upgraded from medium to high.** The Engineering Paradigms paper (Thompson, Levitt, Nelson, Shumlak, FST 2023) describes that virtually all electrical drive power and fusion power deposits in the liquid-metal wall and blanket, and this heat is extracted via a steam cycle. The paper is paywalled but multiple independent summaries and search snippets consistently reference steam/Rankine as the power conversion. No mention of sCO2 or other advanced cycles. The LiPb blanket operates at temperatures compatible with a conventional steam Rankine cycle.

- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Schema definition: "Self-compressed plasma column maintained by its own current. Characteristic of Z-pinch."
- **Notes**: Confirmed. Plasma filament ~50 cm long, a few mm wide, self-compressed by J×B force to pressures up to 1.6 GPa (FuZE-3, Nov 2025). Beta ~ 1. Sheared flow extends pinch lifetime.

### Magnet Type
- **Value**: Self-confined
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: [Zap Energy — How It Works](https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/zap-energy-fusion-reactor) — "Magnetic-Confinement Fusion Without the Magnets"
- **Notes**: Confirmed. No external magnets of any kind. Confining B-theta field is entirely self-generated by axial plasma current. This eliminates superconducting magnet cost, cryogenic systems, and MHD forces on liquid metal coolant.

### Tritium Breeding
- **Value**: LiPb blanket
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Engineering Paradigms paper (2023): LiPb eutectic (17% Li, 83% Pb), TBR ~1.1 with 3 m blanket; [Zap Energy — How It Works](https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works) — "mix of lead and lithium in the walls will produce tritium"
- **Notes**: Confirmed. LiPb serves quadruple duty: outer electrode, heat transfer medium, biological shield, and tritium breeder. Lead provides neutron multiplication. Weir-wall design allows LiPb to cascade under gravity. Note: Century demo uses liquid bismuth (not LiPb) since it's a non-DT engineering platform.

### Neutron Management
- **Value**: Integrated blanket/shield
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Engineering Paradigms paper (2023): LiPb blanket provides tritium breeding AND biological shielding; Monte Carlo simulations show neutron irradiation confined near pinch region
- **Notes**: Confirmed. Thick (~3 m) LiPb blanket is a single integrated system for breeding, shielding, and heat transfer. Compact plasma geometry concentrates neutron emission. No separate shield structure in the published design.

### Operation Mode
- **Value**: Pulsed
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: [Zap Energy — How It Works](https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works) — "ten plasma pulses per second"; Engineering Paradigms paper (2023); APS DPP 2025 abstract: "pulsed, quasi-steady-state, Z pinches"
- **Notes**: Confirmed. Each Z-pinch event is a discrete pulse lasting microseconds. The APS DPP 2025 abstract uses "pulsed, quasi-steady-state" phrasing — this refers to the plasma being quasi-steady during each pulse (sheared flow extends the pinch lifetime), not quasi-steady operation in the schema sense. At 10 Hz with µs-scale burns, the duty cycle is far below the 5-minute threshold for Quasi-steady. Correctly classified as `Pulsed`.

### Repetition Rate
- **Value**: ~10 Hz
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: [Zap Energy — How It Works](https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works) — "ten plasma pulses per second"; Engineering Paradigms paper (2023)
- **Notes**: Confirmed. 10 Hz is the commercial reactor target. Century currently operates at 0.2 Hz (one shot every 5 seconds, up from 0.1 Hz at commissioning). The path from 0.2 Hz to 10 Hz is a major engineering challenge requiring power supply scaling from ~39 kW to ~10 MW average input power.

### Driver Technology
- **Value**: Pulsed power (sheared-flow Z-pinch)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: FuZE-Q specs: ~1 MJ capacitor bank, up to 1.5 MA; [Zap Energy — How It Works](https://www.zapenergy.com/how-it-works); Century paper (FST 2025)
- **Notes**: Confirmed. Capacitor banks discharge through pulse-forming networks (PFNs) delivering shaped current waveforms. Wall-plug to plasma efficiency ~70%. FuZE-3 introduced two separate capacitor banks with a three-electrode design enabling independent control of acceleration and compression. Century integrates repetitive pulsed power, high-duty-cycle cathodes, and liquid metal walls.
