# MagLIF (D-T) — Concept Dossier

**Concept**: MagLIF (D-T) — Magnetized Liner Inertial Fusion
**Companies**: Pacific Fusion, Fuse Energy Technologies
**Confinement Approach**: Magnetized Target Fusion (Magneto-Inertial Fusion)

> **Note on Europa Fusion**: The original concept CSV listed "Europa Fusion" as a company pursuing MagLIF. Extensive searching found only a LinkedIn page (India-based, `in.linkedin.com/company/europa-fusion`) with no public technical information, no website, no press coverage, and no FIA membership. This may be a very early-stage entity or a data error. **Fuse Energy Technologies** (San Leandro, CA) is a well-documented second private company pursuing MagLIF and has been substituted in this dossier.

## Description

MagLIF uses pulsed power (massive current pulses from capacitor banks) to implode a metal liner around pre-magnetized, optionally laser-preheated deuterium-tritium fuel. The axial magnetic field suppresses thermal conduction losses during implosion, allowing fusion conditions at intermediate density and timescale — between traditional MFE and laser ICF. Developed at Sandia National Laboratories on the Z Machine (20+ MA, ~80 TW), with over 70 successful fusion-producing experiments. Pacific Fusion is developing simplified self-magnetizing targets that eliminate external coils and aim to eliminate laser preheat, using impedance-matched Marx generators (IMGs) as the driver.

### Confinement Family
- **Value**: `MIF`
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Schema definition; arXiv:2408.15206 ("magneto-inertial fusion"); Wikipedia MagLIF article
- **Notes**: MagLIF is the canonical MIF concept — magnetized plasma compressed by an external pulsed power driver. Intermediate between MFE and IFE in density (~10²⁰–10²³ cm⁻³) and timescale (~100 ns implosion).

### Confinement Concept
- **Value**: `Magnetized target (pulsed power)`
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Schema vocabulary; Sandia MagLIF program description (https://www.sandia.gov/z-machine/fusion/)
- **Notes**: MagLIF uses pulsed power (Z-machine class) to implode a cylindrical metal liner. This is distinct from `Magnetized target (pneumatic)` (General Fusion) which uses mechanical compression.

### Fuel
- **Value**: `D-T`
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Pacific Fusion founders' letter explicitly states "deuterium-tritium fuel" (https://www.pacificfusion.com/updates/founders-letter); Sandia MagLIF experiments use DD for scientific studies but D-T is the target commercial fuel.
- **Notes**: Sandia lab experiments typically use pure deuterium for diagnostic simplicity (DD neutrons are easier to measure), but the commercial concept targets D-T for its higher reactivity and energy yield. Pacific Fusion explicitly confirms D-T.

### Primary Heating
- **Value**: `Pulsed power implosion`
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Schema definition ("Massive current drives liner implosion; may include laser preheat"); arXiv:2408.15206; Pacific Fusion breakthrough article
- **Notes**: Traditional MagLIF has three stages: (1) axial premagnetization (10–30 T), (2) laser preheat (~100s eV), (3) pulsed power implosion (20+ MA, ~100 ns). Pacific Fusion's self-magnetizing targets eliminate external premagnetization coils, and they are working to eliminate laser preheat entirely. The dominant heating mechanism is adiabatic compression from the liner implosion driven by pulsed current.

### Energy Capture
- **Value**: `Thermal (unspecified)`
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Citation**: arXiv:2408.15206 ("~80% of the fusion energy released streams out as 14 MeV neutrons... absorbed in surrounding blankets for heat recovery"); Z-IFE power plant concept studies
- **Notes**: As a D-T concept, ~80% of energy is in 14 MeV neutrons that must be captured thermally in a blanket. Neither Pacific Fusion nor Fuse has publicly specified the thermal cycle (steam Rankine vs. sCO2 Brayton). The Z-IFE studies assumed thermal conversion but did not specify cycle type. No direct conversion pathway has been discussed for MagLIF — the geometry doesn't lend itself to charged particle recovery.

### Plasma State
- **Value**: `Compressed`
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Schema definition ("Plasma driven to fusion conditions by implosion... Characteristic of IFE and MIF"); MagLIF Wikipedia article
- **Notes**: The fuel is compressed by the imploding liner from initial ~mm radius to ~100 μm, reaching fusion-relevant temperatures (>2 keV demonstrated, multi-keV target) and densities. This is a classic compressed plasma state — short-lived, driven by external implosion.

### Magnet Type
- **Value**: `Pulsed EM`
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: arXiv:2408.15206; Pacific Fusion website ("fast-rising, high-current pulses")
- **Notes**: The driving magnetic field is generated by massive pulsed current (20+ MA on Z Machine) through the liner, creating an intense azimuthal field. The premagnetization field (axial, 10–30 T) is also pulsed (ms timescale). Pacific Fusion's self-magnetizing targets generate the axial field from the drive current itself — no external coils needed. No superconducting magnets are involved; this is purely pulsed resistive/capacitive technology.

### Tritium Breeding
- **Value**: `TBD`
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Citation**: arXiv:2408.15206 ("tritium-producing blanket to replenish burnt, lost, and decayed inventory"); Z-IFE studies mention FLiBe blanket
- **Notes**: Neither Pacific Fusion nor Fuse has publicly specified a blanket type. The Z-IFE power plant concept studies identified FLiBe as a candidate blanket material (could serve as breeder, coolant, and partial shielding). A lithium-bearing blanket is required for any D-T power plant. This will likely be `FLiBe blanket` or `Li blanket (unspecified)` once disclosed. The Z-IFE concept also explored using solid FLiBe as part of the recyclable transmission line — a unique integration concept.

### Neutron Management
- **Value**: `Integrated blanket/shield`
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Citation**: Z-IFE power plant concept (OSTI.GOV); arXiv:2408.15206
- **Notes**: MagLIF produces 14.1 MeV D-T neutrons, requiring heavy shielding. The Z-IFE concept uses a lithium-bearing blanket that serves triple duty: (1) neutron energy absorption, (2) tritium breeding, (3) first wall protection. This matches the `Integrated blanket/shield` pattern. Could also be classified as `Heavy shielding (14 MeV)` — the distinction depends on whether the eventual commercial design uses a single integrated system or separate blanket and shield layers. Given the Z-IFE heritage and the pulsed nature (blanket must also handle debris/shock from each shot), integrated is the likely architecture.

### Operation Mode
- **Value**: `Pulsed`
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Pacific Fusion website ("process is repeated over and over, like in a piston engine"); MagLIF concept description (100 ns implosion pulse)
- **Notes**: Each fusion event is a discrete ~100 ns implosion. The target (liner + fuel) is destroyed and must be replaced each shot. This is unambiguously pulsed operation.

### Repetition Rate
- **Value**: `Sub-Hz`
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Citation**: Z-IFE studies (0.01–0.1 Hz target, one shot every ~10 seconds); arXiv:2408.15206 ("Hertz-scale repetition rates")
- **Notes**: The Z-IFE power plant concept targeted 0.1 Hz (one shot every 10 seconds) with high yields (0.5–30 GJ per shot) to compensate for low rep rate. The arXiv paper mentions "Hertz-scale" as the general target for pulsed magnetic fusion. Pacific Fusion describes the process as "like a piston engine" suggesting they aim for ~1 Hz or higher, but no specific rate has been disclosed. The recyclable transmission line concept and target replacement logistics constrain rep rate. Best estimate: `Sub-Hz` to `~1 Hz` — recording as `Sub-Hz` since the most detailed engineering studies (Z-IFE) target 0.1 Hz, but `~1 Hz` is plausible for next-generation designs with IMGs.

### Driver Technology
- **Value**: `Pulsed power (Z-machine class)`
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Pacific Fusion website; arXiv:2408.15206; Fuse Energy website (https://www.f.energy/)
- **Notes**: Both Pacific Fusion and Fuse use impedance-matched Marx generators (IMGs) as the core driver technology — a next-generation pulsed power architecture with 90% energy efficiency, 200 kV operation (vs 6 MV for Z), and multi-million shot lifetimes. Pacific Fusion's system uses modular "bricks" (two capacitors + switch) assembled into shipping-container-sized modules. The Z Machine at Sandia delivers 20+ MA in ~100 ns pulses; commercial systems target 60+ MA for high-gain fusion. Fuse's TITAN demonstrated 1 TW IMG with 100+ repetitive shots.

### Published Machine/Plant?
- **Value**: No (but Z-IFE power plant concept studies exist from Sandia)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Z-IFE concept (OSTI.GOV biblio/771517); no published reactor design from Pacific Fusion or Fuse
- **Notes**: The Z-IFE power plant concept (ZP3) was a Sandia conceptual study. Pacific Fusion and Fuse have not published specific reactor or plant designs. Pacific Fusion targets net facility gain by 2030 and commercial systems by mid-2030s.

### Lab Experiments
- **Value**: Sandia Z Machine (MagLIF experiments, 70+ shots), NRL (magnetized ICF theory/modeling)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Sandia Z Machine fusion page (https://www.sandia.gov/z-machine/fusion/); MagLIF Wikipedia article; arXiv:2504.03919 (NRL magnetized ICF)
- **Notes**: Over 70 MagLIF experiments on the Z Machine have produced fusion-relevant ion temperatures (>3.1 keV), thermonuclear DD neutron yields up to 1.1 × 10¹³, and demonstrated magnetic trapping of charged fusion particles. Pacific Fusion's self-magnetizing target experiments at Sandia (2026) demonstrated simplified target concepts. NRL has contributed theoretical work on magnetized ICF concepts. Fuse's FAETON I produces 1+ trillion neutrons.

## Remaining Gaps

### Columns with medium/low confidence that could be improved:

1. **Energy Capture** (medium): Neither Pacific Fusion nor Fuse has disclosed whether they plan steam Rankine or sCO2 Brayton cycle. The Z-IFE studies assumed thermal conversion but didn't specify. A company press release or technical paper specifying the balance-of-plant would resolve this.

2. **Tritium Breeding** (medium): No blanket type disclosed by either company. Z-IFE studies point to FLiBe, but this is a 2000s-era study. Modern designs might choose LiPb or liquid Li. Company disclosures or ARPA-E award descriptions would resolve this.

3. **Repetition Rate** (medium): The range spans 0.01 Hz (Z-IFE high-yield) to ~1 Hz (arXiv paper general target). Pacific Fusion's "piston engine" analogy suggests they're aiming higher than 0.1 Hz. A specific company target would resolve this.

4. **Neutron Management** (medium): Classified as `Integrated blanket/shield` based on Z-IFE heritage, but actual commercial architecture not disclosed. Could be `Heavy shielding (14 MeV)` if separate systems are used.
