# Powering a prosperous planet

For billions of years, fusion has powered our sun and the stars.

Harnessing fusion here on Earth could provide limitless, clean, on-demand power to drive prosperity around the world.

But for fusion power to be commercially viable, we need an approach that combines established science, practical engineering, and affordable manufacturing.

## A compelling approach, now practical

One compelling approach has been known for decades. It’s called pulsed magnetic inertial fusion.

Pulsed magnetic means a fuel container is rapidly squeezed by a powerful magnetic field created by driving a large, fast-rising electrical current across it. This compression heats and inertially confines the fuel, fusing hydrogen into helium to release massive energy.

The process is repeated over and over, like in a piston engine.

But there hasn’t been a clear path to use this approach for commercial fusion energy.

Until now.

## Recent breakthroughs chart a clear path

After decades of investment by the US government, the US National Laboratories in 2022 achieved major advances in inertial fusion performance — with both pulsed lasers and pulsed magnetic drivers.

The same year, the National Laboratories also demonstrated a new, highly efficient way to generate large, fast electrical current pulses that can drive pulsed magnetic fusion.

## Building on established science and practical engineering

At Pacific Fusion, we’re building on this remarkable progress to construct a high-gain pulsed magnetic fusion system.

It’s designed to achieve *net facility gain* — more fusion energy output than all stored energy input.

## Designed for pragmatic economics

Our system is highly modular, enabling affordable manufacturing and rapid iteration. It uses common materials, simplifying supply chains. It’s made of three parts:

- A fast electric pulser built from thousands of identical parts
- A small fusion chamber (meter‑scale)
- Many tiny fuel containers (centimeter‑scale)
