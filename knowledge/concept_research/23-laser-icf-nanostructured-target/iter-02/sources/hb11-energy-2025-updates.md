## Energy Conversion — Updated Position

**Current website (2026)**: Explicitly states "conventional steam cycle generator" — "The energy released drives a conventional steam cycle generator. A portion of the energy produced is recycled into the laser system for continued operation."

**Earlier concept (Hora, UNSW newsroom)**: Direct conversion — "Naked helium atoms...positively charged. We just have to collect that charge." Alpha particles create electrical flow channeled "almost directly into an existing power grid with no need for a heat exchanger or steam turbine generator."

**Assessment**: HB11 has definitively pivoted to steam cycle on their current website. The direct conversion concept from Hora's original design appears to have been replaced, likely due to practical engineering considerations.

## Laser Architecture Update

Website now describes: "we deploy a laser system that combines thousands of commercial lasers to achieve required energy levels" — this is a shift from earlier descriptions of single large petawatt lasers to an array of many commercial-scale lasers.

Also mentions focus on "diode-pumped solid-state lasers" with target wall-plug efficiency ~10% (current conventional <1%).

## Low-Density Foam Targets

- HB11 now manufactures low-density foam targets in-house
- "Our low-density foams are roughly ten times more efficient at proton acceleration"
- These create non-critical density plasma, converting laser light into high-energy protons
- This is a shift from the earlier "solid HB11 cylinder" description — now using engineered foam structures

## UNSW Collaboration (2025)

- Assoc. Prof. Patrick Burr leads "Design and Simulations of a Fusion Reaction Chamber" research group
- Collaboration between HB11 Energy and UNSW Nuclear Innovation Centre
- Plant concept resembles "a data centre with big laser halls" — modular, compact, scalable
- Focus on materials selection enabled by aneutronic (low neutron) environment — steel construction possible

## Experimental Status

- "World-first 'material' number of fusion reactions by a private company" (Applied Sciences publication)
- 10x more fusion reactions than expected
- Currently four orders of magnitude from net energy gain
- No new experimental milestones reported in 2025-2026

## Repetition Rate

- Confirmed: ~1 Hz ("fuel pellets injected and burned at a rate of about 1 per second")
