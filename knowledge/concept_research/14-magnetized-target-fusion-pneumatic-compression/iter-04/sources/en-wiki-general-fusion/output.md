# General Fusion

[Fusion power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_power)[Michel Laberge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Laberge)[generalfusion](http://generalfusion.com) .com**General Fusion Inc.** is a Canadian company based in [Richmond, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_British_Columbia), which is developing a [fusion power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_power) technology based on [magnetized target fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetized_target_fusion) (MTF). The firm was founded in 2002 by Dr. [Michel Laberge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Laberge). As of 2024, it has more than 150 employees.

The technology under development injects a magnetized target, a [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics)) mass in the form of a type of [plasmoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmoid) termed a [compact toroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_toroid), into a cylinder of spinning liquid metal. The target is mechanically compressed to fusion-relevant densities and pressures, by anywhere from a dozen to hundreds (in various designs) of steam-driven pistons.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-popsci-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-toronto_star-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-3)

In 2018, the firm published papers on a [spherical tokamak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_tokamak) and a recent conceptual design was presented at the 30th IEEE Symposium of Fusion Engineering (SOFE).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-laberge2018-4) [5] In August 2023, the company announced an updated plan to build a new fusion demonstration machine – Lawson Machine 26 (LM26) – at its Canadian headquarters. The company says LM26 is designed to achieve fusion conditions of over 100 million degrees Celsius (10 keV) by 2025 and progress towards scientific breakeven equivalent by 2026. This was an adjustment to its previously announced Fusion Demonstration Program.


[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-6)In June 2021, the company announced it would build 70% of a full-scale fusion demonstration plant in the UK as part of a public-private partnership with the

[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-7)[UK Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Government).

## Technology

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=General_Fusion&action=edit§ion=3)]

General Fusion's approach is based on the [Linus concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_(fusion_experiment)) developed by the [United States Naval Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Research_Laboratory) (NRL) beginning in 1972.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Megagauss-35)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-sciencemag1-36) [37] Researchers at NRL suggested an approach that retains many of the advantages of liner compression to achieve small-scale, high-energy-density fusion.

According to Laberge, Linus could not properly time the compression using the technology of the era. Faster computers provide the required timing.

[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-38)

[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-BBC1-39)However, this claim is not borne out by the literature as various Linus devices with no timing constraints, including systems using single pistons, were built during the experimental runs during the 1970s and demonstrated fully reversible compression strokes.

[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-live.iop-37)

[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-40)General Fusion's magnetized target fusion system uses a ~3 meter sphere filled with liquid metal. The liquid is spun, creating a vertical cavity in the centre of the sphere. This vortex flow is established and maintained by an external pumping system; liquid flows into the sphere through tangentially directed ports at the equator and exits radially through ports near the poles of the sphere.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-euro-fusion-41)

A plasma injector is attached to the top of the sphere, from which a pulse of magnetically confined [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium)-[tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium) [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics)) fuel is injected into the center of the vortex. A few milligrams of gas are used per pulse. The gas is ionized by a bank of capacitors to form a spherical tokamak plasma (self-confined magnetized [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics)) rings) composed of the deuterium–tritium fuel.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-42)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-fusion_underground-12)

The outside of the sphere is covered with steam pistons, which push the liquid metal and collapse the vortex, thereby compressing the plasma. The compression increases the density and temperature of the plasma to the range where the fuel atoms fuse, releasing energy in the form of fast [neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron) and [alpha particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_particle).[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-fusion_underground-12)

This energy heats the liquid metal, which is then pumped through a heat exchanger to generate electricity via a steam turbine. The plasma forming and compressing process repeats and the liquid metal is continuously pumped through the system. Some of the steam is recycled to power the pistons.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-43)[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-euro-fusion-41)

In addition to its role in compressing the plasma, the liquid metal liner shields the power plant structure from neutrons released by the deuterium-tritium fusion reaction, overcoming the problem of structural damage to [plasma-facing materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma-facing_material).[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-44) [41] The lithium in the mixture breeds tritium.


[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-euro-fusion-41)

[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-45)### Lawson Machine 26 (LM26)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=General_Fusion&action=edit§ion=4)]

In August 2023, General Fusion announced it intends to build a new fusion demonstration machine named Lawson Machine 26 (LM26) to achieve important technical milestones using [magnetized target fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetized_target_fusion). LM26 aims to achieve fusion conditions of over 100 million degrees Celsius by 2025 and progress towards scientific breakeven equivalent by 2026.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-46)

LM26 will use a Marshall gun to inject a deuterium plasma into a target chamber. The target chamber's outer wall is a solid lithium liner contained within a cylindrical composite vacuum vessel. Toroidal coils mounted on the outside of the cylindrical vessel are pulsed and push on the liner to initiate compression. As the liner collapses, the plasma is compressed to higher density and temperature.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-47)

In January 2024, the company reported it had achieved symmetrical compression of a solid lithium ring within a few weeks of announcing LM26 and had built and began operating a compression test bed for LM26 named Prototype 0.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-48)

## Challenges

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=General_Fusion&action=edit§ion=6)]

Magnetized target fusion has a number of [challenges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetized_target_fusion#Challenges). General Fusion's founder and Chief Science Officer noted several specific difficulties that are not present in DC [tokamaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokamak). These include, but are not limited to:

- Confinement at high energy density is not known.
- Liquid metal vaporization. To address this challenge, the company is collaborating with
[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Livermore_National_Laboratory)to better predict how its plasma will behave as it is compressed to fusion conditions in its MTF machine.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-55) - Impurities from the liquid metal cooling the plasma.
- Forming an initial spherical liquid surface and symmetry of implosion. In January 2022, the company announced its primary liquid compression prototype successfully compressed a liquid cavity with symmetry and controlled shape sufficient to achieve fusion conditions. The peer-reviewed results from these experiments validate the compression technology and are scalable to a commercial machine.
[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-56) - Kink instability of the liquid metal shaft.
- Flux diffusion in the liquid metal.

Laberge stated that these challenges were still to be solved. [4] Indeed, General Fusion are yet to demonstrate mechanical compression of a plasma by a liquid metal wall,

despite this being a key technology required for their powerplant. Nor have they demonstrated a liquid metal shaft, or a means of re-establishing high vacuum conditions in the short time interval (<1 s) between pulses.

[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-cruickshank2018-57)In General Fusion's most recent conceptual design, the MTF power plant proposed by General Fusion would produce about 300 MWe from two 150 MW machines running in tandem.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Krotez_Segas_Khalzov_Suponitsky_2023-5)

Chrysalix Energy Venture Capital, a Vancouver-based venture capital firm, led a C$1.2 million seed round of financing in 2007.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-toronto_star-2)[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-news.com-80) [81] Other Canadian venture capital firms that participated in the seed round were

In 2009, a consortium led by General Fusion was awarded C$13.9 million by SDTC to conduct a four-year research project on "Acoustically Driven Magnetized Target Fusion"; [82] SDTC is a foundation established by the Canadian government.

The other member of the consortium is

[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-about_sdtc-83)[Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory).

In May 2015 the government of Malaysia's sovereign wealth fund, [Khazanah Nasional Berhad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazanah_Nasional), led a $27 million funding round.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-86)

SDTC awarded General Fusion a further C$12.75 million in March 2016 to for the project "Demonstration of fusion energy technology" in a consortium with [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) (Shock Wave Physics Group) and [Hatch Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatch_Ltd).[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-sdtc.ca-15)

In October 2018 Canadian Minister for Innovation, Science and Economic Development, [Navdeep Bains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navdeep_Bains), announced that the Canadian government's Strategic Innovation Fund would invest C$49.3 million in General Fusion.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:0-87)

In December 2019, General Fusion raised $65 million in Series E equity financing from [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore)'s [Temasek Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temasek_Holdings), Bezos and Chrisalix, concurrently with another $38 million from Canada's [Strategic Innovation Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Innovation_Fund). The firm said the funds would permit it to begin the design, construction, and operation of its Fusion Demonstration Plant.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-seriese-88)[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-89)

In January 2021, the company announced funding by Shopify founder [Tobias Lütke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_L%C3%BCtke)'s Thistledown Capital.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:1-90)

In November 2021, the company completed an over-subscribed $130M Series E round. Investors included Bezos, Business Development Bank of Canada, hedge fund Segra Capital Management and family-office investors. Funds were to be dedicated to building a commercial reactor.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:5-75)

In August 2023, the company completed the first close of its Series F raise for a combined $25 million USD of funding. The round was anchored by existing investors, BDC Capital and GIC. It also included new grant funding from the Government of British Columbia.[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-91)

In December 2023, the company announced the Canadian government invested an additional CA$5 million through Canada's Strategic Invesment Fund to advance its LM26 fusion demonstration machine at its Richmond headquarters.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-92)

In August 2025, the company completed an $22M funding round. Segra Capital and PenderFund secured representation on the company’s Board of Directors. Shortly thereafter in November 2025 the company raised an additional $51M in the form of [simple agreement for future equity (SAFE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_agreement_for_future_equity_(SAFE)). [93] The company did not publicize this funding, which came primarily from Segra Capital and PenderFund.

## Crowdsourced innovations

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=General_Fusion&action=edit§ion=10)]

Beginning in 2015, the firm conducted three [crowdsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdsourcing) challenges through [Waltham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltham,_Massachusetts), Massachusetts-based firm [Innocentive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InnoCentive).[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-innocentive-94)

The first challenge was Method for Sealing Anvil Under Repetitive Impacts Against Molten Metal. [94] General Fusion successfully sourced a solution for "robust seal technology" capable of withstanding extreme temperatures and repetitive hammering, so as to isolate the rams from the liquid metal that fills the sphere. The firm awarded Kirby Meacham, an MIT-trained mechanical engineer from Cleveland, Ohio, the $20,000 prize.


[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-95)A second challenge, Data-Driven Prediction of Plasma Performance, began in December 2015 with the aim of identifying patterns in the firm's experimental data that would allow it to further improve the performance of its plasma.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-96)

The third challenge ran in March 2016, seeking a method to induce a substantial current to jump a 5–10 cm gap within a few hundred microseconds, and was titled "Fast Current Switch in Plasma Device". [97] A prize of $5,000 was awarded to a post-doctoral researcher at

[University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame).
