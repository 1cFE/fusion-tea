**HYLIFE is die name given to a family of self-healing**
**liquid-wall reactor concepts for ICF (ineitial confinement**
**fusion). This HYUFE-II concept employs the molten salt,**

## **RESULTS FOR HIGH-VISCOSITY FLIBE**

**For the first part of the scoping study, it was assumed**
**that high-viscosity Flibe would be used on both the primary**
**and secondary sides of the IHX. The results for one of the**
**reference cases given in Moir, et al (1990), Case 2H for a**
**100° C temperature drop on the primary side, will be**
**described next. The equations used to model the IHX are**
**given in Moir, et al (1990).**
**The temperatures chosen for Case 2H are shown at the**
**top of Table 2. These temperatures were chosen based on**
**four principal constraints:**
## **1. The minimum temperature of the cold,**

**secondary Flibe, T B, must be well above the melting point**
**to avoid freeze-up in the steam generator.**
## **2. The maximum temperature of the cold,**

**secondary Flibe, T m, must be as high as possible for good**
**thermal efficiency of the steam power plant (SPP).**
## **3. The temperature of the hot primary flibe**

**entering the reactor (normally equal to TJ must not be too**
**high to keep the vapor density within limits for ion or laser**
**beam transmission.**
## **4. The temperature differences between the**

**hot, primary flibe and the cold, secondary flibe must be**
**large enough to provide the required heat transfer in the**
**specified tube lengths.**

**The fourth constraint is especially critical for the high-**
**viscosity Flibe, since the tube flow is laminar and the**
**Prandtl number is very high, resulting in poor hea; transfer**
**and very long thermal development lengths.** **The**
**temperature values shown in Table 2 are the result of the**
**many compromises needed to satisfy all of these constraints**
**simultaneously.**
**The rest of the key results given in Table 2 are for**
**IHX's with a nominal thermal rating of 1000 MW. Note**
**that small 10 mm diameter tubes were chosen for the high-**
**viscosity Flibe because larger diameters resulted in even**
**larger volume IHX's. These numbers indicate that an IHX**
**using high-viscosity Flibe is not out of the question.**
**However, the size and cost will clearly be substantial.**
**It can be seen from the numbers at the bottom of Table**
**2 that the vapor number densities corresponding to the**
**temperature of the Flibe entering the reactor are reasonably**
**close to the limits presently estimated to be permissible for**
**heavy ion beam transmission (Bangerter, 1988) and well**
**below the density required for laser beam transmission.**
**However, for heavy ion beam propagation, it may be**
**necessary to use the cooler Flibe out of the IHX's to lower**
**the vapor pressure in the regions near the beam paths.**

**The maximum permissible outlet temperature of the**
**cold, secondary flibe, TIHX's of Case 2H, as shown in Table 2, which limits the M was found to be 686 K for the**

## **SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS**

**The results of this scoping study have shown that the**
**use of high-viscosity Flibe as both the primary and**
**secondary fluid results in an IHX which is much larger and**
**costlier than a comparable IHX using low-viscosity Flibe**
**for the primary coolant and either sodium fluoroborate or**
**low-viscosity Flibe for the secondary coolant. Therefore,**
**while the high-viscosity Flibe is not out of the question, the**
**IHX's will clearly be large and costly components. For the**
**low-viscosity flibe, on the other hand, the size and cost of**
**the IHX's seem reasonable. Therefore HYLIFE-I1 has been**

![](images/tmp_mwsiw1l.pdf-17-full.png)

**designed using the low-viscosity Flibe for the jet array.**
**The preliminary COE values obtained in this**
**scopingstudy are much higher than we would like even for**
**the single-chamber Base Case D. These high COE's occur**
**even with a high thermal efficiency for the steam power**
**plant.** **More refined cost studies are now in progress.**
**However, it seems clear that major reductions in the capital**
**costs, particularly of the drivers (which are now estimated**
**to be almost 50% of the direct capital cost), are required to**
**reduce the COE of these inertial confinement fusion**
**concepts to more attractive levels.**

**`Chapter 1`** **`Introduction to the steam Generator`**

**`Steam boilers have been used to produce steam for over 200`**

**`years, and constitute one of the earliest subjects for the`**

**`application of engineering principles. A steam generator, a special`**

**`design of steam boilers which uses the hot fluid flow rather than`**

**`the products of combustion as the heat source, is an important unit`**

**`in a fusion power plant. Since some of the materials in the fusion`**

**`reactor become radioactive, in order to ensure the environmental`**

**`and human safety, the reactor must be isolated inside a chamber.`**

**`The way to transfer the heat generated in the reactor to the`**

**`surroundings is by means of a heat exchanger system. A steam`**

**`generator, a special kind of heat exchangers, functions not only`**

**`as a bridge in the heat transfer process, but also as one of the`**

**`barriers to the leakage of tritium, a highly dangerous radioactive`**

**`material produced inside the reactor. The heat extracted in the`**

**`steam generator is used to boil the water to create steam which in`**

**`turn runs a traditional turbine to produce electricity.`**

**`1.1`** **`General Description of a steam Generator`**

**`A steam generator is one of the members of the heat exchanger`**

**`family. Like any common heat exchangers, it consists of thousands`**

**`of tubes running inside a cylindrical shell. The primary (hot)`**

**`fluid flows outside the tubes while the secondary (cold) fluid`**

**`flows inside the tubes or vise versa. The heat is transferred from`**

**`the primary fluid to the secondary fluid through the thin tube`**

**`wall. Although a heat exchanger can be parallel flow, counterflow,`**

**`single cross flow or multi-pass cross flow, a steam generator is`**

**`1`**

![](images/tmp_mwsiw1l.pdf-29-full.png)

**`usually a simple counterflow unit. Another difference between a`**

**`steam generator and a common heat exchanger is that water is the`**

**`secondary fluid in a steam generator and will have a phase change`**

**`during its journey though the tubes. First, water enters a steam`**

**`generator as a single phase compressed liquid. When it reaches the`**

**`boiling point, a two phase water vapor mixture is formed inside the`**

**`tubes. After all the liquid vaporizes, superheated steam is`**

**`produced. Because of these different phases involved, the heat`**

**`transfer mechanisms insides a steam generator are so complicated`**

**`that it becomes a separate field of`** **`engineering.`**

**`1.2`** **`Steam Generator Design`**

**`There are several steam generator design proposals suggested`**

**`by previous studies on fast breeder reactor power plants as listed`**

**`on TABLE 1-1. Based on the simple construction and the completeness`**

**`of the information in the open literature, the hockey stick steam`**

**`generator, which was studied and was designed by Atomic`**

**`International (AI) and General Electric (GE) for the Clinch River`**

**`Breeder Reactor (CRBR), has been selected for the HYLIFE-II fusion`**

**`power plant.`**

**`The original design of the hockey stick steam generator for`**

**`the CRBR consisted of three sets of steam generator systems with`**

**`a total thermal load of 975 MW.`** **`Each set consisted of three`**

**`identical hockey stick steam generators, two evaporators and one`**

**`superheater, with a total thermal rating of 325 MW. The evaporator`**

**`and superheater were identical in design to minimize cost and spare`**

**`parts. They were shell and tube counterflow heat exchangers that`**

**`2`**

![](images/tmp_mwsiw1l.pdf-30-full.png)

**`were vertical except for a 90 degrees bend at the top. The height`**

**`of each hockey stick steam generator was 19.8 m with an outer shell`**

**`diameter of 1.32 m. There were 757 tubes running inside the shell.`**

**`The tubes were arranged in triangle pattern and were made of`**

**`2 1/4-Cr-l Mo steel. Sodium was the primary fluid which flowed`**

**`downward through the shell and the water/steam flowed upward`**

**`through the tubes. This arrangement, as mentioned by GE study,`**

**`provided excellent natural convection flow for decay heat removal`**

**`purpose.`**

**`A scaled-up version of the CRBR hockey stick steam generator`**

**`design will be adopted for the HYLIFE-II study. More information`**

**`will be discussed in chapter 4.`**

**`Chapter 4`** **`HYLIFE-II Steam Generator`**

**`After the careful study of the CRBR study, the hockey stick`**

**`steam generator system was adopted for the HYLIFE-II study. The`**

**`4.1`** **`Modifications of the Steam Generator`**

**`The thermal capacity of the HYLIFE-II is 3312 MW, three times`**

**`as much of the CRBR, so there will be either three times the number`**

**`of steam generator systems used in the CRBR or three times the size`**

**`for the same number (three) of steam generator systems in the CRBR.`**

**`After careful study of the alternatives based on the size and cost`**

**`of the steam generators, three sets of steam generator systems with`**

**`the size upgraded from the CRBR will be used in the HYLIFE-II.`**

**`Another modification is the material used to construct the`**

**`steam generator. Sodium fluoroborate, the primary fluid in the`**

**`steam generator,`** **`is a molten salt which is corrosive to`**

**`2 1/4 Cr-1 No steel. As a result, an alloy which can resist the`**

**`corrosion of the salt will be necessary. From the previous study`**

**`of the Molten Salt Breeder Reactor (MSBR) report (Rosenthal,`**

**`Haubenreich, and Briggs,`** **`1972), Hastelloy N was found to be the`**

**`best candidate for handling the sodium fluoroborate. However, as`**

**`the MSBR pointed out, a more detailed study of the Hastelloy N was`**

**`necessary, especially when water moisture was present in the salt.`**

**`Based on the available information, Hastelloy N has been chosen to`**

**`22`**

![](images/tmp_mwsiw1l.pdf-50-full.png)

**`construct the steam generator in the HYLIFE-II. However, if further`**

**`research indicates that a modified 316 stainless steel is adequate,`**

**`then this less expensive material will be adopted.`**

**`4.2`** **`Evaporator`**

**`There are two evaporators in each set of steam generator`**

**`systems for the HYLIFE-II. Similar to the design of the CRBR, the`**

**`generator with a quality of 0.5. The vapor mixture then enters the`**

**`steam separator which will be discussed in more detail in next`**

**`around the tube bundles. FIGURE 4-1 shows the systematic flow chart`**

**`pumped into the superheater which will be discussed in more detail`**
