# Polywell

The **polywell** is a proposed design for a [fusion reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_reactor) using an electric and magnetic field to heat ions to fusion conditions.

The design is related to the [fusor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusor), the [high beta fusion reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_beta_fusion_reactor), the [magnetic mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_mirror), and the [biconic cusp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biconic_cusp). A set of [electromagnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnets) generates a magnetic field that traps [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrons). This creates a negative voltage, which attracts positive [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ions). As the ions accelerate towards the negative center, their [kinetic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy) rises. Ions that collide at high enough energies can [fuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion).

## Mechanism

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=1)]

### Fusor heating

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=2)]

A Farnsworth-Hirsch [fusor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusor) consists of two wire cages, one inside the other, often referred to as grids, that are placed inside a [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) chamber. The outer cage has a positive [voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage) relative to the inner cage. A fuel, typically, [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium) gas, is injected into this chamber. It is heated past its [ionization temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionization_energy), making positive [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion). The ions are positive and move towards the negative inner cage. Those that miss the wires of the inner cage fly through the center of the device at high speeds and can fly out the other side of the inner cage. As the ions move outward, a [Coulomb force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb%27s_law) impels them back towards the center. Over time, a core of ionized gas can form inside the inner cage. Ions pass back and forth through the core until they strike either the grid or another nucleus. Most nucleus strikes do not result in fusion. Grid strikes can raise the temperature of the grid as well as eroding it. These strikes [conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conduction) mass and energy away from the plasma, as well as spall off metal ions into the gas, which cools it.

In fusors, the [potential well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_well) is made with a wire cage. Because most of the ions and electrons fall onto the cage, fusors suffer from high [conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistivity_and_conductivity) losses. Hence, no fusor has come close to energy break-even.

### Diamagnetic plasma trapping

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=3)]

The Polywell is attempting to hold a [diamagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamagnetic) plasma - a material which rejects the outside magnetic fields created by the electromagnets.

- Most plasma in most fusion reactors (such as
[Magnetic mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_mirror),[tokamaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokamak)and[Stellarators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellarator)) are considered[magnetized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics)#Magnetization). A Magnetized plasma occurs when the external field is so strong that it completely penetrates and controls the plasma, such that the material behavior is dominated by the external field. - Some fusion plasmas are self-magnetized (such as
[field-reversed configurations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field-reversed_configuration), or[Dynomaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynomak)) all of which can create their own weak magnetic fields through the formation of loops of plasma currents and other structures.

Both the Polywell and the [high beta fusion reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_beta_fusion_reactor) pre-suppose that the plasma self-generated field is so strong that it will reject the outside field. Bussard later called this type of confinement the *Wiffle-Ball*. This analogy was used to describe electron trapping inside the field. Marbles can be trapped inside a [Wiffle ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiffle_ball), a hollow, perforated sphere; if marbles are put inside, they can roll and sometimes escape through the holes in the sphere. The magnetic topology of a high-beta polywell acts similarly with electrons. In June 2014 EMC2 published a preprint [3] providing (1) x-ray and (2) flux loop measurements that the diamagnetic effect will impact the external field.

According to Bussard, typical cusp leakage rate is such that an electron makes 5 to 8 passes before escaping through a cusp in a standard mirror confinement biconic cusp; 10 to 60 passes in a polywell under mirror confinement (low beta) that he called cusp confinement; and several thousand passes in Wiffle-Ball confinement (high beta).[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-EMC2-0191-02-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-IAC2006-8)

In February 2013, [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) [Skunk Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skunk_Works) announced a new compact fusion machine, the [high beta fusion reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_beta_fusion_reactor),[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-9) [10] that may be related to the biconic cusp and the polywell, and working at

*β*= 1.

## Other trapping mechanisms

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=4)]

### Magnetic mirror

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=5)]

[Magnetic mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_mirror) dominates in low beta designs. Both ions and electrons are reflected from high to low density fields. This is known as the magnetic mirror effect. [11] The polywell's rings are arranged so the densest fields are on the outside, trapping electrons in the center. This can trap particles at low beta values.

### Cusp confinement

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=6)]

In high beta conditions, the machine may operate with cusp confinement. [12] This is an improvement over the simpler magnetic mirror.

The MaGrid has six point cusps, each located in the middle of a ring; and two highly modified line cusps, linking the eight corner cusps located at cube vertices. The key is that these two line cusps are much narrower than the single line cusp in magnetic mirror machines, so the net losses are less. The two line cusps losses are similar to or lower than the six face-centered point cusps.

[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Spalding_1971-13)In 1955,

[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-patent1989-14)[Harold Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Grad)theorized that a

[high-beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_(plasma_physics))plasma pressure combined with a cusped magnetic field would improve plasma confinement.

A

[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Grad_1955-5)[diamagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamagnetism)plasma rejects the external fields and plugs the cusps. This system would be a much better trap.

Cusped confinement was explored theoretically [6] and experimentally.

However, most cusped experiments failed and disappeared from national programs by 1980.

[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-15)### Beta in magnetic traps

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=7)]

Magnetic fields exert a pressure on the plasma. Beta is the ratio of plasma pressure to the magnetic field strength. It can be defined separately for electrons and ions. The polywell concerns itself only for the electron beta, whereas the ion beta is of greater interest within Tokamak and other neutral-plasma machines. The two vary by a very large ratio, because of the enormous difference in mass between an electron and any ion. Typically, in other devices the electron beta is neglected, as the ion beta determines more important plasma parameters. This is a significant point of confusion for scientists more familiar with more 'conventional' fusion plasma physics.

Note that for the electron beta, only the electron [number density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_density) and temperature are used, as both of these, but especially the latter, can vary significantly from the ion parameters at the same location.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-16)


Most experiments on polywells involve [low-beta plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_(plasma_physics)) regimes (where *β* < 1), [17] where the plasma

[pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure)is weak compared to the

[magnetic pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_pressure). Several models describe magnetic trapping in polywells.

[Tests indicated that plasma confinement is enhanced in a magnetic cusp configuration when β (plasma pressure/magnetic field pressure) is of order unity. This enhancement is required for a fusion power reactor based on cusp confinement to be feasible.

*]*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)

[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-18)## Design

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=8)]

The main problem with the [fusor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusor) is that the inner cage conducts away too much energy and mass. The solution, suggested by [Robert Bussard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bussard) and [Oleg Lavrentiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Lavrentiev), [19] was to replace the negative cage with a "virtual cathode" made of a cloud of electrons.

A polywell consists of several parts. These are put inside a vacuum chamber[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-patent1992-20)

- A set of positively charged
[electromagnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnet)coils arranged in a[polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedron). The most common arrangement is a six sided[cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube). The six[magnetic poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet)are pointing in the same direction toward the center. The magnetic field vanishes at the center by symmetry, creating a null point. [Electron guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_gun)facing ring axis. These shoot electrons into the center of the ring structure. Once inside, the electrons are confined by the magnetic fields. This has been measured in polywells using[Langmuir probes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langmuir_probe).[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-quasi-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-google-22)Electrons that have enough energy to escape through the magnetic cusps can be re-attracted to the positive rings. They can slow down and return to the inside of the rings along the cusps. This reduces[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Carr_thesis_2013-4)[conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conduction)losses, and improves the overall performance of the machine.The electrons act as a negative[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-23)[voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_potential)drop attracting positive ions. This is a virtual[cathode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode).- Gas puffers at corner. Gas is puffed inside the rings where it ionizes at the electron cloud. As ions fall down the potential well, the electric field
[works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_(physics))on them, heating it to fusion conditions. The ions build up speed. They can slam together in the center and fuse. Ions are electrostatically confined raising the density and increasing the fusion rate.

The magnetic energy density required to confine electrons is far smaller than that required to directly confine ions, as is done in other fusion projects such as [ITER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITER).[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-quasi-21)[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-tech-24)[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-tech2-25)

## Other behavior

### Single-electron motion

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=10)]

As an electron enters a magnetic field, it feels a [Lorentz force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_force) and corkscrews. The radius of this motion is the [gyroradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroradius). As it moves it loses some energy as [x-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremsstrahlung), every time it changes speed. The electron spins faster and tighter in denser fields, as it enters the MaGrid. Inside the MaGrid, single electrons travel straight through the null point, due to their infinite [gyroradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroradius) in regions of no magnetic field. Next, they head towards the edges of the MaGrid field and corkscrew tighter along the denser magnetic field lines.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Low_beta_confinement_in_a_Polywell_modeled_with_conventional_point_cusp_theories-17) [26] This is typical

[electron cyclotron resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_cyclotron_resonance)motion. Their

[gyroradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroradius)shrinks and when they hit a dense magnetic field they can be reflected using the magnetic mirror effect.

[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-ReferenceA-28)Electron trapping has been measured in polywells with

[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-P_Chernnin_1978-29)[Langmuir probes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langmuir_probe).

[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-google-22)

[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Carr_thesis_2013-4)The polywell attempts to confine the [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion) and [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) through two different means, borrowed from [fusors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusor) and [magnetic mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_mirror). The electrons are easier to confine magnetically because they have so much less mass than the ions. [30] The machine confines ions using an

[electric field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_field)in the same way a fusor confines the ions: in the polywell, the ions are attracted to the negative electron cloud in the center. In the fusor, they are attracted to a negative wire cage in the center.

### Plasma recirculation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=11)]

Plasma recirculation would significantly improve the function of these machines. It has been argued that efficient recirculation is the only way they can be viable.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31) [32] Electrons or ions move through the device without striking a surface, reducing

[conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conduction)losses. Bussard stressed this; specifically emphasizing that electrons need to move through all cusps of the machine.

### Fuel type

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=14)]

[Nuclear fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion) refers to [nuclear reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reaction) that combine lighter [nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus) to become heavier nuclei. All [chemical elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element) can be fused; for elements with fewer protons than iron, this process changes [mass into energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass%E2%80%93energy_equivalence) that can potentially be captured to provide [fusion power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_power).

The [probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability) of a fusion reaction occurring is controlled by the [cross section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_section_(physics)) of the fuel, [38] which is in turn a function of its temperature. The easiest nuclei to fuse are

[deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium)and

[tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium). Their fusion occurs when the ions reach 4 keV (

[kiloelectronvolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiloelectronvolt)), or about 45 million

[kelvins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin). The Polywell would achieve this by accelerating an ion with a charge of

[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_charge)down a 4,000 volt electric field. The high cost, short

[half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-life)and

[radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive)of

[tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium)make it difficult to work with.

The second easiest reaction is to fuse [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium) with itself. Because of its low cost, deuterium is commonly used by Fusor amateurs. Bussard's polywell experiments were performed using this fuel. Fusion of deuterium or tritium produces a fast neutron, and therefore produces radioactive waste. Bussard's choice was to fuse [boron-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boron-11) with protons; this reaction is [aneutronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aneutronic_fusion) (does not produce neutrons). An advantage of p-11B as a fusion fuel is that the primary reactor output would be energetic alpha particles, which can be directly converted to electricity at high efficiency using [direct energy conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_energy_conversion). Direct conversion has achieved a 48% power efficiency [39] against 80–90% theoretical efficiency.

Energy varies with temperature, density, collision speed and fuel. To reach net power production, reactions must occur rapidly enough to make up for energy losses. Plasma clouds lose energy through [conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conduction) and [radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation). [40] Conduction is when

[ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion),

- Net power – power output
- Efficiency – fraction of energy needed to drive the device and convert it to electricity.
- Fusion – energy generated by the fusion reactions.
- Radiation – energy lost as light, leaving the plasma.
- Conduction – energy lost, as mass leaves the plasma.

Lawson used this equation to estimate conditions for net power [40] based on a

## Criticism

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=16)]

There are several general criticisms of the Polywell:

- The heating mechanism breaks the quasi-neutral assumption. It is not easy or possible to concentrate negative charge robustly or for any long period.
- The plasma does not behave diamagnetically as presupposed. This challenges the basic trapping effect.
- Without a solid heating method, the plasma loses huge amounts of energy to radiation, becoming too cold to fuse (see Rider work below).
- With ions flying in from all directions, there is a buildup in angular momentum, leading to lots of ions being scattered out of the trap (see Nevins' work below).

### Rider critique

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=17)]

Todd Rider (a biological engineer and former student of plasma physics) [41] calculated that

[X-ray radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremsstrahlung)losses with this fuel would exceed fusion power production by at least 20%. Rider's model used the following assumptions:


[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31)

[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Thesis-32)- The plasma was
[quasineutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics)). Therefore, positives and negatives equally mixed together.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31) - The fuel was evenly mixed throughout the volume.
[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31) - The plasma was isotropic, meaning that its behavior was the same in any given direction.
[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31) - The plasma had a uniform energy and temperature throughout the cloud.
[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31) - The plasma was an unstructured Gaussian sphere, with a strongly converged core that represented a small (~1%) part of the total volume.
Nevins challenged this assumption, stating that the particles would build up[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31)[angular momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_momentum), causing the dense core to degrade.The loss of density inside the core would reduce fusion rates.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Nevins95-42) - The potential well was broad and flat.
[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31)

Based on these assumptions, Rider used general equations [43] to estimate the rates of different physical effects. These included the loss of ions to up-scattering, the ion thermalization rate, the energy loss due to

[X-ray radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremsstrahlung)and the fusion rate.

His conclusions were that the device suffered from "fundamental flaws".

[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31)

[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Rider95Paper-31)By contrast, Bussard argued [8] that the plasma had a different structure, temperature distribution and well profile. These characteristics have not been fully measured and are central to the device's feasibility. Bussard's calculations indicated that the

[bremsstrahlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremsstrahlung)losses would be much smaller.


[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-EMC2-0891-04-44)According to Bussard the high speed and therefore low cross section for

[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-EMC2-1291-02-45)[Coulomb collisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb_collision)of the ions in the core makes

[thermalizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermalisation)collisions very unlikely, while the low speed at the rim means that thermalization there has almost no impact on ion velocity in the core.


[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-EMC2-0890-03-46)Bussard calculated that a polywell reactor with a radius of 1.5 meters would produce net power fusing

[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-EMC2-1090-03-47)[deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium).


[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-48)Other studies disproved some of the assumptions made by Rider and Nevins, arguing the real fusion rate and the associated recirculating power (needed to overcome the thermalizing effect and sustain the non-Maxwellian ion profile) could be estimated only with a self-consistent collisional treatment of the ion distribution function, lacking in Rider's work.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Chacon_2000-49)

### Energy capture

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=18)]

It has been proposed that energy may be extracted from polywells using [heat capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rankine_cycle) or, in the case of aneutronic fusion like D-3He or *p*-11B, [direct energy conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_energy_conversion), though that scheme faces challenges. The energetic alpha particles (up to a few MeV) generated by the aneutronic fusion reaction would exit the MaGrid through the six axial cusps as cones (spread ion beams). Direct conversion collectors inside the vacuum chamber would convert the alpha particles' kinetic energy to a [high-voltage direct current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-voltage_direct_current). The alpha particles must slow down before they contact the collector plates to realize high conversion efficiency. [50] In experiments, direct conversion has demonstrated a conversion efficiency of 48%.


[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-51)## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=19)]

In the late 1960s several investigations studied polyhedral magnetic fields as a possibility to confine a fusion plasma.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-52) [53] The first proposal to combine this configuration with an electrostatic potential well in order to improve electron confinement was made by

[Oleg Lavrentiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Lavrentiev)in 1975.

The idea was picked up by

[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Lavrentiev-19)[Robert Bussard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bussard)in 1983. His 1989 patent application cited Lavrentiev,

although in 2006 he appears to claim to have (re)discovered the idea independently.

Research was funded first by the [Defense Threat Reduction Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Threat_Reduction_Agency) beginning in 1987 and later by [DARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA).[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-google-22): 32:30 This funding resulted in a machine known as the high energy power source (HEPS) experiment. [magnetic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field)sent

Starting in 1999, the company was primarily funded by the US Navy.

[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-quick-54)

[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-quick-54)WB-1 had six conventional [magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet) in a [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube) 10 cm across. [54] WB-2 used coils of wires to generate the magnetic field. Each

[electromagnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnet)had a square cross section that created problems. The

[magnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic)fields drove electrons into the metal rings, raising conduction losses and electron trapping. This design also suffered from "funny cusp" losses at the joints between magnets. WB-6 attempted to address these problems, by using circular rings and spacing further apart.

The next device, PXL-1, built in 1996–1997, used flatter rings 26 cm across to generate the field.

[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-google-22)From 1998–2005, the company built a succession of six machines: WB-3, MPG-1,2, WB-4, PZLx-1, MPG-4 and WB-5. All of these reactors were six magnet designs built as a cube or

[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-quick-54)[truncated cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_cube). Their radius ranged from 3 to 40 cm.


[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-quick-54)Initial difficulties in spherical electron confinement led to the research project's 2005 termination. However, Bussard reported a fusion rate of 109 per second running D-D fusion reactions at only 12.5 kV (based on [detecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_detection) nine neutrons in five tests,[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-IAC2006-8) [57] giving a wide

[confidence interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval)). He stated that the fusion rate achieved by WB-6 was roughly 100,000 times greater than what Farnsworth achieved at similar well depth and drive conditions.


[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-fusor.net-58)By comparison, researchers at

[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-randi-59)[University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison)reported a neutron rate of up to 5 billion per second at voltages of 120 kV from an electrostatic fusor without magnetic fields.


[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-60)Bussard asserted, by using [superconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductor) coils, that the only significant energy loss channel is through electron losses proportional to the surface area. He also stated that the density would scale with the square of the field (constant [beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_(plasma_physics)) conditions), and the maximum attainable magnetic field would scale with the radius. Under those conditions, the fusion power produced would scale with [the seventh power of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_law) the radius, and the energy gain would scale with the fifth power. While Bussard did not publicly document the reasoning underlying this estimate, [61] if true, it would enable a model only ten times larger to be useful as a fusion power plant.


[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-IAC2006-8)#### WB-6

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=22)]

Funding became tighter and tighter. According to [Bussard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bussard), "The funds were clearly needed for the more important [War in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Invasion_of_Iraq)." [59] An extra $900k of

[Office of Naval Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Naval_Research)funding allowed the program to continue long enough to reach WB-6 testing in November 2005. WB-6 had rings with circular cross sections that space apart at the joints. This reduced the metal surface area unprotected by magnetic fields. These changes dramatically improved system performance, leading to more electron recirculation and better electron confinement, in a progressively tighter core. This machine produced a fusion rate of 10

9per second. This is based on a total of nine neutrons in five tests, giving a wide confidence interval.


[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-IAC2006-8)Drive voltage on the WB-6 tests was about 12.5 kV, with a resulting potential well depth of about 10 kV.

[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-lab_notes-57)Thus deuterium ions could have a maximum of 10 keV of kinetic energy in the center. By comparison, a

[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-IAC2006-8)[Fusor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusor)running deuterium fusion at 10 kV would produce a fusion rate almost too small to detect.

[Hirsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_L._Hirsch)reported a fusion rate this high only by driving his machine with a 150 kV drop between the inside and outside cages.

Hirsch also used

[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-62)[deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium)and

[tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium), a much easier fuel to fuse, because it has a higher

[nuclear cross section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_cross_section).

While the WB-6 pulses were sub-millisecond, Bussard felt the physics should represent steady state. A last-minute test of WB-6 ended prematurely when the insulation on one of the hand-wound [electromagnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnet) burned through, destroying the device.

## University of Sydney experiments

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=38)]

In June 2019, the results of long-running experiments at the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney) (USyd) were published in PhD thesis form by Richard Bowden-Reid. Using an experimental machine built at the university, the team probed the formation of the virtual electrodes.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-bowden-102)

Their work demonstrated that little or no trace of virtual electrode formation could be found. This left a mystery; both their machine and previous experiments showed clear and consistent evidence of the formation of a [potential well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_well) that was trapping ions, which was previously ascribed to the formation of the electrodes. Exploring this problem, Bowden-Reid developed new field equations for the device that explained the potential well without electrode formation, and demonstrated that this matched both their results and those of previous experiments.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-bowden-102)

Further, exploring the overall mechanism of the virtual electrode concept demonstrated that its interactions with the ions and itself would make it "leak" at a furious rate. Assuming plasma densities and energies required for net energy production, it was calculated that new electrons would have to be supplied at an unfeasible rate of 200,000 [amps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampere).[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-bowden-102)

Initial results indicate negligible charge trapping with little to no potential well formation. Further, it is shown that the existence of potential wells reported in previous publications can be explained without the requirement of a virtual cathode produced by trapped electrons. Moreover, it is shown that potential wells, which produce electron confinement and heating from virtual cathodes, no longer exist with increasing plasma density.

The proposed device had the unusual property that it can avoid both the cusp losses of traditional magnetic fusion systems and the grid losses of traditional IEC configurations.[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-112)

### Fusion One Corporation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polywell&action=edit§ion=48)]

Fusion One Corporation was a US organization founded by Dr. Paul Sieck (former Lead Physicist of EMC2), Dr. Scott Cornish of the University of Sydney, and Randall Volberg. It ran from 2015 to 2017. They developed a magneto-electrostatic reactor named "F1" that was based in-part on the polywell. It introduced a system of externally mounted electromagnet coils with internally mounted cathode repeller surfaces to provide a means of preserving energy and particle losses that would otherwise be lost through the magnetic cusps. In response to Todd Rider's 1995 power balance conclusions, a new analytical model was developed based on this recovery function as well as a more accurate quantum relativistic treatment of the bremsstrahlung losses that was not present in Rider's analysis. Version 1 of the analytical model was developed by Senior Theoretical Physicist Dr Vladimir Mirnov and demonstrated ample multiples of net gain with D-T and sufficient multiples with D-D to be used for generating electricity. These preliminary results were presented at the ARPA-E ALPHA 2017 Annual Review Meeting. [129] Phase 2 of the model removed key assumptions in the Rider analysis by incorporating a self-consistent treatment of the ion energy distribution (Rider assumed a purely Maxwellian distribution) and the power required to maintain the distribution and ion population. The results yielded an energy distribution that was non-thermal but more Maxwellian than monoenergetic. The input power required to maintain the distribution was calculated to be excessive and ion-ion thermalization was a dominant loss channel. With these additions, a pathway to commercial electricity generation was no longer feasible.
