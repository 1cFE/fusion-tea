# Iter-02 research notes — ENN EHL-2 magnet & energy capture

## Magnet Type
- EXL-50U (predecessor, ENN Research site): "toroidal field (TF) coils operated stably at 150 kA, generating 1.2 T magnetic fields." A 150 kA TF current at 1.2 T at this device scale is consistent with copper/Bitter-style resistive coils; HTS tokamak coils operate at higher engineering current densities and much higher fields.
- EHL-2 physics design paper (PST, doi:10.1088/2058-6272/ad981a) mentions "main magnet system and vacuum vessel structure" but does NOT specify HTS, Nb3Sn, or copper in retrievable abstract/preview. Full PDF was not parseable via WebFetch.
- No ENN publication or press release found stating HTS/superconducting magnets for EHL-2. The arXiv roadmap (2401.11338) and ENN Research EHL-2 page describe the device parameters (R₀≈1.05 m, B₀≈3 T, Ip≈3 MA) without naming the conductor technology.
- Best inference: EHL-2 uses **resistive (copper)** TF/PF coils. Confidence raised modestly from "low" to "low–medium" by the EXL-50U precedent (150 kA TF current) and absence of any HTS announcement.

## Energy Capture
- ENN Research Compact Fusion page (https://en.ennresearch.com/researchfield/Compactfusion/) explicitly states the p-11B fuel cycle "offers direct energy conversion capability for higher efficiency, produces minimal neutron radiation, and enables distributed power generation."
- arXiv 2401.11338 frames the roadmap as commercial p-B11 fusion, with EHL-2 as the physics-verification step.
- No detailed engineering for a direct converter (electrostatic DEC, ICC, etc.) is published. EHL-2 itself has no power-conversion system.
- Best inference: **Direct (charged particle)** is consistent with both the aneutronic fuel and ENN's stated roadmap. Confidence raised from "low" to "medium".
