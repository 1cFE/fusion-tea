# Aussie nuclear firm joins world’s largest laser lab to propel unique fusion power

HB11 Energy strategy is a drastic departure from the usual nuclear fusion process, which normally involves heating hydrogen isotopes to millions of degrees.

Australian nuclear fusion energy startup HB11 Energy has signed a deal with a top laser research facility to advance fusion energy research.

The Sydney-based firm agreed with ELI ERIC European Research Infrastructure Consortium, the world’s largest high-power laser research infrastructure provider, to collaborate on advancing fusion technology, according to a press release on Monday.

“ELI ERIC has unique laser capabilities that are not available in Australia, and which are essential to advancing fusion into a clean energy technology,” Dr. Warren McKenzie, Managing Director HB11 Energy, said in the press release.

“ELI ERIC will leverage HB11 Energy’s strength as a supplier of micro-fabricated laser targets and fusion materials that will expand the scientific possibilities of both ELI ERIC and HB11 Energy.”

The agreement, per the press statement, could play a key role in the future of the $12.5 billion global fusion energy market.

## HB11 Energy’s unique fusion approach

In 2022, HB11 accomplished a watershed moment by exhibiting a word-first number of fusion reactions, creating ten times more than previously projected at the same facility.

The experiment demonstrated the non-thermal fusion of hydrogen and [boron-11](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/boron-nuclear-reactors-stop-energy-loss) using high-power lasers- a method anticipated in the 1970s by Australian theoretical physicist and HB11 co-founder Professor Heinrich Hora, HB11 Energy.

This strategy is a drastic departure from the usual [nuclear fusion ](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/laser-cuts-100mm-steel-for-safe-nuclear-cleanup)process, which normally involves heating hydrogen isotopes to millions of degrees.

“The [experiment] results show great potential for clean energy generation: hydrogen-boron reactions use fuels that are safe and abundant and don’t create neutrons in the primary reaction,” *Interesting Engineering* [reported](https://interestingengineering.com/science/non-thermal-nuclear-fusion) in 2022.

The elements used in the research “cause insignificant amounts of short-lived waste and can provide large-scale power for base-load grid electricity or hydrogen generation.”

Utilizing hydrogen-boron pellets, the company’s power plant concept produces energy at a commercial level by powering a traditional steam cycle engine and recycling energy to keep the fusion process going.

“HB11’s approach to fusion does away with rare, [radioactive](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/france-plans-to-turn-nuclear-waste-into-forks), and difficult fuels like tritium altogether – as well as those incredibly high temperatures,” according to a *New Atlas* [report](https://newatlas.com/energy/hb11-hydrogen-boron-fusion-clean-energy/).

And the partnership with ELI ERIC is a step toward making this unique concept commercially viable. ELI ERIC runs state-of-the-art laser facilities in Romania, Hungary, and the Czech Republic.

## ELI ERIC partnership on advancing fusion power

ELI ERIC collaborates with HB11 Energy’s innovative laser fusion approach to create and produce micro-structured laser targets. These targets will facilitate experiments to improve fusion technology, high energy density physics, and other applications.

The parties broadly agreed to work on developing and manufacturing high-precision targets for high-power laser-matter interaction experiments and develop innovative diagnostic tools to analyze plasma properties in laser-matter interaction and fusion-related experiments.

They also agreed to conduct joint experimental campaigns at the ELI Beamlines facility.

“This agreement with HB11 Energy showcases how ELI ERIC’s facilities can drive scientific progress and foster innovation in the pursuit of sustainable energy solutions and advanced laser technologies,” said Roman Hvězda, Director of ELI Beamlines facility.

The alliance also seeks to foster scientific progress, the professional development of young researchers, and the publication of high-quality joint research, noted the [press release](https://hb11.energy/2024/12/16/hb11-energy-signs-agreement-with-worlds-largest-high-power-laser-infrastructure-provider-for-fusion-technology-development/).
