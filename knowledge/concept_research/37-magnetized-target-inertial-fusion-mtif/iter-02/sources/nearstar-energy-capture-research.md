# NearStar Fusion — Energy Capture research notes (iter-02)

## Key finding

NearStar's public messaging emphasizes retrofitting **existing hydrocarbon (coal) power plant heat sources** with the MTIF fusion core, leveraging the existing **turbines and grid infrastructure**.

Quote (paraphrased from nearstarfusion.com / learn-more, surfaced via web search 2026-05-17):
> "The modular NearStar Fusion approach is able to retrofit the heat source in traditional hydrocarbon (e.g., coal) power plants with a fusion power core to leverage existing turbines and power grid infrastructure."

Also: "pulsed plasma operation coupled with a liquid first wall dramatically simplifies plasma formation, control and thermal extraction".

## Interpretation

- Coal plant turbines are **steam Rankine cycle** turbines. Retrofit framing strongly implies NearStar intends steam Rankine power conversion (matching legacy thermal plant balance-of-plant).
- Molten Pb first wall = primary heat sink / intermediate loop; couples to steam generator → existing steam turbine.
- No mention of sCO2 Brayton, direct conversion, or any non-thermal pathway.

## Schema mapping

- **Energy Capture**: `Thermal (steam)` — confidence **medium-high** (was low/TBD).
  - Rationale: company explicitly markets brownfield coal-plant retrofit using "existing turbines"; coal turbines are steam Rankine. Not a peer-reviewed disclosure of cycle choice, but a direct company marketing statement about the conversion architecture.
