# Shielding Materials in the Compact Spherical Tokamak

## Source
Humphry-Baker & Smith (2019), Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A, 377(2141)
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6365859/
- Co-author George D W Smith is from Tokamak Energy Ltd

## Key Findings

### Space Constraints
- Central column region: only ~0.32 m radial space for neutron shielding
- Device studied: 1.35 m major radius, 0.75 m minor radius (smaller than ST-E1)
- Optimal design: five concentric annular shields, 5.4 cm thickness each, 1.25 cm cooling channels

### Recommended Materials
- **Tungsten carbide (WC) and WC-based cermets** preferred over pure tungsten
- Specific candidate: WC-FeCr (tungsten carbide with iron-chromium binder)
- Advantages:
  - Flexural strength 10× higher than pure W
  - 3-4× better thermal shock resistance
  - Better high-temperature hardness

### Neutron Flux
- Predicted fast neutron flux (E > 0.1 MeV) into SC core: ~1.4 × 10^17 s^-1 m^-2 through 32 cm WC shield

### Center Stack Challenge
- Most spatially restricted component in spherical tokamak
- Must protect HTS tapes from radiation damage and associated heating
- Cryogenic system energy consumption must be kept acceptable

### Research Gaps
- Irradiation damage behavior of WC cermets under fusion neutrons not well characterized
- Hydrogen trapping effects need study
