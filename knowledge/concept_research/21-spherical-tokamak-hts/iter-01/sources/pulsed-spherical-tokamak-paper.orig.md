# Pulsed Spherical Tokamak — A New Approach to Fusion Reactors

## Source
- MDPI journal paper: https://www.mdpi.com/2571-6182/5/2/19
- Authors include Tokamak Energy researchers (Gryaznevich et al.)

## Key Findings
- Pulsed spherical tokamak reactors are competitive with and more desirable than steady-state ST reactors
- Pulsed operation advantage is even more pronounced for spherical tokamaks vs conventional tokamaks
- Starting point for reactor-relevant pulses: tens of minutes
- Pulsed operation reduces requirements on central solenoid — CS only needs to sustain plasma current at flat-top
- HTS central solenoid can provide order-of-magnitude higher magnetic field and flux for inductive current drive

## Operation Mode
- Pulsed, with pulse lengths of tens of minutes (quasi-steady in some classifications)
- ST80-HTS targets up to 15 minute pulses
- Not truly steady-state — the limited CS in spherical tokamaks constrains pulse length
- But ECRH-assisted non-inductive current drive could extend pulses

## Implications for Schema
- Operation mode: "Pulsed" (but long pulses, minutes-scale, making it borderline "Quasi-steady")
- Repetition rate: N/A for long-pulse operation (not a high-rep-rate pulsed concept)
