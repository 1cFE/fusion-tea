## Company
- Founded: 2009, spin-out from UK Atomic Energy Authority (UKAEA)
- HQ: Near Oxford, UK; subsidiaries in US and Japan
- Staff: ~280 people
- Total funding: $335M ($275M private, $60M government UK+US)
- Selected for US DOE Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program (May 2023)

## Core Technology Approach
- Combines spherical tokamak geometry with HTS (REBCO) magnets
- Low aspect ratio → higher plasma beta, more compact
- HTS magnets: wound from REBCO tape (12mm wide, <0.1mm thick)
- World record: 24 Tesla at 20K (2020)
- HTS magnets >5x more energy-efficient than conventional superconductors

## Machines

### ST40 (operational)
- World's highest-field spherical tokamak
- Achieved 100 million °C plasma ion temperature (March 2022)
- Plasma volume: <1 cubic meter (15x less than other tokamaks reaching 100M°C)
- Highest triple product of any private fusion company
- Heating: NBI (2 MW) + ECRH (1 MW gyrotron from Kyoto Fusioneering, installed 2025)
  - Gyrotron: 1 MW RF power, tunable at 104 GHz or 137 GHz (matching electron cyclotron resonance)
  - Plans to use NBI and ECRH simultaneously

### ST80-HTS (planned, build completion ~2026)
- World's first high-field spherical tokamak with large-scale HTS magnets
- Target: replicate 100M°C plasma with extended pulse durations up to 15 minutes
- Higher sustained triple product than any previous device
- Purpose: demonstrate spherical tokamak operations, inform ST-E1 design

### ST-E1 (pilot plant, mid-2030s target)
- Earlier specs (2023): aspect ratio 2.0, major radius 4.25m, B=4.25T, 800 MW fusion / 85 MW net electric
- Revision D (DPP 2025): major radius 5.0m, aspect ratio 2.3, on-axis B=5.25T, 450-750 MW net electric
- Outboard-only liquid lithium breeder blanket, TBR = 1.2
- Maintenance scheme integrated early in design

### Demo4 (HTS magnet demonstrator)
- World-first HTS fusion magnet system
