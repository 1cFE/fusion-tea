## Hybrid Direct Drive (HDD) Target Physics

Xcimer published a peer-reviewed paper in Physics of Plasmas introducing a novel "hybrid direct drive" (HDD) approach using a two-sided ultraviolet laser.

### How HDD Works (Two-Stage Process)

1. **First pulse (P1)**: A short laser pulse burns through a thin window and heats a high or mid-Z lined cavity (hohlraum) to ~10⁴ eV. The x-rays generated ablate the capsule and form a thick plasma atmosphere. The hohlraum and capsule radii ratio is similar to indirect drive.

2. **Subsequent pulses**: The capsule is driven directly by later pulses. Because of the thick plasma atmosphere created by P1, the laser is absorbed in a large volume of underdense plasma — improving coupling efficiency.

### Key Distinction from Pure Indirect/Direct Drive

- **Still uses a hohlraum** — but primarily for the first pulse (x-ray generation to form plasma atmosphere)
- **Most energy couples directly** to the capsule (>90% per earlier Xcimer claims)
- **Two-sided illumination** — only two beam penetrations needed in the chamber
- Addresses inefficiencies of both traditional direct-drive (uniformity) and indirect-drive (hohlraum losses)
- Improves compression symmetry and enables higher target gain

### Classification Implications

HDD is a true hybrid — it uses a hohlraum but in a different way than NIF indirect drive. The first pulse is indirect (laser → hohlraum → x-rays → capsule atmosphere), but the main drive is direct. This blurs the indirect/direct boundary. For schema purposes, classified under "Laser ICF (indirect drive)" due to NIF heritage and hohlraum presence, but a "hybrid drive" category may be warranted.
