# Polywell Technical Details (Aggregated from Multiple Sources)

## Physics Principle

- Portmanteau of "polyhedral cusp" + "electrostatic potential well" (Bussard, 1985)
- Combines inertial electrostatic confinement (IEC) with high-beta magnetic cusp confinement
- Electromagnet coils arranged in polyhedral configuration (typically 6-sided cube)
- Electron beams injected, confined magnetically by cusp fields
- Confined electrons create deep negative electrostatic potential well
- Ions attracted into well, accelerated to fusion energies
- High-beta cusp theory: at high β, sharp boundary forms between plasma and magnetic field → substantially smaller loss rates

## Experimental History

- **WB-1 through WB-5**: Early development devices
- **WB-6**: Deuterium fuel, produced ~1 billion neutrons/second at 12.5 kV drive, 10 keV ion energy
- **WB-7**: First plasma Jan 2008, peer-reviewed results (classified under Navy contract)
- **WB-7.1**: Ion gun improvements, longer pulse capability
- **WB-8**: Navy-funded ($7.9M), 0.8 Tesla, measured 6x higher plasma density, but produced NO fusion
- **WB-X**: New device with coaxial plasma gun startup, demonstrated high-beta electron confinement (2013)

## Key Publication

- Park et al., "High-Energy Electron Confinement in a Magnetic Cusp Configuration," Phys. Rev. X 5, 021024 (2015)
- First demonstration of high-β plasma in 6-coil Polywell
- Dramatically improved 7 keV electron beam confinement
- Described as "a major achievement and a prerequisite to concept success"

## Published Reactor Design

- Rogers, J.G., "A Polywell Fusion Reactor Designed for Net Power Generation," J. Fusion Energy 37, 1-17 (2018)
- Simulated p-B11 reactor, required size somewhat larger than ITER for unity power balance
- Steady-state operation reached in few milliseconds via diocotron-pumping
- NOT an EMC2 company publication — independent researcher

## Magnet Type

- Early experiments (WB-1 through WB-8): resistive copper electromagnets
- Pulsed operation required (coils not cooled) — test pulses < 1 ms
- Bussard envisioned superconducting coils for reactor-scale device
- 2012: "work began on building the world's first superconducting Polywell" (outcome unclear)
- WB-8 operated at 0.8 T
- FPNS design: 2-3 T boundary, 4-5 T on coil surface (magnet type not specified)

## Operation Mode

- Experimental devices: pulsed (< 1 ms to ~100 ms pulses)
- Theoretical reactor design: steady-state operation target
- Rogers (2018) simulated steady-state via diocotron-pumping
- Bussard's vision: continuous operation with superconducting coils

## Energy Capture (D-T case)

- D-T version: thermal conversion (neutrons carry 80% of energy)
- Overall thermal efficiency ~40% cited
- p-B11 version: direct conversion of charged alpha particles (~80% efficiency)
- For D-T fuel, no specific blanket/cycle has been published by EMC2

## Funding History

- US Navy: $1.8M (2007), $7.86M (WB-8, 2009), option for $4.46M more
- Total Navy funding: ~$12M
- Navy phased out support
- EMC2 sought $30M commercial program (2014)
- DOE FPNS proposal with SHINE (2023-2025)
- One source states "EMC2 ceased operating in 2019" — but FPNS proposal (2023-2024) suggests revival or continuation
