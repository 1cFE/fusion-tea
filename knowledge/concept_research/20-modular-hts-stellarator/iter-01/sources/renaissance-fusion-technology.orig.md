## Key Specifications

- **Configuration**: Stellarator (quasi-isodynamic, low aspect ratio — "more like cored apples than bicycle tires")
- **Target power**: 1 GW electric
- **Fuel**: Deuterium-Tritium (D-T)
- **Magnetic field**: 10 T (HTS magnets)
- **Magnet technology**: Novel — HTS film directly deposited on cylindrical surfaces, then laser-patterned to create current paths generating 3D stellarator fields. No traditional tape/cable winding.
  - Modular cylinders of ~1 meter diameter
  - Simplified coil-winding surfaces (1D/2D geometry on cylinders)
- **Plasma radius**: ~4 meters (doughnut plasma radius)

## Blanket / Tritium Breeding
- **Liquid metal wall**: Li-LiH, ~35 cm thick (Berkeley seminar) or 32 cm Pb + Li-LiH (journal paper)
  - Also uses GaInSn alloy experimentally
  - Electromagnetic forces levitate and control liquid metal flow
- **Neutron shielding**: Stops 99.99% of neutron energy (company claim)
- **TBR**: ~1.60 (from neutronic analysis paper)
- **Tritium extraction**: Simple precipitation of lithium tritide (LiT)
- **Full radial build**: 32 cm Pb + Li-LiH, 54 cm VH₂ (vanadium hydride), 1.3 m concrete bioshield

## Energy Conversion
- Liquid metal serves as working fluid — extracts heat, transfers to steam turbines
- Combined Brayton-Rankine cycle envisioned
- Liquid metal wall temperature: 700-900°C
- Power conversion efficiency: 51% (cycle), 34% net plant efficiency
- "More efficient than the traditional Rankine cycle of nuclear power plants"

## Distinctive Features
- Only magnetic fusion entity (public or private) claiming liquid metal wall thick enough to stop most neutrons
- Laser-patterned HTS film approach is unique among stellarator companies
- Focus on simplified coil geometry (cylindrical surfaces) vs. complex 3D winding forms
