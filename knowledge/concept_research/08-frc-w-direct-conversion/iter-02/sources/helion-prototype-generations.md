(2025)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-1)[www](https://www.helionenergy.com/) .helionenergy .com**Helion Energy, Inc.** is an American fusion research company located in [Everett, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everett,_Washington). [2] It is developing a

[magneto-inertial fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magneto-inertial_fusion)technology to produce

[fusion power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_power)and

[helium-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium-3)via

[aneutronic fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aneutronic_fusion),


[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Helion-3)which, if successful, would produce low-cost clean electric energy using a fuel that can be derived exclusively from water.

[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-helionenergy.com-5)## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=1)]

The company was founded in 2013 by David Kirtley, John Slough, Chris Pihl, and George Votroubek. [6] The management team were awarded a 2015 ARPA-E ALPHA contract, "Staged Magnetic Compression of FRC Targets to Fusion Conditions".


[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-ARPA-E_2015-7)Helion was one of five finalists for the 2022 * GeekWire* Awards for innovation of the year.

It was one of five finalists for the 2023

[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-8)*GeekWire*Best workplaces of the year.


[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-9)On May 10, 2023, Helion Energy announced that it had agreed to provide 50 [MWe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watt#MWe) to [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft) starting in 2028. [10] This is the first commercial agreement for fusion energy.

Commentators are skeptical.

[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:5-11)

[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-12)In October 2023, Helion and [Nucor Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucor), the largest steel producer and recycler in North America, agreed to develop a 500-MWe fusion power plant at one of Nucor's manufacturing facilities. [13] Their target for beginning operations is 2030.


[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:6-13)Helion was a finalist for the 2025 *GeekWire* Awards in the category of Sustainable Innovation of the Year.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-14)

In 2026, Helion announced that their machine had achieved [D-T fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-T_fusion) and a plasma temperature of 150 million °C.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-15)

## Technology

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=2)]

Helion's approach uses a linear fusion system with pulsed magnetic compression and differs from the traditional design for fusion reactions, which relies on [tokamaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokamak).[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:5-11)

The system is intended to operate at 1 Hz, injecting plasma, compressing it to fusion conditions, expanding it, and recovering the energy to produce electricity. [16] The pulsed-fusion system used is said to be theoretically capable of running 24/7 for electricity production. Due to its compact size, the systems would be able to replace current fossil fuel infrastructure without major needs for investment.


[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:2-17)### Fuel

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=3)]

Helion uses a combination of [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium) and [ 3He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium-3) as fuel. Deuterium and

3He allows mostly

[aneutronic fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aneutronic_fusion), releasing only 5% of its energy in the form of fast

[neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron). Commercial

3He is rare and expensive. Instead Helion plans to produce

3He by

[deuteron–deuteron (D–D)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium_fusion)side reactions to the D–

3He reactions. D–D fusion has an equal chance of producing a

3He atom and of producing a

[tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium)(

3H) atom plus a proton. Tritium beta decays into more

3He with a half-life of 12.32 years. Helion plans to capture the

3He produced this way and reuse it as fuel. Helion has a patent on this process.


[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Google_Patents_2015-18)### Development history

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=4)]

The company's fusion generator is based on the inductive plasmoid accelerator (IPA) experiments performed from 2005 through 2012.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Slough_JFE-19) [20] These experiments used deuterium–deuterium fusion, which produced a 2.45-MeV neutron in half of the reactions. The IPA experiments claimed 300 km/s velocities, deuterium neutron production, and 2-keV deuterium ion temperatures.

Helion and MSNW LLC published articles describing a

[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Slough_IAEA-20)[deuterium–tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium%E2%80%93tritium_fusion)implementation that is the easiest to achieve but generates 14 MeV neutrons. The Helion team published peer-reviewed research demonstrating D–D neutron production in 2011.


[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Slough_IAEA-20)#### 5th prototype, 'Venti'

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=5)]

In 2018, the 5th prototype, Venti, had magnetic fields of 7 T and at high density, an ion temperature of 2 keV. [17] Helion detailed D–D fusion experiments producing neutrons in an October 2018 report at the

[United States Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy)'s

[ARPA-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARPA-E)'s annual ALPHA program meeting.

Experiments that year achieved plasmas with multi-keV temperatures

[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-21)and a

[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Kirtley_Milroy_Votroubek_Slough_2018_p.-22)[triple product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawson_criterion)of 6.4 × 10

3.


[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:1-23)#### 6th prototype, 'Trenta'

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=6)]

In 2021, the firm announced that after a 16-month test cycle with more than 10,000 pulses, its sixth prototype, Trenta, had reached 100 million degrees C, the minimum temperature where a commercial generator could run. [17] This made Helion the first private fusion company to reach these temperatures.

Magnetic compression fields exceeded 8 T, ion temperatures surpassed 8 keV, and electron temperatures exceeded 1 keV.

[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-24)The company further reported ion densities up to 3 × 10

[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-25)22ions/m

3and confinement times of up to 0.5 ms.


[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-auto-26)#### 7th prototype, 'Polaris'

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=7)]

Helion's seventh-generation prototype, Polaris, started in 2011 and completed in 2024. [27] The device is expected to increase the pulse rate from one pulse every 10 minutes to one pulse per second for short periods.

This prototype is expected to be able to heat fusion plasma up to temperatures greater than 100 million degrees C.

[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-28)Polaris is planned to be 25% larger than Trenta to ensure that ions do not damage the vessel walls.

[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-29)In August 2024, Helion received a Large Broad Scope license from

[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:4-27)[Washington State Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Department_of_Health). This allowed Helion to possess and use necessary quantities of byproduct material to support the operation of Polaris.


[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-30)Helion completed construction of its seventh-generation prototype, Polaris, in late 2024 and began initial operations. While the company's original 2021 target was to demonstrate net electricity production in 2024, this milestone was not met. As of mid-2025, Helion reported that Polaris was operational and successfully forming the largest Field Reversed Configuration (FRC) plasmas the company had created to date, with the goal of demonstrating net electricity production by the end of the year. [31] As of December 2025, Helion had not announced that Polaris has achieved net production. Achieved 150 million degrees C D-T fusion as of February 13, 2026 .


[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-32)#### 8th prototype, 'Orion'

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=8)]

As of January 2022, an eighth iteration was in the design stage. [33] In July 2025, Helion announced plans to build a 50-megawatt fusion plant at property owned by the

[public utility district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_utility_district)operators of

[Rock Island Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Island_Dam)near

[Malaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaga,_Washington), in Chelan County, Washington. The company said the plant is planned to be operational by 2028, supplying power to nearby

[Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft)datacenters.


[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-reuters20250730-34)In May, then-Governor of Washington Bob Ferguson signed into law House Bill 1018. This bill classified fusion as a clean energy source and legally distinguished it from traditional nuclear fission.

[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-35)The bill gave Helion Energy the ability to pursue a local permitting option to gain approval for its proposed plant site in Chelan County.

[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:7-38)## Criticism

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Helion_Energy&action=edit§ion=11)]

Retired [Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_Plasma_Physics_Laboratory) researcher Daniel Jassby mentioned Helion Energy in a letter included in the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) newsletter *Physics & Society* (April 2019) as being among fusion start-ups allegedly practicing "voodoo fusion" rather than legitimate science. He noted that the company is one of several that has continually claimed "power in 5 to 10 years, but almost all have apparently never produced a single D–D fusion reaction".[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-voodoo-49)

The same 2018 [MITRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MITRE)/JASON report, commissioned by the US Department of Energy's [ARPA-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARPA-E), said that Helion project leads or literature stated that they need a 40 tesla magnetic field for commercial viability, had the capability for an 8 tesla field in their prototype, and projected they would achieve breakeven in 2023. The report stated that the primary challenge with Helion's approach is "whether they can simultaneously achieve sufficiently high compression while maintaining plasma stability".[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:1-23)
