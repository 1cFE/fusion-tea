## Project Overview
- Named after Celtic god of sky, lightning, and thunder
- Consortium: GenF (Thales spin-off), CEA, CNRS (via LULI and CELIA labs)
- Selected February 2024 under France 2030 "innovative nuclear reactor" call

## Funding
- Phase 1: €12-18.5M (sources vary), 36 months from April 2024
- Phase 2 (projected 2027): €200M
- Phase 3 (demo plant): €600M additional
- Thales committed €6M (€4M initial + €2M additional)
- Bpifrance: €12M+

## Roadmap
- **2024-2027 (Phase 1)**: Modeling, simulation, calibrated through experiments on LMJ
- **2027-2035 (Phase 2)**: Technology maturation — multiple laser synchronisation, cryogenic target production, new reactor wall materials
- **2035+**: Demonstrator → first-of-a-kind commercial reactor
- **2040**: Target commercialization
- **2050**: Grid connection, 1000 MW plant

## Target Details
- Spherical capsule ~2mm diameter
- DT fuel mixture
- Target gain >100 (from Assystem article: "targeted gain of over 100 MW" — likely means gain factor >100)

## Direct Drive Selection
- GenF explicitly chose direct drive over indirect drive (NIF approach)
- Direct drive: 4-5x more efficient use of laser energy
- Lower energy needed for ignition, higher fusion energy yield

## Partners
- Thales: high-power laser supplier
- CEA: nuclear expertise, LMJ facility access
- CNRS LULI: laser-plasma interaction research (École Polytechnique)
- CNRS CELIA: high-average-power laser technology, patented active cooling for 10 Hz operation (University of Bordeaux)
- Assystem: systems engineering, digital twin, safety (from Dec 2024)
- Dassault Systèmes: mentioned in some sources
- École Polytechnique: scientific partner

## Lab Facilities
- LMJ (Laser Mégajoule) at Le Barp — world's most powerful military laser
- PETAL (Petawatt laser) at CEA
- ELI Beamlines (Prague) — L4n ns-kJ laser used for experimental campaigns
