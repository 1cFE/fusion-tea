Most fusion reactors rely on the deuterium–tritium (DT) fuel reaction, where the two hydrogen isotopes interact to form helium, expending huge amounts of energy in the process. While deuterium is abundant and can be harvested from seawater, tritium is extremely scarce, with global civilian stocks estimated at less than 20kg. As such, developing a commercial fusion sector will require new methods to enhance tritium supplies.

### Related content

First Light Fusion believes its FLARE fusion plant is a potential solution to the tritium challenge. The concept design features a large liquid lithium bath which surrounds the fusion reaction. As the reaction takes place, high-energy neutrons interact with the lithium to create new tritium.

According to First Light, studies have shown that FLARE can achieve a tritium breeding ratio (TBR) of 1.8, meaning it produces 1.8 times the tritium needed to continue fuelling the reactor’s operations. It’s claimed that this is the highest TBR of any system announced to date and could help deliver a supply of tritium for the wider fusion sector.

Due to its scarcity, a gram of tritium currently costs around $30,000. At FLARE’s current 333 MWe design point, a 1.8 TBR would imply a net tritium surplus-per-plant of 25kg annually, reaching tritium self-sufficiency in as little as a week and providing a potentially highly lucrative source of additional income for FLARE plants, alongside their primary goal of electricity generation.

“Solving the tritium challenge is essential for fusion energy to scale,” said Mark Thomas, First Light Fusion, CEO.

“Validation of the tritium breeding ratio of 1.8 shows FLARE’s design not only powers itself, but could provide this critical fuel supply to the broader fusion industry, fuelling rapid growth.”

To attain validation of FLARE’s tritium breeding capability, First Light worked with the radiation physics team at Nuclear Technologies, part of TUV Sud UK. In a statement, TUV Sud confirmed the breeding ratio of 1.8: “We have worked with our neutronics consultants within the radiation physics team at Nuclear Technologies; a business unit of TÜV SÜD UK, who have undertaken analysis corroborates the TBR =1.8 figure for the FLARE reactor geometry.”

First light says that further development of the tritium breeding technology is being progressed with the support of the UK Atomic Energy Authority (UKAEA) Fusion Industry Program.
