## Approach — accelerator-driven beam-target fusion

- **High-current particle beams strike a tritium gas target**, producing an
  intense flux of **14 MeV D-T fusion neutrons** (the "FLARE" system). There is
  **no confined thermonuclear plasma** — energy/neutrons come from the
  accelerated beam on the target.

## Four-phase roadmap

1. Industrial component inspection (>175,000 parts inspected in Phase 1).
2. **Medical-isotope production** — the **Chrysalis** facility: positioned as
   the world's largest source of molybdenum-99 (Mo-99); NRC licensing under the
   **Part 50** reactor framework; ~**8 fusion (neutron) systems** deployed
   inside Chrysalis; supported by the DOE National Nuclear Security
   Administration (NNSA).
3. Spent-nuclear-fuel recycling (R&D begun).
4. **Fusion power** — long-horizon objective ("continue iterating and scaling
   its technology to … generate fusion power").

## Status

- "Visible proof of fusion" announced 2023.
- Current operational focus: neutron sources, medical isotopes, radiation
  testing/imaging; ongoing R&D on neutron intensity, energy efficiency,
  tritium-management and materials.
- Company HQ: Janesville / Fitchburg, Wisconsin.
- **No public commercial fusion-power-plant parameters** (accelerator energy,
  beam current, net electric power, capital cost, LCOE) — Phase 4 is not yet
  engineered (gap — `truly-unknown` / long-horizon).
