# Detailed Characterization of kHz-rate Laser-Driven Fusion at a Thin Liquid Sheet
**Source**: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/high-power-laser-science-and-engineering/article/BE37DB81EB33A9E60DC770BFEA37DC08
**Published**: 2024, High Power Laser Science and Engineering

## Relevance to Cortex Fusion
This is NOT a Cortex Fusion paper, but demonstrates the underlying physics concept of kHz-rate laser-driven D-D fusion on liquid targets that Cortex builds upon.

## Key Technical Details
- **Laser**: 1-kHz Ti:sapphire laser, 8 mJ, 40 fs pulses
- **Intensity**: ~5×10^18 W/cm² (relativistic intensity)
- **Target**: Thin (<1 μm) D2O liquid sheet formed from two intersecting 25 μm diameter cylindrical jets at room temperature
- **Rep rate**: 1 kHz demonstrated
- **Results**: D-D fusion neutron production verified, ~10^5 neutrons/second
- **Neutron energy**: 2.45 MeV (consistent with D-D fusion)
- **Fusion mechanism**: Two processes — (1) hot electron → ion energy transfer → D-D fusion, and (2) target explosion → high-energy deuteron collisions

## Significance
- Demonstrates the basic viability of kHz-rate laser + liquid jet → D-D fusion
- Very far from energy-relevant neutron rates (10^5/s vs Cortex's projected 10^19/s)
- "Sub-micrometer scale target is extremely stable and can operate at kHz or above"
- Positioned as neutron source application, not energy production
