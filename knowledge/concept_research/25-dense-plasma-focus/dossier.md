# Dense Plasma Focus (p-B11)

**Company**: LPPFusion
**Last updated**: 2026-03-08
**Iterations completed**: 1
**Overall confidence**: medium

## Summary

LPPFusion is developing "Focus Fusion" — a fusion energy approach combining the dense plasma focus (DPF) device with proton-boron-11 fuel. Pulsed coaxial electrodes discharge up to 2.7 MA, creating a dense, short-lived plasmoid with extreme ion temperatures (>200 keV demonstrated). The company claims a path to p-B11 net energy via the quantum magnetic field effect, which theoretically reduces bremsstrahlung losses in strongly magnetized plasmas. The device is remarkably simple and compact (<$1M, ~3 tons), targeting 5 MW electric output via direct energy conversion of charged fusion products (ion beam decelerator + x-ray photoelectric conversion) at ~200 Hz repetition rate.

## Differentiation Table Values

### Confinement Family
- **Value**: Other
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Schema definition; DPF explicitly listed under "Other"
- **Notes**: Dense plasma focus does not fit cleanly into MFE, IFE, MIF, or Electrostatic categories. The plasma is self-confined by its own magnetic fields generated by the current, but the confinement is extremely transient (~10 ns) and the mechanism (pinch/plasmoid formation) is distinct from standard MFE or MIF categories.

### Confinement Concept
- **Value**: Dense plasma focus
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/
- **Notes**: LPPFusion's proprietary name is "Focus Fusion" but the underlying device is a standard dense plasma focus (DPF) geometry — two concentric cylindrical electrodes with a pulsed discharge creating a plasmoid at the end of the inner electrode.

### Fuel
- **Value**: p-B11
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/; Lerner et al. (2023) J. Fusion Energy 42:7
- **Notes**: p-B11 is the target commercial fuel. Current experiments use deuterium for development/optimization. The company plans to transition to decaborane (B₁₀H₁₄) as the boron source. All public communications emphasize p-B11 as the defining fuel choice.

### Primary Heating
- **Value**: Electromagnetic pinch (DPF)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/; Lerner et al. (2024) Frontiers in Physics
- **Notes**: A pulse of electricity from a capacitor bank is discharged across coaxial electrodes. The resulting current sheath (up to 2.7 MA in FF-2B) runs down the electrodes and self-organizes into filaments that pinch together at the end of the anode, forming a dense plasmoid. The heating is ohmic/pinch compression — no external magnets, no lasers, no neutral beams.

### Energy Capture
- **Value**: Direct (charged particle)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/; https://www.lppfusion.com/investing-in-lppfusion/executive-summary/
- **Notes**: Two conversion channels: (1) Ion beam (~2/3 of plasmoid energy) enters a "decelerator" — a particle accelerator in reverse — that converts kinetic energy directly to electricity. (2) X-rays (remaining ~1/3) converted via photoelectric effect (patented technology). No thermal cycle. The p-B11 reaction produces only charged particles (three alpha particles per reaction), making direct conversion the natural and only viable approach.

### Plasma State
- **Value**: Pinch
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/; Lerner et al. (2024) Frontiers in Physics
- **Notes**: The plasmoid is a self-compressed plasma ball formed by the pinch of current filaments. Duration is ~10 ns. Density approaches solid density (up to 10²¹ cm⁻³). Ion energies >200 keV achieved. Schema describes Pinch as "self-compressed plasma column maintained by its own current" — matches the DPF plasmoid, though it's a ball/toroid rather than a column.

### Magnet Type
- **Value**: Self-confined
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/; schema definition
- **Notes**: No external confinement magnets. The plasma generates its own confining magnetic field through the pinch current. The schema description for Self-confined explicitly mentions DPF. The only "magnets" are the current-carrying electrodes and the self-generated fields in the plasmoid.

### Tritium Breeding
- **Value**: N/A (aneutronic)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/
- **Notes**: p-B11 fuel cycle does not involve tritium. The reaction is p + ¹¹B → 3α (three helium-4 nuclei). No tritium breeding needed.

### Neutron Management
- **Value**: Minimal (aneutronic)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/; Lerner et al. (2023) J. Fusion Energy 42:7
- **Notes**: The primary p-B11 reaction produces no neutrons. Side reactions (e.g., ¹¹B(p,n)¹¹C) produce a very small neutron flux — <1% of fusion energy. LPPFusion claims the device "requires no costly containment structure" and is "safe enough to place in residential neighborhoods." Thin shielding for secondary radiation would be needed but not the heavy shielding required for D-T.

### Operation Mode
- **Value**: Pulsed
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/investing-in-lppfusion/our-plan-to-net-energy/; https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/
- **Notes**: Discrete short pulses. Each plasmoid exists for ~10 ns. The capacitor bank discharges, the plasmoid forms and burns, energy is extracted, then the system recharges. Definitively pulsed operation with very short burn events.

### Repetition Rate
- **Value**: High (>10 Hz)
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Citation**: https://www.lppfusion.com/investing-in-lppfusion/our-plan-to-net-energy/; Wikipedia DPF article
- **Notes**: LPPFusion targets ~200 Hz for a commercial power plant (5 MW at ~25 kJ/pulse). This is a design target, not yet demonstrated — current experiments are single-shot. The NX2 device (Singapore) has demonstrated 16 Hz repetition for DPF devices in a different context (X-ray source), providing some physics basis. Electrode erosion at high rep rate is identified as a major engineering challenge. Medium confidence because the 200 Hz target is stated by the company but not yet demonstrated at fusion-relevant conditions.

### Driver Technology
- **Value**: Pulsed coaxial electrodes (capacitor bank, 2.7 MA)
- **Confidence**: high
- **Citation**: Lerner et al. (2024) Frontiers in Physics; https://www.lppfusion.com/technology/focus-fusion-energy/dpf-device/
- **Notes**: The driver is remarkably simple: a capacitor bank discharges through coaxial cylindrical electrodes (beryllium anode, 2.8 cm radius in FF-2B). The current sheath self-organizes into the pinch. No lasers, no magnets, no neutral beams. The "hard technology bet" is achieving sufficient compression and confinement in the plasmoid for p-B11 ignition, enabled by the quantum magnetic field effect (Lerner's theoretical contribution). Device cost <$1M. The simplicity is the key differentiator.

## Remaining Gaps

1. **Repetition Rate** (medium): The 200 Hz target is LPPFusion's claim. Independent assessment of DPF rep rate limits at fusion-relevant energies would strengthen this. The NX2 16 Hz demonstration is for a much smaller device used as an X-ray source.
   - Searched: LPPFusion website, peer-reviewed papers, Wikipedia DPF article

2. **Energy Capture** (high for type, low for conversion efficiency): LPPFusion describes the ion beam decelerator and x-ray photoelectric conversion conceptually, but no efficiency numbers or prototype test results are published.
   - Searched: LPPFusion website, executive summary, patents

3. **Driver Technology** (high for type, incomplete specs): Capacitor bank specifications (voltage, capacitance, stored energy) for FF-2B are not fully detailed in public sources. The Frontiers paper gives current (2.7 MA) and anode radius (2.8 cm) but not input energy per shot.
   - Searched: Frontiers paper, LPPFusion website
   - Sources that might help: Lerner (2011) J. Fusion Energy 30:367 earlier theoretical paper with power plant conceptual design
