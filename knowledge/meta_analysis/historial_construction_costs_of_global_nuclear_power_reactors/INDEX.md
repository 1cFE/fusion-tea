Introduces a study analyzing Overnight Construction Cost (OCC) trends for 153 nuclear reactors across seven countries (US, France, Japan, South Korea, West Germany, Canada, India) from 1954–2015, expanding beyond prior US/France-focused literature that covered only 26% of global reactors and was disrupted by the Three Mile Island accident. Covers conclusions from a new dataset of historic overnight nuclear construction cost experience curves across seven countries, finding wide variance in cost trends (US rising, South Korea declining sustainably) and arguing there is no intrinsic learning rate for nuclear power, with non-learning factors like utility structure, reactor size, regulation, and international collaboration dominating cost evolution.

### 5.1 Policy implications
**Lines:** 327-336

Policy implications of the analysis: cautions modelers and policy makers against applying theoretical learning/experience curves to future nuclear cost projections (since historical curves like those from Koomey/Hultman 2007 and Grubler 2010 reflect context-specific US/France deployment conditions), and recommends grounding cost forecasts in relevant historical experience while pursuing innovation, industrial, trade, and energy policies to drive clean energy costs down.
