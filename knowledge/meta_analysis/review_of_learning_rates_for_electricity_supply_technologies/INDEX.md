### 3.9 Discussion
**Lines:** 342-353

Section 3.9 (Discussion) summarizes meta-analysis findings on learning rates across 11 electric power generation technologies, noting wide variability within/across technologies, lower rates for fossil fuels vs. renewables (e.g., 12% onshore wind, 23% solar PV), and the nuclear exception of non-declining costs. It also discusses confounding factors—geographic scope of learning spillovers, market price vs. true production cost distortions, and the limited use of two-factor models separating learning-by-doing from learning-by-researching (R&D).

### 4.2 Models employing exogenous learning curves
**Lines:** 403-422

Section 4.2 contrasts exogenous technology cost/performance specifications (fixed annual rates or direct parameter values set by modeler judgment) with endogenous learning curves in large-scale energy-economic models like GCAM and US-REGEN, showing how exogenous approaches defer technology investment until costs decline and produce implied learning rates that often fall outside literature ranges. It uses EPRI's US-REGEN model and PNNL's GCAM as case studies to illustrate that exogenous cost trajectories remain fixed regardless of deployment levels, contrary to one-factor learning curve behavior.
