## 2 **Fusion power experience rates are overestimated**
**Lines:** 11-33

Covers the introduction to a paper arguing that fusion power plant (FPP) experience rates (ERs) — the percentage cost reduction per doubling of deployment — are overestimated in current literature at 8–20%, when technological characteristics (large unit size, extraordinary complexity, intermediate customization needs) empirically link FPPs to ERs of only 2–8%. Introduces the study's methodology of using expert interviews to rate magnetic and laser-based inertial fusion on three characteristics, and includes Figure 1 cataloguing ERs and rationales from prior literature.

## 4 **Technology-inherent characteristics of FPPs**

## 5 **Initial costs of FPPs**
**Lines:** 123-141

Covers initial CAPEX cost estimates for first-of-a-kind (FOAK) fusion power plants (FPPs), reporting a wide range of US$1,400–43,000 per kW from expert elicitation and literature, with private-sector estimates (avg ~$7,000/kW) systematically lower than public-sector ones (avg ~$26,000/kW). ## 6 **Discussion**
**Lines:** 142-155

Argues that fusion power plants' assumed Experience Rates (ERs) of 8–20% are unjustified — empirical evidence suggests ~2–8% (median 5%), making FPPs likely orders of magnitude more expensive than competing low-carbon technologies. Recommends energy system modelers revise ER assumptions downward, treats FPPs as high-risk investments, and suggests researchers explore alternative fusion concepts (field-reversed configurations, z-pinch, D-D or p-B fuels) with more favorable cost-reduction potential.
