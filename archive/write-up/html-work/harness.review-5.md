## Findings

1. **Emphasis added throughout that the markdown does not mark.** The page wraps the lead sentence of a very large number of markdown paragraphs and list items in `<em>` or `<strong>`, where the markdown has plain text. This is not one of the adaptations the render prompt allows ("Carry every heading, paragraph, list, table, code block, figure and link into the page, in the same order and with the same wording. Only these adaptations are allowed: [drop scaffolding; anchor ids; link targets; section-reference links; inline source-pointer styling]" — emphasis is not on that list). Representative locations: `<em>` on "This part is about the rest of the loop..." and "Fighting that entropy is the job of the harness." (§1); "Ours is far from refined." (§1, closing paragraph); "The research workflow therefore separates judgment from writing..." (§5 opener); "Our assessment has three parts..." (§8 opener). `<strong>` added to the first sentence of every bullet in "The discrepancy, step by step" (§4, e.g. `<strong>The model's stored energy was 9 percent above the paper's.</strong>`, including the nested sub-bullets), every "Findings" bullet under round 2 task 1 and task 3 (§4, e.g. `<strong>Stored energy fell from 551 to 519.9 MJ...</strong>`, `<strong>Most of the earlier driven points now ignite.</strong>`), and all three bullets after the WI-042 results table in §6 (`<strong>A second implementation of the calculation...</strong>`, `<strong>At the paper's printed peak values...</strong>`, `<strong>The new program got its own pin...</strong>`). None of these spans are bold or italic in the markdown. (cites: html-render-prompt.md § Keep the prose)

2. **Figure 1's kept alt text is reworded, not carried unchanged.** The render prompt requires "Keep the markdown's alt text and caption" when redrawing a figure. The markdown's alt text reads "...an approach, then tasks one at a time, with research and model changes before the model is pinned..."; the page's `aria-label` for the same figure reads "...an approach, then tasks one at a time or independent tasks in parallel, with research and model changes before the model is pinned..." — a clause has been added that is not in the source alt text (it appears to be pulled in from the body prose at line 73 of the markdown, "one at a time or independent tasks in parallel run in parallel"). (cites: html-render-prompt.md § What the page adds → Figures)

3. **Figure 1's caption drops the markdown's closing sentence.** The markdown caption ends "...runs through the review and the owner. Rendered by `harness-assets/render_goal_loop.py`." The page's figcaption ends at "...runs through the review and the owner." with no equivalent of the attribution sentence anywhere on the page. This may be a reasonable adaptation, since the diagram was rebuilt in HTML rather than left as an image the script renders, but the render prompt's instruction is to keep the caption, and this is a dropped block, not a listed adaptation. (cites: html-render-prompt.md § What the page adds → Figures; html-review-prompt.md item 1)

4. **Main-flow markdown content is moved wholly behind closed-by-default collapsibles, with only a paraphrase left visible.** The render prompt says "Collapse detail, not the story: a reader who opens nothing still follows the whole argument," and reserves collapsing for "a lower-level subsection" or "the information a repository reference points to" — not for the markdown's own top-level explanatory prose. Three instances:
   - §3's six-area bullet list (main-flow prose, no heading of its own in the markdown) is entirely relocated into the closed `<details id="the-six-areas">`; the only visible-by-default version is Figure 2's card text, which is shorter and drops real content. For example the markdown's Sources bullet — "...One directory, `knowledge/holdout/`, is quarantined for the test in Part 4: agents are told never to read it, and the registration script refuses anything from it." — becomes the card's "...`holdout/` is quarantined," dropping the "why" (Part 4's test) and the "how" (agents told never to read it; the registration script refuses it) unless the reader opens the detail.
   - §4's "The discrepancy" bullet list (also main-flow prose with no heading) is entirely relocated into the closed `<details id="the-discrepancy-step-by-step">`; Figure 3's chain diagram is the only visible-by-default version.
   - §7's two bulleted lists under "Code checks: is the program the model, and do the numbers reproduce?" and "Agent reviews: is the reading right?" (both markdown bold-lead paragraphs introducing a list, in the main flow) are relocated into closed `<details id="code-checks">` / `<details id="agent-reviews">`; Figure 9 is the only visible-by-default version, and its "ck-ask" one-line questions are noticeably thinner than the markdown's descriptions (e.g. the Integration check's full explanation of what it confirms and where, versus the figure's "Is the pinned program exactly what the model describes?").
   (cites: html-render-prompt.md § What the page adds → Collapsed detail)

5. **Detail/dropdown summaries inherited from the markdown's italic task labels are colon-joined double clauses.** E.g. `<summary><span>Task 1, research: how does the paper define stored energy?</span></summary>`, `<summary><span>Task 3, study: re-run the earlier window at the new pin.</span></summary>`, `<summary><span>Task 1, model change: compute the ash profile from the paper's rule.</span></summary>` — these match the pattern the shared feedback flags: "Write a summary line the way you write a heading: a bare noun phrase, or one dead-simple claim... Bad: colon-joined double clauses." Note this wording is taken verbatim from the markdown's own italic lead-ins (which the render prompt requires keeping unchanged), so resolving it would mean either rewording the source text or restructuring the summary — a call for the coordinator, not something to fix by rewriting on the page. (cites: html.md § Dropdown summary line written as a double clause)

6. **New summary microcopy (the "st-r" headline and "dg-note" fields on Figures 4, 5, 7 and 8) is often compressed into a subject-less or verb-less fragment.** Examples: "Up to 6 searches and 3 registrations" (Figure 7, step 1); "The question, who waits for the answer, where to look." (Figure 7, step 3 note); "The ash follows the paper's rule; electrons from charge balance; one pressure for stored energy and beta; nothing else tuned." 7. **The "Explore" script does more than track navigation state.** The render prompt's hard constraint reads: "The page reads in full with scripts disabled. An inline script may only track navigation state, as the style sample's script does." The page's second script block (backing the three `.live[data-explorer]` panels on Figures 1, 2 and 9) goes well beyond that: on click it toggles opacity/highlight state on diagram parts, builds `<dl>`/`<ul>` readout content by cloning nodes from elsewhere in the page, and manages a set of dynamically created "chip" buttons and their pressed state. The page does still read in full with scripts disabled (the panels stay `hidden`, and the same content sits in the open-able detail sections), but the script itself is not navigation-state tracking. (cites: html-render-prompt.md § Hard constraints)
