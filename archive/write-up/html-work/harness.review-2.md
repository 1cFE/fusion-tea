2. Figure 1's alt text is reworded, not carried unchanged. The markdown alt text (harness.md:57) reads "...a round run by the AI: an approach, then tasks one at a time, with research and model changes..." The page's `aria-label` (harness.html:401) reads "...a round run by the AI: an approach, then tasks one at a time or independent tasks in parallel, with research and model changes..." — the clause "or independent tasks in parallel" was added, echoing later prose ("tasks one at a time (or multiple independent tasks run in parallel)", harness.md:73) but not present in this alt text. 3. Figure 3 (the round-2 checks diagram, harness.html:1133–1135) has no text equivalent. Figures 1 and 2 both carry `role="img"` with a full descriptive `aria-label` (harness.html:401, and the `<svg>` at harness.html:691). Figure 3's `<ol class="diagram fig-checks">` has neither `role="img"` nor `aria-label`, and its parent `<figure>` has none either. Cites: html-render-prompt.md hard constraint "Give every diagram a text equivalent, so that color, position and shape never carry meaning alone," and the inconsistency with the treatment given the page's other two figures.

4. Abstraction given a decision-making verb. The page-composed diagram label at harness.html:421, "each result decides the next task," gives the verb "decides" to "result" rather than to whoever acts on the result. The markdown's own account of the same mechanism (harness.md:73) attributes the decision to the agent: "that return feeds the decision on what to do next." This is the pattern the shared feedback names directly: "Give the verb to whoever or whatever does the thing, the way an engineer would say it out loud" (bad examples given: "stored energy decides the check," "the dominant lever is"). Cites: synthesis.md, "Abstraction performing a verb."
