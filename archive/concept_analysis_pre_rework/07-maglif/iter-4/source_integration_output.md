### F-1: DS machine gain target is net facility gain (Q>1), not 100× NIF gain (Q≈0.7)
- **Target:** Section 3 (Pulsed Power Driver / IMG — TRL 4–5) and Section 6 (Gap 3)
- **Finding:** The analysis states "Pacific Fusion DS goal: 100× higher facility-level gain than NIF." Since NIF facility gain ≈ 0.007, this translates to Q_facility ≈ 0.7 — still below breakeven. The new sources contradict this framing: the breakthrough article explicitly states the DS machine targets **net facility gain** (Q_facility > 1) by 2030, and the CRADA announcement references "100+ megajoules high-yield fusion" — which from ~80 MJ stored implies Q > 1.25. These are consistent with each other but inconsistent with the "100× NIF" framing in the analysis. The current framing understates Pacific Fusion's stated ambition by implying the DS machine targets sub-breakeven performance; the actual claim is net energy gain (more fusion energy out than electrical energy stored in the driver). This is material for Section 3 TRL risk assessment because achieving Q>1 at DS scale would be a substantially more aggressive physics claim than the "100× NIF" language suggests, and the 2030 milestone deadline makes the risk profile concrete. Section 6 Gap 3 currently says "DS experiment publications expected 2028+" — this should be updated to reflect the stated milestone of net facility gain by 2030, followed by commercial deployment in the mid-2030s.
- **Recommendation:** In Section 3 (Pulsed Power Driver), update the DS machine goal statement to: "Pacific Fusion DS target: net facility gain (Q_facility > 1, >80 MJ fusion yield from ~80 MJ stored) by 2030, followed by commercial deployment mid-2030s." Add a brief sentence noting this is more aggressive than the "100× NIF facility gain" framing, which resolves to Q_facility ≈ 0.7. In Section 6 Gap 3, update the timeline from "DS experiment publications (expected 2028+)" to "net facility gain milestone (2030 target); commercial deployment (mid-2030s target per Pacific Fusion public statements)." Add the timeline milestones as a row in the Section 5 parameter table under "Milestones / Schedule."
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: CRADA with Sandia formalizes a technology transfer relationship, not just co-authorship
- **Target:** Section 1 (Availability of Data) and Section 6 (Gap 3, Source Recommendation)
- **Finding:** The analysis characterizes Pacific Fusion's Sandia relationship through co-authorship of the Ellison et al. arxiv paper. The CRADA announcement (December 2024) establishes a formal Cooperative Research and Development Agreement — a DOE/NNSA legal instrument that grants Pacific Fusion access to Z Machine experimental time, Sandia technical staff support, and potentially non-public performance data. This is materially different from academic co-authorship: CRADAs typically include protected periods for proprietary results and give the private partner preferential access to government facilities. This matters for Section 1 (the data availability context — Pacific Fusion may have access to unpublished Z experimental results that don't appear in public literature) and for Gap 3's source recommendation (which currently suggests watching for "Pacific Fusion DS experiment publications" without noting that future Z Machine experiments supporting DS will likely occur under CRADA terms, potentially with a confidentiality period before public release).
- **Recommendation:** In Section 1, add a sentence after the Ellison et al. reference noting that Pacific Fusion formalized a CRADA with Sandia (December 2024), giving them privileged access to Z Machine experimental capacity. In Section 6 Gap 3, update the source recommendation to note that near-term MagLIF physics data may appear through CRADA-enabled Z experiments rather than independent Pacific Fusion DS publications, and that some results may have a confidentiality period before public disclosure.
- **Priority:** minor
