VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Key Differentiators missing explicit cost disposition labels
- **Target:** Key Differentiators section (before Section 1)
- **Finding:** The differentiators section lists eight bullets with narrative commentary, but several do not carry an explicit cost disposition (advantage / penalty / neutral). The differentiators for "pulsed operation, not continuous," "rep rate as primary LCOE lever," "yield scaling entirely unvalidated," and "D-T fuel cycle (shared)" all describe architectural or physics features without stating whether that feature is a cost advantage, penalty, or neutral relative to a conventional tokamak. The TEA implications are covered in depth in Section 2 — but only for the three dominant cost drivers. A reader cannot quickly audit whether all differentiators are accounted for in the TEA framing without cross-referencing the full narrative. (Goal 3: each differentiator should have a stated cost implication.)
- **Recommendation:** Add a one-line cost tag to each differentiator bullet (e.g., "Cost penalty: novel capital account with no MFE analogue" or "Cost neutral: same D-T constraints apply"). At minimum, the four currently untagged bullets need explicit dispositions. Pulsed operation is a structural cost reshaper (not cleanly advantage/penalty — "neutral in aggregate but restructures all cost accounts" is a valid tag); rep rate is the primary LCOE lever (a structural characteristic, not intrinsically a penalty or advantage); yield scaling is a cost risk (captures what Section 2 calls "TEA consequence of failure"); D-T fuel cycle is neutral/shared.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: CAS-level cost structure differences not mapped
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges / Modeling Recommendation)
- **Finding:** The checklist requires CAS-level cost structure differences from the reference concept to be noted (Goal 3). The analysis describes the cost structure informally — driver at 96% of CapEx, per-shot consumables as a novel O&M category, thick liquid wall potentially eliminating first-wall replacement — but never maps these to the CAS hierarchy (CAS10-LCOE). For the modeling approach (Goal 4), this matters because MagLIF's cost model requires different accounts from a tokamak reference: a novel driver account (no MFE CAS equivalent), absent superconducting magnet account (no CAS22 analogue), per-shot consumables as a variable O&M line not present in any MFE cost template, and potentially absent scheduled first-wall replacement. Without this mapping, the modeling recommendation ("free-form parametric model") lacks structure for which CAS accounts to build from scratch vs. borrow vs. omit.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief CAS-level mapping — even a short table or paragraph — in Section 2 identifying: which standard cost accounts are absent (e.g., superconducting magnet system), which are replaced by novel accounts (e.g., pulsed power driver in place of steady-state magnet capital), and which novel O&M accounts must be introduced (per-shot consumables, RTL remanufacturing or disposal). This gives the cost modeler a structural starting point and makes the "free-form model required" recommendation actionable.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Thick liquid wall capacity factor upside not parameterized
- **Target:** Section 5 parameter table
- **Finding:** The Key Differentiators section identifies the thick liquid wall as potentially eliminating the capacity-factor-limiting maintenance cycle that constrains tokamaks — one of the clearest structural cost advantages stated in the analysis. Section 5 lists capacity factor as 85% (assumed, no experimental basis), but does not include a sensitivity parameter for the capacity factor range under two scenarios: (a) thick liquid wall works as designed, removing scheduled first-wall replacement, and (b) thick liquid wall fails, forcing scheduled chamber replacement analogous to solid first-wall tokamaks. The analysis explicitly identifies chamber lifetime as a blocking gap in Section 6 (Gap 5), so this is a known unknown with direct TEA impact. Without parameterizing this sensitivity, the capacity factor assumption is opaque — the 85% number appears to assume the liquid wall works, but that assumption is not surfaced. (Goal 5: key assumptions with TEA impact should be flagged as sensitivity parameters.)
- **Recommendation:** Add a capacity factor sensitivity row to Section 5 (or a sub-row under the existing capacity factor entry) with two scenario values: e.g., "85–90% if thick liquid wall eliminates periodic replacement" vs. "60–75% if chamber replacement required on tokamak-analogous schedule." Cite the Z-IFE study's capacity factor assumption basis. This makes explicit that the 85% assumption is a thick-liquid-wall success scenario.
- **Priority:** minor
