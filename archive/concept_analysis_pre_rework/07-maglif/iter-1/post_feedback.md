VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Modeling approach recommendation absent
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges in Capturing System Function)
- **Finding:** The analysis recognizes that pulsed fusion TEA tools are underdeveloped — it even quotes the roadmap paper stating that "cost optimization models and tools have been developed for tokamaks; investment in new reduced models is required." It identifies that Z-IFE is the only published systems-level model and that it predates the modern IMG architecture. However, it does not translate this into a modeling recommendation (Goal 4). There is no statement of whether a reference-class scaling approach (e.g., 1costingfe or ARIES-analogous), a free-form parametric model, or an analogy-based estimate is appropriate for MagLIF — and critically, no explanation of why. The analysis also does not state the key hypotheses the cost model should test as testable propositions (e.g., "At what rep rate does MagLIF reach cost parity with advanced fission?" or "What target cost threshold makes cryo ice-layer targets viable?"); instead, hypotheses are embedded in the challenge narrative without being extracted as explicit propositions.
- **Recommendation:** Add a paragraph at the end of Section 2 that (1) states whether 1costingfe/reference-class scaling is applicable or whether free-form modeling is required, with justification — likely the latter given the novel cost structure (driver capital, per-shot consumables, rep rate as primary lever), and (2) lists 2–3 explicit testable hypotheses the cost model should evaluate, framed as propositions rather than open questions.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Key differentiators from conventional tokamak not explicitly listed
- **Target:** Section 2 or a dedicated differentiator section
- **Finding:** The differentiators from a conventional tokamak are present in the analysis but scattered across Sections 2, 4, and 7 — never consolidated into an explicit list (Goal 2). A reader cannot quickly answer "what are the defining differences between MagLIF and a tokamak?" without reading the full document and synthesizing across sections. The cross-concept notes in Section 7 compare MagLIF against Helion, Tokamak Energy, and laser IFE, but are not framed as "differences from the mainstream approach." Key structural distinctions — pulsed vs. continuous operation, no superconducting magnets, rep rate as primary LCOE lever (vs. capacity factor for MFE), per-shot consumables with no MFE analogue, novel driver capital cost category, thick liquid wall vs. solid first wall — are each discussed well but require assembly from multiple locations.
- **Recommendation:** Add an explicit bulleted list of key differentiators from a conventional tokamak near the top of the analysis (after the header block, or as an opener to Section 2). Each differentiator should indicate whether it is novel to MagLIF, borrowed from another concept family, or shared with other MIF concepts. This list should be 6–8 items maximum and anchor the narrative that follows.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Technical bets lack explicit failure consequences in TEA terms
- **Target:** Section 2 and Section 6
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies the key technical bets clearly (rep rate ceiling, yield scaling unvalidated above χ ≈ 0.1, 10× component cost reduction required, self-magnetizing target gain at commercial scale). However, it does not consistently state what happens to the TEA if each bet fails (Goal 5). For example: if rep rate cannot exceed 0.1 Hz, the Z-IFE study shows COE of ~20 ¢/kWeh — that's the failure consequence, and it should be stated explicitly as such. If the gain scaling formula fails and GJ-class yields require substantially higher driver energy, how does that affect CapEx? The Section 6 gap table marks these as "blocking" but does not describe their TEA impact if they remain unresolved. Without failure consequences, the risks read as physics challenges rather than LCOE constraints.
- **Recommendation:** For each of the 3–4 most critical technical bets in Section 2 (rep rate, yield scaling, component cost reduction, target cost), add one sentence stating the TEA consequence of failure — what the resulting cost regime would be and whether it would take MagLIF out of commercial viability entirely or into a specific LCOE band. The Section 6 gap table could add a "TEA impact if unresolved" column or inline note for the blocking rows.
- **Priority:** important
