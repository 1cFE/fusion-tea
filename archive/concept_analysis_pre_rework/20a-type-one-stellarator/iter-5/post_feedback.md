### F-1: Divertor scenario branch capital dimension not modeled
- **Target:** Section 2 (island divertor discussion) and model CAS22 computation
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly identifies the classical vs. LIBD divertor choice as "scenario-determining" with two distinct TEA dimensions: (a) availability risk — classical's 0.44–2.9% particle exhaust efficiency is marginal for 2-year cycles, and (b) capital cost — the LIBD dome adds an actively-cooled structure in constrained geometry that should raise CAS22 relative to the classical option. The model implements an availability sweep (80%/85%/93%/96%) that partially captures dimension (a), but the capital dimension is entirely absent: the model runs a single CAS22 value regardless of divertor scenario. This means the LIBD case (higher capital, protected availability) and the classical case (lower capital, availability at risk) cannot be compared. The availability sweep alone conflates the two scenarios because it cannot show that higher availability in the LIBD branch comes at a capital cost premium. Gap #17 in Section 6 marks this as blocking.
- **Recommendation:** Add a divertor scenario branch to the model. The classical scenario should use the current CAS22 divertor line item as-is (lower capital, availability range 80–85%). The LIBD scenario should apply an upward CAS22 multiplier for the dome system — even a rough 1.5×–2× sensitivity on the divertor sub-account would make the capital trade-off visible. Present both scenarios alongside the coil cost sweep so the reader can see all three dominant uncertainty axes simultaneously.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: η_th discrepancy between analysis text and model
- **Target:** Section 2 (power balance), Section 5 (parameter table), and model input parameters
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis text in both Section 2 and Section 5 explicitly states "model uses 0.45 as conservative central estimate" for thermal efficiency. The model output reports η_th = 0.40. These values are inconsistent. With elasticity −0.16, the ~11% difference in η_th produces roughly 1.8% LCOE error — small but enough to make the analysis text unreliable as a description of what the model actually computes. The model output notes η_th = 0.40 is "UNCERTAIN — conservative below implied 47%," which is accurate, but the mismatch with the analysis text will mislead any reader who cross-checks Section 5 against the model output.
- **Recommendation:** Reconcile the two values. The most defensible choice is to set η_th = 0.45 in the model (matching the analysis text and consistent with the power balance derivation's implied 47% minus a conservative margin), then document the rationale: η_th = 0.45 is derived from 415 MWe gross / 880 MW thermal at M_b = 1.10, conservatively rounded down from the implied 47%. If 0.40 is retained as the model value, update Section 2 and Section 5 to say 0.40, not 0.45, and document the additional conservatism.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Stellarator nearest-neighbor comparison absent
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** Section 7 is almost entirely devoted to comparing Infinity Two with 21-spherical-tokamak-hts (a spherical tokamak). The two declared nearest neighbors — 09-qi-stellarator-hts (Proxima Fusion, smaller QI HTS stellarator at R ≈ 1.8 m) and 10-large-scale-stellarator (Gauss Fusion, similar scale at 40 non-planar coils, LTS+HTS) — receive a single sentence naming them with no comparative content. This leaves the reader unable to understand what distinguishes Infinity Two within the stellarator design space: is its QI optimization similar to or sharply different from Proxima's? How does its scale and field choice compare to Gauss Fusion's approach? These distinctions matter for TEA because they determine whether cost model results from the stellarator peers can be used as sanity checks. The tokamak comparison in Section 7 is useful but is already well-covered in the stellarator advantages list; the peer comparison is the missing piece.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief comparative paragraph in Section 7 for each stellarator neighbor. For 09-qi-stellarator-hts: note the scale contrast (R = 1.8 m vs. 12.5 m), the shared QI topology and REBCO HTS, and the capital cost implications of the scale difference. For 10-large-scale-stellarator: note the similar coil count (40), the LTS+HTS vs. HTS-only conductor choice, and what the Gauss Fusion analysis reveals about large-stellarator cost structure. Even three to four sentences per neighbor would anchor Infinity Two's position in the stellarator cost landscape and enable cross-validation of coil cost and scale assumptions.
- **Priority:** important
