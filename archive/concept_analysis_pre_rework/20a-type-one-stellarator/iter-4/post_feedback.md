### F-1: Construction time sweep absent despite being identified as 3rd-ranked LCOE lever
- **Target:** Model output / sensitivity section
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis explicitly identifies construction schedule as the third-highest LCOE lever (elasticity ≈ +0.55) and states that "3D HTS coil manufacturing complexity makes schedule overrun the dominant financial cost driver during construction." The model provides a coil cost sweep (1×/3×/5×) and an availability sweep (80%/85%/93%/96%), but no construction time sweep, despite labeling the 10-year estimate as "UNCERTAIN." The three top LCOE drivers are treated asymmetrically: two are swept, one is a point estimate. At elasticity +0.55, a construction overrun from 10 to 15 years adds roughly 27% to LCOE — comparable in magnitude to the availability range (279–329 $/MWh).
- **Recommendation:** Add a construction time scenario sweep to the model output alongside the coil cost and availability sweeps. A reasonable bracket is 8 yr (optimistic, parallel manufacturing) / 10 yr (baseline) / 13 yr / 16 yr (overrun, analogue to W7-X coil schedule). Present the resulting LCOE range in the same format as the existing sweeps so all three dominant uncertainties are visible together.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Divertor scenario branch identified as blocking but not implemented in model
- **Target:** Model output / CAS22 scenario modeling
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis calls the classical-vs-LIBD divertor choice a "scenario-determining decision" and Section 6 gap #17 classifies it as blocking. The analysis explicitly recommends: "Model as two LCOE scenarios — classical (lower capital, availability risk from marginal exhaust) vs. LIBD (higher capital, availability protected by 12.6% exhaust efficiency)." The model output contains only a single C220107 value ($44.7M, framework default) with no scenario branch. The classical divertor risk is availability degradation from marginal helium ash exhaust over a 2-year cycle — this interacts directly with the availability sweep already present. The LIBD branch adds unquantified capital (actively-cooled dome in constrained geometry) to C220107. Neither scenario is computed.
- **Recommendation:** Implement two divertor scenarios in the model. Classical: keep C220107 at framework default but run the scenario at reduced availability (e.g., 80–85%) to represent helium ash accumulation risk. LIBD: apply a capital multiplier to C220107 (e.g., 2×–3×, analogous to the coil cost bracket rationale) and run at the canonical 85% availability. Present both scenarios in the model output so the reader can see the capital-vs-availability trade-off quantitatively. The classical scenario combined with pessimistic availability and the LIBD scenario combined with its capital premium bound the divertor decision space.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Li-6 supply blocking risk not converted to a testable model assumption
- **Target:** Section 4 (Supply Chain) and Section 5 (Parameters) / model assumptions
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** Section 4 correctly identifies the Li-6 enrichment situation as a "supply creation problem, not a diversification problem" with Western commercial supply "effectively zero" and the leading alternative (ICOMAX) potentially decades away. This is a potential infeasibility flag for the HCPB blanket on the Infinity Two timeline — yet neither Section 5 nor the model captures it as a testable assumption. The HCPB blanket cost basis silently assumes Li-6 enrichment supply exists. If it does not, the entire blanket design requires reconsidering. The analysis notes an alternative (natural lithium blanket, characterized as "technically challenging but not impossible") but does not frame this as a branch or a hypothesis the model can test.
- **Recommendation:** In Section 5, add a row flagging Li-6 enrichment supply as a blocking assumption (not a parameter) on which the HCPB cost basis depends. State explicitly: "HCPB blanket feasibility on the Infinity Two timeline assumes Western Li-6 enrichment capacity is established; if not, a blanket redesign is required with unknown cost and TBR impact." In Section 2 or Section 6, add a note that the natural lithium alternative blanket (no enrichment required, but lower TBR margin and redesign needed) represents an alternative scenario branch distinct from the existing capital cost and divertor branches. This converts the supply risk from a background narrative note into a stated dependency that downstream cost model iterations can branch on.
- **Priority:** important
