### F-1: NRL Electra converted from KrF to argon fluoride — KrF heritage in Section 3 is incomplete
- **Target:** Section 3 (Maturity — KrF Excimer Laser at MJ Scale, TRL 2–3)
- **Finding:** The Optica OPN source (June 2023) reveals that NRL converted Electra from krypton fluoride to argon fluoride after the KrF program, in order to exploit ArF's shorter wavelength (193 nm vs. 248 nm KrF) and larger native bandwidth (~10 THz), which are superior properties for suppressing laser-plasma instabilities in direct-drive implosions. The existing analysis cites "NRL Electra laser demonstrated KrF excimer operation at ~750 J, 5 Hz continuous for days" as a heritage milestone for Xcimer's KrF architecture — but does not note that NRL's current program is ArF, not KrF. This framing overstates the active government research support behind Xcimer's specific laser medium. Xcimer is scaling a technology (KrF) that the principal government KrF program has since superseded with a different gas medium, making Xcimer's MJ-scale KrF path more architecturally isolated than Section 3 implies.
- **Recommendation:** Add a clarifying sentence to the KrF maturity entry noting that NRL subsequently converted Electra from KrF to argon fluoride to pursue ArF's bandwidth and wavelength advantages. Note that Xcimer's KrF scaling path does not have an active government co-development program at the same medium — the main government IFE gas laser effort has moved to ArF. This does not lower the KrF TRL rating (the 750 J demonstrations stand) but qualifies the heritage narrative and updates the landscape of active KrF research programs.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Focused Energy targets ~80 beamlines — fills explicit gap in Section 7
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes — Focused Energy nearest neighbor comparison)
- **Finding:** The Laser Focus World source (2021, Ditmire interview) states that Focused Energy's commercial ignition facility "will ultimately need around 80 beamlines, each using the standard chirped-pulse amplification technique." Section 7 of the existing analysis explicitly states that "available sources do not describe Focused Energy's… beam count, so this comparison cannot be made from current sources." The 80-beamline figure directly fills this stated gap and has material TEA implications: 80-beam illumination geometry is architecturally incompatible with the two-beam thick-liquid-wall approach Xcimer uses. This confirms that the two concepts represent distinct architectural families — not merely driver cost or efficiency tradeoffs — and that Focused Energy cannot adopt Xcimer's FLiBe liquid-wall geometry without abandoning its multi-beam illumination architecture. The beam count difference also implies different optics exposure environments (80 final focusing optics in the chamber vs. Xcimer's zero), different chamber penetration requirements, and different constraints on chamber clearing geometry.
- **Recommendation:** Update Section 7's Focused Energy comparison to include the ~80-beamline figure (with appropriate caveats about source vintage — 2021 plans may have evolved). Revise the statement that beam count is unknown. Add a sentence noting the TEA implication: 80-beam geometry likely requires a dry-wall or thin-liquid-wall chamber with exposed final optics, incurring the optics replacement costs that Xcimer's two-beam liquid-wall design explicitly avoids. Flag that this architectural incompatibility means the two companies are not on converging design paths — commercial viability of one approach does not validate the other.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: DPSSL wall-plug efficiency may reach 15–20%, not ~10% — Focused Energy efficiency advantage larger than analysis implies
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes — Focused Energy nearest neighbor, driver efficiency comparison)
- **Finding:** The Optica OPN source cites independent benchmarks from laser advocates: glass/solid-state laser systems (the category including DPSSL) are claimed capable of "15%–20%" wall-plug efficiency, while gas laser advocates claim "around 10%." The existing analysis attributes "~10% wall-plug efficiency" to Focused Energy's DPSSL approach and frames the comparison as a modest gap over Xcimer's 5–7% KrF. If Focused Energy's DPSSL architecture targets 15–20% (not 10%), the efficiency ratio vs. Xcimer grows to 2–3× rather than ~1.4×. At Qsci = 250 and 15% laser efficiency, Focused Energy's wall-plug gain would be ~37.5 (vs. Xcimer's ~17.5 at 7%) — more than doubling the electrical output per unit of fusion yield. This substantially shifts which cost lever dominates: if DPSSL efficiency reaches 15%, the higher driver $/J cost (cited as $700–1,000/J for DPSSL in the XEC whitepaper) could be offset by proportionally more net electricity per joule of driver input.
- **Recommendation:** Add a note in Section 7's Focused Energy comparison acknowledging that the ~10% efficiency cited for Focused Energy may be a near-term target rather than the community's long-run expectation for DPSSL. Reference the Optica benchmark (15–20% for solid-state lasers). Flag that if DPSSL achieves the upper end of this range, the economic comparison is substantially more favorable to the high-efficiency/high-cost-driver approach than the current framing suggests, and recommend that the TEA model include a scenario branch for Focused Energy-class DPSSL at 15% efficiency to bound the competitive outcome.
- **Priority:** minor
