### F-1: Beta ceiling for QI stellarators is higher than Stellaris's design point
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 2: Low-Beta Machine Scale Penalty) and Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes)
- **Finding:** arxiv-2512-08825 (CIEMAT-QI4X, December 2025) demonstrates a QI stellarator configuration achieving beta tolerance up to 4% while maintaining resilient divertor island structure, small neoclassical and turbulent transport, and good fast-ion confinement across the full beta range. The analysis currently frames Stellaris's 2.76% beta as an inherent QI stellarator characteristic ("roughly half the 5–8% beta typical of compact tokamaks") and bases the scale penalty argument on treating this as the physics-imposed operating point. The CIEMAT-QI4X data provides a concrete counterexample: a QI configuration achieves ~45% higher beta (4% vs 2.76%) while retaining the properties (island divertor compatibility, alpha confinement, low bootstrap current) that Stellaris was designed to preserve. This makes Stellaris's 2.76% a v1 design-point choice, not a QI ceiling — a distinction that materially affects how the scale penalty is framed for TEA purposes. The analysis already notes that Proxima themselves stated "more commercially attractive designs are possible," but without a specific achievable beta target to anchor that claim.
- **Recommendation:** In Challenge 2, add a sentence distinguishing between the Stellaris design-point beta (2.76%) and the demonstrated QI beta ceiling (~4%, per CIEMAT-QI4X arxiv:2512.08825). Frame the scale penalty as specific to v1 Stellaris rather than an inherent QI limitation. In the modeling approach section (H2 sensitivity discussion or as a new sensitivity axis), note that a follow-on QI design operating at 4% beta would materially reduce the low-beta scale penalty and should be treated as a scenario branch rather than a fixed parameter. Cite CIEMAT-QI4X in Section 8 as a cross-concept comparator confirming higher-beta QI configurations are feasible.
- **Priority:** important
