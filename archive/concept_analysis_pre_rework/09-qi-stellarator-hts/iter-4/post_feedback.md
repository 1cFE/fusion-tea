### F-1: H4 capital cost implication inconsistent between narrative and model
- **Target:** Section 7 (CAS-level cost delta table) and model Scenario A/B comparison
- **Finding:** Section 7 claims CAS22 (H&CD) carries a "Large −: −50% to −80%" cost advantage conditional on H4=true (ignition achieved). But the Scenario A vs. B model comparison shows only a 3.8 $/MWh delta ($106.4 vs $110.2), which captures only the recirculating power difference from running 5 MW vs. 50 MW ECRH steady-state. The C220104 line item is 353 M$ in both scenarios — the model uses the same installed ECRH system capital regardless of whether ignition is achieved. If H4=true means a smaller installed ECRH system is needed (5 MW rated vs. 50 MW rated), C220104 should be materially lower in Scenario A, not identical to Scenario B. The "large −" claim implies capital equipment reduction that the Scenario A/B delta does not reflect. A 10:1 reduction in installed ECRH capacity would eliminate roughly 300 M$ or more from C220104, not 3.8 $/MWh worth (~310 M$ total across all accounts). Either the cost model correctly treats C220104 as the startup-rated 50 MW system in both scenarios (and the "large −" claim is wrong), or the model underestimates the H4=true capital savings.
- **Recommendation:** Resolve the inconsistency explicitly. Option A: Confirm that C220104 represents the 50 MW installed system needed for startup in both scenarios, and revise Section 7 to say the H&CD advantage is primarily operational (lower recirculating power, ~3–4 $/MWh) rather than capital. Option B: Add a Scenario C that models a smaller installed ECRH system under H4=true (e.g., 10 MW rated capital), compute C220104 reduction, and show the resulting LCOE. The analysis's central competitiveness argument — that H&CD savings offset the coil premium — depends on this being a capital cost reduction, not just a recirculating power benefit.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Magnet replacement lifecycle cost absent from parameter table and gap inventory
- **Target:** Section 5 (Missing Parameters) and Section 6 (Data Gap Inventory)
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly identifies in Section 3 and the model output (Key Assumption #9) that REBCO fluence limits (~3×10²² m⁻²) require at least two full magnet replacement events over a 30-year plant lifetime, and notes this is "unique among the concepts analyzed" and "not captured by framework CAS72." However, this cost driver does not appear as a named entry in the Section 5 missing parameters table, and Section 6's Gap 7 covers only "O&M cost breakdown (fixed vs. variable, scheduled maintenance intervals)" — it does not call out magnet replacement as a distinct deferred-CAPEX item. As a result, the 106.4 $/MWh LCOE is a lower bound for two independent reasons: (1) the coil cost multiplier (acknowledged), and (2) the omission of two major scheduled capital replacement events (not presented to the reader as a compounding lower-bound caveat). Given that replacing the coil system at 10-year intervals carries costs potentially comparable to the original C220103, this omission is material.
- **Recommendation:** Add a row to the Section 5 missing parameters table: "Coil replacement lifecycle cost (2× over 30-yr plant life)" — gap type: truly-unknown, criticality: blocking, notes: REBCO fluence limit sets replacement interval; cost is order of original C220103 (~500 M$ at default, up to 2.5 B$ at 5× multiplier); not modeled in CAS72. Add a corresponding entry to Section 6, separate from Gap 7. Add a sentence to the LCOE interpretation section noting that the quoted LCOE is a lower bound for both the coil cost multiplier and magnet replacement omission.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Coil cost multiplier not integrated into sensitivity ranking
- **Target:** Model output sensitivity table and Section 2 (Primary Sensitivity Axes)
- **Finding:** The model's computed sensitivity table lists availability (−0.89), construction time (+0.40), and R0/elon (+0.31) as the top levers. The model's own note states: "C220103 gradient reflects wound-coil framework defaults, not actual 3D coil cost structure." The coil multiplier sweep is provided separately and shows a 36 $/MWh swing ($106 to $143) across the 1×–5× range, compared to an ~11 $/MWh swing from varying capacity factor across 85–95%. By the analysis's own framing (Section 2: "3D coil cost multiplier (CAS21)...this single parameter dominates the competitiveness question"), the coil multiplier is the primary sensitivity axis. But a reader comparing the quantitative sensitivity table across concepts would rank this concept as availability-driven, not coil-cost-driven — the opposite of the analysis's conclusion. The separation of the multiplier sweep from the sensitivity table creates an inconsistency in how the concept's risk profile is communicated.
- **Recommendation:** Add a summary sentence immediately before or after the sensitivity table that explicitly states: "The dominant sensitivity axis is the C220103 coil cost multiplier (see sweep above: 106–143 $/MWh across 1×–5×), which is not captured by the elasticity table because the framework uses wound-coil defaults. The elasticity table reflects geometry and operational sensitivities only." Alternatively, reorder Section 2 to lead with the coil multiplier sweep as the primary sensitivity finding, referencing the LCOE swing directly.
- **Priority:** important
