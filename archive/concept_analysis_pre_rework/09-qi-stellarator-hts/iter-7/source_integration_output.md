### F-1: W7-X cost reference in Section 2 conflates magnet cost with total site cost
- **Target:** Section 2 (3D Non-Planar HTS Coil CAPEX challenge) and Section 7 (CAS account delta table)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis states "W7-X (LTS conductors, 6 T, mid-1990s design, approximately €1 billion for the full magnet system)" as the primary LTS stellarator cost reference point for the coil CAPEX challenge. The Wikipedia W7-X source establishes that the total W7-X stellarator investment 1997–2014 was €370 million, and the full 18-year site cost including personnel and operations was €1.06 billion. The €1 billion figure appears to conflate total site cost over 18 years (including ~€690M operations) with hardware cost. If taken at face value, the analysis overstates the W7-X hardware cost by roughly 3×, which distorts the LTS baseline that the 1.5–5× HTS coil multiplier range is implicitly anchored against. The Wikipedia source also notes the cost "exceeded the original budget estimate, mainly because the initial development phase was longer than expected, doubling the personnel costs" — indicating the overrun was ops-driven, not hardware-driven.
- **Recommendation:** Correct the W7-X cost reference in Section 2 to distinguish hardware investment (~€370M for the full stellarator, 1997–2014) from total site cost including operations (~€1.06B over 18 years). Note that the operations overrun was driven by schedule slip (9-year delay from expected 2006 to 2015 completion), not by coil manufacturing cost alone. Adjust the framing of the LTS cost baseline accordingly — the hardware reference point for a full W7-X-scale stellarator is closer to €370M than €1B. This does not change the direction of the coil CAPEX challenge (3D HTS coils will still be expensive) but removes a potentially misleading quantitative anchor. Also note in the C220103 CAS account delta table that the W7-X cost reference cited in the footnotes should carry this clarification.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: W7-X 2025 experimental records absent from capacity factor and island divertor TRL assessments
- **Target:** Section 3 (Island Divertor at Burning Plasma Power Density, TRL 5–6) and Section 5 (Capacity factor parameter row)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The Wikipedia W7-X source documents two recent milestones that directly bear on two of the analysis's key claims. First, in February 2023, W7-X achieved up to 30 minutes of continuous plasma discharge, explicitly described as demonstrating "an essential feature of a future fusion power plant: continuous operation." Second, in June 2025, W7-X achieved 1.8 GJ energy turnover in a 6-minute run using continuous pellet injection, surpassing the EAST tokamak's 2025 record. The analysis (created March 2026) references W7-X for ">97% run-time availability in experimental context" and for island divertor steady-state demonstration, but cites neither the 30-minute discharge nor the 1.8 GJ energy record. These milestones are the most direct experimental evidence for the 85–95% capacity factor range and for the island divertor's steady-state capability, and their absence leaves the capacity factor claim supported only by the Helios analogue design target (88%) and a general disruption-free argument.
- **Recommendation:** In Section 3 (Island Divertor, Demonstrated subsection), add the 2023 30-minute discharge and 2025 1.8 GJ energy record as concrete milestones validating steady-state island divertor operation. In Section 5, update the capacity factor row's Notes cell to reference these records alongside the Helios 88% analogue — noting that W7-X's 2025 energy record exceeded the EAST tokamak record, providing cross-device context for the stellarator's long-pulse advantage. The TRL 5–6 rating for the island divertor remains appropriate (power density gap to Stellaris is unchanged), but the experimental basis for it should include the most recent data.
- **Priority:** important
