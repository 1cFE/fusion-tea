VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Capacity factor absent from Section 5 parameter table
- **Target:** Section 5 (LCOE-Relevant Parameters) and Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes)
- **Finding:** Section 7 identifies capacity factor as potentially the second-largest LCOE lever for this concept and provides a quantified threshold (≥3–5 percentage point advantage needed to offset the low-beta CAPEX penalty), making it a first-class TEA parameter. However, Section 5 contains no row for capacity factor — not even a modeled range or scenario assumption. The missing parameters table acknowledges the data gap, but for a parameter the analysis itself treats as the primary counterweight to higher CAPEX, the absence of even a placeholder modeled range (e.g., "85–95% [modeled assumption: disruption-free steady-state; no published data]") leaves the parameter set incomplete relative to Goal 3.
- **Recommendation:** Add a capacity factor row to the Section 5 available parameters table with a modeled range derived from the analysis's own framing (steady-state, disruption-free → high end of MFE range; blanket replacement interval unknown → range floor), clearly flagged as a modeled assumption rather than a sourced datum. This makes the Section 7 quantified threshold testable against a named parameter rather than floating in the narrative.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Island divertor failure consequence not stated
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 5: Island divertor scaling) and the Key Hypotheses block
- **Finding:** Section 2 identifies island divertor scaling to burning plasma power density as a unique stellarator physics gap and correctly notes it is a "validated-physics-at-scale" issue. However, it does not state what happens to the concept — or to the TEA — if the island divertor fails to scale. The four hypotheses (H1–H4) cover coil cost, scale/capacity factor, TBR adequacy, and ignition, but not the island divertor. This leaves a stellarator-specific technical bet (the tokamak's poloidal divertor has substantially more design flexibility) without a formalized failure consequence. Goal 5 requires each key technical bet to be stated with its failure mode.
- **Recommendation:** Add a fifth hypothesis (H5) for the island divertor, or extend the Section 2 challenge discussion to state the consequence explicitly: if the island divertor fails to manage exhaust at fusion power density, the required mitigation (geometry-constrained redesign, or acceptance of higher target erosion rates and shorter replacement intervals) and its TEA implication (increased maintenance cost or concept infeasibility) should be named. Even a brief "if false: [consequence]" framing would satisfy Goal 5 for this item.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: 3D tungsten first wall cost delta missing from CAS-level table
- **Target:** Section 4 (Key Materials) and Section 7 CAS-level cost delta summary
- **Finding:** Section 4 identifies 3D curved tungsten first wall fabrication as a cost and manufacturing challenge specific to the QI stellarator's helical plasma boundary — explicitly distinguishing it from the flat or cylindrical tungsten tiles used in tokamak designs. However, no row appears in the Section 7 CAS-level cost delta table for plasma-facing components or first wall. The directional cost implication relative to the HTS compact tokamak reference is left uncharacterized, inconsistent with Goal 3's requirement that each identified differentiator carry a stated cost implication.
- **Recommendation:** Add a plasma-facing components / first wall row to the Section 7 CAS-level cost delta table. The direction is positive (Stellaris more expensive) due to 3D geometry requiring custom-curved tungsten tile fabrication; the magnitude is uncertain but likely small relative to the magnet premium — "small positive delta, 5–15% premium over tokamak FW cost" with basis in the geometric complexity argument from Section 4 would be appropriate.
- **Priority:** minor
