## Assessment Notes

The new source (`compoundsemiconductor-119149-us-team-reinvents-the.md`) describes a diamond-based
photoconductive semiconductor switch (PCSS) developed by Can Bayram et al. at the University of
Illinois (published in IEEE Electron Device Letters). The device achieves:

- 44 A/cm current density at 60 V DC
- ~2 ns rise/fall switching times
- >10^11 on/off ratio
- Buried metallic p+ channel design that eliminates filament formation
- Band-gap laser triggering (UV) enabling larger device sizes

This technology is nominally relevant to LPPFusion's proposed diamond-film UV-laser switches for
the ion beam decelerator. However, the existing analysis already characterizes diamond-film switches
as "research-stage technology not commercially available for this application" (Section 4) and
classifies them as a **nice-to-have** gap (#15 in Section 6 data gap inventory), the lowest
priority tier. The new source:

1. Confirms the research-stage characterization — the device is at approximately TRL 3-4, not
   commercial-ready.
2. Does not provide any LCOE-relevant parameters (no cost data, no efficiency figures for the
   decelerator application, no power density sufficient to assess DPF-scale adequacy).
3. Does not change the ion beam decelerator TRL (still TRL 2 — the decelerator as a system has
   no prototype regardless of switch component advances).
4. Does not affect any blocking or important gap in the analysis.
5. Does not alter any conclusion about the physics feasibility risks (QMF, yield gap), the DEC
   efficiency sensitivity, or the recirculating fraction constraint.

The application context of the source (grid protection switching, not pulsed fusion power) and the
gap between demonstrated specs (44 A/cm at 60 V) and DPF-scale requirements further limit its
material relevance. The analysis's structural conclusions and all parameter values are unaffected.
