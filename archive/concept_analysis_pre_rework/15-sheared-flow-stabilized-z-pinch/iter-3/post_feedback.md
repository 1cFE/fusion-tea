VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Driver cost framing contradicted by model
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 5) and Section 4 (pulsed power supply chain)
- **Finding:** The analysis states the pulsed power driver is "the primary capital cost driver for this concept — it replaces the magnet system cost that dominates tokamak economics" (Goal 3). The model contradicts this: driver capital is $8.1M/module (C220107) while the LiPb blanket/first wall is $58.5M/module (C220101), and the driver cost sensitivity sweep from $0.5M to $10M/MJ stored produces only a 6% LCOE change ($22.25 → $23.74 ¢/kWh). The actual LCOE is dominated by (a) the high recirculating power fraction (~47.5%) inflating specific capital, (b) the LiPb blanket system, and (c) scheduled replacement costs ($71.9M/yr). The supply chain narrative in Section 4 is important and well-supported, but the cost-driver framing is wrong.
- **Recommendation:** Revise the Section 2 and Section 4 driver cost narrative to reflect the model's finding: the pulsed power driver is a *supply chain and reliability* risk, not the dominant capital cost item. The actual capital cost structure is LiPb-blanket-dominated, with LCOE primarily driven by the recirculating power fraction (a function of Q). State explicitly that the driver matters most through its effect on recirculating power (which is Q-dependent), not through its direct capital cost.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Nearest-neighbor concept positioning absent
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and overall framing (Goal 1)
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies the concept family (MFE — Z-pinch) and compares well against the ST-HTS, but does not name or briefly characterize 2–3 nearest-neighbor concepts to position the SFS Z-pinch within the design space. Obvious candidates not addressed: MagLIF (magnetized liner inertial fusion — also a pulsed pinch concept at Sandia's Z machine, shares the pulsed-driver economics and compression-based confinement), Dense Plasma Focus (another Z-discharge concept with some structural similarity), and/or laser ICF (shared pulsed-driver-dominated economics). Without these comparators, a reader cannot determine whether the pulsed-driver supply chain risks, the rep-rate gap, or the electrode erosion challenge are unique to Zap's approach or generic to the pinch/pulsed-fusion family.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief paragraph to Section 7 naming 2–3 nearest neighbors and distinguishing what the SFS Z-pinch shares with them versus what is unique. Focus on the TEA-relevant differences: does MagLIF face the same rep-rate gap? Is the electrode erosion challenge unique to sheared-flow geometry? How does the driver cost structure compare across pulsed-pinch concepts? Even a short comparative statement per neighbor is sufficient.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Modeling approach not stated in analysis
- **Target:** Section 2 or a dedicated modeling approach section (Goal 4)
- **Finding:** The checklist criterion requires the analysis to state whether 1costingfe or free-form modeling is appropriate and why. The model output footer confirms 1costingfe was used with several [OVERRIDE] accounts, but the analysis.md text contains no discussion of this choice, no rationale for which accounts were overridden, and no acknowledgment of the modeling approach. A reader of the analysis alone cannot determine how the TEA should be structured.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short paragraph (could be at the end of Section 2 or beginning of Section 5) stating: (a) 1costingfe with account-level overrides is the appropriate approach because the standard CAS22 structure maps onto this concept with substitutions (pulsed driver replaces magnets, LiPb electrode replaces first-wall/blanket); (b) which accounts require free-form overrides (C220103 → $0, C220107 → driver, C220108 → electrodes); and (c) which free parameters are effectively unconstrained (driver cost $/MJ, electrode replacement interval) and will require assumption documentation in the model.
- **Priority:** minor
