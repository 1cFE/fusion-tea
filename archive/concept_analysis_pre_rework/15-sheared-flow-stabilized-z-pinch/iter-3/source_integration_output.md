VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Pulsed power supply chain severity is materially underrepresented
- **Target:** Section 4 (Materials and Supply Chain) and Section 2 (Challenge 5: Pulsed power driver cost)
- **Finding:** The analysis characterizes the capacitor supply chain as a "specialty market" requiring "significant expansion" — a moderate framing. The OSTI source (Challenges and Gaps in Pulsed Power for Fusion, LLNL-JRNL-2001600, 2025) quantifies the actual constraint: building 150 fusion plants at current Western production capacity would take 125–250 years; delivery times for current orders run 4–6 years; a single plant requires 10,000–216,000 capacitors. This is a program-level constraint comparable in severity to tritium supply or Q demonstration — not a "moderate risk." The analysis's Goals 3 and 5 (TEA implications and risk capture) require this gap to be framed at its true severity.
- **Recommendation:** Upgrade the capacitor supply chain discussion in Section 4 from a paragraph-level mention to a named risk item with the OSTI-sourced quantification (125–250 plant-years of manufacturing backlog, 4–6 year lead times, 10k–216k capacitors/plant). In Section 2's "Pulsed power driver cost" challenge, add a sub-bullet noting that supply chain maturation is a 10–20 year timeline per the OSTI roadmap — placing it alongside rep-rate scaling and Q demonstration as a commercial timeline constraint.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Component lifetime gap magnitude missing from TRL assessment
- **Target:** Section 3 (High-Rep-Rate Pulsed Power Driver — TRL 3) and Section 6 (Gap #14)
- **Finding:** Section 3 flags solid-state switch and capacitor lifetimes as "on paper only" for commercial operation, but does not state the magnitude of the gap. The OSTI source quantifies it: current capacitors (Z Marx bank baseline) achieve 10⁴–10⁵ shots; commercial pulsed fusion requires 10⁸–10⁹ shots — a 4–6 order of magnitude shortfall. This magnitude fundamentally changes how the TRL-3 rating should be read and is essential context for Goal 5 (risks and assumptions). A gap measured in orders of magnitude is categorically different from an engineering extrapolation.
- **Recommendation:** Add quantitative framing to Section 3's driver subsystem assessment: state the 10⁴–10⁵ demonstrated vs. 10⁸–10⁹ required lifetime range and label it as a 4–6 OOM gap. Update Gap #14 in Section 6 to carry this magnitude. This does not change the TRL-3 rating but materially changes the risk interpretation — a reader should understand this is not a "1.5× extrapolation" like the 0.2→10 Hz rep rate, but a multi-decade development challenge.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Switching technology gap is a capability problem, not only a lifetime problem
- **Target:** Section 3 (High-Rep-Rate Pulsed Power Driver) and Section 2 (Challenge 5)
- **Finding:** The analysis frames the solid-state switch challenge as a lifetime issue ("solid-state switch lifetime under 10 Hz, 10⁸+ cycle commercial operation"). The OSTI source reveals a more fundamental problem: current commercial Si MOSFETs top out at 6.5–10 kV and ~500 A, which is insufficient for fusion switching requirements (100–200 kV, 100–200 kA). Wide-bandgap materials (4H-SiC) reach 15–20 kV in custom devices but have no demonstrated path to the required voltage/current class. Lifetime is a secondary concern — the switches that exist today cannot meet specifications at any lifetime. The analysis's current framing understates the risk level for Goals 3 and 5. (Partial mitigation: the OSTI source notes Z-pinch operates at lower voltages, 50–200 kV, vs. other pulsed fusion approaches at 5–10 MV, which is a relative advantage worth capturing.)
- **Recommendation:** Revise Section 3's driver subsystem "On paper only" bullet to distinguish the capability gap (no commercial switch meets required voltage/current specs) from the lifetime gap (demonstrated switches won't survive commercial duty cycles). Add a note that the Z-pinch's lower operating voltage (50–200 kV vs. MV-class competitors) is a relative advantage in switch development. This sharpens Section 6 Gap #14 from "lifetime unknown" to "requires new switch technology class before lifetime can even be measured."
- **Priority:** important
