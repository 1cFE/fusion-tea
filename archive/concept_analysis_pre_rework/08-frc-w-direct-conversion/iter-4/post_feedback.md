### F-1: f_rep elasticity near zero despite being stated as the #2 LCOE lever
- **Target:** Sensitivity output and model_setup.py power balance
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis (Section 2.3, Section 2 Modeling Approach) correctly argues that rep rate scales output power linearly — halving rep rate from 2 Hz to 1 Hz halves energy yield from identical capital, roughly doubling LCOE. This makes rep rate elasticity the dominant or near-dominant engineering lever (~−1.0 expected). The sensitivity output shows `f_rep` elasticity = −0.027, which is essentially flat. The discrepancy indicates the model fixes `net_electric_mw = 1000.0` as a hard input rather than deriving it from (MJ/pulse × rep_rate × N_modules × η). As a result, rep rate doesn't propagate into capital cost allocation and the stated #2 LCOE sensitivity parameter is not actually tested. The sensitivity sweep is therefore misleading: `availability` (−0.73) and `q_eng` (−0.46) are the top levers only because rep rate is structurally neutralized as a free variable.
- **Recommendation:** Add explicit rep rate scenarios rather than relying on `f_rep` as a sensitivity parameter within the fixed-output framework. At minimum, run the model at three rep rate points (0.5 Hz, 1.0 Hz, 2.0 Hz) by scaling `p_driver` and the per-module output proportionally. Present LCOE vs. rep rate as a scenario table. This directly tests the analysis's H2-adjacent claim that "capital cost per MWe scales inversely with rep rate" and shows the 1 Hz → 2 Hz sensitivity quantitatively.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Low-Q scenario missing; base case Q_sci=3.5 inconsistent with ARPA-E implied Q~1.2
- **Target:** Sensitivity output and Key Assumptions block
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis (Section 2, Challenge 1) identifies Q as the "central modeling blocker" with "no published gain figure; ARPA-E implies Q~1.2." The energy balance in Section 2.2 shows that at Q=1.2, η_coupling=0.2, η_recovery=0.9, round-trip recovery = 0.216 + 0.72 = 0.936 < 1.0 — the concept does not produce net electricity at the ARPA-E design point. Yet the model uses Q_sci=3.5, Q_eng=3.0, which is ~3× the only published Q-adjacent figure. The sensitivity sweep varies `q_eng` by ±10% (elasticity −0.46), but this doesn't capture the threshold behavior at Q~1.2 where net output crosses zero. The model's note acknowledges the missing Q data but doesn't test the go/no-go boundary numerically.
- **Recommendation:** Add a low-Q scenario parameterized at the ARPA-E-consistent operating point (Q_sci ≈ 1.2–1.5 by adjusting `p_driver` upward to reflect the η·Q=0.24 constraint). If the model produces negative or undefined LCOE at that Q, document this explicitly as "concept is below net-positive threshold at the only published Q figure" — this is the most important model output for communicating risk. The base case Q_sci=3.5 should be labeled as the D-He3 commercial design target (not the current experimental state) to avoid implying the ARPA-E data supports it.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Capacitor bank $/J cost not in sensitivity sweep; H2 hypothesis untested
- **Target:** Sensitivity output (capacitor bank / C220104)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis (Section 2 H2, Section 4 High-Voltage Capacitors) identifies the capacitor bank cost as a go/no-go economic threshold: "For LCOE < 50 c/kWh, the effective bank cost must fall below approximately $0.50/J... If the current pulse power industry baseline of ~$5/J holds, LCOE exceeds 500 c/kWh." This is a 10× cost reduction requirement and the analysis's most explicit quantitative hypothesis. The model bakes in the optimistic NOAK $0.50/J assumption (C220104 = $10M/module for ~20 MJ) but the sensitivity sweep does not include capacitor bank $/J as a free variable. H2 is stated but not numerically validated — the model never shows what LCOE looks like at $2/J or $5/J, so the reader cannot verify whether the analysis's threshold claim is consistent with the model's accounting.
- **Recommendation:** Add a capacitor bank cost scenario sweep covering at least three points: (a) NOAK $0.50/J (current base, $10M/module), (b) intermediate $2/J ($40M/module, mid-term learning curve), and (c) today's $5/J ($100M/module, no learning). Present LCOE for each. This directly tests H2 and lets the model output confirm or refine the analysis's claim that $5/J produces LCOE > 500 c/kWh. Implement as a post-hoc override loop on C220104 rather than a structural model change.
- **Priority:** important
