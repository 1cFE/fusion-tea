VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Modeling approach recommendation absent
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges) and Section 4 (Modeling Recommendations — implicit)
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies five critical/high-impact LCOE challenges, two of which are blocking-unknown (driver capital cost, target gain), and explicitly notes that no published cost study exists for the electromagnetic gun driver. Yet the analysis never states whether a standard costing framework (1costingfe) is appropriate for this concept or whether free-form scenario modeling is required instead. This is a core deliverable of Goal 4. Given the depth of the physics and cost unknowns, the modeling approach decision is non-obvious and consequential — failing to make it leaves the analysis without a usable recommendation.
- **Recommendation:** Add a paragraph in Section 2 (or a dedicated subsection) stating explicitly whether 1costingfe applies to projectile ICF or not, and why. Given the blocking gaps in driver cost and target gain, the likely recommendation is free-form scenario modeling anchored on gain and driver cost as free parameters, rather than parameterization within a standard IFE cost structure. State the key hypotheses the cost model should test as testable propositions (e.g., "If target gain ≥ 200× is achievable at 60 km/s, then LCOE < $X/MWh at the stated rep rate — test this by sweeping gain from 10× to 1000×").
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: No CAS-level cost structure mapping
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges) and Section 5 (Parameters)
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies cost-relevant differentiators in detail but never maps them to the CAS cost account structure. This is required by Goal 3 (TEA Implications). A conventional tokamak has well-characterized CAS22 (reactor plant), CAS23 (turbine plant), CAS27 (special materials), etc. Projectile ICF departs structurally: the electromagnetic gun driver has no CAS analogue in any reference plant, the liquid lithium chamber integrates blanket + first wall + tritium breeding into a single flowing system unlike any separate CAS22 subsystem, and the consumable target/projectile introduces a recurring per-shot cost structure absent from steady-state references. Without CAS-level mapping, it is unclear which accounts dominate, which are suppressed (no superconducting magnets → CAS22 magnet cost is zero), and which have no cost basis.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief CAS-level cost structure paragraph noting the major structural departures: (1) CAS22 reactor plant — dominated by liquid Li chamber + EM gun driver, both without published cost basis; (2) CAS22 magnet systems — zero (no external magnets); (3) CAS23 turbine plant — conventional steam Rankine, mature; (4) recurring consumable cost — target + projectile per shot (no CAS equivalent in fission/tokamak reference structures). Flag which CAS accounts are cost-dominant unknowns vs. borrowed from reference.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Conventional tokamak differentiator list missing
- **Target:** Cross-concept comparison (Section 7 and headers)
- **Finding:** The checklist requires that key differentiators from a conventional tokamak be explicitly listed (Goal 2). The analysis compares well to MagLIF and Laser IFE, but the conventional tokamak — the standard TEA reference concept — is never used as a comparison baseline. Differentiators such as pulsed vs. steady-state operation, absence of any magnetic confinement, consumable targets replacing plasma fuel cycles, no superconducting magnet infrastructure, and the role of driver efficiency vs. plasma heating efficiency are implied throughout but never assembled into an explicit list.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short explicit differentiator list in Section 7 (or at the top of the analysis) that enumerates the key structural differences from a conventional tokamak. Five to seven bullet points are sufficient: operation mode (pulsed vs. steady-state), confinement mechanism (kinetic impact vs. magnetic equilibrium), fuel delivery (discrete targets vs. gas puffing), first wall (flowing liquid Li vs. solid plasma-facing components), driver vs. heating subsystem cost structure, and absence of superconducting magnet costs. For each, note whether the departure is a cost advantage, penalty, or neutral (per Goal 3).
- **Priority:** important
