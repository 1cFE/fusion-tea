### F-1: Modular architecture LCOE not modeled at stated commercial threshold
- **Target:** Section 2 (energy balance analysis) and model Q_sci sweep
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly identifies that Q_sci ≈ 52.5 is required for 1,500 MWe from a single system, while Inertia's stated grid-scale threshold is >30 — which yields only ~440 MWe per system. The analysis suggests modular architecture as the resolution. However, the Q_sci sweep in the model conflates two scenarios: (a) a single system varying gain toward 1,500 MWe, and (b) multiple modules at the stated gain. The Q_sci = 30 row shows $271/MWh at 441 MWe — but this is not the commercially relevant scenario. If three modules at Q_sci = 30 are used to achieve ~1,320 MWe, the total capital is approximately 3× the single-module capital, yielding a different LCOE than either the single-system sweep value or the base-case 52.5× result. The modular LCOE at the stated commercial gain threshold is the key question the model should answer, and it is not currently computed.
- **Recommendation:** Add a modular scenario sweep: at Q_sci = 30, 35, and 40 (below single-system closure), compute the number of modules needed to reach ~1,500 MWe net (ceiling integer of 1500 / net_MWe_per_module), scale total capital by module count, and report the resulting LCOE. This directly tests whether Inertia's stated >30× commercial target leads to a competitive LCOE when module count is accounted for.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Nearest neighbor concept 22 is a poor structural match
- **Target:** Header nearest-neighbor selection and Section 7 cross-concept comparison
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** Concept 22 (First Light Fusion, projectile ICF) is listed as a nearest neighbor but is the most structurally distant of the three — it uses a projectile driver, shares no laser technology with Inertia, and its capital cost structure diverges completely on the driver axis. Concepts 31 (Blue Laser Fusion OEC, DPSSL direct drive, D-T) and 32 (GenF French National, DPSSL direct drive, D-T) share Inertia's DPSSL semiconductor-diode driver technology and provide a direct TEA comparison on the laser capital axis — the dominant cost driver for this concept. The Section 7 narrative does a thorough job comparing to Xcimer (concept 17a), but omits comparison to the DPSSL direct-drive laser ICF concepts where the driver technology is most analogous and the indirect vs. direct drive coupling penalty can be directly quantified.
- **Recommendation:** Replace concept 22 with concept 31 or 32 as the third nearest neighbor in the header. Add a brief comparison in Section 7 between Inertia's indirect-drive DPSSL design and the DPSSL direct-drive concepts (31 or 32), specifically on laser capital cost per joule and required Q_target, to quantify the TEA penalty of the indirect drive coupling architecture relative to same-driver-technology alternatives.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Indirect drive coupling loss not stated as an explicit capital cost multiplier
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 4, coupling efficiency) and Section 7 (differentiators from MFE)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly states that indirect drive achieves ~12% laser-to-capsule coupling and that the remaining 88% heats the hohlraum/first wall as usable thermal energy. But the cost implication of this coupling structure is not explicitly stated as a capital multiplier: 12% coupling means the laser must deliver approximately 8× the capsule implosion energy, which is the primary structural reason Inertia requires a 10 MJ DPSSL driver while a hybrid direct-drive concept (Xcimer, >50% coupling) could achieve the same capsule yield at ~2–2.5 MJ. The analysis shows the $700–1,000/J vs. $100–120/J laser cost comparison in Section 7 but does not connect the coupling efficiency to this capital divergence in the Section 2 challenge discussion or Section 7 differentiator list. The reader cannot derive from the current framing that indirect drive's coupling geometry structurally requires ~4–5× more laser energy per unit target yield than direct drive, making it the dominant driver of the laser capital penalty relative to other IFE concepts.
- **Recommendation:** In Section 2 Challenge 4 (indirect drive coupling), add an explicit statement of the capital implication: given the ~12% coupling and the ~$1,000/J NOAK laser cost, each joule of capsule implosion energy requires ~8 J of laser delivery, structurally multiplying laser capital by ~4–5× relative to direct or hybrid-drive concepts at the same Q_target. This should also appear as a bullet in Section 7's divergences-from-MFE list as the reason laser capital dominates IFE cost structure in a way that has no MFE analogue.
- **Priority:** important
