## 1. The ambition: accelerate progress in physical technology

[OWNER-VERBATIM] "What would it take for us (humanity) to rapidly accelerate real innovation and progress in the world of physical technology?"

[OWNER] This is the umbrella for the work. Fusion embodies both the promise of technological progress and the difficulty of realizing it. The ambition leads into the focus on concept design and technoeconomics, and into the challenge of bringing scientific and engineering knowledge together through automated research.

[OWNER] Hardware still has to be built. AI working in bits will not deliver a working fusion reactor by itself. The opportunity for leverage is to improve where we invest and what we learn from that investment:

- [OWNER] Invest in the concepts with the most promise.
- [OWNER-VERBATIM] Understand "exactly *what* needs to be demonstrated for some larger concept to be viable".

## 2. The recurring question: useful feedback before building

[AGENT] (ratified by owner, 2026-09-04): "How much useful feedback can we obtain before building, and how can that feedback make the building count for more?"

[OWNER] Specific tests, indicators, and questions should connect the project's work to the larger ambition. This connection can recur throughout the narrative.

## 3. The technical motif: AI for Software → AI for Systems

[OWNER] Two threads recur together:

- [OWNER] Transfer learnings and patterns from AI-assisted software development.
- [OWNER] Identify the core differences encountered along the way and explain the challenges those differences introduce.

[AGENT] Candidate connecting idea: textual models, version control, automated checks, and agent workflows offer transferable patterns. Executing a physical-system model reveals consequences of its assumptions; determining whether those assumptions describe reality requires engineering evidence. This difference helps connect the tooling back to the feedback-before-building question.
