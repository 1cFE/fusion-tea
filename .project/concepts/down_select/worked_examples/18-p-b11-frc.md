# Trace: 18-p-b11-frc

## Stage 1 (time-to-Stage-2 discount)
- **Current TRL / Q achieved**: Confinement TRL 5–6 at subscale (C-2W: Ttot ~3 keV, Te ~1 keV peak, ~40 ms pulses, fast-ion/thermal pressure ratio ~1.5×). p-B11 in magnetic confinement TRL 1–2 (fusion products observed 2023; Q_plasma <0.001). Net energy gain for p-B11 never demonstrated in any device [analysis §3].
- **Estimated capital and time to Stage-2 entry**: TAE has spent >$1.3B over ~25 years; Da Vinci construction start 2026, first plasma 2029, net energy 2030 [dossier]. The intermediate Copernicus step is being skipped — meaning the entry into Stage 2 *is itself* the foundational physics demonstrator (Q>1 for p-B11 has never been shown anywhere).
- **Paradigm co-development depth**: Shallow. The beam-driven FRC paradigm is essentially a TAE-only program — no parallel national-lab or international effort validates the sustained T_i>>T_e regime at fusion conditions. Adjacent FRC work (Helion) is pulsed-compression with a different operating regime. Aneutronic peers (HB11, Marvel, LPPFusion, Pale Blue) attack p-B11 from entirely different geometries.
- **Open scientific heritage**: Mixed. FRC physics has open academic lineage (Rostoker, UC Irvine roots); recent disclosures are peer-reviewed (Nature Communications 2025, Nuclear Fusion 2024 / OSTI 2441289). Da Vinci commercial parameters are proprietary; ICC remains patent-stage only [analysis §1].
- **Workforce depth**: Narrow. Beam-driven FRC expertise is concentrated at TAE; high-energy NBI capability sits across a handful of fusion labs (ITER NBI team, JT-60U, JAEA). Far thinner than the tokamak workforce.
- **Discount applied**: **Heavy**. A shallow co-development paradigm plus a foundational physics question that has no validating program elsewhere means time-to-Stage-2 is long and high-variance.

## Stage 2 (FOAK affordability)
- **F2.a (minimum-viable-plant capital cost)**: Da Vinci targeted at 50 MWe initial / 350–500 MWe scaled; explorer baseline overnight cost ~$3,089/kW at 1 GW. Unit size and capital scale put TAE in the "hyperscaler / strategic-investor accessible" tier rather than the "sovereigns only" tier.
- **F2.b (build-time risk)**: High. Construction time baseline 6 years (sensitivity elasticity +0.37). Most of the build risk is *not* civil/site construction but **physics-validation risk during commissioning** — if the plasma doesn't reach commercial conditions, the build doesn't matter.
- **F2.c (regulatory framework state) — pole**: **Leverage pole.** Aneutronic operation with <1% neutron fraction, no tritium, hands-on maintenance fits squarely within the NRC Part 30 fusion framework (Feb 2026). TAE is the best-positioned concept in the pipeline for the lighter framework.
- **F2.d (critical component supply maturity) — pole + slack/bottleneck**: **Failure pole, bottleneck.** Weighting by CAPEX share at FOAK, the high-energy NBI driver (250 keV protons, ~30 MW) dominates and has no commercial supply chain at fusion scale [ecosystem brief: minimal non-fusion industrial base for fusion-relevant high-energy drivers]. ITER NNBI is an adjacent precedent, not commercial supply. The absence of REBCO/FLiBe/Li-6/Be exposure is a real structural win but is not the cost-weighted dominant component at FOAK.
- **E2.a (crossover platform attracts non-fusion investment)**: Weak. NBI components have some defense crossover (particle accelerator tech, RF power supplies, ion implantation), but TAE's specific 250 keV proton NBI does not map to a large non-fusion revenue platform the way CFS HTS magnets do.
- **E2.b (intra-fusion early-mover supply-chain effect)**: Negligible. TAE's NBI, FRC vessel, and copper coil configuration are bespoke; no earlier-shipping fusion concept builds the supply chain TAE's FOAK uses.
- **Plausible FOAK buyer**: Strategic / hyperscaler-adjacent (Trump Media merger suggests data-center power wedge), or DoD-backed via aneutronic-fusion procurement. 50 MWe initial unit size + aneutronic safety profile + hands-on maintenance is a hyperscaler-fit wedge.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 2**: **F2.b at the failure pole** — Da Vinci's commissioning campaign *is* the foundational physics demonstration; non-converging plasma returns the plant to Branch A with no LCOE.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 2**: **F2.c at the leverage pole** — TAE is structurally the best-fit concept in the pipeline for the new NRC Part 30 fusion framework.

## Stage 3 (chasm crossing)
- **F3.b (site-specialization fraction)**: Low-to-moderate. Resistive copper coils are wound on-site (per C1 scoring). NBI injectors are factory-built and site-assembled into an array. Vessel is site-welded but small. No FLiBe loops, no breeding blanket, no remote-handling cells.
- **F3.c (replication unit size for one buyer)**: Favorable. 50 MWe initial unit means one hyperscaler could commit to ~12 units for ~600 MWe within a single-buyer envelope. 350–500 MWe scaled units broaden the buyer set without forcing sovereign-only deals.
- **F3.a (supply-chain maturity at chasm scale) — pole + slack/bottleneck**: **Failure pole, bottleneck.** The dominant subsystem (high-energy NBI) sits on a supply chain with no non-fusion industrial demand to keep it scaling alongside fusion (contrast REBCO where MRI/grid/transport pull capacity faster than fusion alone could) [ecosystem brief, NBI analogue].
- **F3.d (regulatory amortization path) — pole**: **Leverage pole.** Aneutronic + steady-state + standardized Da Vinci design is highly amortizable: the first NRC Part 30 fusion license sets precedent that subsequent identical units inherit. No D-T-specific per-plant tritium accountancy re-litigation.
- **E3.a (intra-fusion fleet co-development)**: Weak. Few other concepts share TAE's specific component stack (no other concept in the pipeline uses 250 keV proton NBI with FRC).
- **E3.b (shared sub-problem solution leverage)**: Moderate-to-weak. Shared: high-energy NBI development (some leverage from ITER NNBI, JT-60SA), alpha-channeling / aneutronic-shielding R&D across the p-B11 cohort, direct-energy-conversion R&D shared with Helion and Pale Blue. Not shared: sustained T_i>>T_e bremsstrahlung suppression — TAE's bespoke physics bet.
- **Most plausible chasm-crossing path**: Hyperscaler fleet of 50 MWe units, replicated 10–20× per buyer, leveraging amortized Part 30 license and aneutronic safety case. Conditional on Branch B physics closing.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 3**: **F3.a at the failure pole** — NBI supply chain has no external-market scaling partner; per-plant driver cost stays high through the chasm.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 3**: **F3.d at the leverage pole** — aneutronic + standardized Da Vinci design is unusually well-suited to regulatory amortization under the Part 30 framework.

## Stage 4 (learning-curve descent)
- **F4.b (volume-driven vs. R&D-driven learning mechanism)**: **R&D.** Steady-state plants at 50–500 MWe per unit cannot reach the "many cheap modules" volumes that drive Wright's Law solar-style descent. Cost reductions come primarily from NBI efficiency gains, ICC demonstration, and confinement-time improvements rather than mass-manufacturing volume.
- **F4.d (modularization-vs-scale crossover)**: Modularity score 3.0 per synthesis — middling. NBI is the only meaningfully modular CAS22 element. Plant is not over-modularized (avoiding NuScale-style failure mode) but doesn't enjoy strong modular learning benefits.
- **F4.a (cost-reduction knobs and non-fusion ride-along) — pole**: **Failure pole.** Few independent cost-reduction knobs, most fusion-funded. NBI efficiency, ICC efficiency, Q_plasma, capacity factor — all fusion-specific R&D problems with limited non-fusion industries paying for the innovation.
- **F4.c (specialty-input external-market position) — pole + slack/bottleneck**: **Leverage pole, slack.** B-11 separation chemistry is mature; semiconductor-doping market provides baseline non-fusion demand [ecosystem brief: B-11 lowest-severity bottleneck among fuel-cycle constraints].
- **E4.a (crossover platform revenue funds R&D)**: Weak. ICC patents have potential applications (ion-beam energy recovery, charged-particle direct conversion) but no current revenue stream. TAE-specific driver R&D is not amortized across non-fusion markets.
- **E4.b (talent inflow from adjacent industries)**: Moderate. NBI / RF / pulsed-power engineering draws from accelerator physics, semiconductor ion implantation, and defense pulsed-power. FRC niche is thin.
- **Plausible learning-curve mechanism**: **R&D-driven** — closer to gas-turbine LBR/LBD pattern than solar Wright's Law.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 4**: **F4.a at the failure pole** — narrow set of cost-reduction knobs, almost all fusion-funded; the curve runs out before reaching D-T-competitive LCOE.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 4**: **F4.c at the leverage pole** — B-11 fuel supply is the cleanest specialty-input position in the pipeline.

## Cross-stage carriers
- **Tritium / Li-6 / Be supply — where it bites hardest**: Bites nowhere. Aneutronic operation removes the entire carrier — a structural advantage TAE shares with the other aneutronic concepts in the pipeline.
- **First-wall / divertor / blanket lifetime — where it bites hardest**: Stage 4, as a recurring capex line item; bite is lighter than D-T because alpha particles (not 14 MeV neutrons) deposit energy, structural materials don't activate to clearance-relevant levels, and no breeding blanket replacement cycle exists. Linear FRC divertor materials are TRL 2 hardware (synthesis F4).
- **HTS conductor cost trajectory — where it bites hardest**: Bites nowhere. Resistive copper coils at ~1 kG external field exploit FRC near-unity beta.
- **Fuel-ecosystem R&D position**: Isolated from D-T R&D cohort; B-11 has no fuel-supply problem worth sharing R&D on. Net: neutral.
- **Workforce / intellectual depth carrier**: Helps modestly at Stage 2 and Stage 4 through accelerator/ion-implantation/defense pulsed-power adjacent workforce; hurts at Stage 1 because the bespoke beam-driven FRC paradigm has no co-developer concept to broaden the PhD pipeline.

## Dominant failure mode (single biggest gate across all stages)
- **Stage and F-factor (at failure pole)**: **Stage 2, F2.b at the failure pole.**
- **One-sentence rationale**: Da Vinci's commissioning campaign carries an unprecedented physics-validation burden (sustained T_i>>T_e regime for p-B11, never demonstrated at fusion conditions per analysis §3), and a non-converging campaign during the build window collapses the plant to no-LCOE rather than degraded-LCOE.

## Dominant leverage (single biggest tailwind across all stages)
- **Stage and factor**: **Stage 2, F2.c at the leverage pole.**
- **One-sentence rationale**: TAE is the cleanest fit to the new NRC Part 30 fusion framework — no tritium accountancy, no remote handling, hands-on maintenance — and that regulatory leverage is structurally locked in by the aneutronic fuel choice rather than dependent on physics success (also reinforces F3.d at Stage 3).
- **Slack vs. bottleneck status**: Slack — the regulatory framework has substantial headroom for additional fusion entrants; TAE's fit is not contingent on being first to use it.

## What this trace surfaces for the deep-dive
- The 2–3 highest-value questions a deep-dive should answer:
  1. What is the Q_plasma viability threshold as a function of η_NBI and f_rad, and where on that grid does any plausible Da Vinci operating point sit? Without this, every downstream LCOE number is conditional on an unmeasured binary.
  2. What is the realistic η_NBI(E_beam) curve for 250 keV proton NBI at ≥30 MW, drawing on ITER NNBI commissioning data and accelerator-physics analogues? Collapses the largest non-physics elasticity.
  3. How much of the aneutronic regulatory + supply-chain leverage (F2.c leverage pole, absent tritium carrier, F3.d amortization) translates to a quantitative capital-cost delta vs. the D-T tokamak baseline, separable from the physics-risk discount?

## Methodology friction encountered
- The Stage-2 dominant failure for this concept is a foundational physics-viability binary (Branch A vs. Branch B/C); F2.b is the closest rubric code but is being used as a proxy for a commissioning-phase physics-validation failure that the F-factor vocabulary does not natively name.
