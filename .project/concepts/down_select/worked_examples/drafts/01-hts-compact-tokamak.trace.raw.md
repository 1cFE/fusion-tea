# Trace: 01-hts-compact-tokamak

## Stage 1 (time-to-Stage-2 discount)
- **Current TRL / Q achieved:** SPARC under construction (first TF coil installed Jan 2026, first plasma targeted 2027, Q~11 target). ARC is conceptual (Sorbom 2015, published peer-reviewed design). CFS demonstrated 20 T large-bore HTS magnet in Sept 2021 validating the core magnet bet; Tokamak Energy's Demo4 validated an 18-coil HTS system at 11.8 T in Nov 2025 — paradigm-level burning-plasma physics is the SPARC milestone, not ARC's.
- **Estimated capital and time to Stage-2 entry:** SPARC capitalized at ~$2B raised through 2024; CFS total raised >$2B with reported plans for ARC capital in the multi-billion range. Time-to-Stage-2 ≈ 4–6 years from now: SPARC first plasma 2027, burning-plasma demonstration 2027–2028, ARC FOAK construction start late-2020s, FOAK operation early-2030s per CFS public timeline.
- **Paradigm co-development depth:** Very deep. Compact-tokamak/high-field physics draws on ITER, JET, JT-60SA, EAST, KSTAR, DIII-D, Alcator C-Mod (CFS's direct predecessor), plus Tokamak Energy's parallel HTS-tokamak program. Ten-plus active programs share the tokamak physics base.
- **Open scientific heritage:** Strong. Sorbom 2015, Creely 2020, Lin 2020 are all peer-reviewed and openly published; ARC's radial build, neutronics, and component cost methodology are in the public literature — unusual for a private fusion venture.
- **Workforce depth:** Deep. Plasma-physics PhD pipeline (US, EU, JP, KR) and tokamak engineering workforce at ITER/EUROfusion/QST scale. ICRF and HTS-magnet specialists are the tighter bottlenecks, but both have established academic-industrial bases.
- **Discount applied (low / moderate / heavy):** **Low.** Paradigm is the most-validated in fusion, scientific heritage is fully open, workforce is the largest of any concept, and SPARC will retire the burning-plasma physics question within the next ~24 months.

## Stage 2 (FOAK affordability)
- **F2.a (minimum-viable-plant capital cost):** High failure exposure. ARC nuclear-island fabricated cost is $5.56B (2014 USD), magnet system alone $5.1–5.2B [analysis §1]. Full-plant overnight cost from the explorer model is ~$15,554/kW at 1 GWe scale → ~$15B-class FOAK plant. This is well above the ~$5B sovereign/hyperscaler-consortium threshold; the FOAK buyer pool is narrow.
- **F2.b (build-time risk):** Moderate-high. Model baseline 5-year construction; Cohen/Eash-Gates 2020 mechanism (7+ yr build → ~2× cost) is plausible if ARC's site-specific construction (FLiBe plant, tritium plant, large civil works) drifts. ARC is intrinsically less site-built than ITER (magnets + vessel are factory-modular at 95% of CAS22 cost weight per synthesis C1), which partially mitigates this.
- **F2.c (regulatory framework state) — pole + rationale:** **Leverage pole.** NRC's 2023 decision to regulate D-T fusion under 10 CFR Part 30 (byproduct material) rather than Part 50 fits ARC's hazard profile directly; Araiinejad & Shirvan 2025 quantify the Part-50-equivalent penalty at 2.2× building cost + indirect inflation, which ARC sidesteps. Caveat: Part 30 rulemaking for D-T plants is incomplete — leverage is realized but not yet locked in.
- **F2.d (critical component supply maturity) — pole + slack/bottleneck + rationale:** **Leverage pole, slack-for-FOAK-only.** REBCO has fallen from $144–792/kA-m (2014) to ~$100/kA-m (2025) via non-fusion-driven capacity (~3,500–4,000 km/yr globally) [ecosystem brief]. ARC's FOAK gets that price reduction "for free." But: a single ARC needs ~5,730 km against ~3,500–4,000 km/yr capacity → FOAK consumes ~1.5 yr of global supply. Tritium (failure pole, bottleneck-likely), FLiBe/Be/Li-6 (failure pole, must be built — Kairos partnership is the only crossover lever) drag F2.d toward the failure pole for *those* lines; REBCO is the dominant CAPEX line and lands on the leverage pole. Net: **leverage pole with REBCO-slack for FOAK, hard-bottleneck on tritium/Li-6/Be lines that are smaller CAPEX but no-go if unsolved.**
- **E2.a (crossover platform attracts non-fusion investment):** Strong. CFS's HTS-magnet program has explicit non-fusion demand pull from MRI (Bruker, GE), HTS power cables, FCC/muon-collider accelerator magnets, HTS motors (GE Aerospace, Airbus ASCEND), and DoD directed-energy programs [ecosystem brief]. CFS is a canonical example of this E-factor — REBCO capex is partially defrayed by non-fusion REBCO demand growth.
- **E2.b (intra-fusion early-mover supply-chain effect):** ARC is the *first mover* on commercial HTS-tokamak deployment; later HTS concepts (Tokamak Energy ST-E1, Type One Energy, Thea, Renaissance) will benefit from CFS's REBCO winding capacity, joint fabrication, and quench-protection know-how — but ARC itself does not benefit from E2.b. Mildly negative.
- **Plausible FOAK buyer:** Hyperscaler PPA stack (Google + ENI already subscribed to the announced Virginia 400 MWe ARC plant per dossier) backed by sovereign/utility co-financing. This is the "tech-buyer-funds-FOAK" wedge, not utility-procurement.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 2:** **F2.a** — even the optimistic 400 MWe nuclear-island estimate puts FOAK overnight cost in the $10–15B range, which narrows the buyer pool to hyperscaler consortia and requires the magnet-cost-learning bet (Hypothesis 1 in analysis §2) to hold concurrently.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 2:** **E2.a** — REBCO's non-fusion demand pull (MRI, grid, accelerators, defense) is what makes CFS's vertically-integrated tape strategy financeable; without it, CFS would be the sole funder of the global HTS scale-up.

## Stage 3 (chasm crossing)
- **F3.b (site-specialization fraction):** Mixed. Magnets and vacuum vessel are factory-modular (~95% of CAS22 weight per synthesis C1 calculation); blanket is a liquid FLiBe fill on a factory-built tank; BOP, FLiBe chemistry plant, and tritium plant are site-built. Roughly 60–70% of CAPEX is factory-fabricated content — substantially better than ITER's ~30%, materially worse than NuScale-style fully-modular SMRs. Net: ARC sits in the favorable half of MFE on F3.b but is not "ship-and-install."
- **F3.c (replication unit size for one buyer):** At 400 MWe/unit, a 3-unit hyperscaler commitment is ~$30–45B — large but plausible for a Microsoft/Google/Meta consortium; a 5–10-unit utility fleet is feasible. Not in the "single buyer can't afford one round" failure mode of 1.5 GW units, not in the "12-pack hyperscaler" sweet spot of 50 MWe units.
- **F3.a (supply-chain maturity at chasm scale) — pole + slack/bottleneck + rationale:** **Leverage pole, bottleneck-likely.** REBCO ride-along is real (MRI/grid/accelerator/defense demand keeps expanding capacity), but a 10-plant 2035 fleet implies ~100,000 km against announced ~10,000–15,000 km/yr by 2027 [ecosystem brief]. F3.a sits on the leverage pole because the external markets exist and scale, but turns **bottleneck** before fleet-scale unless capacity grows another 10×. FLiBe/Be/Li-6 supply remains failure-pole-bottleneck at chasm scale; tritium is the sharpest constraint (civilian inventory ~25–30 kg vs. fleet need ~50–500 kg startup, CANDU production declining).
- **F3.d (regulatory amortization path) — pole + rationale:** **Leverage pole.** Part 30 is intrinsically more amortizable than Part 50 because it does not require site-specific operating-license proceedings of the same scope; CFS's Virginia plant licensing will set the precedent for follow-on ARCs and for other Part-30 D-T concepts. Standardized design supports clean amortization across the fleet.
- **E3.a (intra-fusion fleet co-development):** Strong. ~8 concepts in the broader landscape need REBCO at fleet scale (per ecosystem brief); combined fusion demand drives capacity expansion that no single concept could justify alone. ARC's REBCO supply chain co-develops with Tokamak Energy, Type One, Thea, Renaissance, and the HTS spherical-tokamak path.
- **E3.b (shared sub-problem solution leverage):** Very strong. FLiBe tritium extraction R&D is shared with concept 07 (MagLIF FLiBe liquid wall) and the broader Kairos-FHR/MSR community; high-heat-flux divertor R&D is shared with all MFE; HTS quench protection is shared with all HTS concepts; ICRF physics is shared with every tokamak. ARC inherits and contributes to ~5 distinct shared-problem cohorts.
- **Most plausible chasm-crossing path:** Hyperscaler-anchored fleet of 3–10 ARCs at standardized Part-30-licensed sites (Virginia + follow-on US states), each with PPAs from data-center operators. Regulatory amortization across the fleet + REBCO co-scaling with parallel HTS-tokamak deployments is the structural path; capacity-factor demonstration on plants 1–2 is the gate.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 3:** **F3.a (failure-pole sub-line: tritium)** — the D-T fuel-cycle supply (tritium startup inventory, Li-6 enrichment capacity, Be supply) has no commercial ride-along and is at hard-bottleneck for any fleet beyond ~3–5 plants. REBCO bottleneck is also real but solvable with capex; tritium has no equivalent lever.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 3:** **E3.a** — combined HTS-fusion-cohort demand is what unlocks REBCO capacity at chasm scale; ARC alone could not fund 10× capacity expansion, but ~8 concepts combined plus MRI/grid demand can.

## Stage 4 (learning-curve descent)
- **F4.b (volume-driven vs. R&D-driven learning mechanism):** **Mixed, weighted toward R&D-driven.** ARC is a low-unit-count, high-unit-cost machine (closer to gas turbines than to solar PV); the dominant cost line — REBCO conductor and magnet fabrication — has both volume-learning (Wright's Law on tape) and R&D-learning (Jc improvement via 1→4 μm film thickness, joint engineering). Vacuum vessel replacement on 6–12 month cycles introduces a volume-learning channel on a single recurring CAPEX line.
- **F4.d (modularization-vs-scale crossover):** ARC sits at the high end of the per-unit-scale axis (400 MWe is sub-1 GWe but not modular). Demountable TF coils enable replacement-component modularization, which is closer to the right side of the curve than NuScale-style over-modularization. The crossover concern is less acute than for SMRs.
- **F4.a (cost-reduction knobs and non-fusion ride-along) — pole + rationale:** **Leverage pole.** Kavlak-style enumeration for ARC: (1) REBCO $/kA-m (non-fusion-funded via MRI/grid), (2) REBCO Jc / film thickness (semiconductor-style materials R&D, partially non-fusion), (3) magnet fabrication labor (fusion-specific), (4) Inconel-718 vessel cost (commodity), (5) FLiBe chemistry-plant learning (shared with MSR fission), (6) steam BOP (mature commercial), (7) site/civil/electrical (commodity). ≥4 of the ≥6 knobs have non-fusion ride-along — favorable, matching the solar-semiconductors pattern at the magnet-conductor level. Distinctly better than IFE concepts where the driver knob is fusion-only.
- **F4.c (specialty-input external-market position) — pole + rationale:** **Mixed.** REBCO at fleet scale: MRI + grid + transport + accelerator demand is at the ~1,000–2,000 km/yr non-fusion baseline today, while a 50 GWe ARC fleet would need ~125,000 km/yr — fusion demand is likely to *dominate* REBCO demand at fleet scale, not the other way around. So F4.c on REBCO is closer to the failure pole than the ecosystem brief's leverage-pole-default — fusion has to drive its own Wright's Law on the conductor. FLiBe/Li-6/Be: failure pole unambiguously, no external market. Net: F4.c on the failure pole.
- **E4.a (crossover platform revenue funds R&D):** Moderate-strong on the magnet side (CFS magnet IP licensed to MRI, accelerator, motor markets generates non-fusion revenue funding continued REBCO R&D); negligible on FLiBe/blanket/tritium-plant side.
- **E4.b (talent inflow from adjacent industries):** Strong. RF engineering (telecom, broadcast), cryogenics (MRI, LNG, space launch), precision-engineering (semiconductor capital equipment), HTS magnet engineering (MRI manufacturers) all feed into ARC operations at scale. Workforce inflow is one of ARC's strongest distinct E-factors.
- **Plausible learning-curve mechanism (volume / R&D / mixed):** **Mixed**, with R&D-learning dominant on the magnet line (the dominant cost) and volume-learning on recurring vessel replacement and FLiBe/tritium-plant scale-up.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 4:** **F4.c (failure pole, FLiBe/Li-6/Be subline; partial-failure on REBCO at fleet-dominant fusion demand)** — without external markets to amortize specialty-input Wright's Law, fusion alone must drive the cost descent on Li-6, FLiBe, and Be, which is unprecedented in scale and adds toxicology/regulatory overhead.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 4:** **F4.a (leverage pole)** — the breadth and non-fusion-funded share of cost-reduction knobs (REBCO conductor materials R&D, MSR fission FLiBe learning, commodity BOP, demountable-coil manufacturing learning) is the closest match in the MFE space to the solar-semiconductors leverage pattern.

## Cross-stage carriers
- **Tritium / Li-6 / Be supply — where it bites hardest:** Stage 3. Stage 2 FOAK can be supplied from declining CANDU inventory for startup (~1 kg); Stage 3 fleet (5–10 plants, 5–50 kg startup against decaying ~25–30 kg inventory + Watts Bar TPBARs at ~0.5–1 kg/yr) is where the supply gap becomes existential. Stage 4 self-sufficiency (TBR ≥1.05 with <5-day FLiBe residence time) is the resolution path but only matters if Stage 3 is survived.
- **First-wall / divertor / blanket lifetime — where it bites hardest:** Stage 4 (recurring capex). ARC's 6–12 month inner-vessel replacement cycle (~$123M fabricated × 30+ cycles over 30-yr life) is the largest recurring O&M-capex line and the primary capacity-factor constraint per synthesis. Stage 2 bite is "survives long enough to demonstrate"; Stage 4 bite is "must be cheap and fast at every replacement."
- **HTS conductor cost trajectory — where it bites hardest:** Stage 2 (binary: REBCO must be cheap enough to make FOAK financeable) and Stage 4 (must descend further to defensible floor). Stage 2 is the gate — 2025 prices of ~$100/kA-m vs. $10/kA-m commercial target is a ~10× gap that, applied to 5,730 km, dominates magnet CAPEX uncertainty.
- **Fuel-ecosystem R&D position:** ARC is in the largest D-T cohort (~15+ concepts) — strong shared R&D amortization on tritium plant, Li-6 enrichment, FLiBe chemistry, breeding-blanket neutronics. The full tritium-extraction-from-FLiBe stack is shared with concept 07 and the MSR community.
- **Workforce / intellectual depth carrier:** Stage 1 (physics PhDs, deepest pool of any concept) → Stage 2 (precision magnet engineering, ICRF, cryogenics — all have non-fusion talent inflow) → Stage 3 (operational tokamak expertise from JET/ITER alumni) → Stage 4 (industrial workforce inflow from MRI, semiconductor, aerospace adjacent industries). Helps strongly at every stage.

## Dominant failure mode (single biggest gate across all stages)
- **Stage and F-factor (at failure pole):** Stage 2 / **F2.a (minimum-viable-plant capital cost)**, with Stage 3 F3.a (tritium-supply failure pole) as a close second contingent on surviving Stage 2.
- **One-sentence rationale:** ARC's $15B-class FOAK overnight cost is at the upper edge of what any private-capital buyer pool can absorb — and it must close *before* the REBCO-cost learning curve and the capacity-factor demonstration arrive to justify it, making the Stage-2 financing gate the single binding constraint that the rest of the program is racing against.

## Dominant leverage (single biggest tailwind across all stages)
- **Stage and factor:** Stage 2 / **E2.a (crossover platform attracts non-fusion investment to FOAK)**, with Stage 4 F4.a (leverage pole) as the long-horizon equivalent.
- **One-sentence rationale:** REBCO's non-fusion demand pull (MRI, grid, accelerators, aerospace motors, defense directed-energy) is what defrays the global HTS scale-up cost that ARC FOAK depends on — no other MFE concept (and few in fusion overall) has a comparably broad non-fusion industrial market funding its dominant CAPEX line.
- **Slack vs. bottleneck status:** **Slack** for FOAK (current ~3,500–4,000 km/yr capacity exceeds first-3-plant draw); **bottleneck-likely** for fleet (10-plant fleet needs ~25× current global output). The leverage is real but time-limited — it must convert to capacity-expansion investment before chasm-era demand arrives.

## What this trace surfaces for the deep-dive
- **Output format implication:** **1costingfe extension.** ARC has rich CAS-decomposed cost data (per the explorer JSON), well-documented sensitivities, and parametric levers (REBCO $/kA-m, capacity factor, blanket-replacement frequency, regulatory multiplier) that map directly onto the existing pyFECONS/1costingfe parameter space. A SysML structural model is unnecessary — the value-add is parametric: extending 1costingfe with (a) REBCO-tape-cost-trajectory scenarios, (b) capacity-factor / vessel-replacement-tempo model, (c) Part-30 vs. Part-50 regulatory branch, (d) FOAK→NOAK step-function. The synthesis already articulates the parameter elasticities; the deep-dive operationalizes them.
- **The 2–3 highest-value questions a deep-dive should answer:**
  1. **What capacity factor does ARC's 6–12-month inner-vessel-replacement cycle actually support, given demountable-coil replacement times of X weeks and Y unplanned-outage rate?** This is the single dominant LCOE sensitivity (elasticity −0.96) and the single least-anchored parameter — a defensible CF range from explicit maintenance modeling would shift the LCOE central estimate from $216/MWh (explorer baseline at 75% CF) into a defensible range or surface that no CF assumption is defensible.
  2. **What is the FOAK-financing structure that closes a $10–15B ARC at hyperscaler-PPA + sovereign-backstop, and what REBCO-price + Part-30-licensing milestones does it require by year?** This operationalizes the dominant failure mode (F2.a) and the dominant leverage (E2.a) as a deal structure, not a parameter sweep.
  3. **At what fleet size does the tritium/Li-6/Be supply gap force either (a) breeding-self-sufficiency-from-plant-1, (b) a Western non-mercury Li-6 enrichment capacity build, or (c) ARC fleet pacing constrained by fuel-cycle ramp?** This is the dominant Stage-3 failure mode and the question that the existing analysis flags as "existential" but does not quantitatively close.
