# Trace: 01-hts-compact-tokamak

## Stage 1 (time-to-Stage-2 discount)
- **Current TRL / Q achieved:** SPARC under construction (first TF coil installed Jan 2026, first plasma targeted 2027, Q~11 target). ARC is conceptual (Sorbom 2015, peer-reviewed). CFS demonstrated 20 T large-bore HTS magnet in Sept 2021; Tokamak Energy's Demo4 validated an 18-coil HTS system at 11.8 T in Nov 2025 — paradigm-level burning-plasma physics is the SPARC milestone, not ARC's.
- **Estimated capital and time to Stage-2 entry:** SPARC capitalized ~$2B raised through 2024; CFS total raised >$2B. Time-to-Stage-2 ≈ 4–6 years: SPARC first plasma 2027, burning-plasma demo 2027–2028, ARC FOAK construction start late-2020s, FOAK operation early-2030s per CFS public timeline.
- **Paradigm co-development depth:** Very deep. Compact-tokamak/high-field physics draws on ITER, JET, JT-60SA, EAST, KSTAR, DIII-D, Alcator C-Mod (CFS's direct predecessor), plus Tokamak Energy's parallel HTS-tokamak program.
- **Open scientific heritage:** Strong. Sorbom 2015, Creely 2020, Lin 2020 are peer-reviewed and openly published; ARC's radial build, neutronics, and component cost methodology are in the public literature.
- **Workforce depth:** Deep. Plasma-physics PhD pipeline (US, EU, JP, KR) and tokamak engineering workforce at ITER/EUROfusion/QST scale. ICRF and HTS-magnet specialists are tighter bottlenecks but both have established academic-industrial bases.
- **Discount applied:** **Low.** Paradigm is the most-validated in fusion, scientific heritage is fully open, workforce is the largest of any concept, and SPARC will retire the burning-plasma physics question within the next ~24 months.

## Stage 2 (FOAK affordability)
- **F2.a (minimum-viable-plant capital cost):** High failure exposure. ARC nuclear-island fabricated cost is $5.56B (2014 USD), magnet system alone $5.1–5.2B [analysis §1]. Explorer model puts full-plant overnight cost ~$15,554/kW at 1 GWe scale → ~$15B-class FOAK plant. Well above the ~$5B sovereign/hyperscaler-consortium threshold; FOAK buyer pool is narrow.
- **F2.b (build-time risk):** Moderate-high. Model baseline 5-year construction; Cohen/Eash-Gates 2020 mechanism (7+ yr build → ~2× cost) is plausible if site-specific construction (FLiBe plant, tritium plant, civil works) drifts. ARC is intrinsically less site-built than ITER (magnets + vessel are factory-modular at ~95% of CAS22 weight per synthesis C1), which partially mitigates.
- **F2.c (regulatory framework state) — leverage pole.** NRC's 2023 decision to regulate D-T fusion under 10 CFR Part 30 (byproduct material) rather than Part 50 fits ARC's hazard profile directly; Araiinejad & Shirvan 2025 quantify the Part-50-equivalent penalty at 2.2× building cost + indirect inflation, which ARC sidesteps. Caveat: Part 30 rulemaking for D-T plants is incomplete — leverage is realized but not yet locked in.
- **F2.d (critical component supply maturity) — leverage pole, slack.** Pole committed by weighting on CAPEX share: REBCO drives magnet system cost (~$5.1–5.2B of $5.56B nuclear-island fabricated cost per analysis §1), and external REBCO supply has driven price down to ~$20/m in 2025 vs. $36–198/m in 2014 [ecosystem brief], a reduction ARC's FOAK receives without funding. Capacity ~3,500–4,000 km/yr globally exceeds first-plant draw, so slack at FOAK scale. (Tritium/Li-6/Be sub-supply concerns are carried in the F-carrier section where their stage-by-stage bite is the right framing, not as a competing pole on F2.d.)
- **E2.a (crossover platform attracts non-fusion investment):** Strong. CFS's HTS-magnet program has explicit non-fusion demand pull from MRI (Bruker, GE), HTS power cables, FCC/muon-collider accelerator magnets, HTS motors (GE Aerospace, Airbus ASCEND), and DoD directed-energy programs [ecosystem brief]. CFS is the canonical example of this E-factor.
- **E2.b (intra-fusion early-mover supply-chain effect):** ARC is the *first mover* on commercial HTS-tokamak deployment; later HTS concepts will benefit from CFS's REBCO winding capacity, joint fabrication, and quench-protection know-how — ARC itself does not benefit from E2.b. Mildly negative.
- **Plausible FOAK buyer:** Hyperscaler PPA stack (Google + ENI already subscribed to the announced Virginia 400 MWe ARC plant per dossier) backed by sovereign/utility co-financing.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 2:** **F2.a.** $10–15B FOAK overnight cost narrows the buyer pool to hyperscaler consortia and requires the magnet-cost-learning bet (Hypothesis 1 in analysis §2) to hold concurrently with financing close.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 2:** **E2.a.** REBCO's non-fusion demand pull is what makes CFS's vertically-integrated tape strategy financeable; without it CFS would be the sole funder of the global HTS scale-up.

## Stage 3 (chasm crossing)
- **F3.b (site-specialization fraction):** Magnets and vacuum vessel are factory-modular (~95% of CAS22 weight per synthesis C1); blanket is a liquid FLiBe fill on a factory-built tank; BOP, FLiBe chemistry plant, and tritium plant are site-built. Roughly 60–70% of CAPEX is factory-fabricated — substantially better than ITER's ~30%, materially worse than NuScale-style fully-modular SMRs.
- **F3.c (replication unit size for one buyer):** At 400 MWe/unit, a 3-unit hyperscaler commitment is ~$30–45B — large but plausible for a Microsoft/Google/Meta consortium; a 5–10-unit utility fleet is feasible.
- **F3.a (supply-chain maturity at chasm scale) — leverage pole, bottleneck.** Pole committed by weighting on chasm-era CAPEX share: REBCO continues to dominate the conductor-heavy CAS22 line, and the ride-along is real — MRI/grid/accelerator/defense demand keeps external capacity expanding (announced trajectory ~10,000–15,000 km/yr by 2027 per ecosystem brief). Bottleneck because combined-fusion-cohort demand pushes against that announced trajectory before a 10-plant ARC fleet is deployable.
- **F3.d (regulatory amortization path) — leverage pole.** Part 30 is intrinsically more amortizable than Part 50 — no site-specific operating-license proceedings of the same scope; CFS's Virginia plant licensing will set precedent for follow-on ARCs and for other Part-30 D-T concepts. Standardized design supports clean amortization across the fleet.
- **E3.a (intra-fusion fleet co-development):** Strong. ~8 concepts need REBCO at fleet scale [ecosystem brief]; combined fusion demand drives capacity expansion that no single concept could justify alone. ARC's REBCO supply chain co-develops with Tokamak Energy, Type One, Thea, Renaissance, and the HTS spherical-tokamak path.
- **E3.b (shared sub-problem solution leverage):** Very strong. FLiBe tritium extraction R&D shared with concept 07 and the broader Kairos-FHR/MSR community; high-heat-flux divertor R&D shared with all MFE; HTS quench protection shared with all HTS concepts; ICRF physics shared with every tokamak. ARC participates in ~5 distinct shared-problem cohorts.
- **Most plausible chasm-crossing path:** Hyperscaler-anchored fleet of 3–10 ARCs at standardized Part-30-licensed sites, each with PPAs from data-center operators. Regulatory amortization across the fleet + REBCO co-scaling with parallel HTS-tokamak deployments is the structural path; capacity-factor demonstration on plants 1–2 is the gate.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 3:** **F3.a (bottleneck).** The leverage pole holds in principle but combined fusion-cohort REBCO demand collides with the announced external capacity trajectory before the chasm-era fleet is built; capacity expansion has to be financed faster than non-fusion demand alone would drive it.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 3:** **E3.a.** Combined HTS-fusion-cohort demand is what unlocks REBCO capacity at chasm scale; ARC alone cannot fund a step-change in capacity, but ~8 concepts combined plus MRI/grid demand can.

## Stage 4 (learning-curve descent)
- **F4.b (volume-driven vs. R&D-driven learning mechanism):** **R&D.** ARC is a low-unit-count, high-unit-cost machine (closer to gas turbines than to solar PV); the dominant cost line — REBCO conductor and magnet fabrication — moves principally on Jc improvement (1→4 μm film thickness), joint engineering, and process R&D rather than on unit-count Wright's Law alone. (Vessel-replacement volume-learning is a secondary channel on a single recurring CAPEX line.)
- **F4.d (modularization-vs-scale crossover):** ARC sits at the high end of the per-unit-scale axis (400 MWe). Demountable TF coils enable replacement-component modularization, which is closer to the right side of the curve than NuScale-style over-modularization. Crossover concern is less acute than for SMRs.
- **F4.a (cost-reduction knobs and non-fusion ride-along) — leverage pole.** Kavlak-style enumeration for ARC: (1) REBCO $/m (non-fusion-funded via MRI/grid), (2) REBCO Jc / film thickness (semiconductor-style materials R&D, partially non-fusion), (3) magnet fabrication labor (fusion-specific), (4) Inconel-718 vessel (commodity), (5) FLiBe chemistry-plant learning (shared with MSR fission), (6) steam BOP (mature commercial), (7) site/civil/electrical (commodity). ≥4 of the ≥6 knobs have non-fusion ride-along — favorable, matching the solar-semiconductors pattern at the magnet-conductor level.
- **F4.c (specialty-input external-market position) — failure pole.** Pole committed by weighting on fleet-era specialty-input CAPEX share. REBCO at fleet scale: the ecosystem-brief baseline of non-fusion REBCO demand grows but a combined HTS-fusion fleet plausibly comes to dominate the conductor market, so Wright's Law on REBCO has to be driven substantially by fusion itself rather than by external demand. FLiBe/Li-6/Be sit at the failure pole unambiguously (no external market). Aggregate: failure pole.
- **E4.a (crossover platform revenue funds R&D):** Moderate-strong on the magnet side (CFS magnet IP licensable to MRI, accelerator, motor markets generates non-fusion revenue funding continued REBCO R&D); negligible on FLiBe/blanket/tritium-plant side.
- **E4.b (talent inflow from adjacent industries):** Strong. RF engineering (telecom, broadcast), cryogenics (MRI, LNG, space launch), precision-engineering (semiconductor capital equipment), HTS magnet engineering (MRI) all feed ARC operations at scale.
- **Plausible learning-curve mechanism:** **R&D.**
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 4:** **F4.c.** Without external markets to amortize specialty-input Wright's Law on FLiBe/Li-6/Be, and with fusion plausibly dominating REBCO demand at fleet scale, fusion has to drive the cost descent on its dominant specialty inputs alone — an unprecedented scale-up burden with no solar-style demand-side accelerant.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 4:** **F4.a (leverage pole).** The breadth and non-fusion-funded share of cost-reduction knobs is the closest match in the MFE space to the solar-semiconductors leverage pattern.

## Cross-stage carriers
- **Tritium / Li-6 / Be supply — where it bites hardest:** Stage 3. Stage 2 FOAK can be supplied from declining CANDU inventory for startup (~1 kg per analysis §6); Stage 3 fleet (5–10 plants, 5–50 kg startup against decaying ~25–30 kg inventory + Watts Bar TPBARs at ~0.5–1 kg/yr per analysis §6) is where the supply gap becomes existential. Stage 4 self-sufficiency (TBR ≥1.05 with <5-day FLiBe residence time) is the resolution path but only matters if Stage 3 is survived.
- **First-wall / divertor / blanket lifetime — where it bites hardest:** Stage 4. ARC's 6–12 month inner-vessel replacement cycle (~$123M fabricated × 30+ cycles over 30-yr life per synthesis) is the largest recurring O&M-capex line and the primary capacity-factor constraint. Stage 2 bite is "survives long enough to demonstrate"; Stage 4 bite is "must be cheap and fast at every replacement."
- **HTS conductor cost trajectory — where it bites hardest:** Stage 2. REBCO must be cheap enough at FOAK to make a $15B-class plant financeable; the ~$20/m 2025 price [ecosystem brief] vs. the commercial trajectory needed for ARC FOAK economics is the gap that dominates magnet-CAPEX uncertainty. Stage 4 then requires further descent to a defensible floor.
- **Fuel-ecosystem R&D position:** ARC is in the largest D-T cohort (~15+ concepts) — strong shared R&D amortization on tritium plant, Li-6 enrichment, FLiBe chemistry, breeding-blanket neutronics. The full tritium-extraction-from-FLiBe stack is shared with concept 07 and the MSR community. Failure-side caveat: shared dependency means a cohort-wide failure to scale Li-6 or breeding becomes a shared-failure carrier, not just a shared-leverage one.
- **Workforce / intellectual depth carrier:** Stage 1 (physics PhDs, deepest pool of any concept) → Stage 2 (precision magnet engineering, ICRF, cryogenics, all with non-fusion talent inflow) → Stage 3 (operational tokamak expertise from JET/ITER alumni) → Stage 4 (industrial workforce inflow from MRI, semiconductor, aerospace adjacent industries).

## Dominant failure mode (single biggest gate across all stages)
- **Stage and F-factor (at failure pole):** Stage 2 / **F2.a (minimum-viable-plant capital cost).**
- **One-sentence rationale:** ARC's $15B-class FOAK overnight cost is at the upper edge of what any private-capital buyer pool can absorb, and it must close *before* the REBCO-cost learning curve and the capacity-factor demonstration arrive to justify it, making the Stage-2 financing gate the single binding constraint that the rest of the program is racing against.

## Dominant leverage (single biggest tailwind across all stages)
- **Stage and factor:** Stage 2 / **E2.a (crossover platform attracts non-fusion investment to FOAK).**
- **One-sentence rationale:** REBCO's non-fusion demand pull (MRI, grid, accelerators, aerospace motors, defense directed-energy) is what defrays the global HTS scale-up cost that ARC FOAK depends on — no other MFE concept has a comparably broad non-fusion industrial market funding its dominant CAPEX line.
- **Slack vs. bottleneck status:** **Slack** at Stage 2 (current global REBCO capacity exceeds first-plant draw per ecosystem brief); converts to **bottleneck** at Stage 3 fleet scale unless capacity expansion is financed ahead of combined-cohort demand.

## What this trace surfaces for the deep-dive
- **Output format implication:** **1costingfe extension.** ARC has rich CAS-decomposed cost data (per explorer JSON), well-documented sensitivities, and parametric levers (REBCO price, capacity factor, blanket-replacement frequency, regulatory multiplier) that map directly onto pyFECONS/1costingfe parameter space. A SysML structural model is unnecessary — the value-add is parametric. Caveat: the format recommendation is driven by data shape, not directly by the dominant F2.a / E2.a coordinates.
- **The 2–3 highest-value questions a deep-dive should answer:**
  1. **What capacity factor does ARC's 6–12-month inner-vessel-replacement cycle actually support, given demountable-coil replacement times and unplanned-outage rates?** The single dominant LCOE sensitivity (elasticity −0.96 per synthesis) and the single least-anchored parameter.
  2. **What FOAK-financing structure closes a $10–15B ARC at hyperscaler-PPA + sovereign-backstop, and what REBCO-price + Part-30-licensing milestones does it require by year?** Operationalizes F2.a and E2.a as a deal structure.
  3. **At what fleet size does the tritium/Li-6/Be supply gap force (a) breeding-self-sufficiency-from-plant-1, (b) a Western non-mercury Li-6 enrichment capacity build, or (c) ARC fleet pacing constrained by fuel-cycle ramp?** The Stage-3 carrier question that analysis flags as "existential" but does not quantitatively close.

## Methodology friction encountered
The rubric's single-pole commitment on ecosystem-relational F-factors forces tritium/Li-6/Be supply concerns out of F2.d and F3.a (where REBCO's CAPEX weight dictates the pole) and into the F-carrier framing; this is the right answer for ARC, but it means a non-trivial Stage-3 failure mode is surfaced only by reading the carrier section, not by the dominant-coordinate output.
