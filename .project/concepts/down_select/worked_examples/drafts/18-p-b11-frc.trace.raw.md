# Trace: 18-p-b11-frc

## Stage 1 (time-to-Stage-2 discount)
- **Current TRL / Q achieved**: Confinement TRL 5–6 at subscale (C-2W: Ttot ~3 keV, Te ~1 keV peak, ~40 ms pulses, fast-ion/thermal pressure ratio ~1.5×). p-B11 in magnetic confinement TRL 1–2 (fusion products observed 2023; Q_plasma <0.001). Net energy gain for p-B11 never demonstrated in any device [analysis §3].
- **Estimated capital and time to Stage-2 entry**: TAE has spent >$1.3B over ~25 years, plans Da Vinci construction start 2026, first plasma 2029, net energy 2030 [dossier]. The intermediate Copernicus step is being skipped — meaning the entry into Stage 2 *is itself* the foundational physics demonstrator (Q>1 for p-B11 has never been shown anywhere). Realistic additional capital to actual Stage-2 entry: likely $2–4B more if Da Vinci must serve as both physics demonstrator and FOAK; timeline 5–10 years from now optimistically.
- **Paradigm co-development depth**: Shallow. The beam-driven FRC paradigm is essentially a TAE-only program — no parallel national-lab or international effort validates the sustained T_i>>T_e regime at fusion conditions. Adjacent FRC work (Helion) is pulsed-compression with a different operating regime. Aneutronic peers (HB11, Marvel, LPPFusion, Pale Blue) attack p-B11 from entirely different geometries.
- **Open scientific heritage**: Mixed. FRC physics has open academic lineage (Rostoker, UC Irvine roots); recent disclosures are peer-reviewed (Nature Communications 2025, Nuclear Fusion 2024 / OSTI 2441289). But Da Vinci commercial parameters are proprietary; ICC remains patent-stage only [analysis §1].
- **Workforce depth**: Narrow. Beam-driven FRC expertise is concentrated at TAE; high-energy NBI capability sits across a handful of fusion labs (ITER NBI team, JT-60U, JAEA). Far thinner than the tokamak workforce.
- **Discount applied**: **Heavy**. A shallow co-development paradigm plus a foundational physics question (sustained T_i>>T_e at ~150 keV) that has no validating program elsewhere means time-to-Stage-2 is long and high-variance.

## Stage 2 (FOAK affordability)
- **F2.a (minimum-viable-plant capital cost)**: Da Vinci targeted at 50 MWe initial / 350–500 MWe scaled; explorer baseline overnight cost ~$3,089/kW at 1 GW (~$1.0B at scale, considerably less at 50 MWe). Far below the $5B sovereigns-only threshold — *if* the cost model is anywhere near right. The unit size and capital scale put TAE in the "hyperscaler / strategic-investor accessible" tier rather than the "sovereigns only" tier.
- **F2.b (build-time risk)**: Moderate-to-high. Construction time baseline 6 years (sensitivity elasticity +0.37). Most of the build risk is *not* civil/site-construction (the plant is small and aneutronic) but **physics-validation risk during commissioning** — if the plasma doesn't reach commercial conditions, the build doesn't matter. This is a different failure mode than the Cohen/Eash-Gates regulatory-ratcheting story.
- **F2.c (regulatory framework state) — pole + rationale**: **Leverage pole.** Aneutronic operation with <1% neutron fraction, no tritium, hands-on maintenance fits squarely within the NRC Part 30 fusion framework (Feb 2026). TAE is the *best-positioned* concept in the pipeline for the lighter framework — no D-T radiological burden, no tritium accountancy, no remote-handling licensing overhead.
- **F2.d (critical component supply maturity) — pole + slack/bottleneck + rationale**: **Mixed but leaning failure pole, slack-for-FOAK-only.** TAE bypasses the REBCO/FLiBe/Li-6/Be/tritium bottlenecks entirely (a major structural win). However, the dominant component — reactor-scale high-energy NBI (250 keV protons, ~30 MW) — has **no commercial supply chain at fusion scale** [ecosystem brief: minimal non-fusion industrial base for kJ-MJ pulsed/high-energy fusion-relevant drivers]. ITER NNBI provides a 1-MeV negative-D adjacent precedent but not commercial supply. Slack at FOAK volume (one plant's NBI is procurable through bespoke build); bottleneck at fleet scale.
- **E2.a (crossover platform attracts non-fusion investment)**: Weak. NBI components have some defense crossover (particle accelerator tech, RF power supplies, ion sources for ion implantation) but TAE's specific 250 keV proton multi-functional NBI does not map to a large non-fusion revenue platform the way CFS HTS magnets do for grid/MRI/transport. The DJT/Trump Media merger provides capital but is not a *crossover platform* in the operating sense — it is a financing vehicle.
- **E2.b (intra-fusion early-mover supply-chain effect)**: Negligible. TAE's NBI, FRC vessel, copper coil configuration are bespoke; no earlier-shipping fusion concept builds the supply chain TAE's FOAK uses.
- **Plausible FOAK buyer**: Strategic / hyperscaler-adjacent (Trump Media merger suggests data-center power wedge), or DoD-backed via DPA-style aneutronic-fusion procurement. The 50 MWe initial unit size + aneutronic safety profile + hands-on maintenance is a hyperscaler-fit wedge — distinct from the GW-scale sovereign-utility wedge that ARC-class concepts need.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 2**: **F2.b (build-time / commissioning physics risk)** — Da Vinci's commissioning *is* the foundational physics demonstration; a non-converging plasma campaign returns the plant to Branch A with no LCOE.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 2**: **F2.c at the leverage pole** — TAE is structurally the best-fit concept in the pipeline for the new NRC Part 30 fusion framework, with no tritium/no-remote-handling cleanly matching what the framework was written to enable.

## Stage 3 (chasm crossing)
- **F3.b (site-specialization fraction)**: Low-to-moderate. Resistive copper coils are wound on-site (per C1 scoring). NBI injectors are factory-built and site-assembled into an array. Vessel is site-welded but small. No FLiBe loops, no breeding blanket assembly, no remote-handling cells. Site-specialization fraction substantially lower than ITER/ARC-class plants.
- **F3.c (replication unit size for one buyer)**: **Favorable.** 50 MWe initial unit means one hyperscaler could commit to ~12 units for ~600 MWe at a few-$B commitment — within plausible single-buyer envelope (NuScale-pattern). 350–500 MWe scaled units broaden the buyer set without forcing sovereign-only deals.
- **F3.a (supply-chain maturity at chasm scale) — pole + slack/bottleneck + rationale**: **Failure pole, bottleneck-likely at fleet scale.** The dominant subsystem (high-energy NBI) sits on a supply chain with no non-fusion industrial demand to keep it scaling alongside fusion (contrast with REBCO where MRI/grid/transport pull capacity faster than fusion alone could). Each new TAE plant pays close to full NBI capital with limited external-market learning [ecosystem brief, high-power lasers/NBI analogue: minimal non-fusion industrial base].
- **F3.d (regulatory amortization path) — pole + rationale**: **Leverage pole.** Aneutronic + steady-state + standardized Da Vinci design is highly amortizable: the first NRC Part 30 fusion license sets precedent that subsequent identical units inherit. No D-T-specific per-plant tritium accountancy re-litigation. Probably the strongest regulatory-amortization position in the pipeline.
- **E3.a (intra-fusion fleet co-development)**: Weak. Few other concepts share TAE's specific component stack (no other concept in the pipeline uses 250 keV proton NBI with FRC). The aneutronic cohort (HB11, Marvel, LPPFusion, Pale Blue) shares fuel and shielding philosophy but not driver hardware.
- **E3.b (shared sub-problem solution leverage)**: Moderate-to-weak. *Shared with TAE*: high-energy NBI development (some leverage from ITER NNBI, JT-60SA), alpha-channeling / aneutronic-shielding R&D shared across p-B11 cohort, direct-energy-conversion R&D shared with Helion and Pale Blue. *Not shared*: sustained T_i>>T_e bremsstrahlung suppression — this is TAE's bespoke physics bet.
- **Most plausible chasm-crossing path**: Hyperscaler fleet of 50 MWe units, replicated 10–20× per buyer, leveraging amortized Part 30 license and aneutronic safety case. *Conditional on Branch B physics closing.*
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 3**: **F3.a at the failure pole** — NBI supply chain has no external-market scaling partner; per-plant driver cost stays high through the chasm.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 3**: **F3.d at the leverage pole** — aneutronic + standardized Da Vinci design is unusually well-suited to regulatory amortization under the new Part 30 framework.

## Stage 4 (learning-curve descent)
- **F4.b (volume-driven vs. R&D-driven learning mechanism)**: **Mixed-leaning-R&D.** Steady-state plants at 50–500 MWe per unit cannot reach the "many cheap modules" volumes that drive Wright's Law solar-style descent. Cost reductions will come primarily from R&D-driven NBI efficiency gains, ICC demonstration, and confinement-time improvements rather than mass-manufacturing volume.
- **F4.d (modularization-vs-scale crossover)**: Modularity score 3.0 per synthesis — middling. NBI is the only meaningfully modular CAS22 element. The plant is not over-modularized (avoiding NuScale-style failure mode) but also doesn't enjoy strong modular learning benefits.
- **F4.a (cost-reduction knobs and non-fusion ride-along) — pole + rationale**: **Failure pole.** Few independent cost-reduction knobs, most fusion-funded. NBI efficiency, ICC efficiency, Q_plasma, capacity factor — all are fusion-specific R&D problems with limited non-fusion industries paying for the innovation. Compare CFS where HTS cost descent is funded by MRI/grid/transport demand: TAE has no equivalent ride-along.
- **F4.c (specialty-input external-market position) — pole + rationale**: **Leverage pole, slack.** B-11 fuel is commodity-adjacent — separation chemistry is mature, capacity expansion is engineering not invention, semiconductor-doping market provides baseline non-fusion demand [ecosystem brief: B-11 lowest-severity bottleneck among fuel-cycle constraints]. TAE essentially has no critical specialty input with a bottleneck failure mode.
- **E4.a (crossover platform revenue funds R&D)**: Weak-to-moderate. ICC patents have potential applications (ion-beam energy recovery in accelerators, charged-particle direct conversion) but no current revenue stream. NBI components share some technology with ion implantation and particle accelerators but TAE-specific driver R&D is not amortized across non-fusion markets.
- **E4.b (talent inflow from adjacent industries)**: Moderate. NBI / RF / pulsed-power engineering draws from accelerator physics, semiconductor ion implantation, and defense pulsed-power — moderate adjacent-industry workforce pool. Plasma physics talent is shared with the broader fusion community but the FRC niche is thin.
- **Plausible learning-curve mechanism**: **R&D-driven, with limited volume amplification** — closer to gas-turbine LBR/LBD pattern than solar Wright's Law. The dominant cost driver (NBI capital + recirc power) descends mainly through efficiency R&D, not unit-count.
- **Dominant failure factor at Stage 4**: **F4.a at the failure pole** — narrow set of cost-reduction knobs, almost all fusion-funded; the curve runs out before reaching D-T-competitive LCOE under steam.
- **Dominant leverage factor at Stage 4**: **F4.c at the leverage pole** — B-11 fuel supply is the cleanest specialty-input position in the pipeline (no bottleneck, no toxicology, no NPT/dual-use overhang).

## Cross-stage carriers
- **Tritium / Li-6 / Be supply — where it bites hardest**: **Bites nowhere.** Aneutronic operation removes the entire carrier. This is one of TAE's most consequential structural advantages — it is the single concept in the pipeline that pays zero across the sharpest fleet-scale supply constraint [ecosystem brief: tritium is "arguably the single sharpest fleet-scale supply constraint for D-T concepts"].
- **First-wall / divertor / blanket lifetime — where it bites hardest**: Stage 4 most, as a recurring capex line item; but bite is much lighter than D-T because alpha particles (not 14 MeV neutrons) deposit energy, structural materials don't activate to clearance-relevant levels, and no breeding blanket replacement cycle exists. Linear FRC geometry adaptation of tokamak divertor materials is an open engineering question (TRL 2 hardware per synthesis F4).
- **HTS conductor cost trajectory — where it bites hardest**: **Bites nowhere.** Resistive copper coils at ~1 kG external field exploit FRC near-unity beta. TAE is structurally insulated from REBCO supply tightening at fleet scale.
- **Fuel-ecosystem R&D position**: Isolated from D-T R&D cohort, but B-11 has no fuel-supply problem worth sharing R&D on. Net: neutral-to-slight-disadvantage (no shared cost amortization but also no shared cost to amortize).
- **Workforce / intellectual depth carrier**: Helps at Stage 2 and Stage 4 modestly through accelerator/ion-implantation/defense pulsed-power adjacent workforce; hurts at Stage 1 because the bespoke beam-driven FRC paradigm has no co-developer concept to broaden the PhD pipeline.

## Dominant failure mode (single biggest gate across all stages)
- **Stage and F-factor (at failure pole)**: **Stage 2, F2.b (build-time / commissioning physics risk)** — but the underlying mechanism is the foundational physics binary in the synthesis: sustained T_i>>T_e and Q_plasma>1 for p-B11, neither of which is demonstrated. Across the F-factor vocabulary this manifests as F2.b because the failure surfaces during Da Vinci commissioning.
- **One-sentence rationale**: Every downstream factor is conditioned on Da Vinci's commissioning campaign actually crossing the bremsstrahlung-balance threshold at commercial ion temperature — a ~50–80× T_i extrapolation with no validating program elsewhere — and below that threshold there is no LCOE, not a degraded one.

## Dominant leverage (single biggest tailwind across all stages)
- **Stage and factor**: **Stage 2 / Cross-stage, F2.c at the leverage pole reinforced by the tritium/Li-6/Be carrier being absent** — together, the aneutronic regulatory-and-supply position. Among the formal codes the strongest single one is **F2.c (regulatory framework state, leverage pole)**.
- **One-sentence rationale**: TAE is the cleanest fit to the new NRC Part 30 fusion framework — no tritium accountancy, no remote handling, hands-on maintenance, minimal secondary shielding — which both compresses FOAK regulatory cost and amortizes cleanly through the chasm (F3.d), and that regulatory leverage is structurally locked in by the aneutronic fuel choice rather than dependent on physics success.
- **Slack vs. bottleneck status**: Slack — the regulatory framework has substantial headroom for additional fusion entrants; TAE's fit is not contingent on being first to use it.

## What this trace surfaces for the deep-dive
- **Output format implication**: **SysML model is the wrong output format** for this concept. The dominant uncertainty is a *physics-viability boundary* (Q × η_NBI × f_rad → Branch A vs. Branch B/C), not a cost-decomposition refinement. The right output is a **1costingfe extension with explicit Branch A/B/C scenario logic**, exactly as analysis §2 prescribes — a viability-map tool rather than a central-case LCOE estimator. A SysML structural model would add false precision over a foundational binary risk.
- **The 2–3 highest-value questions a deep-dive should answer**:
  1. **What is the Q_plasma viability threshold as a function of η_NBI and f_rad, and where on that grid does any plausible Da Vinci operating point sit?** This is the single decision-making artifact — without it, every other LCOE number is conditional on an unmeasured binary.
  2. **What is the realistic η_NBI(E_beam) curve for 250 keV proton NBI at ≥30 MW, drawing on ITER NNBI commissioning data and accelerator-physics analogues?** This collapses the largest non-physics elasticity in the model.
  3. **How much of the aneutronic regulatory + supply-chain leverage (F2.c leverage pole, absent tritium carrier, F3.d amortization) translates to a quantitative capital-cost delta vs. the D-T tokamak baseline, separable from the physics-risk discount?** This isolates the structural value of the aneutronic choice from the physics bet, which is the analytically interesting decomposition for an investor.
