# 14 General Fusion (MTF, pneumatic compression, liquid lithium wall) — Tagged Record

**Stage-1 timeline note.** General Fusion is the only major program pursuing mechanical (pneumatic/steam) compression of a flowing liquid-metal liner. LM26 (operational 2025–26, 50% plasma scale) targets 10 keV ion temperature and the Lawson criterion as a 2026 milestone, using an electromagnetic theta-pinch surrogate compressing a solid lithium liner — a different mechanism from the commercial pneumatic concept. The commercial driver (steam-driven piston array compressing a flowing liquid-metal vortex at 1 Hz) has never been built or tested at any scale; the synthesis records an 86,400× rep-rate gap from demonstration (1 shot/day) to commercial (1 Hz). The paradigm has thin co-development depth — historical analogues (LANL FRX-L, MAGO, LINUS) are discontinued and produced limited public data, and there is no parallel national-lab or private program pursuing pneumatic-liquid-metal MTF. Workforce draws on plasma physics (shared with the broader MFE community) and on mechanical / liquid-metal handling expertise that exists primarily in the sodium fast-reactor lineage. Distance to a Stage-2-ready commercial regime is long and the path is uncertain; no facility for pneumatic-driver demonstration has been announced. Descriptive only — these are timeline inputs, not failure-mode tags.

## Stage 2 — FOAK affordability

### Consequential failure modes
- `F2.a/$5-10B-financing` — Synthesis model output is ~$2.1B overnight at 300 MWe ($6,959/kW), implying a minimum-viable plant in the $2–3B range and below the $5B sovereign-only threshold. Consequential because driver-capital uncertainty (±100%) could push the plant above $5B in the high case, but base case sits in the strategic-investor / hyperscaler-consortium accessible band rather than the sovereign-only band. Treated as consequential at the lower-cell variant; tag descriptor distinguishes from ARC's `above-$5B-financing`.
- `F2.b/build-time-5-to-7` — Synthesis assumes a 5-year construction window with no HTS commissioning, but the novel pneumatic compression system, liquid-metal handling, and unprecedented piston synchronization add substantial schedule risk. Plausibly 5–7 years if the driver demonstration is collapsed into FOAK construction; pushes toward the regulatory-ratcheting zone but does not sit squarely in the 7+ band.
- `F2.d-pneumatic-driver/capability-gap` — The commercial pneumatic piston array has no external supply chain at any scale; industrial reciprocating compressors operate at <0.1 Hz, non-synchronized, in non-activated environments. The synthesis flags this as a capability-class gap — "no system approaching 1 Hz synchronized multi-piston operation in activated liquid metal environment." Closest in shape to TAE's `F2.d-driver-supply/capability-gap`. Synthesis §5: "the concept's viability is binary on pneumatic compression feasibility."
- `F2.d-liquid-Li-wall/no-external-market` — Flowing liquid-lithium wall at fusion scale has no commercial supply; nearest analogues are sodium fast-reactor liquid-metal loops (steady-state, not pulsed, not lithium). Pulsed 1 Hz liquid-metal handling at this energy density is unprecedented.
- `F2.d-Li6/no-external-market` — Same shape as ARC's tritium-Li6 subline: no commercial market outside fusion, Western enrichment capacity ~1–5 t/yr (Russia/China-dominated), startup tritium pool dependent on declining CANDU output.
- `F2.d-tritium-startup/no-external-market` — Kg-scale tritium for FOAK startup from a shrinking commercial pool. Shared failure mode across all D-T concepts; the synthesis does not give General Fusion any architectural advantage here despite the higher TBR target.
- `F2.d-Li-Steam-IHX/specialty-low-volume` — Intermediate heat exchangers isolating reactive Li from steam are not commodity. ITER TBM programs and SFR experience provide a design basis but not a commercial supply class at FOAK scale; specialty-low-volume rather than capability-gap because the underlying technology class exists.

### Consequential leverages
- `F2.c/Part-30-fit` (unconditional) — D-T fusion plant with no fission-class fissile inventory fits the February 2026 NRC Part 30 fusion framework. Same cell as ARC; no aneutronic regulatory advantage. Locked in by fuel class regardless of driver outcome.
- `F2.d-Cu-coils/mature-commodity` (unconditional) — Normal-conducting copper guide-field coils for the CT injector sit on a mature commodity supply chain (~$10M, 1.5% of capital). Same shape as TAE's `F2.d-magnets/mature-commodity`. No HTS supply exposure.
- `F2.d-steam-BOP/mature-commodity` (unconditional) — Rankine steam cycle, turbine, condenser, feedwater, electrical plant, cooling are all on commodity supply chains with massive non-fusion demand (coal, gas, fission). ~50% of capital sits on this leverage pole — unusually high for a fusion FOAK and consequential at the capital-fraction level.

## Stage 3 — Chasm crossing

### Consequential failure modes
- `F3.a-pneumatic-driver/capability-gap-at-chasm` — Capability gap carries from Stage 2. Pneumatic piston arrays have no non-fusion industrial pull at chasm-era volumes; the only demand driver at fleet scale is fusion itself. Same shape as TAE's `F3.a-driver-supply/capability-gap-at-chasm` — both concepts have a driver-side capability gap that does not co-scale with external markets.
- `F3.a-liquid-Li-wall/fleet-supply-absent` — Fleet-scale flowing-Li-wall infrastructure does not exist. Sodium fast-reactor lineage provides component knowledge (pumps, piping, IHX) but not pulsed-Li integration at scale.
- `F3.a-Li6/fleet-enrichment-absent` — Same row as ARC: Western Li-6 enrichment capacity must scale 2–10× for a D-T fleet; the COLEX restart is a shared chasm-era bottleneck across all D-T concepts.
- `F3.a-tritium/fleet-breeding-required` — Same row as ARC: scaled tritium breeding required before fleet rollout. General Fusion's 4π geometry with TBR=1.5 target relaxes the Li-6 enrichment requirement but does not eliminate the chasm-scale tritium accountancy and breeding-loop validation problem.
- `F3.b/site-build-fraction-high` — Synthesis modularization scoring shows the dominant capital item (compression driver, 26.6% of capital) is stick-built around the cavity, the liquid-metal wall and heat-exchange systems (17.9%) are site-assembled welded piping, and buildings (18.7%) are site-built. >35% of capital is site-built; this lands in the Eash-Gates 2020 megaproject-cost-disease zone where steel labor hours run 3–4× and concrete 8–13× expectation.
- `F3.c/replication-300MWe-feasible` — 300 MWe is large for hyperscaler PPA template (Google/CFS pattern) but accessible to strategic-sovereign and utility anchor buyers. Not consequential as a failure mode at base case — included only because driver-capital uncertainty could push per-unit price into a band where single-buyer multi-unit commitment becomes harder.

### Consequential leverages
- `F3.a-steam-BOP/fleet-pull-dominant` (unconditional) — ~50% of capital on commodity BOP (turbines, electrical, cooling, buildings, shield steel, Cu) rides external markets that vastly exceed fusion demand at chasm scale. This is structurally similar to TAE's commodity-magnet position but extended across a larger capital fraction. Rare in this candidate set — most concepts have a smaller commodity-BOP fraction.
- `F3.d/standardized-amortization` (gate-conditional) — Standardized 300 MWe design supports an amortizing Part-30 licensing template across fleet units. Conditional on the FOAK clearing licensing without forcing major design changes; pneumatic-driver novelty makes precedent-setting harder than ARC's SPARC-precedent inheritance.

## Stage 4 — Learning-curve descent

### Consequential failure modes
- `F4.b/volume-driven-mismatch` — At 300–1000 MWe per unit and the synthesis's stated 1.5 GW/unit market context, deployment counts at Stage 4 are low (tens to low hundreds globally). Wright's-Law volume-driven learning has a thin numerator. Concept needs an R&D-driven curve on the driver and liquid-metal subsystems but has neither the academic depth (no parallel programs) nor the cross-industry R&D pull (no non-fusion pneumatic-piston-at-1Hz demand). Risk that no learning-curve mechanism is structurally available for the driver.
- `F4.d/modular-tax-no-crossover` — Synthesis modularization score is 2.3 (poor); driver is stick-built, no identical-module repetition. Modularization tax with no plausible volume-driven crossover for the pneumatic system, mirroring NuScale's mode of failure but for a different reason (single-unit-uniqueness rather than over-modularization).
- `F4.c-pneumatic-driver/no-external-market` — Driver-specialty cost floor bounded by fusion-deployment volume alone. No semiconductor / MRI / grid / transport analogue.
- `F4.c-Li6/no-external-market` — Same cell as ARC; D-T cohort-shared cost floor.
- `F4.c-tritium-extraction-tech/no-external-market` — Fusion-specific specialty, no external market driving the cost floor.

### Consequential leverages
- `F4.a/many-knobs-commodity-fraction` (unconditional) — ~50% of capital on commodity BOP carries non-fusion cost-reduction innovation across multiple Kavlak-2018-style knobs (turbine efficiency, cooling, shield steel, electrical). Steam-cycle and turbine-side cost curves run regardless of fusion deployment. Mirror-pole leverage to F4.b's mismatch: the commodity fraction has a learning curve even though the fusion-specific fraction does not.
- `F4.c-steam-BOP/external-market-dominant` (unconditional) — Power-conversion specialty inputs ride coal/gas/fission demand at fleet scale. Same shape as ARC's `F4.c-REBCO/external-market-dominant` but for a different specialty class.

## Cross-stage carriers

D-T fuel cycle (tritium startup, Li-6 enrichment, tritium extraction) is a failure-side carrier across Stages 2, 3, and 4 — the same underlying mechanism (no external commercial market for D-T fuel cycle inputs) drives sublines at each stage. Pneumatic-driver capability gap is a second failure-side carrier across Stages 2, 3, and 4 — a driver-class supply problem that has no external industry to co-scale with it. Note that General Fusion carries **two** failure-side cross-stage carriers simultaneously (D-T fuel cycle and driver-capability gap), whereas ARC carries only the D-T fuel cycle carrier and TAE carries only the driver-capability gap. On the leverage side, commodity steam-BOP / electrical / building share is a cross-stage carrier on the leverage side, biting at Stage 3 (capital-fraction commodity pull) and Stage 4 (external-market-dominant cost floor) — this is the closest General Fusion has to ARC's REBCO leverage carrier, but spread across many commodity classes rather than concentrated in one specialty class with a single rising external industry.

## Structural absences

- **HTS / REBCO sublines absent across F2.d, F3.a, F4.c.** General Fusion eliminates the HTS magnet system entirely (synthesis §4.A1). No cryoplant, no quench-protection R&D cohort participation. This is a structural feature — concept is outside the ~8-concept HTS cohort.
- **FLiBe sublines absent across F2.d, F3.a, F4.c.** Liquid lithium wall (or PbLi), not FLiBe — concept is outside the molten-salt-handling sub-cohort (concepts 01, 07).
- **Beryllium sublines absent across F2.d, F3.a, F4.c.** No Be multiplier required; 4π liquid-metal geometry with TBR=1.5 target eliminates the Be subline that ARC carries.
- **E2.a (crossover platform) absent.** Pneumatic-piston-at-1Hz-in-activated-environment has no defense / industrial / grid / MRI / transport adjacent revenue leg. Closest analogue is large industrial steam-piston / reciprocating-compressor industry, which operates in a categorically different duty-cycle and synchronization regime.
- **E2.b (intra-fusion early-mover supply-chain effect) absent for driver.** No earlier-shipping fusion concept builds the pneumatic-driver supply chain; General Fusion is the cohort of one for this driver class. (E2.b is partially present on the tritium-extraction and Li-6 side, where ITER TBM and any earlier D-T concept generates shared R&D — see E-carrier note.)
- **E3.a (intra-fusion cohort co-development) absent for driver.** Zero other concepts in the candidate set need pneumatic-driver components.
- **E3.b (shared sub-problem solution leverage) thin.** Liquid-metal handling shares some sub-problem space with PbLi blanket concepts and with sodium fast-reactor lineage, but the pulsed regime is unique to General Fusion. Tritium extraction sub-problem is shared with the broader D-T cohort (ITER TBM).

## Methodology friction notes

1. **F2.a band granularity.** ARC sits at `above-$5B-financing`; TAE sits at `$1-5B-financing`. General Fusion's base case (~$2.1B at 300 MWe) is below $5B at base case but plausibly $5–10B in the high driver-cost case. I used `$5-10B-financing` for the failure-mode entry, but the vocabulary doesn't cleanly accommodate concepts whose F2.a *cell* is uncertain because driver-capital uncertainty straddles a band boundary. Either the cells need to be wider, or the methodology needs a "band-spanning" qualifier.

2. **F2.b middle band.** ARC uses `build-time-7-plus`; the methodology mentions "under-5" as the complement. General Fusion is plausibly 5–7 years — a middle band where regulatory-ratcheting risk is moderate but not dominant. I used `build-time-5-to-7` as a descriptor neither ARC nor TAE carries; recommend adding to the cross-concept vocabulary.

3. **Two failure-side cross-stage carriers simultaneously.** ARC has one (D-T fuel cycle). TAE has one (driver-capability gap). GF has *both*. The cross-stage-carrier section in the canonical format reads as if a concept carries zero, one, or a few carriers, but the methodology doesn't surface "multi-carrier concept" as a structural observation. This may belong in cross-concept analysis rather than per-trace, but worth flagging.

4. **`F2.d` for "pneumatic driver" as a class.** TAE's `F2.d-driver-supply` is the canonical capability-gap-class subline. General Fusion's driver is also a capability gap, but the *class* is mechanically different (pneumatic pistons vs high-energy NBI). I used `F2.d-pneumatic-driver` to disambiguate. The methodology may want a rule for whether sublines name the *function* (driver) or the *technology class* (pneumatic-driver, NBI-driver, laser-driver, Z-pinch-driver). Naming by technology class is more decision-relevant for cohort assessment but creates a sparser cell map.

5. **`F4.b/volume-driven-mismatch` is a new descriptor.** Neither ARC nor TAE carried an explicit F4.b failure-mode tag (both had leverages or implicit assumptions about volume-driven learning). For GF the F4.b mismatch is consequential and central — the deployment-count math doesn't support Wright's Law and the paradigm-co-development depth doesn't support R&D-driven learning either. Recommend adding `volume-driven-mismatch` and a corresponding `R-and-D-driven-mismatch` to the cross-concept vocabulary; concepts may carry one, both, or neither.

6. **`F4.d/modular-tax-no-crossover` for stick-built concepts.** Methodology describes the modularization tax-vs-crossover question but ARC and TAE didn't produce a Stage-4 F4.d failure-mode tag. For GF the stick-built driver creates a clean failure-mode entry. Suggest formalizing the descriptor.

7. **Specialty inputs without bespoke external-market shape — e.g., `F2.d-Li-Steam-IHX/specialty-low-volume`.** Some sublines aren't quite `no-external-market` (the technology class exists — IHX from SFR lineage) and aren't quite `external-market-pull-slack` (no commercial-scale supply at fusion duty). The vocabulary has `capability-gap` for "doesn't exist" and `no-external-market` for "exists at small scale but no commercial pull"; I needed a middle descriptor (`specialty-low-volume`) for "exists in adjacent industry but not at commercial fusion duty cycle." May be one descriptor too many; alternatively `no-external-market` could be broadened.

8. **Commodity-BOP as a single leverage cluster.** GF's strongest leverage is that ~50% of capital sits on commodity supply chains (turbines, electrical, cooling, buildings, shield steel, Cu coils). ARC and TAE record individual commodity sublines (`F2.d-magnets/mature-commodity` for TAE) but the methodology doesn't have a clean way to surface a *commodity-fraction* observation that's primarily about the *aggregate*, not any single subline. The capital-fraction observation is decision-relevant and recurs across concepts; consider a `commodity-fraction-X-percent` descriptor or a cohort-level summary axis.

9. **Stage 1 reframe sits awkwardly for GF specifically.** GF's largest historical failure-mode framing (pneumatic compression has never been demonstrated) is genuinely an engineering / integrated-system risk that under the new methodology gets routed to F2.d-pneumatic-driver and F3.a-pneumatic-driver capability-gap sublines. This works — but the routing requires interpretation. The friction is that the *physics gate* (Lawson, TBR) and the *commercial-driver engineering gate* (pneumatic at 1 Hz) genuinely sit at different methodological points (Stage-1 timeline vs Stage-2 supply), and the synthesis blurs them because it framed both as "binary viability gates." The methodology handles this correctly, but the routing should probably be documented in the explainer to keep traces consistent across analysts.

10. **No descriptor for "F3.b stick-built fraction observed."** F3.b is sign-neutral (failure pole = site-build-heavy, leverage pole = ship-and-install). I used `site-build-fraction-high` to anchor a >35%-of-capital observation; ARC and TAE did not record explicit F3.b tags. Suggest formalizing thresholds: e.g., `site-build-fraction-low` <15%, `mid` 15–35%, `high` >35%.
