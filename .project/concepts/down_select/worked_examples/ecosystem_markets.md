## REBCO HTS tape

Global REBCO production capacity reached an estimated 3,500–4,000 km/year by 2024, up from <100 km/year in 2014 (Molodyk & Larbalestier, *Science* 2023; Hartwig et al., 2023). Major producers: SuperPower (US), Faraday Factory Japan, Fujikura, SuNAM (Korea), Shanghai Superconductor, SuperOx, Bruker HTS, AMSC. CFS-affiliated Commonwealth and Tokamak Energy have catalyzed capacity expansion; CFS alone reportedly consumed ~10,000 km for SPARC TF coils (CFS press, 2023). Cost trajectory has fallen from ~$400/kA-m (77 K, self-field) in 2014 to ~$30–50/kA-m in 2024 for high-volume orders, with $10–20/kA-m targets by ~2030 (Bruzzone et al., *Fus. Eng. Des.* 2018; Whyte, MIT 2023 testimony).

Non-fusion demand drivers: MRI/NMR (Bruker, GE — moving from LTS to HTS for >20 T magnets), grid fault-current limiters and HTS cables (~50 km/yr installed, IEA 2023), high-field accelerator magnets (FCC, muon collider R&D), HTS motors/generators for aerospace and naval (GE Aerospace, Airbus ASCEND), and DoD directed-energy/rail-launch programs. Combined non-fusion baseline demand ~1,000–2,000 km/yr (Hartwig 2023 est.).

A single ARC-class plant requires ~10,000 km; a 10-plant fleet by 2035 implies ~100,000 km — roughly 25× current global output. Capacity expansions announced (Faraday Factory 1,000 km/yr line; SuperOx; Fujikura) total ~10,000–15,000 km/yr by 2027 (industry tracking, *Superconductor Week* 2024).

**Concepts depending on REBCO**: CFS SPARC/ARC, Tokamak Energy ST40/ST-E1, Tokamak Energy spherical tokamak path, Type One Energy stellarator, Thea Energy planar-coil stellarator, Renaissance Fusion, Zap (HTS-augmented sheared-flow stabilization variants), and most "compact, high-field" MFE concepts post-2018.

**Assessment for fusion downselection**: Currently **leverage pole** (strong non-fusion industrial trajectory and falling $/kA-m), but **bottleneck-likely** at fleet scale 2030–2035 absent another 10× capacity expansion. Slack only for the first ~3–5 plants.

## High-power lasers (kJ-class, MJ-class, high-rep-rate)

Global industrial laser market: ~$22 B in 2023, dominated by sub-kW fiber/CO₂/diode for cutting, welding, marking (Optech Consulting 2024; Strategies Unlimited 2023). Almost none of this volume is relevant to IFE: ICF drivers need kJ–MJ pulse energies at 1–10 Hz with >10% wall-plug efficiency. NIF delivers 2.05 MJ at 0.5–1 shot/day, ~0.5% wall-plug (Spaeth et al., *Fus. Sci. Tech.* 2016; Zylstra et al., *Nature* 2022). LMJ (France) and SG-III (China) are comparable single-shot facilities.

High-rep-rate kJ-class diode-pumped solid-state laser (DPSSL) state of art: HiPER/Mercury (LLNL, 55 J @ 10 Hz, ~10% efficiency, decommissioned), DiPOLE-100 (STFC/CLF UK, 100 J @ 10 Hz, ~20% efficiency; Mason et al., *Optica* 2017), ELI-Beamlines L4 (1.5 kJ, single shot to 1/min). None approach the ~1–10 MJ at 10 Hz needed for an IFE plant.

Defense pulsed-laser programs (HELSI, IFPC-HEL, Iron Beam, Russian/Chinese analogues) drive 100 kW–1 MW CW solid-state and fiber lasers — adjacent but different regime (CW power, not ns pulses). DoD investment ~$1 B/yr (CRS 2023) supports diode-pump and thermal-management tech transferable to IFE.

Vendors: Coherent/II-VI, IPG, Trumpf (industrial); Lawrence Livermore/General Atomics (NIF target/optics); Logos/Marvel Fusion partner with TRUMPF and Thales for DPSSL development.

**Concepts depending on lasers**: NIF-line indirect-drive (LLNL national program, Longview Fusion), direct-drive (LLE Rochester, Focused Energy), shock ignition (HiPER lineage), Marvel Fusion (p-B11 ultra-short pulse), Xcimer Energy (KrF excimer, claims $0.10/J), Logos Technologies/Pacific Fusion-adjacent laser-driven concepts.

**Assessment for fusion downselection**: **Failure pole** for IFE-relevant regime — minimal non-fusion industrial base for kJ-MJ ns-pulse high-rep-rate. Must internalize R&D and manufacturing. **Bottleneck-likely** indefinitely; defense crossover helps components (diodes, gain media) but not full systems.

## Pulsed-power capacitors and Marx generators

Global power-capacitor market: ~$5 B (2023), but **energy-density pulsed-discharge capacitors** (>1 kJ/kg, >50 kV, low-ESR) are a niche subset, ~$200–400 M/yr (Frost & Sullivan 2022; *IEEE Trans. Plasma Sci.* survey, McDonald 2020). Major suppliers: General Atomics Electromagnetic Systems, Aerovox, ICAR, TDK, Maxwell/Eaton (formerly), NWL.

Defense pulsed-power base: NIF capacitor bank (422 MJ stored, 192 lines; Spaeth 2016), Z-machine at Sandia (22 MJ, 26 TW; Sinars et al., *Phys. Plasmas* 2020), Air Force Research Lab IMG (Impedance-Matched Marx) programs, naval railgun and EM launcher R&D (~$500 M cumulative DoD investment). Stockpile-stewardship demand provides stable but non-scaling baseline (~10 MJ/yr replacement).

Commercial/medical: lithotripters, defibrillators, X-ray modulators — small unit energies, large unit count, not directly transferable to MJ-class banks. Particle-accelerator klystron modulators (CERN, SLAC, FNAL) drive ~10–100 J/pulse capacitor technology.

Helion uses solid-state semiconductor switches (IGBT/SiC stacks) instead of spark gaps, leveraging the >$10 B power-electronics market — a different supply chain (Helion technical disclosures 2023). This is a critical distinction: solid-state pulsed power has strong commercial leverage; classical Marx/spark-gap does not.

**Concepts depending on pulsed power**: Sandia MagLIF/Z-pinch path (Pacific Fusion explicitly targets ~60 MJ driver at $0.50/J using IMG; Pacific Fusion 2024 announcement), TAE Technologies FRC formation banks, Helion FRC (semiconductor-switched), Zap Energy sheared-flow Z-pinch, General Fusion magnetized target (compression drivers), LPP Fusion dense plasma focus.

**Assessment for fusion downselection**: **Mixed** — classical capacitor/spark-gap path is **failure-pole leaning**, slim non-fusion base. Solid-state pulsed power (Helion-style) is **leverage pole** via semiconductor industry. Fleet-scale bottleneck likely for classical Marx; slack-likely for solid-state. Pacific Fusion's IMG bet sits between.

## FLiBe / Li-6 / Be

**FLiBe (2LiF–BeF₂)**: No commercial production. Only multi-ton historical batches were from ORNL's MSRE program in the 1960s (~5 tonnes; Rosenthal et al., ORNL-4548 1972). Current US production: Kairos Power's KP-FHR program is reconstituting FLiBe production at pilot scale (~tonnes/yr) via partnership with Materion (Kairos Power filings, 2023). Required for a fusion blanket: ~500–1,000 tonnes per GWe-class plant (Forsberg et al., *Nucl. Tech.* 2020).

**Li-6 enrichment**: US production ceased in 1963 (COLEX mercury-amalgam process at Oak Ridge); current US inventory ~[unknown, classified] but the DOE NNSA restarted enrichment R&D in 2022 with a target of demonstrating non-mercury enrichment by ~2028 (NNSA Y-12 program announcement 2023). Russia and China maintain Li-6 enrichment (chemical exchange and laser-based); commercial sales blocked by NPT/dual-use controls. Natural Li is 7.5% Li-6; fusion blankets typically need 30–90% enrichment. Global commercial Li-6 supply ~[unknown, but <1 tonne/yr].

**Beryllium**: Global production ~260 tonnes/yr (USGS 2024), 90% from Materion (US, Spor Mountain Utah) and Ulba (Kazakhstan). Used in aerospace alloys, X-ray windows, neutron reflectors. Toxicology: chronic beryllium disease (CBD) constraints make fabrication facilities expensive (~10× standard metalworking; OSHA 0.2 µg/m³ PEL since 2017). ITER's TBM uses ~10 tonnes of Be; a DEMO blanket using Be multiplier needs ~300–500 tonnes (Boccaccini et al., *Fus. Eng. Des.* 2016). Be₁₂Ti is preferred for higher temperature stability but is not commercially produced.

**Concepts depending on these**: All D-T MFE with liquid blanket (CFS ARC FLiBe, Type One Energy, Tokamak Energy, EU-DEMO HCPB) and many MIF/IFE concepts. Helion (D-He3) and TAE (p-B11) bypass entirely. Z-pinch / MagLIF can use Li-Pb instead.

**Assessment for fusion downselection**: **Failure pole** — essentially no commercial market to ride. Li-6 supply is the sharpest **bottleneck-likely** constraint; Be is leverage-poor with toxicology overhead; FLiBe must be built from scratch (Kairos-FHR partnership is the only crossover lever). Concepts using Li-Pb or aneutronic fuels avoid this.

## Tritium

**Current global civilian inventory**: ~25–30 kg, ~90% held in CANDU heavy-water reactors (Ontario Power Generation Darlington TRF produces ~2.5 kg/yr; Kovari et al., *Fus. Eng. Des.* 2018). Military inventories [unknown, classified] but US production restarted at TVA Watts Bar via TPBARs (~0.5–1 kg/yr DoE/NNSA; GAO-21-184 2021).

**Decay**: T half-life 12.3 yr, ~5.5%/yr loss.

**Burn requirement**: A 1 GWe D-T fusion plant consumes ~56 kg/yr (Abdou et al., *Nucl. Fus.* 2021). Startup inventory: 5–20 kg per plant depending on tritium-burn fraction and processing efficiency. ITER alone will consume ~1 kg over its lifetime, sourced from CANDU.

**Breeding requirement**: At fleet scale, plants must achieve net TBR > 1 from Li blankets. Abdou (2021) estimates that a fleet must self-sustain (TBR ≥1.05 with rapid recycle, <5 day residence time) or growth is supply-limited; doubling time from external supply alone is decades.

**Gap**: Even a 5-plant D-T fleet ~2035 needs ~25–100 kg startup — exceeding civilian inventory. CANDU production is declining as Pickering decommissions; Darlington refurb may extend supply, but no new heavy-water reactors are being built (Pearson et al., *Fus. Sci. Tech.* 2018).

**Concepts depending on tritium**: All D-T concepts — CFS, Tokamak Energy, Type One Energy, Thea, EU-DEMO, ITER. Helion (D-He3 with internal T-breeding from D-D side reactions), TAE (p-B11), Marvel (p-B11), LPP (p-B11) avoid the constraint.

**Assessment for fusion downselection**: **Failure pole** — no scalable external market. **Bottleneck-likely** and arguably the single sharpest fleet-scale supply constraint for D-T concepts; first plants race CANDU decline. Aneutronic concepts get a distinct E-factor advantage here.

## Cryogenic D-T target fabrication

**Current capacity**: General Atomics' Inertial Fusion Technologies division produces ~1,000–2,000 NIF targets/yr (capsule shells of HDC/Be/CH, ~2 mm dia, ~µm wall, atomic-scale roughness; Hoppe et al., *Fus. Sci. Tech.* 2018). LLE Rochester produces glass and CH shells for OMEGA. Targets cost ~$50,000–$100,000 each (NIF), driven by metrology and bespoke handling. Cryogenic D-T layering: ~24 hr per target at NIF using β-layering.

**IFE-rep-rate requirement**: At 10 Hz, a single IFE plant consumes ~300 M targets/yr. Cost target: <$0.30/target to reach LCOE viability (Goodin et al., *Fus. Sci. Tech.* 2008; Dunne et al., LIFE study 2011). That's a 5–6 orders of magnitude unit-cost reduction and ~5 orders of magnitude throughput scale-up vs. current.

**Industrial precedents**: Fluidized-bed and microfluidic shell production demonstrated at lab scale (Paguio et al., LLNL/GA 2007); no commercial production line exists. Injection and tracking at 10 Hz to sub-100 µm precision in a hot chamber remains unsolved at integrated scale (Petzoldt et al., *Fus. Sci. Tech.* 2007).

**Non-fusion crossover**: Microencapsulation industries (pharma, drug delivery) produce billions of shells/yr but at mm-scale and without cryogenic precision requirements. Minor leverage.

**Concepts depending**: All hot-spot IFE — NIF-line, Longview Fusion, Focused Energy, LLE direct-drive path. Xcimer (KrF). Marvel Fusion uses solid p-B11/D targets without cryogenic D-T layering — sidesteps this entirely. Shock/fast ignition concepts have similar requirements.

**Assessment for fusion downselection**: **Failure pole** — no external market to leverage. **Bottleneck-likely** at any rep-rated IFE fleet scale; cost reduction by 10⁵–10⁶× is unprecedented in any analogous manufacturing line. One of the hardest single supply-chain problems in IFE.

## Boron-11 (enriched p-B11 fuel)

**Natural abundance**: B-11 is 80.1% of natural boron; B-10 is 19.9%. p-B11 fusion requires B-11 enrichment to >99% to suppress side reactions (Putvinski et al., *Nucl. Fus.* 2019).

**Current isotope-separation capacity**: B-10 (not B-11) is the commercial product, used in nuclear control rods, BNCT cancer therapy, and semiconductor doping for SOI/BCDMOS where natural B activation is undesirable. Global B-10 production: ~5–10 tonnes/yr, dominated by Eagle-Picher (US), Ceradyne/3M (US), and Russian (PA Mayak) and Chinese facilities. Methods: distillation of BF₃-anisole complex or chemical exchange (Palko et al., 1976; recent: Sandia/ORNL laser-isotope R&D).

**B-11 as a "co-product"**: B-11 is the depleted tail when B-10 is enriched. Highly enriched B-11 (>99%) is sold for semiconductor doping (specifically to avoid α-particle emission from B-10(n,α) in chips near memory) at ~$3,000–$10,000/kg (specialty isotope vendors: Trace Sciences, Isoflex, ORNL Stable Isotope Group). Global market <1 tonne/yr (Isoflex catalog 2023; ORNL SIG 2022).

**Fuel requirement**: A 1 GWe p-B11 plant at perfect efficiency burns ~70 kg B-11/yr; realistic with recirculation, ~100–500 kg/yr (Ruggiero, TAE 2023 estimate). Even a single plant exceeds current global enriched-B11 sales by ~100×. Scale-up of distillation columns is well-understood chemical engineering; capital cost moderate (~$100 M for kt-scale, Levenspiel-type estimates) — not a fundamental bottleneck like Li-6.

**Non-fusion demand**: Semiconductor doping market is small but growing with advanced-node demand (Samsung, TSMC use enriched B); BNCT consumes B-10 not B-11.

**Concepts depending**: TAE Technologies (FRC + neutral beams), HB11 Energy (laser-driven), Marvel Fusion, LPP Fusion (dense plasma focus). All assume future commodity B-11.

**Assessment for fusion downselection**: **Failure pole currently** (no fusion-scale market), but **slack-likely** at fleet scale — separation chemistry is mature, capacity expansion is engineering not invention, and B-11 has no toxicology or regulatory overhang like Be or Li-6. Lowest-severity bottleneck among fuel-cycle constraints, contingent on aneutronic concept viability.
