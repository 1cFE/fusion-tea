# 08 Helion (FRC + direct conversion, D-He3) — Tagged Record

**Stage-1 timeline note.** Helion is the only program pursuing pulsed-FRC + direct electromagnetic energy recovery on D-He3 fuel. Polaris is operating (achieved 150M°C / 13 keV D-T plasma temperature, Feb 2026) and Orion is under construction in Malaga WA with a Microsoft PPA targeting 50 MWe grid delivery in 2028. Distance to a Stage-2-ready commercial regime is long: the commercial D-He3 operating point requires ~65 keV ion temperature (~5× the demonstrated D-T temperature), 40 T compressed field (~5× Trenta's 8 T, ~2.7× Polaris's 15 T target), and 1–2 Hz sustained rep rate (~500–1000× Trenta's ~0.002 Hz). Paradigm co-development depth is thin: FRC physics has a small academic lineage (Slough, Kirtley & Milroy 2023) and one parallel private program on a different FRC architecture (TAE, beam-driven steady-state); pulsed-compression + direct-conversion FRC is Helion-only with no national-lab or co-developer cohort. Workforce draws on plasma physics broadly shared with MFE and on pulsed-power engineering shared with defense (Sandia MagLIF, ICF labs). The synthesis notes the architecture has an explicit Plan-B: if D-He3 ignition is not reached, Helion converts to pulsed D-T operation at substantially higher LCOE (synthesis §5: "LCOE would rise to ~150–200 $/MWh") rather than ending the program. Descriptive only — timeline inputs, not failure-mode tags. The fallback path is recorded under Structural absences / mode-switching below.

## Stage 2 — FOAK affordability

### Consequential failure modes
- `F2.a/$1-5B-financing` — Synthesis overnight cost is ~$1.7B/kW × 50 MWe Orion module = $85M for a single-module FOAK, scaling to ~$1.7B at 1 GWe (20 modules). The single-module FOAK is well below the $5B sovereign-only threshold and is in fact accessible to a single hyperscaler PPA (Microsoft Orion contract is the live example). Same band cell as TAE's `$1-5B-financing`. Consequential because the capacitor-cost uncertainty (10× spread) and the embedded module count both swing the practical FOAK financing requirement; not consequential at the band-boundary level.
- `F2.b/build-time-under-5` — Synthesis assumes 4-year construction; Orion targets 2028 grid delivery from a 2024–25 construction start. Below the regulatory-ratcheting band that ARC and GF carry. Consequential only insofar as Polaris-to-Orion physics demonstration is on the critical path of FOAK readiness; recorded for completeness, not as a dominant Stage-2 failure.
- `F2.d-pulsed-power-capacitor/capability-gap` — Helion's commercial capacitor target is $0.50/J vs. current commercial pulsed-power baseline of $5/J — a 10× cost-reduction requirement with no public demonstration. Helion's mitigation is in-house capacitor manufacturing. Capability-class gap in the sense that capacitors at Helion's voltage / energy-density / pulse-rate / lifetime spec do not exist at commercial supply; same shape as TAE's `F2.d-driver-supply/capability-gap` and GF's `F2.d-pneumatic-driver/capability-gap`. Synthesis §1: capacitor cost is "the dominant uncertainty" — at $5/J the plant-wide bank costs ~$2B and LCOE exceeds 500 $/MWh.
- `F2.d-He3-supply/capability-gap` — No commercial He-3 supply exists at fusion fuel-cycle scale. Helion's architecture self-breeds He-3 from D-D side reactions with a 12.3-year tritium-to-He-3 decay lag, but the startup inventory and the breeding plant are both fusion-unique. Closest analogue to ARC's `F2.d-tritium-Li6/no-external-market`, but the He-3 case is structurally worse: no declining-CANDU pool exists at all. Capability-class — no existing supply class to scale.
- `F2.d-He3-separation-plant/capability-gap` — Isotopic separation of He-3 from D-D/D-He3 exhaust at GW-scale fuel throughput has never been demonstrated. Synthesis lists this as a $40M cost-overrides placeholder with no independent validation; the real cost is unknown. Distinct from the He-3 supply subline because this is the plant-internal separation/storage equipment rather than the fuel inventory.
- `F2.d-pulsed-Al-coil/specialty-low-volume` — Room-temperature aluminum coils under 40 T pulsed compression at 10^9-shot lifetime are not a commercial supply class but are not capability-gap-class either; the technology exists in adjacent pulsed-power facilities (Sandia Z, university plasma labs). Same shape as GF's `F2.d-Li-Steam-IHX/specialty-low-volume`.

### Consequential leverages
- `F2.c/Part-30-fit-low-tritium` (unconditional) — D-He3 commercial operation has ~5% neutron fraction (D-D side reactions) and no continuous tritium recirculation; tritium is only stored for the He-3 decay-breeding cycle, not handled through the plasma loop. Fits the NRC Part 30 fusion framework (Feb 2026) cleanly, with a lighter tritium accountancy footprint than ARC or GF. Stronger than ARC's `Part-30-fit` but weaker than TAE's `Part-30-fit-aneutronic-accelerated`. Locked in by fuel cycle rather than by any physics gate. Caveat: if the D-T fallback path triggers, this descriptor downgrades to ARC's plain `Part-30-fit`.
- `F2.d-aluminum-coils/mature-commodity` (unconditional) — Aluminum stock and pulsed-coil winding are commodity / industrial-component supply. Same shape as TAE's `F2.d-magnets/mature-commodity` and GF's `F2.d-Cu-coils/mature-commodity`. No HTS / REBCO supply exposure.
- `E2.a/crossover-defense-pulsed-power` (unconditional) — Helion's pulsed-power capacitor R&D has direct adjacency to defense pulsed-power applications (DOD pulsed-power programs, ICF facility pulsed banks); the gigafactory analogue is the in-house Helion capacitor line whose volume is partially underwritten by non-fusion pulsed-power demand. Weaker than ARC's `crossover-grid-MRI-transport` (defense-pulsed-power is a smaller adjacent market than the grid/MRI/transport coalition behind REBCO) but distinctly stronger than TAE's zero-E-code position and GF's zero-E-code position. Lowers effective FOAK financing cost.
- `F2.d-steam-cycle-eliminated/structural-removal` (unconditional) — Direct electromagnetic energy recovery replaces the entire Rankine steam plant ($127M at 50 MWe in conventional fusion). This is not a leverage on an existing supply chain — it is the *absence* of a supply-chain exposure that ARC, GF, and most candidate concepts carry. Recorded here as a Stage-2 affordability leverage because it removes a major capital item the FOAK would otherwise carry. Methodology friction: this is a "subsystem absent" leverage rather than an "external market favorable" leverage — see note 5.

## Stage 3 — Chasm crossing

### Consequential failure modes
- `F3.a-pulsed-power-capacitor/capability-gap-at-chasm` — Capability gap carries from Stage 2. At chasm-era volumes, only fusion (Helion + MagLIF + Z-pinch concepts) drives demand for high-voltage pulsed capacitors at Helion's spec; the defense-pulsed-power adjacent market is too small to co-scale at GW-fusion deployment. Same shape as TAE's `F3.a-driver-supply/capability-gap-at-chasm` and GF's `F3.a-pneumatic-driver/capability-gap-at-chasm`.
- `F3.a-He3-breeding/fleet-supply-absent` — Fleet-scale He-3 self-breeding has never been demonstrated; the 12.3-year tritium-to-He-3 decay lag creates a fleet-inventory ramp problem (tritium produced today becomes He-3 fuel 12+ years from now). Distinct shape from ARC's `F3.a-tritium/fleet-breeding-required` because the timing lag is intrinsic to the decay physics, not a function of breeding-loop optimization.
- `F3.b/site-build-fraction-low` — Helion's modularization score is the highest in the candidate set (C1=5.0 clamped from 4.71). 20 identical 50 MWe factory-built modules per 1 GWe plant; buildings (~$400M) are the dominant site-built fraction at ~24% of capital. Not consequential as a failure mode; recorded for cross-concept comparison (opposite pole to GF's `site-build-fraction-high`).
- `F3.c/replication-50MWe-feasible` — At 50 MWe per module the unit size is the most replication-friendly in the candidate set; a hyperscaler-PPA template (the Microsoft Orion contract) is the live demonstration of this. Not consequential as a failure mode.

### Consequential leverages
- `F3.a-commodity-fraction-balance-of-plant` (unconditional) — Aluminum coils + buildings + electrical plant + power electronics + cooling are all on commodity / industrial supply chains; the synthesis C3 sub-factor C identifies ~40% of capital in components with >$1B external markets. Same shape as GF's `F3.a-steam-BOP/fleet-pull-dominant` but at smaller capital fraction (40% vs. GF's ~50%); ARC has no analogue at this fraction.
- `F3.d/standardized-amortization-modular` (unconditional within Part-30) — 20-module factory architecture supports template licensing across identical modules; the first Part-30 license amortizes across the module fleet without per-module re-litigation. Cleaner than ARC's gate-conditional `standardized-amortization` (which depends on SPARC clearing licensing) and similar to TAE's `standardized-amortization-cleanest`, though Helion's amortization is locked at the *module* level rather than the *plant* level.
- `E3.b/shared-pulsed-power-cohort` (gate-conditional) — Pulsed-power R&D solves shared sub-problems for Helion + MagLIF (07) + Z-pinch concepts; the cohort is small (~3 concepts) but real. Distinct from GF's pneumatic-driver cohort-of-one. Conditional on the pulsed-power cohort cohering at chasm scale.

## Stage 4 — Learning-curve descent

### Consequential failure modes
- `F4.b/volume-driven-feasible` — 20-module-per-plant architecture provides Wright's-Law-friendly volume numerator (each 1 GWe plant produces 20 module-units of manufacturing learning); deployment counts in the hundreds of plants imply thousands of identical modules. Not consequential as a failure mode; opposite pole to GF's `volume-driven-mismatch`. Recorded for contrast.
- `F4.c-pulsed-power-capacitor/no-external-market` — Fusion-spec pulsed capacitors at Helion's voltage / energy-density / lifetime spec have no dominating external market at fleet scale; defense pulsed-power is too small to drive cost-floor. Cost floor bounded by Helion's in-house manufacturing learning curve plus the small pulsed-power cohort. Distinct from `F4.c-pneumatic-driver/no-external-market` only in technology class.
- `F4.c-He3-fuel-cycle/no-external-market` — He-3 separation and tritium decay-tank technology has no external market driving the cost floor at fleet scale. Same row as ARC's `F4.c-Li6/no-external-market` (D-T cohort-shared) and GF's `F4.c-tritium-extraction-tech/no-external-market`, but a different fuel-cycle path.
- `F4.d/modular-scale-favorable` — 50 MWe / 20-module architecture sits on the favorable side of the modularization-vs-scale crossover (high manufacturing volume, identical units, ship-and-install dominant). Not consequential as a failure mode; recorded for contrast.

### Consequential leverages
- `F4.a/many-knobs-commodity-fraction` (unconditional) — Aluminum, electrical plant, buildings, cooling, power electronics all ride non-fusion learning curves at fleet scale. Same shape as GF's `F4.a/many-knobs-commodity-fraction`; smaller commodity fraction than GF (~40% vs. ~50%) but augmented by the absence of expensive specialty subsystems (REBCO, FLiBe).
- `E4.a/architecture-proof-crossover` (gate-conditional, unconditional-on-success) — If Helion demonstrates commercial direct electromagnetic energy recovery at >90% round-trip efficiency, the architecture-proof is portfolio-valuable for fusion broadly: any future D-He3, D-D, or aneutronic concept that captures charged-particle energy directly benefits from the proof point. Independent of Helion company-level commercial success. Mechanically distinct from a supply-chain crossover (E2.a) — this is an *architecture-proof* crossover. New descriptor not in ARC / TAE / GF; see methodology-friction note 4.
- `E4.b/talent-pulsed-power-from-defense` (unconditional) — Pulsed-power engineering workforce inflow from defense (ICF labs, Sandia, NNSA-adjacent industry) scales operational talent without bespoke fusion-only training. Same shape category as ARC's HTS workforce inflow but a different adjacent industry.

## Cross-stage carriers

Helion carries **three** failure-side cross-stage carriers, which is one more than General Fusion and two more than ARC or TAE:

- **Pulsed-power capacitor cost trajectory** — failure-side carrier across Stages 2, 3, and 4 (`F2.d-pulsed-power-capacitor/capability-gap` → `F3.a-pulsed-power-capacitor/capability-gap-at-chasm` → `F4.c-pulsed-power-capacitor/no-external-market`). Same single-mechanism shape as ARC's D-T fuel cycle and GF's pneumatic-driver carrier. The 10× cost-reduction requirement is the underlying mechanism.
- **He-3 fuel cycle (supply + breeding + separation)** — failure-side carrier across Stages 2, 3, and 4 (`F2.d-He3-supply`, `F2.d-He3-separation-plant` → `F3.a-He3-breeding/fleet-supply-absent` → `F4.c-He3-fuel-cycle/no-external-market`). Structurally analogous to ARC's D-T fuel-cycle carrier, but isolated — see "Fuel ecosystem R&D position" carrier comment under Structural absences.
- **D-He3 ignition physics** — although Stage-1 is descriptive-only, the ignition threshold has a *structural* downstream effect on which failure-mode list applies: if D-He3 fails and the fallback triggers, ARC's entire D-T fuel-cycle carrier (FLiBe, Li-6, Be, tritium-startup, tritium-extraction) joins Helion's record retroactively, and the direct-conversion architectural-proof leverage collapses. This is recorded as a **mode-switching carrier** rather than a Stage-2/3/4 failure, and is the methodologically novel structural feature of this concept — see friction notes 1 and 2.

On the leverage side: **direct-conversion architecture-proof** is a leverage-side cross-stage carrier biting at Stages 2 (capital eliminated) and 4 (architecture-proof crossover); commodity balance-of-plant fraction is a second leverage-side carrier across Stages 3 and 4.

## Structural absences

- **All D-T fuel-cycle sublines absent across F2.d / F3.a / F4.c — in the D-He3 operating mode.** No `F2.d-FLiBe`, `F2.d-Be`, `F2.d-tritium-Li6` (the kg-scale startup tritium for the He-3 breeding lag is a *He-3 fuel-cycle* subline, not a tritium-recirculation D-T subline); no `F3.a-FLiBe`, `F3.a-Li6`, `F3.a-tritium`; no `F4.c-FLiBe`, `F4.c-Li6`, `F4.c-Be`. Concept is outside the D-T cohort that carries 9 fuel-cycle sublines in ARC's record. **Conditional on D-He3 operating mode** — see mode-switching note below.
- **All REBCO / HTS sublines absent across F2.d / F3.a / F4.c.** Helion eliminates superconducting magnets entirely (room-temperature pulsed Al coils). Concept is outside the ~8-concept HTS cohort, same shape as GF's HTS absence. No cryoplant, no quench-protection R&D cohort participation.
- **Entire Rankine steam plant absent.** Recorded as the F2.d leverage `F2.d-steam-cycle-eliminated/structural-removal` — distinct from a "supply chain favorable" leverage because there is no supply chain at all, the subsystem is gone. ARC and GF both carry the steam BOP (GF prominently so, ~50% of capital); Helion does not. Methodology friction: the canonical format has no clean way to record "subsystem absent" as a leverage axis — see note 5.
- **Fuel-ecosystem R&D position: structurally isolated.** Per `concept_part2.md` Cross-stage carriers § E-carriers: D-He3 concepts (08, 19) are structurally isolated from shared fuel-ecosystem R&D. ARC shares D-T tritium-breeding R&D with ~10 other candidate concepts; TAE shares p-B11 / commercial boron supply with the aneutronic cohort. Helion shares its He-3 fuel-cycle problem with no other candidate (concept 19 is the only other D-He3, and may itself drop out of the candidate set). Recorded here because the absence of a leverage carrier is itself a structural feature.
- **Mode-switching: D-T fallback architecture.** If D-He3 ignition fails at 40 T, the architecture converts to pulsed D-T operation at $150–200/MWh LCOE (synthesis §5 line 144). In D-T mode, the entire ARC-style D-T fuel-cycle carrier activates: tritium-startup, Li-6 enrichment, tritium-breeding-blanket, FLiBe (or alternative breeder), Be (or alternative multiplier). The Stage-2 leverage `F2.d-steam-cycle-eliminated/structural-removal` likely persists (direct conversion still operates on D-T fusion products at degraded efficiency), but the per-MWh cost rises 3–4×. **The trace above describes the D-He3 operating mode only.** Recording two parallel per-stage records (one D-He3, one D-T fallback) was rejected as out-of-scope for the methodology-test trace — see friction note 1.

## Methodology friction notes

1. **The architecture-fallback path has no canonical home.** Helion's D-T fallback is decision-relevant (it changes the LCOE band by 3–4× and reactivates 9+ fuel-cycle sublines from ARC's record) but is not a Stage-2/3/4 failure mode — it is a *conditional alternate operating mode*. I placed the fallback annotation in Stage-1 timeline note (briefly) and in Structural absences (the substantive treatment), and chose to describe only the D-He3 operating mode in the per-stage records. The alternatives I considered: (a) two parallel per-stage records — rejected as doubling artifact length; (b) annotating each affected subline with a "D-He3-mode-only" qualifier — rejected as making the cell vocabulary noisier; (c) routing the fallback to F2.d as a "concept-internal alternate path" subline — rejected as conflating mode-switching with supply-chain shape. **Recommendation for methodology evolution (out-of-scope here, flagged only):** consider a top-level "mode-switching" annotation as a sibling to Cross-stage carriers, used when a concept's per-stage record applies *conditionally on an operating-mode choice* rather than uniformly. This shape did not appear in ARC, TAE, or GF.

2. **Joint multi-gate (three independent risks composing jointly) is not first-class in the canonical format.** Helion has three independent gates that must *all* close for the central LCOE story to hold: D-He3 ignition (Stage-1 timeline), capacitor cost trajectory ($0.50/J target — F2.d / F3.a / F4.c sublines), and direct-conversion efficiency (≥90% at commercial scale — implicit in the `F2.d-steam-cycle-eliminated/structural-removal` leverage). The canonical format records each as its own entry, which is correct mechanically — but the *jointness* (the fact that one gate failing is qualitatively different from one gate failing in a single-gate concept) does not surface. The carrier-count partially captures it (Helion has 3 failure-side carriers), but a 3-failure-carrier concept whose carriers are *jointly* required is structurally different from a 3-failure-carrier concept whose carriers are *independently* required. **Recommendation flagged:** a "joint" vs. "independent" qualifier on the carrier annotation would distinguish these.

3. **Capability-gap descriptor is becoming overloaded.** Helion's record has three `capability-gap` sublines at Stage 2 (`pulsed-power-capacitor`, `He3-supply`, `He3-separation-plant`); ARC has zero, TAE has one, GF has one. The descriptor is doing different work in each case: pulsed-power capacitors *exist commercially* at the wrong spec (a cost-trajectory capability gap); He-3 supply *does not exist at all* (a true commodity absence); He-3 separation plant *exists in adjacent industry* (uranium isotope separation) at wrong scale (a scale-up capability gap). Three different structural shapes share one descriptor. **Recommendation flagged:** split into `capability-gap-cost-trajectory`, `capability-gap-commodity-absent`, `capability-gap-scale-up`.

4. **New leverage descriptor invented: `E4.a/architecture-proof-crossover`.** ARC's E-codes are crossover-platform leverages on the supply-chain side; TAE has no E-codes; GF has no E-codes. Helion's strongest unconditional-on-success leverage is that *the architecture itself*, if proven, is portfolio-valuable independent of Helion's company outcome — a future direct-conversion concept can ride the proof. This is mechanically distinct from a supply-chain crossover and required a new descriptor. Recommend `architecture-proof-crossover` as a candidate cross-concept vocabulary entry (would also apply to any IFE concept proving target-factory economics, any aneutronic concept proving sustained ion-electron decoupling, etc.).

5. **"Subsystem eliminated" is a leverage shape the canonical format strains on.** The Rankine steam plant absence is one of Helion's strongest Stage-2 affordability levers — but it isn't a "supply chain pole" observation or an E-code observation; it's "the subsystem is not present." I recorded it as `F2.d-steam-cycle-eliminated/structural-removal` to keep it in-format, but the descriptor name is awkward and the F2.d slot is mechanically about *component supply maturity*, not *component existence*. This may need a separate leverage axis ("subsystem-eliminated leverages") or a fixed convention for routing them through F2.d with a `structural-removal` qualifier.

6. **D-He3 fuel-cycle sublines are new and concept-specific by construction.** I used `F2.d-He3-supply`, `F2.d-He3-separation-plant`, `F3.a-He3-breeding`, `F4.c-He3-fuel-cycle`. These mirror ARC's D-T fuel-cycle sublines structurally (supply / breeding / specialty-input) but the descriptor strings are concept-unique to Helion in the current candidate set — only concept 19 (also D-He3) would share them. This is by construction (D-He3 is genuinely rare in the set) and is decision-relevant rather than a methodology problem, but it does mean the cross-concept map will have rows occupied by only one concept. **Recommendation flagged:** in the map step, the empty-or-singleton-row pattern *is* the structural-isolation signal the methodology already names — no change needed, just confirm in the map that singleton D-He3 rows are read correctly as isolation rather than as missing data.

7. **`F4.b/volume-driven-feasible` and `F4.d/modular-scale-favorable` are "opposite-pole-of-GF" leverage observations, not failure modes.** GF surfaced `F4.b/volume-driven-mismatch` and `F4.d/modular-tax-no-crossover` as new failure descriptors. Helion sits at the opposite pole on both — but recording leverage-pole versions of these as Stage-4 *failure-mode* entries (consequential = no) is awkward; recording them as leverages is also awkward because they aren't actively driving cost reduction, they're absences-of-failure. I left them as not-consequential entries in the failure-mode list. **Recommendation flagged:** the canonical format would benefit from explicit handling of "concept sits at the favorable pole of a sign-neutral factor without that pole actively driving cost reduction" — possibly a third list ("structural-non-risks") sibling to consequential failure modes and consequential leverages.
