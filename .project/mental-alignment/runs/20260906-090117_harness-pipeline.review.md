## Findings

1. The synthesis narrows the owner's motivating question from accelerating physical innovation to finding cheap-fusion corridors. The title, TLDR, and first section make 1 cent/kWh the governing purpose; the wider question appears only as a claim about better investment near the end of section 1. Rebuild the opening around the physical-innovation problem, then introduce fusion and cheap-power corridors as the concrete test case. (cites: design synthesis rule 5, "Opening with no reason to care," and the owner's named `physical-innovation-narrative.md` referent)

2. The body explains a sequence of project machinery, but it does not sustain a single argument about why that machinery creates useful feedback before hardware is built. Sections 2–4 successively describe textual SysML, code generation, TEAx, studies, files, agents, and reviews; the reader must infer how each capability changes an engineering or investment decision. Give each section a specific question the capability makes answerable and state the decision that answer informs. The historical stellarator investigation can carry this thread from missing physics, through model change, to a changed next investment question. (cites: design synthesis rules 5–6; "Structure presented as an inventory"; project-local feedback requiring meaningful section content)

3. The strongest concrete story arrives in section 5, after three abstract process sections. Until then, claims such as "connect evidence," "evaluate candidate designs," and "choose the next task from the evidence" ask the reader to remember abstractions without seeing the investigation they describe. Introduce the stellarator tradeoff near the start and use it as the worked thread through model editing, execution, study, and review; reserve the final section for what the example teaches about the larger ambition. (cites: design synthesis requirement that important things come first and that abstractions be grounded; rules 5 and 7; "Opening with no reason to care")

4. The TLDR assumes knowledge it is expressly required to supply. It introduces `1costingFE`, "corridors," "the harness," "textual SysMLv2," "generated Python," and `TEAx` before explaining any of them, and it never says in plain language what the reader can now decide that they could not decide before. Rewrite the five bullets as a causal story in ordinary language; attach product and tool names only after their role is understandable. (cites: TLDR requirement to use no loaded or undefined terms and give a source-naive reader the whole story; design synthesis rules 1 and 4)

5. Several headings name activities or topics instead of delivering the compressed answer. "Editing a plant description," "Calculating the consequences," and "Choosing the next investigation" could head many unrelated engineering workflows. Rewrite the heading sequence so it states this system's distinctive claims, such as why the model must be editable during an investigation, what executable models reveal before hardware exists, and why evidence rather than a fixed plan chooses the next step. (cites: design synthesis rules 2–3; shared feedback "Heading that names what is present"; project-local feedback that titles must state the finding, mechanism, or number)

6. Section 2 presents the repository split and software-development concerns as an inventory rather than explaining the decisive difference between software and physical systems. The paragraph beginning "The harness supports branching" lists scoped tasks, architecture, generated slop, hallucinations, citations, checks, and review without giving the reader a clean relationship to follow. Center the section on the fact that executable consistency is not physical truth, then show why source evidence, model checks, and independent challenge cover different failure modes. (cites: design synthesis rules 4, 6, and 11; "Structure presented as an inventory")

7. Section 3 is too close to an architecture summary for a synthesis skeleton. The descriptions of dependency order, generated interfaces, maintained Python, preflight version recording, compatibility checks, storage, and written interpretation distribute similar weight across implementation details. Keep only the mechanism needed for the mental model: a design choice propagates through connected equations, produces both performance and constraint verdicts, and can be repeated under a fixed model so differences mean something. Move the remaining implementation detail to the appendix for the render's detail layer. (cites: design synthesis rules 7–8; "same level of detail everywhere" and "same words the HTML will use" under bad synthesis)

8. The repeated "Source and authority" and "Visual cue" paragraphs interrupt the explanation because they are long enough to feel like parallel narrative sections. Provenance and render cues are required, but here the prose repeatedly stops just as a claim lands. Compress provenance to the exact claim-source distinction, and make each visual cue specify the relationship the reader should inspect rather than rehearse the preceding paragraph. (cites: design synthesis requirement to note provenance and name a visual form; rules 1 and 3; shared feedback "Directness comes from selecting the thought")
