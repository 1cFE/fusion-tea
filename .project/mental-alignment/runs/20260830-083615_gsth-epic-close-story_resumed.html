1. What was built: a paper trail that lets a stranger run the investigation

The TEA project already had good specialist tools before this epic: research a source, edit the SysML plant model, regenerate the cost package, sweep it in a study, read the results. What it did not have was anything connecting them. Why one step should follow another — which question is being pursued, what the last result decided, whether the current plan still holds — lived in the builder's head and chat history. If the builder walked away mid-run, the run died.

The epic built the goal layer: for each question the project pursues, one directory holding three plain-text files, plus a shared runbook that a human and an AI agent follow identically. A goal cannot start until it is "grounded" — five field classes present and non-hollow — and every round of work ends with a review by a fresh session that had no part in the work.

Two deliberate design choices, both OWNER rulings (.project/concepts/goal-strategy-task-harness-design-review.md § Resolutions): the files cite the specialist workflows' records rather than copying them, and no automation machinery — event ledgers, retry logic, dispatchers — may be added unless a recorded run failure proves plain files failed. That rule was then measured: across the three proof runs roughly two dozen failures were recorded (ten in Experiment 1, eight in Experiment 2, six in Experiment 3), every one caught by the route's existing defenses — a fresh session, a reviewer, an existing gate or test battery, or the operator reading files. Nothing earned promotion; the epic ships almost no control code. The verdicts are dated amendments on ADR-003.

3. Experiment 1 — strangers can start, resume, and close a goal (Item 4)

The first proof asked: does the paper trail actually work for someone who didn't build it? Thirteen kept cold sessions across twelve fresh agents — none the builder — grounded a real goal (work/orchestration/goals/cryo-volume-basis/) and ran it through a full bounded round. Audit verdict: Certify (.project/completed/20260827_goal-cold-pickup-proof/). Every ordering claim is a git-ancestry check, not a timestamp.

The rehearsal question was itself real modeling substance. The stellarator's magnet coils sit inside a cryogenic cold mass that the cryoplant must hold near 4 K — and the size of that cold mass drives the cryoplant's cost. The model already computes how much electrical current the coils must carry (Ampère's law inside the magnet-cost calculation). Given the current density the superconductor can sustain (insight DI-010), the volume of the winding pack follows from those two numbers. So the goal asked: should the model derive the cold volume (vol_cold_cryo) instead of holding a hand-entered figure? — the operator's question verbatim, restating discovery row 20260823-magnet-technology-ab#2.

4. Experiment 2 — a real model error found, criticized, and fixed: p_pump 1.0 → 195 MW (Item 5 + WI-033)

The second proof ran on a genuine open error, and this is where the epic touched the TEA model itself. The model (models/designs/stellarator_09/stellarator_plant.sysml) describes a whole stellarator power plant: plasma physics feeding a power balance, the power balance feeding costed subsystems, the costs rolling up to LCOE — the levelized cost of the electricity it sells.

One of its inputs, p_pump, is the electrical power for the compressors that push helium coolant through the breeding blanket — the meter-thick shell around the plasma that absorbs the fusion neutrons and turns their energy into ~3.2 GW of usable heat. Helium is a gas — thin stuff. To carry gigawatts of heat you must move enormous mass flow through narrow channels, and compressing gas is expensive: the EU-DEMO engineering literature puts helium pumping near 6 % of thermal power (~150 MW), where water — dense, pumpable as a liquid — does the same job for ~15 MW. The model carried 1.0 MW: a water-like placeholder in a helium-cooled plant, roughly two orders of magnitude low. Both committed A/B studies had been built on it.

How the round ran — the critic bound for the first time

The goal round (p-pump-basis, closed 2026-08-28) wrote its reading and proposed dispositions, and the new pre-execution checkpoint did what it was built for: a fresh non-author critic refused the first version (C-001.r1) with three required changes; the author revised; r2 passed; only then did the dispositions land. That is the OWNER-required checkpoint binding and releasing on a live round for the first time — the thing Item 5 exists to prove. Honest limit, kept honest in every record: the repository itself answered the basis question, so the research seam's request path never ran, and 4 of 9 item criteria were recorded as non-exercised or retired rather than dressed up (audit POSITIVE, .project/completed/20260828_goal-research-model-proof/).

WI-033 then landed the fix through the modeling PM: two EU-DEMO sources registered through the new research seam — zero hand-edited index entries — and p_pump set to 195.0 MW in both model twins, first-order re-derived in the verification record (work/completed/20260828_WI-033_p-pump-rebase/verification_record.md).

Why 195, and why a held number — two owner rulings

  • Why the top of the band OWNER 2026-08-28: 195.0 is 6 % of the baseline computed thermal power (3238.1 MW → 194.3, rounded), deliberately erring against the direction the model had been optimistic. The documented lower bound, ~130 MW, is recorded beside it. Cismondi's ~150 MW is a preliminary figure for one unoptimized layout its own authors expect to fall (a ~9 km piping run they intend to shrink to ~3 km).
  • Why held, not computed OWNER 2026-08-28: a formula computing p_pump as a fraction of p_th would assert that pumping scales linearly across every swept machine geometry — a linearity no source establishes — and would silently retire a lever that two committed studies sweep. So it stays a held, settable input, with the rule "re-derive the scalar when the design point moves" (doc comment, stellarator_plant.sysml:502-523).
Detail: the first-order re-derivation from the registered sources

WI-033's verification record re-derives the band from the registered extractions, not from memory:

  • Cismondi (output.md:174): "In case of helium the pumping power is ~150MW, one order of magnitude higher than in case of water (~15MW)" — plus the 9 km → ~3 km piping lever in the same passage.
  • Moscato: blanket thermal 2101.7 MW across 9 loops (3 inboard + 6 outboard), 2 compressors per loop at 6.8 / 7.5 MW → 3×2×6.8 + 6×2×7.5 = 130.8 MW ≈ 6.2 %. The near-term table (absent from the text extract, re-derived from the registered raw PDF p.7): 16 compressors at 5.9 / 5.2 MW → 83–94 MW ≈ 4 %.

Registration receipts 39bd3b41 / 891b95bc; both raw hashes byte-identical to earlier independent probes of the same URLs. The registration surfaced and fixed a real seam defect on the way (a hard-coded raw.html that broke PDF-URL capture — OWNER-ruled in-session fix + regression test).

5. Experiment 3 — the full loop, and what the fix does to the economics (Item 6)

The final proof drove the corrected model through the entire chain: integrate → study → fresh reading → criticized closure, under successor goal p-pump-fence asking: with p_pump re-based to 195 MW, where does the recirculation fence move, and what happens to LCOE? The physics of the +21 %: the power-balance chain, drawn with the study's numbers

The model's balance (models/library/analyses/mfe_power_balance.sysml:119-147) in words: fusion heat — neutron power multiplied in the blanket, plus alpha and heating power, plus half the pump work, which the model recovers as coolant heat — makes thermal power p_th. The steam plant converts p_th to gross electricity at η ≈ 0.33. Then the plant pays its own bills — coils, pumps, cryogenics, heating — out of that gross, and only the remainder is sold. A 195 MW pump bill therefore hits twice: it inflates p_th, and nine capital cost accounts scale with p_th (more heat means physically bigger blankets, heat exchangers, turbine plant — p-pump-basis learning L-003); and it inflates the recirculating sum, shrinking net power. Cost up, saleable output down — both ends of the LCOE ratio move the wrong way.

The design space: where can this machine live at all?

The study swept 948 grid points of machine geometry — R, the radius of the torus ring (4–20 m), and a, the thickness of the plasma tube (0.8–2.2 m); 906 evaluated. Bigger plasma makes more fusion power, but the ~195 MW pump bill is a held input, nearly size-independent — so small machines spend an outsized share of their output running their own pumps. The recirculation fence (rec_frac ≤ 0.5: no more than half the gross electricity consumed internally) accordingly swallowed the small-machine region.

A model defect, surfaced and routed — not patched

42 points with negative net power cannot be evaluated at all: the CAS10 land-cost term takes √p_net, the square root of a negative fails, and the package crashes before the net-power constraint can report "violated". At 1.0 MW this region lay entirely outside the swept space (the old record shows p_net = +8.3 MW at the worst corner; the same corner is −154.4 MW now). The study pre-screened the 42 out with the package-owned oracle, disclosed the boundary as a result (sharp: smallest evaluated p_net is +0.061 MW), and the OWNER routed the producer defect to backlog item WI-034 — the guard must survive a net-negative point. Nothing was patched in-round.

A fresh administrator (a session that had not run the study) read the record directory alone and reproduced every recomputable headline number exactly; it also found three prose numbers with no committed artifact behind them — repaired by addendum, accepted as learning L-004 ("a record can be arithmetically perfect and still fail its own contract"). The one-paragraph version for a general audience. We built a written procedure that lets anyone — a new person or a fresh AI session — pick up a research question about our fusion cost model, run it through evidence-gathering, model changes, and design-space studies, and hand it off mid-stream, with every claim checked by someone who didn't make it. We proved it three times, on progressively more real work. Along the way it caught and fixed a real error of roughly two orders of magnitude (~130–195× against the sourced band) in the model's coolant pumping power, and the corrected model says the reference stellarator's electricity is about 21 % more expensive and its viable design space about a third smaller than we previously recorded — which is the system working: the process exists to make the numbers trustworthy, and it just made two committed studies' numbers honestly obsolete.

  • The epic's demonstrated TEA-model substance is one parameter correction plus its consequences. That is real and quantified, but most of the epic is process; the story should not imply broad model enhancement.
