1 · What was built: a paper trail that lets a stranger run the investigation

The TEA project already had good specialist tools before this epic: research a source, edit the SysML plant model, regenerate the cost package, sweep it in a study, read the results. What it did not have was anything connecting them. Why one step should follow another — which question is being pursued, what the last result decided, whether the current plan still holds — lived in the builder's head and chat history. If the builder walked away mid-run, the run died.

The epic built the goal layer: for each question the project pursues, one directory of plain-text files plus a shared runbook that a human and an AI agent follow identically.

What the detail layer adds: the grounding gate, measured

A goal cannot start until it is grounded — five field classes present and non-hollow. The runbook (GOAL_RUNBOOK.md § Grounding a goal) is blunt about the consequence: a goal hollow in any class "authorizes no task, not one, not a small one." The five classes:

  1. Grounding evidence — repository paths for what is already known. This is what stops a run from spending a week on a question the repo already answered.
  2. Answer contract ("Answered when") — the condition that ends the goal, concrete enough that two people would agree it was met.
  3. Invariants — what a comparison must preserve, so a later round cannot drift the meaning of "better".
  4. Limits — retry caps, checkpoint-revision caps, round limits.
  5. Reserved gates — the decisions the owner keeps (merge, push, close, model mutations).

The five-class rule was not designed in from the start — it was promoted on measured failure. The shipped rule defended only class 1; Experiment 1's probe (§3 below) showed cold sessions running full tasks on goals missing the other classes, and the owner amended the runbook on that record (amendment note dated 2026-08-27 [OWNER] inside § Grounding a goal).

The two owner rulings that shaped everything

Cite, never copy [OWNER]

Goal files cite the specialist workflows' own records by path@sha. Copying a work item's status or a study's numbers into the trail creates a second copy that will disagree with the first. Source: .project/concepts/goal-strategy-task-harness-design-review.md § Resolutions.

Lean-first / hardening rule [OWNER]

No automation machinery — task envelopes, event ledgers, digests, idempotency keys, retry logic, dispatchers — enters the build unless a recorded run failure proves the plain-files route failed. ADR-003 (.project/adr/003-lean-first-persistence.md).

The lean rule's scoreboard, from ADR-003's dated amendments and the Item 6 evidence: the three proof runs recorded roughly two dozen failures of the prose route — 10 in Experiment 1 (Item 4 close, ADR-003 amendment 2026-08-27), 8 in Experiment 2 (Item 5 close, amendment 2026-08-28), and 6 in Experiment 3 (four route-equivalence findings, the stale-expectation class, the record-contract gap — epic_evidence.md § 4). Every one was caught by a cold session, a fresh reviewer, an existing gate or test battery, or the operator reading files. Nothing was promoted. The epic ships almost no control code; both ADR amendments end with the same verdict: "No mechanism is promoted."

Provenance: the contract is Item 1, closed 2026-08-27 (.project/completed/20260827_goal-harness-contract/).

The unglamorous idea doing the work: a return class is computed from receipts on disk, not from what an agent claims it did.

3 · Experiment 1 — strangers can start, resume, and close a goal (Item 4)

The first proof asked: does the paper trail actually work for someone who didn't build it? Thirteen kept cold sessions across twelve fresh agents — none the builder — ran a real goal end to end (work/orchestration/goals/cryo-volume-basis/; run enumeration in .project/completed/20260827_goal-cold-pickup-proof/freshness-record.md, which closes with a completeness statement: every cold session there was is a row, kept or discarded).

The rehearsal question was real modeling substance. The magnet coils sit in a cryogenic cold mass the cryoplant must hold near 4 K. The model already computes how much electrical current the coils carry (Ampère's law inside the magnet-cost calc gives total kiloamp-meters), and domain insight DI-010 gives the current density the superconductor sustains — so the winding-pack volume should follow from the two. The goal asked: should the model derive that cold volume (vol_cold_cryo, held at 136.56 m³) instead of holding a hand-entered number? The stakes were quantified before the goal opened: DI-010 puts Nb₃Sn current density 4–8× below the REBCO value the held volume assumes, so "holding the REBCO volume flatters LTS" — the held number was potentially flattering an entire magnet technology in the A/B comparison.

4 · Experiment 2 — a real model error found, criticized, and fixed: p_pump 1.0 → 195 MW

The second proof ran on a genuine open error, and this is where the epic touched the TEA model itself. The model (models/designs/stellarator_09/stellarator_plant.sysml) describes a whole stellarator power plant: plasma physics feeding a power balance, the power balance feeding costed subsystems, the costs rolling up to LCOE (levelized cost of electricity).

The physical system, plainly

One of the model's inputs, p_pump, is the electrical power for the compressors that push helium coolant through the breeding blanket — the meter-thick shell around the plasma that absorbs the fusion neutrons — and carry roughly 3.2 GW of heat off to the steam plant. Helium is a gas: thin stuff, low density, little heat carried per cubic meter. To move 3.2 GW of heat you must push an enormous volume of it through kilometers of piping, and that volume flow is what the compressors pay for. The EU-DEMO engineering literature puts the bill near 6% of thermal power (~150 MW); water, dense and heat-hungry, does the same job for ~15 MW. The model carried 1.0 MW — a water-like placeholder in a helium-cooled plant, roughly two orders of magnitude low — and both committed A/B studies had been built on it.

How the number 195 was built

The goal round (p-pump-basis, closed 2026-08-28) traced the basis; WI-033 landed the fix. Two EU-DEMO sources were registered through the new research seam — zero hand-edited index entries — and re-derived to first order in the verification record (work/completed/20260828_WI-033_p-pump-rebase/verification_record.md):

Cismondi et al. (EUROfusion, 2017)

States ~150 MW helium pumping (~6.3% of the 2389 MW deposited) for a preliminary 9-loop layout its own authors expect to fall — larger pipes would shrink ~9 km of loop piping toward ~3 km. Registered receipt commit 39bd3b41.

Moscato et al. (EUROfusion, 2018)

Loop-by-loop compressor data: 9 loops (3 inboard + 6 outboard), 2 compressors each at 6.8 / 7.5 MW → 3×2×6.8 + 6×2×7.5 = 130.8 MW (~6.2% of 2101.7 MW blanket thermal). A near-term 8-loop table (re-derived from the registered raw PDF — the table body was absent from the text extract) gives 16 compressors × 5.9 / 5.2 MW = 83–94 MW (~4%). Receipt commit 891b95bc.

Why 195 stays a held input, not a formula [OWNER]

It would be easy to write p_pump = 0.06 × p_th and call it physics. The owner ruled against it, for a reason worth internalizing: a computed fraction would assert that pumping power scales linearly with thermal power across every swept geometry — a 4 m machine and a 20 m machine alike — and no source establishes that. It would also silently retire p_pump as a lever that two committed studies sweep. So the model holds 195.0 as a settable input with the full derivation in its doc comment, to be re-derived when the design point moves. Choosing traceability over false sophistication is the recorded, knowing choice.

What the model says: the power balance, before and after

The physics of why a 195 MW pump bill moves LCOE by a fifth is the model's power-balance chain (models/library/analyses/mfe_power_balance.sysml:119-147). The plant makes heat, turns a third of it into electricity, then pays its own bills out of that gross — and only the remainder is sold. The pump bill hits this chain twice:

  1. Into the heat. The model recovers half the pump work as coolant heat (eta_p = 0.5), so p_th gains 0.5 × 194 ≈ +97 MW. That sounds helpful — more heat, more gross electricity — but nine capital cost accounts scale with p_th (bigger blankets, heat exchangers, turbine plant to handle the extra heat; learning L-003 of p-pump-basis), so capital rises with it.
  2. Into the bills. The full 195 MW joins the recirculating sum — the electricity the plant consumes to run itself — which more than doubles, from 163 to 358 MW.

Why that is +21% on LCOE. LCOE is roughly annualized cost ÷ electricity sold. The sold side fell 17.8% (915 → 752 MW): scaling that alone is 1/(1−0.178) ≈ +21.6%. The cost side rose slightly (p_th +3% feeding nine capital accounts). Together: 275.3 → 333.1 $/MWh, +21.0% — verified at the baseline point with all six viability verdicts still satisfied there. Cross-pin caveat, carried from the record's own § 12: the study proves 333.07 under the new pin and quotes 275.26 from the old record at its own pin; the license to attribute the difference to p_pump alone lives at the goal layer, in WI-033's audited single-change delta — the record is candid that it cannot license that attribution by itself.

A model defect, surfaced and routed — not patched

42 points have negative net power, and they crash rather than report: the CAS10 land-cost account takes √p_net, which is undefined for a plant that consumes more than it makes, so the package returns execution_failed before the net-power constraint can say "violated". The boundary is razor-sharp in the data: the worst crash is p_net = −154.4 MW at (4.0, 0.80); the smallest surviving point is +0.06 MW at (8.0, 0.85). The exclusion also hands net_positive a clean sheet it did not earn — satisfied at all 906 evaluated points only because every point that would violate it cannot be evaluated at all (learning L-003 of this goal). Disclosed as a result, routed by owner ruling to backlog item WI-034 — not patched in-round.

The one-paragraph version, for a general audience

We built a written procedure that lets anyone — a new person or a fresh AI session — pick up a research question about our fusion cost model, run it through evidence-gathering, model changes, and design-space studies, and hand it off mid-stream, with every claim checked by someone who didn't make it. We proved it three times, on progressively more real work. Along the way it caught and fixed a real error of roughly two orders of magnitude (~130–195× against the sourced band) in the model's coolant pumping power, and the corrected model says the reference stellarator's electricity is about 21% more expensive and its viable design space about a third smaller than we previously recorded — which is the system working: the process exists to make the numbers trustworthy, and it just made two committed studies' numbers honestly obsolete.

Judgment — the synthesis agent's assessment, not system truth

Concerns

  • The research seam's request/return bookkeeper (research_seam.py open→log→close) has never run end to end — both live rounds found the answer without it. Only the registration write door has live evidence (WI-033). The records say this plainly (epic_evidence § 3), but any story claiming "the research seam is proven" overclaims.

  • The epic's demonstrated TEA-model substance is one parameter correction plus its consequences. That is real and quantified, but most of the epic is process; the story should not imply broad model enhancement.
