This fails the density test design_synthesis.md sets at its own top ("Take the sentence away from everything around it. Say what it means in different words. If you cannot, the reader cannot either, and you rewrite it.") and runs against the shared feedback entry "Heading that stacks counts and coined terms," whose governing instruction is "JUST SAY THE THING" (feedback/synthesis.md lines 21–31).

This is the pattern the shared feedback names directly in "Count standing in for the members": "Name the members. If you give only a count, the reader still has to ask which ones you mean" (feedback/synthesis.md lines 49–61). None of the twelve requirements, seven decisions, five phases, or three commits are named or exemplified.

4. Section 4's "ten gates" claim does not add up to ten as written. The sentence "It runs ten gates ... : regenerating in place must move zero bytes, the recaptured snapshot must equal the tracked one, the manifest pin must recompute to its recorded value, the six study preflight gates must pass, the oracle must agree, and the fingerprints must match the lineage the caller named" (line 132) lists five named checks, one of which ("the six study preflight gates") itself bundles six items — so the enumerated items sum to either 6 (if the other five are each one of the ten and the "six preflight gates" nests inside one of those five) or 11 (if all named items are counted separately), and the text does not say which. A reader cannot reconstruct what the ten gates actually are from what is written, which is the same failure the "Count standing in for the members" feedback entry flags (feedback/synthesis.md lines 49–61) and violates rule 3 in design_synthesis.md ("Every heading and every sentence carries distinct meaning").

5. Section 2's capability table (lines 82–87) reads as a wall of implementation rather than skeleton-level abstraction: cells for each repo cram in specific file and module paths — e.g. "PDF extraction (`src/agentic_mbse/extraction/pipeline.py`) ... six-level SysML validation (`validation/runner.py`)"; "cycle check in `elaboration/graph.py`; sealing and fingerprints in `contracts/seal.py`"; "`simkit/core` (executor), `simkit/evaluation` (gates → verdicts), `simkit/study` (runner, store, `teax-study` CLI)". Do not write around it." Four dense cells of module-level detail packed into one table, at the point the explainer first introduces the four repositories to a reader who (per the owner's question) is "not much else" than a hardware engineer aware of 1cFE, is close to that wall rather than a clean grounding.
