## Why this item exists

Availability is a held constant that multiplies every $/MWh and schedules the replacements, and nothing in the design reaches it. `availability = 0.85` is bound in the instance to a 1costingFE reference value (`models/designs/stellarator_09/stellarator_plant.sysml:1117-1118`); the DCF core divides by `8760 · P_net · A` (`models/library/analyses/mfe_lcoe_dcf.sysml:62-63`), the 1cfe-form LCOE divides by the same (`mfe_account_costs.sysml:971-973`), the fuel cost scales by it (`:768`), and the CAS72 chain uses it twice — as the calendar-life divisor `L_cal = L_FPY / A` and as the life cap `N · A` (`exploration/stellarator_e2e/generated/handwritten/mfe_account_costs/levelized_replacement_cost_impl.py:80-93`). So a heavier wall load buys more replacements but never loses a day of production: the committed studies read `availability` as `no_constraint_response` twice (`20260821-power-cycle-ab#1`, re-confirmed at three later pins), and `wall-and-heating` L-011 records that the cost chain charges replacement capital while holding availability regardless of the count (`work/orchestration/goals/wall-and-heating/learnings.md` L-011). Rubric Row 11 scores P1 against a target of P3 ("availability derived from the maintenance/replacement schedule and feeds the economics, pushing back on design choices") and Row 2b P2 against P3 ("lifetime feeds replacement schedule and availability") — two of the twelve outstanding cells (`goal.md` § Question fact 5).

The source supplies the missing anchor. Stellaris §2.11 (`work/orchestration/goals/plant-closure/evidence/grounding_sources/stellaris_p29_maintenance.png`, read at grounding) estimates seven months for the whole in-vessel replacement including the breeder blankets, sets a five-month window and a 90 % overall availability as targets on an approximate 4.5-year cycle with four years between major maintenance, and p. 28 (`stellaris_p28_maintenance.png`) puts about 30 days of cooldown and about 30 days of recommissioning inside that estimate. Table 6 (`stellaris_p21_table6.png`) prints the first-wall structure lifetime ~4–6 FPY at 10.7 DPA/FPY peak and a coil lifetime of ~10 FPY. The research's reading (§ Answer, lines 14–22): one finite calendar should drive damage accumulation, replacement dates, downtime, energy sold and replacement cost; the seven-month estimate does not itself attain the 90 % target; feeding a steady average availability into today's periodic CAS72 puts the first event a whole outage late.

The grounding probe (`work/orchestration/goals/plant-closure/evidence/grounding_probe/summary.md`) predicts what the calendar does at the design point: physical life 4.524 FPY, five replacements at 4.52 / 9.63 / 14.74 / 19.85 / 24.95 yr, availability 0.9028 at zero unplanned downtime (0.8576 at 5 %, 0.8125 at 10 %), six replacements fitting before the horizon at a five-month outage (availability 0.9167) — the integer step that `minor-radius` L-002 found along `a` is live in the outage duration too. LCOE moves from 322.32 to 304.61 on the calendar alone at `u` 0.

#### MR-WI046-2: Physical life is separate from the horizon, and nothing numerical masquerades as material evidence
`physical_life_fpy = fluence_limit / q_n` in the live mode, with no `1e-6` inner floor, no `0.5 FPY` floor and no `N · A` cap. Domains: `N > 0`, `fluence_limit > 0`, `q_n ≥ 0`, `d ≥ 0`, `C ≥ 0`, `0 ≤ u < 1`, `i > −1`; `q_n = 0` means infinite life (no replacement, no planned outage, `availability = 1 − u`); a negative or non-finite input RAISES in the impl and the oracle — fail loudly, never clamp (the WI-037 amended-MR-2 posture). The `i = 0` case is supported explicitly (`CRF = 1 / N`). *Priority:* P0. *Rationale:* the research § Common physical basis, lines 84–88 ("the existing 0.5 FPY floor prevents a numerical gradient blowup; it is not a material property"; the `N · A` cap is circular once availability is computed). *Validation:* unit tests on the impl and the oracle for each domain edge. *Source:* research lines 84–88; `goal.md` § Invariants ("nothing is tuned").

#### MR-WI046-3: The live mode is the deterministic finite-horizon calendar (Option B) with its closed-form check
Starting at commissioning with new in-vessel components already in capital, the impl SHALL advance scheduled-online intervals until the damage life is reached or the horizon ends; over an interval of length `Δt` productive time grows by `b · Δt` and unplanned downtime by `(1 − b) · Δt` with `b = 1 − u`; at a damage limit it records an outage at that date, charges the event, advances by `d`, and resets the bundled clock; nothing ages during an outage. For the one bundled event the `k`-th outage starts at `t_k = k · L / b + (k − 1) · d` — the closed-form check the impl's interval walk and the oracle's independent derivation must both satisfy. *Priority:* P0. *Rationale:* research § Option B, lines 100–110; packet § 8 item 5. *Validation:* the five dates above reproduced by both routes to 1e-12 yr at the design point; the synthetic case `L 4, b 1, d 7/12, N 10` gives outages at 4 and 8 + 7/12, two replacements, `7/6` yr planned downtime, `53/6` FPY, `A = 0.883333`. *Source:* research lines 100–110.

#### MR-WI046-4: Payment at outage start; restart strictly before the horizon; the time balance closes
A replacement is bought only if restart (`t_k + d`) falls strictly before `N`; if the final damage limit is reached too late for restart, production ceases at that date and the remainder is `terminal_downtime_yr`, with no terminal purchase. Each event is discounted at its outage-start date: `replacement_pv = Σ C / (1 + i)^{t_k}`, `cas72_annual = CRF(i, N) · replacement_pv` — the existing dollar and CRF conventions unchanged. The identity `productive_fpy + planned_downtime_yr + unplanned_downtime_yr + terminal_downtime_yr = N` SHALL hold to 1e-12 yr in every case and is asserted in the impl's tests and the oracle's. *Priority:* P0. *Rationale:* research lines 104–106; the equality boundary (`N = 9 + 2/12` with restart exactly at retirement applies the one-replacement policy; just above it the second is bought) is a discontinuity the old `ceil` shortcut cannot verify. *Validation:* the research's three boundary cases (`N` 10 / 8.7 / 9.167) as unit tests; the identity in every test. *Source:* research lines 104–110, § Verification and study matrix rows "First event", "End-of-life equality", "Horizon ends during prospective outage".
