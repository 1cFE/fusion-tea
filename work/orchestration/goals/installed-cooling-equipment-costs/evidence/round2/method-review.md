## Verdict

**Conditional acceptance of conceptual method candidates; HOLD production integration and any complete installed-cost claim.** The evidence now supports more than an acquisition blocker. ANL supplies a usable delivered fabricated-stainless mass method; NETL supplies disaggregated conceptual installation allowances; Seider supplies a gas-compressor purchase analogy worth retaining. None requires vendor qualification merely to enter an explicitly conditional estimate. The missing step is a concrete quantity/scope/price-year design, not proof that every component can be built.

### Circulator analogy: retain, with a narrower claim

The research overstates one restriction. Seider says compressors are widely used at compression ratios greater than two; it does **not** state this as a hard validity bound for Eq. 22.36. Its explicit equation range is consumed power 200–30000 hp. The same page describes centrifugal machines across broad flow and pressure ranges. The target power lies inside the equation range. Thus low ratio alone does not warrant declaring the analogy unusable.

A stainless centrifugal compressor purchased-cost analogue is defensible as an explicit conceptual scenario. It supplies motor-inclusive purchase cost at CE500, not a calibrated high-pressure helium package quote. Constant casing pressure with changing very low pressure rise is a real transfer concern: a power-only correlation tends toward zero price as hydraulic work decreases even though the pressure casing remains. Magnetic bearings, seals, internal cooler, shutoff valve and controls identified by the NGNP source may also fall outside the conventional package. These are cost-structure uncertainties, not demands for a performance map or vendor guarantee.

Accept the unmodified source equation as a clearly named analogue, with those inclusions/omissions exposed. Do not invent a helium correction, claim a conservative bound or assign a statistical uncertainty band without evidence. Stainless-versus-nickel factors are material scenarios, not confidence limits. A source-independent sensitivity can be labeled an assumed sensitivity, but cannot establish how likely or complete the estimate is. Before native integration, decide whether omitted package items have separate prices, a supported allowance, or remain openly unpriced. A more directly relevant PROCESS method can replace this analogue if independently checked.

### Fabricated pipe: a reasonable same-family conceptual transfer

ANL derives its rate from type-304 nuclear components and recommends $310000/metric tonne, 2017 USD, for customer-delivered stainless components, explicitly applying it to fabricated large reactor coolant pipes. Applying the unchanged rate to 316L(N) as a first-order stainless fabrication analogy is reasonable if marked `[AGENT]`; an exact grade-specific premium is not established. Requiring a new vendor rate solely because 304 differs from 316L would be excessive for this stage. Conversely, raw alloy ratios or NETL generic material factors cannot automatically correct an ANL rate dominated by fabrication and nuclear QA.

The purchase quantity still needs explicit pipe/fitting mass. Representative assumed lengths and wall geometry are admissible; a complete stellarator layout is unnecessary. Retain diameter, wall, pressure/material basis and layout sensitivity. Do not multiply the entire source network length by its largest pipe section. Delivery is included, so reconcile existing shipping charges. The ANL field account has a broader equipment/field scope; dividing it by NSSS pipe mass does not establish an installation rate.

### Installation: factors are usable if applied once

Original NETL Table 5 confirms the stated high-temperature/high-pressure gas-process percentages. Their material-plus-labor sum is 1.327 times bare equipment cost. Adding the source's 0.20 exchanger setting allowance gives 2.527 times purchase for that fully inclusive illustrative scope. This is an estimating convention, not measured helium installation labor. The source warns that setting factors perform poorly for very large equipment; its usual chart accuracy bands cannot be claimed for these transferred conditions.

Select individual categories against actual retained accounts. In particular, the 0.60 piping contribution cannot coexist with separately priced process piping covering the same scope; 0.08 buildings and 0.098 instrumentation likewise need ownership decisions. Removing a category means assigning it elsewhere, not declaring it unnecessary. A generic factor cannot stand in for the separately sized piping child required by S3. No compressor setting row is provided: use another justified method, an explicitly identified analogue, or leave that cost unresolved. Do not silently copy the exchanger setting factor. Bare-module factors require stripping overlaps with CAS30/CAS50 before insertion.

### Exchanger and lifecycle boundaries

The updated required-versus-installed area distinction resolves the earlier ambiguity. Modularizing a large exchanger solely to fit the cost equation remains insufficient: declare actual module/header geometry and a hydraulic assumption. A bounded fixed-hydraulics cost sensitivity may be useful without a new qualified hydraulic design, but must not be promoted to a redesigned plant prediction. Source pressure-factor side, tube length and material/technology must match the proposed analogue. The existing conditional purchase diagnostics do not yet meet that test.

Initial-capital methods do not establish equipment life or maintenance. Explicit service-life and replaceable-internals scenarios are acceptable; their intervals and fractions remain assumptions unless sourced. Do not reinterpret vessel design life as whole-exchanger life or depreciation as maintenance evidence.

### Additional scope review: bare-module route and large-exchanger extrapolation

Seider Table 22.11 does supply a **gas compressors and drivers** bare-module factor of 2.15; the separate pumps-and-drivers factor is 3.30. Thus missing NETL compressor setting labor does not eliminate every source-based installed analogy. However Table 22.11 is explicitly for ordinary materials, low-to-moderate pressures and smaller single units. Seider's adjacent discussion warns against multiplying alloy/high-pressure purchase corrections through every installation category: a more expensive alloy does not proportionally enlarge foundations or electrical work. The complete corrected method, rather than `2.15*alloy_purchase`, needs to be selected and documented.

The book explains that bare-module costs include direct field materials/labor and freight/insurance/tax, construction overhead and contractor engineering. This supports an alternative accounting route: retain a source-defined module cost in a distinct subtotal and exempt its covered scope from corresponding downstream charges, or separate its direct and indirect portions using an equipment-applicable documented decomposition. Either can be a declared conceptual accounting convention. Do not import the exchanger example's 8%, 57.1% and 29.6% splits into compressors as if they were compressor-specific evidence. An algebraic subtraction of present CAS fractions without reconciling their bases and scopes does not demonstrate that the source's embedded charges were removed. Intermodule piping, dedicated controls and field infrastructure still need single ownership. A reviewed full-method application could therefore support a conditional installed compressor analogue without inventing a NETL setting row.

The fixed source exchanger's 10310.69 m² equals about 111000 ft², approximately 9.25 times Seider's explicit 12000 ft² upper area limit. That is a materially stronger extrapolation than the descriptive compressor pressure-ratio observation. It does **not** compel physical redesign merely to obtain a price. Possible conceptual scenarios are: retain installed source geometry and explicitly extrapolate a stated purchase law; derive a manufactured-mass estimate; or adopt a coherent modular configuration. The first is mathematically evaluable but its magnitude and error sign are unvalidated, because shell diameter, tube length, support structure, pressure boundary and field assembly need not scale with heat-transfer area alone. Applying a quadratic-in-log equation far outside its data range also changes its effective scale exponent. Retain the result as a separate extrapolation scenario, not a source-valid prediction or calibrated interval.

MIT's water-reactor steam-generator calibration at 5126 m² demonstrates that large nuclear heat exchangers can be estimated conceptually. Its fitted multiplier combines nuclear effects and extrapolation error for that reference. It cannot be adopted as a universal helium uplift, though it can inform an explicitly named alternative architecture analogy. The present evidence permits transparent scenarios and partial estimates; it does not justify averaging unrelated methods or selecting the cheapest result as the estimate. A large-exchanger scenario can remain informative without complete hardware qualification, provided its material/pressure/length and accounting assumptions are concrete and its unvalidated extrapolation is visible in every reported total.
