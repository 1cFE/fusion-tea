## Direct answer

**Yes: a concrete design can be released with separately sized/priced principal machines, a representative pipe/fitting/branch schedule and geometry-based exchangers, while explicit minor accessories or layout qualification remain unresolved.** It also needs appropriate maintenance treatment; pricing spares and replacements alone does not satisfy the complete general S3 wording. Final S3 assessment follows executable integration and evidence. It is not conditional on having supplier-qualified machines, mechanical-code certification, a complete plant layout, calibrated statistical uncertainty or a purchased quotation for every valve.

The exact row-specific S3 anchor is “Pumps, piping, heat exchangers as separately sized subaccounts.” The general test is “Independently sized child components or subaccounts, rolling up through quantity / fabrication / installation / spares / replacement / maintenance logic appropriate to the subsystem.” Broad CAS arithmetic or splitting one inherited lump into named children fails that test. S4 adds a design-based estimate validated against an appropriate reference; that additional requirement must not be silently imposed on S3.

## Minimum closure coverage

1. **Actual independently sized children.** Machine count/flow/head/power, representative pipe/fitting/branch quantities and installed exchanger geometry must drive their own costs. Source-based conceptual analogies, stated construction assumptions and representative layouts are acceptable. Fixed installed modules can be priced by count while showing their conditional required duty/area; no continuous redesign is required.
2. **Appropriate capital scope.** Principal equipment fabrication/procurement and installation need explicit ownership and price basis. Shared services and inherited allowances must have a declared inclusion/exclusion treatment. Prevent duplicated IHX, delivery, installation, procurement, engineering or contingency. Declared ownership assumptions are permissible where source accounts lack perfect granularity; renaming an overlapping aggregate is not reconciliation.
3. **Appropriate lifecycle logic.** State initial spare policy, replaceable versus plant-life components, replacement timing/cost treatment and routine maintenance ownership. Scenario intervals and approximate service allowances can be explicit conceptual assumptions. No separate replacement event is required for each long-lived part; a reasoned no-scheduled-replacement case is valid. Existing O&M may own routine labor under an explicit coverage assumption, with incremental consumables/service work priced or identified. Maintenance cannot simply disappear behind an unexplained zero. Avoid duplicate pump-electricity charges and state whether planned work is coincident with existing outages.
4. **Executable evidence.** Verify dimensions, source arithmetic, machine/count/quantity response, exact account replacement, lifecycle cash flows and native rollup. Preserve current failed physical screens. A source-quality or pressure-rating limitation is separate from software parity and should stay visible. A new source qualification or new physical limit is not required merely to earn this structural/costing score.

## Which omissions can remain

Small unitemized nozzles, supports, local instruments, detailed valve selections, weld counts, final routing and fabrication detail can remain within explicit allowances or declared unpriced residual scope. They do not automatically prevent S3. Their status must survive into outputs and conclusions; “unpriced” does not mean free. An estimate can reach S3 while remaining incomplete in estimate maturity or engineering qualification.

“Minor” needs a reason, not a newly invented numeric threshold. Explain whether an omission is subordinate to an already priced equipment package, plausibly included in an existing service allowance, or unrelated to the principal count/size/lifecycle comparison. If its importance is unknown, say so and qualify economic conclusions; uncertainty alone does not necessarily erase demonstrated structural depth.

An omitted entire branch-distribution network, intermediate circulation train, principal exchanger, substantial drive/package class, or all recurring cooling maintenance is different: it can leave one of the named child accounts or the general lifecycle test materially unrepresented. Those gaps require an explicit modeled scope treatment, a reasonable stated allowance/scenario, or a narrower completion claim. Their absence cannot be cured solely by adding an omissions label.

For this candidate, the previous lack of secondary selection is resolved only as an architectural choice. The HITEC machinery, piping and transfer-interface ownership still need a concrete treatment. Do not demand exact secondary operating qualification, but do not count primary-only research prices as a complete replacement of primary-plus-intermediate C220200. Whether a downstream steam generator belongs to Row 7 or conversion Row 8 is an explicit boundary decision; whichever account owns it must be named, without duplicating the IHX.

## Review disposition

Submit the principal equipment/lifecycle ledger with explicit residual scope, ownership assumptions and validation plan. Release can be granted if it meets the coverage above and the outstanding omissions do not undermine the declared implementation scope. A complete installed-plant-price claim is stronger than R7.S3 and should remain qualified separately.
