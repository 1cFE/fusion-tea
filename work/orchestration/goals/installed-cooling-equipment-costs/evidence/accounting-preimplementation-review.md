## Verdict

**HOLD substantial implementation.** The proposed account ownership is sensible, and the specification identifies the right evidence obligations. No transferable helium equipment price method, complete lifecycle basis, concrete design or executable study plan is available in this review set. Therefore CE-05, CE-06 and the combined CE-10 gate are not satisfied. This is an evidence gap, not a conclusion that admissible methods do not exist. Ongoing NGNP acquisition remains useful.

## Account replacement and omissions

1. **Use one plant-facing coolant total.** The current two-term coolant account enters CAS22 once. Replacing its supported primary term with separately priced children while retaining an explicitly labeled intermediate allowance is a viable partial implementation. Adding full equipment costs to the unchanged aggregate is not. The inherited primary term has no defensible pump/pipe/IHX allocation, so arbitrary fractional deductions do not solve duplication. A partial replacement cannot itself be described as completion of all requested equipment scope.
2. **Define the primary/intermediate interface physically.** Assign the primary-to-secondary IHX to exactly one account. Identify secondary pumps, pipes, salt inventory, storage vessels and downstream transfer equipment as included, retained allowance, or omitted. A helium-to-helium NGNP transfer loop and helium-to-HITEC source exchanger do not have identical equipment boundaries. Neither a complete secondary loop nor its price can be inferred from primary IHX duty.
3. **Installed-package boundaries must reconcile the wider plant.** C220111's 14% installation term excludes coolant, as the map states. However CAS29 contingency, CAS30 indirects and CAS50 shipping/tax/insurance still respond to its direct cost. A quote already including these items needs a declared normalization or deduction before entering the direct account. Existing thermal-scaled buildings explicitly cover heat-exchanger/service-water buildings. Dedicated drives, controls, wiring, foundations and service cooling require reconciliation with electrical/I&C/building/auxiliary allowances; retaining those aggregate accounts does not prove disjointness.
4. **Inventory and maintenance remain missing.** Existing coolant fill is PbLi, not primary helium or secondary HITEC. CAS50 spares exclude CAS22, and CAS72 replaces only blanket/first-wall and divertor. Neither creates cooling spares, replenishment, seals/bearings work, tube inspection or scheduled machine replacement. Generic staffing-based O&M may include activities, but its equation supplies no allocation. Explicit exclusions may be justified for a conceptual estimate; declaring everything covered by generic O&M without evidence is insufficient.
5. **Preserve physical and financial boundaries.** Pump electricity already reduces net power; charging its electricity again as a separate operating expense duplicates that consequence. Initial spares belong to initial capital only once; replacements and maintenance belong to dated or consistently levelized operating costs. Do not fold operating costs into overnight capital and also annualize them. Cooling outages need a stated relation to the existing in-vessel outage schedule, including coincident work, before changing availability. Installed estimates including financing must remove that financing before the existing DCF construction-interest calculation.

## Verification adequacy and required concrete plan

The specification correctly separates source applicability from software parity and requires unchanged-input comparisons before design variations. The current material is a verification outline, not yet a concrete test/study contract.

The entering batch reports 131 passes and six failures. Four oracle tests fail at output-key equality before reaching their heat-accounting assertions. The native comparison fails at channel-count equality before numerical parity checks. The adapter coverage test also fails on changed mappings. These are observed schema/coverage failures; the stopped assertions do **not** certify the downstream heat/parity checks. Repair or replace the affected coverage with reviewed current mappings, preserving frozen historical records and independent expected calculations. An exclusion list must name channels and reasons; broad tolerance or dropping equality assertions would hide regressions.

Before execution, specify the following bounded evidence:

- Independent per-machine flow/head/power, geometry-derived area, actual terminal approaches, pipe dimensions/mass and source-range checks, with invalid-domain behavior.
- An unchanged-input cost delta identity: new coolant total minus replaced allowance, followed through CAS22, contingency, indirects, supplementary costs and LCOE. Verify both no duplicated children and no disappearance of retained scope.
- Dedicated lifecycle examples, including no replacement within life, one replacement, and coincident versus additional outage assumptions if outages are modeled. Verify price-year/escalation conventions separately from financing.
- Current default/reference, selected 18-loop and matched 14-loop controls, retaining r2 failures as diagnostics. Define which source/design inputs are held for the circuit-count comparison; pipe routing and machine sizes must have explicit count-response rules.
- A small demand perturbation and supported layout/price sensitivities. Separate capital changes from changes in pumping, recovered heat, net power and denominator. No global optimum or feasible-plant claim follows from this bounded set.

## Evidence that would release implementation

For each equipment class, obtain checked original numerical prices or a usable estimating method; currency and actual price year; operating/material/construction/size domain; quantity basis and scaling rule; procurement/fabrication/installation inclusions; exclusions; and uncertainty. A report's publication year is not automatically its price year. An aggregate NGNP loop price without equipment decomposition cannot establish separately priced children.

Then produce a concrete design linking current calculated quantities to those methods. For circulators, state topology, inlet volumetric flow, head, shaft/drive power and package coverage. For exchangers, justify transfer across fluid pair, pressure boundary, alloy and technology and retain the effective-UF caveat from the sizing review. For piping, supply a routed-length assumption and diameter/wall/fittings/support/installation basis covering the actual large diameters. Add a justified treatment of spares, replacement and incremental maintenance, or explicit scope exclusions with a resulting incomplete-target verdict.

Finally provide the replacement/overlap reconciliation and bounded study/test contract above for independent review. The currently registered low-temperature INL installed-estimating workflow explains how to build an estimate; it does not provide this helium price basis. Original acquisition alone will not release the gate unless applicability and accounting scope survive review.
