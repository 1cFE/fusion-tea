## Question

> Once the magnet chain computes the conductor peak field, the stored magnetic energy and the coil casing mass from coil geometry — the sourced shapes, anchored at the design point — does the minor radius stop being a free ride, and where do the feasible region and the cheapest feasible machine sit, at what aspect ratio, once it does?

`[AGENT] — proposed; the form ratified by the owner (§ Reserved gates 1).`

**What the model does today** `[INHERITED]`: the minor radius `a` is a settable input swept independently of `R` over 1.3–2.2 m in every committed study. The radial build computes the coil bore from it (`r_coil = vessel_or = a + 1.70 m` of fixed layers; 3.00 m at the design point — `models/library/analyses/mfe_plasma_scaling.sysml` 'MFE Radial Build'; instance `stellarator_plant.sysml` the `R0` binding's note), but no magnet channel consumes it in the executed package: the on-axis field is `mu0 k_link n_coils I_coil / (2π R0)` ('Coil Set Axis Field'), the peak field is the axis field times a **held** ratio 2.7667 calibrated to the printed pair 24.9 T / 9.0 T ('Conductor Peak Field'), the winding length is `k_coil × R0` (WI-036 D3), the casing mass is a held per-coil floor of 63 t (WI-035 D5, the seam named), and the magnet capital, cold volume and cryo power follow from those. At the WI-043 pin the probe read **ten channels bit-constant in `a`** on every column (`evidence/grounding_probe/summary.md` Probe A: `heat_coupled`, `B_axis`, `B_peak`, `sigma_wp`, `eps_cond`, `vol_cold`, `p_cryo`, cryo cost, magnet capital, heating capital), while the plasma volume rises 2.86×, fusion power 1.65×, and LCOE falls to 0.72× from a 1.3 to 2.2 on the cheapest machine's column. Every committed optimum sits at the window's top (`a` 2.2) at both heating levels; the ten-verdict cheapest machine `c2823` (202.19 $/MWh; R 15.7, a 2.2, 13 MA, 13 keV, n 1.0×) has aspect ratio 7.1 against the source's 9.8.

**The facts that decide the form** `[AGENT]`, each verified this session (`evidence/grounding_sources/report.md`, page renders included; `evidence/grounding_probe/`):

1. **The sister systems codes do not treat the minor radius as a free lever.** Stellarator-PROCESS *can* scale "the minor plasma radius a at constant coil radius" (Lion 2021 `output.md` L118) but freezes coil number and shape (L120), and every published run holds the aspect ratio as a fixed input and iterates R, B, n, T and the winding pack: Lion 2021 Table 2 (page render `grounding_sources/lion2021_p16_full.png`) marks "Aspect ratio" *fixed input parameter* and "Major plasma radius" *iteration parameter*, the minor radius derived; the thesis's two Helias 5 optima (Table 4.3 render) both sit at R/a = 12.3. The Stellaris paper's Appendix C says a lower aspect ratio is a *different* configuration at the same minor radius (report § Q3). No run in the corpus sweeps `a` at fixed `R`.
2. **The sourced bound on the minor radius is the coil-to-plasma distance, and in this model's geometry it is satisfied by construction.** Eq. 58–59 (Lion 2021; images `pdf-0013-06.png`, `pdf-0013-09.png`): the coil–plasma distance scales with R, shrinks as `a` grows inside a fixed coil set (`f_geo`, unprinted), and must exceed half a coil plus vessel, shield, blanket, first wall, SOL and a gap (1.15 m fixed stack + 15 cm SOL in the 2021 runs; Stellaris: minimum coil–plasma distance 1.37 m to the centre filament, averaged radial build 1.50 m — Table 3 and Fig. 34 renders). The model builds the coil bore *outward* from `a`, so the stack always fits and nothing bounds `a` that way. What the model must then carry is the consequence the codes carry: a bigger coil.
3. **The sources give three shapes through which a bigger coil bore costs something, and the design point anchors each.** (i) Peak field on the conductor, Lion 2021 eq. 39 (image `pdf-0009-19.png`): `B_max = μ0 I N / (R − a_coil) × (a0(C) + R a1(C)/√A_wp)` — the field on the coil rises as the bore approaches the major radius; the thesis states `B_max/B_axis` "scales with ~1/A at fixed minor radius" (report § Q4 side fact). Anchor: 24.9 T at 9.0 T axis (Table 2 render `stellaris_p03_table2.png`). (ii) Coil-set inductance, thesis eq. 2.82 (image `pdf-0074-12.png`): `L = L(C) (a_coil/â_coil)² (R̂/R)`, so `W_mag = ½ L I²` grows with the bore squared at fixed current and R. Anchor: **stored magnetic energy 111 GJ** at 15.4 MA (Table 2 render). (iii) Coil structure mass, Lion 2021 eq. 56 (image `pdf-0012-18.png`): `M_struct = 1.348 W_mag^0.78`, "an empirical scaling law from existing machines". Anchor: the casing floor the model already carries (63 t per coil, WI-035 D5 — a knowing lower bound; the anchor keeps the seam and the scale responds). The configuration coefficients `a0(C)`, `a1(C)`, `L(C)` are not printed for Stellaris: each form is anchored at the design point and scaled by the sourced shape — the WI-041 wall-peak calibration's pattern.
4. **How hard the three shapes push is not known until the study runs, and the two probes bracket it.** On the design column (R 12.7) the eq.-39 factor `R/(R − a_coil)` rises about 10 % from a 1.3 to 2.2 (bore 3.15 → 4.05 m at the coil centre), enough to break the conductor ceiling from a ≈ 1.3 upward with the baseline already at equality (24.9 against 24.9); at the cheapest machine's R 15.7 it rises about 1 %, the larger R absorbing the bore. Stored energy rises about 1.65× and the casing mass about 1.48× over the same bore change at fixed current and R. The earlier linear magnet shadow (`magnet_capital_1cfe_form`, +30 % over the window) flattened the `a`-trend without moving the optimum interior (`20260904-wall-and-heating` Addendum item 6). Whether a 2.2 still wins is a measurement, not a premise.
5. **Where the answer sits today at the source's aspect ratio.** Among the committed ten-verdict feasible points with `a ≤ R/9.8 + 0.1`, the cheapest at 100 MW is `c1661` at 283.61 $/MWh (R 14.2, a 1.5, 16 MA, 17 keV, n 0.8×); 47 of 307 feasible 100 MW points and 158 of 419 at 220 MW sit there (`20260907-burn-control/results/points.csv`, this session's count). The gap from the headline is about 80 $/MWh and rests on an unsourced lever.
6. **The transport facts have no sourced aspect-ratio dependence, so the carried research request closes as a bounded negative.** τ*/τ_E is an assumption (8 in Stellaris; 8 vs 4 for one configuration in the thesis; W7-X bounds "very weak, 2 to 80"); helium suppression is a heuristic ½ tied to drift-orbit displacement, never formulated in `a`; ι is a configuration input (HSR4/18 and HSR5/22 near-identical at different A); ISS04 rewritten in `a` and `A` carries an explicit A exponent of 0.08; the τ_GB · f(A, ι) correction "was not yet retrieved from simulations or data" (report § Q4). The item states the transport facts as point-A facts carried, and the round's discovery rows say so.
7. **The wall-peak calibration's re-anchoring, WI-044's other half, has no sourced rule either** (report § Q2: the first wall sits 30 cm from the LCFS in every HELIAS case; nothing gives the peaking factor's dependence on `a`). The calibration stays anchored at the design geometry and carried constant, with its sensitivity shadow (`wall-and-heating` L-010: 257 / 142 driven survivors at 1.15× / 1.83×) reported beside every geometry claim, not re-anchored in this goal.

**Two forms were put to the owner; option 2 was chosen.** Option 1 (hold the aspect ratio as a configuration fact, derive `a = R/A`) is the sister codes' practice but changes only which knob a study turns; the owner prefers the underlying model freely settable `[OWNER-VERBATIM 2026-09-07]` "I would much prefer the underlying model to be freely set so we would be able to vary this in a study", and option 1 adds no push-back. Option 2 keeps `a` free and adds the three computed relationships of fact 3, so the fences and the cost push back on the bore; the aspect ratio is reported as a derived quantity, never held.
