## Findings

1. **Violated: primary and secondary machine purchase prices describe demand-matched equipment.** Operating shaft duty, suction pressure and salt flow feed equipment prices, spare prices and future replacement prices directly. There is no supplied machine rating kept fixed when demand changes. The independent primary flow ceiling does not repair that cost binding.
2. **Compliant limited relationship: exchanger required area is compared with installed area.** The exchanger is never enlarged to pass. However, tube count, diameter, length and shell bore are fixed implementation constants, so a supplied alternative exchanger cannot be evaluated. Whether that restricted scenario satisfies the intended interface remains unverified. Changing area alone would leave mass, inventory, source-normalized U and facility envelopes inconsistent.
3. **Compliant as operating closures, unverified as equipment: matched steam and cooling water.** Computed flow, shaft duty and required UA are identified as operating quantities; installed steam-generator capacity and turbine qualification stay false. No turbine, steam-generator or cooling-water capacity model is present. The demand-scaled cost accounts must disclose that absence.
4. **Cryogenic power is a load/COP calculation, not installed capacity.** Its demand-scaled capital proxy has no installed refrigeration capacity evaluation. This can remain an explicitly limited proxy, but it cannot stand in for costing a supplied cryoplant.

## Diagnostics and downstream preservation

E exposes separate IHX capacity, salt straight-head and transition-flow screens; pump/motor empirical range checks; temperature-interface and source-scale checks. `pressure_qualified`, `helium_price_transfer_validated`, `salt_pump_transfer_validated`, and `inventory_complete` remain false. Preserve these meanings. A pump-cost range failure is neither proof of physical inability nor permission to extrapolate silently as validated equipment. Code presently returns extrapolated prices alongside false range flags; that distinction must survive repair and reporting.

All E price subdivisions (vendor, installation, design, spare, pipe/HX purchases, fluid inventory, event purchase/installation/removal), totals, annual replacements and makeup are derived accounts. They have no independent design choice beyond their quantity/price inputs. The confirmed machine defect propagates to these accounts. Aggregate shipping exclusion also depends on the primary package price. HX/pipe bills do not resize with demand at fixed geometry/count; fluid inventory has state dependence as described above.

The generated instance interface exposes circuit count, reference hydraulic facts, limited geometry, efficiencies, lifetime and price policies, steam/water operating states and cryogenic load/COP facts. It does not expose installed helium/salt machine ratings, steam-generator installed UA, turbine nameplate or cold/intercept refrigeration capacities. E's standalone generated calculation inputs are not evidence these absent instance choices are available: the pipeline binds its power and flow inputs to upstream calculated channels.

`exploration/stellarator_e2e/run_stellaris_single.py:351` records primary-loop outputs including capacity margin. `run_stellaris.py:61,67` consumes cryogenic draw and turbine cost. `verify_stellaris.py:1182,1200,1227` independently reproduces primary demand and sends it into the cooling oracle; `oracle_cooling.py` is therefore an affected numerical consumer if machine design price inputs change. `verify_stellaris.py:1369` forwards cooling machine counts into facilities. ## Concrete unresolved choices before implementation

[AGENT] The first architecture decision is the admitted equipment contract: fixed per-circuit topology with supplied design-point machine ratings, or independently chosen machine count/topology. The existing equations support evaluating required duty and pricing a declared price point; they do not supply compressor or pump curves, speed control, turndown, shutoff head or parallel-machine performance. A constant-efficiency envelope check can be proposed, but is a new approximation requiring explicit scientific disposition. A rating multiplier would preserve the hidden selection defect.

[AGENT] The second decision is the intended freedom of loop analysis. Heat plus temperature rise currently solves flow. Heat plus flow could solve temperature rise. Current helium constant-density loss transfer is admitted only near a source pressure/temperature window, and salt chemistry/properties are hardcoded to 270/465°C. Supporting additional directions must state what temperature/property/loss validity is supported; reversing arithmetic alone does not establish it.

[AGENT] The third decision is exchanger/pipe generality. The existing fixed geometry preserves failure, but cannot evaluate arbitrary supplied exchangers or pipes. A minimal explicit fixed-geometry scenario contract can preserve that limit. A general geometry interface needs independent U calibration and a stated treatment of altered hydraulics, shell void and helium volume (currently fixed 61.5m3 per exchanger), not merely an exposed installed-area scalar.

[AGENT] Cryogenic, turbine and heat-rejection accounts can honestly remain limited demand-matched proxies under MR-7, with absent installed-capacity evaluation declared. If the intended scope requires supplied equipment evaluation for those components now, additional capacity/performance and cost relationships are missing. Do not invent them to make every table row appear complete.

## Rated operating point versus nameplate: focused design finding

[AGENT] A declared design operating point can be costed with the existing correlations without changing their algebra or relabeling their arguments. For helium that point must contain pumping shaft duty and suction pressure, with the former retaining the 50hp pumping-duty normalization. A motor nameplate power is not interchangeable: the executable explicitly rejects that interpretation in its comment at E:50. For salt the selected point comprises pump volumetric flow, head and shaft duty, and the motor cost uses electrical hp under the declared efficiency convention. A separate selected design-point record can be held fixed and priced independently of current operating demand. This is routine separation of arguments and consumers **conditional on retaining the existing unvalidated cost-transfer meaning**; it does not newly validate the correlations for the target machines. No original source was reopened in this bounded inventory, so stronger source validation is unverified.

[AGENT] What remains scientifically missing is the inference from that chosen point to a feasible operating envelope. Rated shaft duty greater than required shaft duty is only a power-limit screen. Simultaneously comparing flow and head to design-point values assumes a rectangular performance envelope that the present implementation does not establish. A real pump curve can fail at a requested flow/head combination even when each scalar is below a rating. Helium compression additionally depends on suction state, pressure ratio and machine map. Thus the proposed design can safely separate and price an explicit declared point now, and report exact scalar margins as diagnostics, but cannot call those comparisons sufficient equipment adequacy without an explicitly adopted performance approximation or new supporting data. Restricting evaluation to the selected design point would avoid an off-design map claim but would not meet the intended demand-varying fixed-hardware tests as a general equipment model.
