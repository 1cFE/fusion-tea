## 1. The starting point, in plain terms

The plant is the WI-093 C-1 assembly: the Stellaris helium primary loop delivers 3,301 MW at 773 K (3,126 MW of blanket heat plus 175 MW of loop pump friction) through a 50,000 m² exchanger with 50 MW/K of conductance into the ARIES closed helium Brayton chain (three compressor stages with intercooling, a recuperator of 0.8 effectiveness, one turbine). Its design point is a cycle flow of 2,500 kg/s and an equal stage pressure ratio of 1.518 (overall 3.5). At that point the cycle removes all the heat, the turbine sees 677.5 K, the compressors take 2,451 MW of the turbine's work, the generator makes 666.9 MW gross, and after the loop pumps (175.3 MW) and the ARIES auxiliary register (65 MW electric: 40 heating wall-plug, 10 cryogenic, 5 fuel, 5 control, 5 other) the plant nets 426.6 MW. This is the C-1 assembly's own design point; the 423.1 MW ARIES integrated nominal of earlier goals is a different configuration (the ARIES plant on its own loops) and is not this case.

Costed on the re-selected inventory I-R (the WI-093 case ratings: compressor 3,200 MW, turbine 7,000, generator 3,600, heat rejection 5,000, helium duty 3,500, all extrapolated beyond 1.5× their ARIES reference), the overnight capital is 4,873 MUSD2004. The conditional cost of electricity has two parts worth keeping apart: the nonfuel part, 133.1 USD2004/MWh (capital 103.5, O&M 22.0, replacement, overhaul, consumables, terminal and salvage 7.5), and the fuel part, 1426.3 USD/MWh of purchased tritium under the no-breeding-credit convention at an assumed 30 MUSD/kg; the total is 1559.4. The nonfuel figure is conditional on an ARIES-scope rest-of-plant constant (2,158 MUSD, `[ASSUMED]`); the fuel figure is a convention. Paired deltas at equal fuel are the comparison (`evidence/comparison-contract.md` § 6).

## 2. What improves net electricity and the cost of electricity

Lowering the stage ratio at fixed flow raises net electricity until the heat-removal check fails; raising the flow lowers the ratio at which that happens. On the 8 × 16 grid (2,000–4,000 kg/s, ratio 1.20–1.80, refined near the boundary), 52 of the 100 stored I-R points satisfy every check. Operating choices alone, with the existing equipment, raise net electricity by about 171 MW (+40%) and the annual energy from 3.18 to 4.45 TWh. The nonfuel cost of electricity falls by 38 USD/MWh (133 → 95, −29%), because every nonfuel cost is a constant of the sweep spread over more electricity. The tritium term falls by 408 USD/MWh for the same reason and dominates the total change only because the assumed tritium price dominates the total; the ranking of operating points does not depend on it. The two top points are 2.9 MW apart, below the contract's 5 MW materiality, so they are one band.

## 3. What stops the improvement

**Below the passing band, the thermal limit is heat removal at the exchanger.** As the ratio falls, the compressor exit cools less and the recuperator returns more heat, so the helium arriving at the heater is hotter (453 K at 2,250 / 1.5183, 473 K at the electricity-only best, 423 K at the design point). The 773 K source can only deliver what 50 MW/K of conductance carries across that temperature difference; once the heater inlet is too hot, source heat goes unremoved (7.8 MW at 2,500 / 1.425, 46 MW at 2,250 / 1.50, 52 MW at 2,750 / 1.35) and the case fails. Where the boundary lies inside the grid (2,250–3,000 kg/s) the ridge of net sits one grid step on the failing side (figure `results/figures/flow-ratio-map.svg`): that is why the best passing point differs from the electricity-only best, by 22 MW at the design flow.

**Above the band, the equipment limit is the compressor rating**, then net itself. On I-R the 3,200 MW compressor is exceeded at 2,250 / 1.80, 2,500 / 1.80, 2,750 / 1.70 and 3,000 / 1.60; from 3,250 kg/s upward the net goes nonpositive before any rating binds (27 such points per inventory refused by the scan, 54 in all, plus the Brayton precooler guard at 4,000 / 1.80 on each inventory, 2). Higher flow lowers the turbine inlet (735.8 K at 2,250, 708.0 K at 2,750 at their best ratios) and the compressor work rises with the flow, so the band narrows and its best point falls beyond 3,000 kg/s.

**Exchanger capacity limits further improvement.** With the existing 50,000 m² exchanger the passing net cannot exceed the band above; with a 75,000 m² exchanger (priced 87.5 MUSD through the same purchase law, +29 MUSD, not extrapolated) the boundary at 2,500 kg/s moves from ratio 1.45 to 1.40 and the passing net rises to 671.6 MW, 74 MW above the band. Its price adds 0.95 USD/MWh to the capital contribution at unchanged net; at its new passing point the nonfuel cost is 85.1 and the total 991.1 USD/MWh.

**The ARIES-selected inventory I-A supports none of it.** At the same 100 points its 1,500 MW helium-duty package is 1,801 MW short of the constant 3,301 MW duty, the 1,600 MW compressor is exceeded at 79 points and the 2,500 MW heat rejection at 52; I-A prices the best band 8 USD/MWh lower (booked purchases 461 against 812 MUSD) because its ratings are too small for the operation. Hardware is selected before the sweep and priced from what was selected (MR-7); no point was resized.

## 4. The helium return condition

The loop definition holds the blanket inlet at 573.15 K (Moscato's 300 → 500 °C helium window, `models/designs/stellarator_09/stellarator_plant.sysml:1257-1260`) and states that the exchanger must return the helium to the circulator at the compressor inlet temperature `T_comp_in`, 561.9 K here, "not evidence that it can". The assembly never compares the exchanger's computed return with that requirement: the condition is missing as a check, not deliberately waived. Its residual, `T_comp_in` minus the stored return, equals the stored helium hot-bound margin wherever all heat is removed (163 of 163 cases), so every case is checked from stored channels (`evidence/return-condition-check.md`, last column of the table above). A positive residual means the exchanger has more capability than the point uses: an uncontrolled loop would settle its blanket inlet that far below 300 °C; a controlled one would bypass a share of the primary flow around the exchanger to lift the return (18.8 % at the starting point, 0.3 % at 2,250 / 1.5183, 3.9 % at 2,750 / 1.375, 6.0 % at 2,500 / 1.45, 1.4 % at the S6 point), which changes no cycle-side heat or temperature to first order but is not in the model. The exactly consistent operating points are the boundary points themselves, where the residual goes to zero; the best band is the most consistent of the leading cases and the starting point the least. The condition tightens the claim without reordering it; a return-condition screen with an owner-declared tolerance, or an explicit bypass fraction, is the missing piece (§ 7).

## 6. How flow and ratio should be chosen together

Choose the ratio at each flow just above the heat-removal boundary, and choose the flow where that boundary point has the most net: on this assembly that is 2,250–2,750 kg/s at ratios 1.375–1.518, about 595 MW, a 40 % gain over the design point from operating choices alone. The best band sits on the boundary, so a robust choice at fixed machine efficiencies is one step inside it: 2,750 / 1.375 keeps an 8.6 K helium margin against 0.7 K at 2,250 / 1.5183, and 2,500 / 1.45 (575.6 MW) passes every S1 and S2 level; the return condition of § 4 favours the boundary side of that trade. Rankings between flows far from 2,500 kg/s are model exploration at fixed efficiency (contract § 8 a). Further improvement beyond the band is limited by exchanger capacity: a 50 % larger exchanger moves the boundary down and adds 74 MW for 0.95 USD/MWh of added capital contribution; the compressor rating is the next limit.

## 7. What this answer does not claim

Fixed isentropic efficiencies at every flow and ratio (no off-design map; S1 bounds the first-order effect); the ARIES auxiliary register on a Stellaris loop (S2 bounds it, including the 13 MW of heat-rejection pumping the balance omits); the Stellaris pump relation as a hydraulic model (S3); an ARIES-scope rest-of-plant (S4); unsupported breeding (S5); the blanket-inlet control that would absorb the return residual of § 4 (a bypass fraction, not modeled). No point is qualified as a design by passing the checks. Offered, not opened: a return-condition screen or bypass model; the exchanger area as a chosen design variable with its own screen; off-design machine maps.
