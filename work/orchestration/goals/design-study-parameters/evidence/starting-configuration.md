## 1. What the starting design is, in plain terms

The starting point is the WI-093 case `c1-aries-ratios-reselected-ratings`: the Stellaris helium primary loop feeding the ARIES helium Brayton power cycle, with the cycle's five pieces of equipment re-selected large enough to pass their screens. It is one assembly (`combinations_loop_brayton`, root part `loop_brayton`) built only from existing definitions.

**Heat source.** A supplied blanket heat of 3,125.93 MW (`blanket_source.q_source`, the Stellaris baseline's `blanket__source_heat__q_source`, which the Stellaris plant computes from its 2,652.6 MW fusion power). No plasma, blanket, magnet or fuel component is assembled; the heat is a fixed input.

**Primary loop** ('Primary Coolant Loop' at the Stellaris circuit values): helium at 8 MPa enters the blanket at 573.15 K and leaves 200 K hotter at 773.15 K (500 °C); 14 loops, each rated for 225.08 kg/s. The loop's flow is calculated from the duty and the rise (3,009.76 kg/s total, 214.98 kg/s per loop, margin 10.10 kg/s under the rated ceiling), the audited operating-point direction inherited from Stellaris and disclosed. Its circulators draw 175.28 MW of electricity and return all of it to the helium as friction heat, so the loop delivers 3,301.21 MW to the cycle (`primary_loop.q_ihx`).

**Power cycle** (the ARIES definitions): 2,500 kg/s of cycle helium (`cycle.selected_flow`, an analyst re-selection recorded in the WI-093 design) at a return pressure of 4.2857 (the ARIES pressure unit, giving 15.0 after compression) compressed in three stages of ratio 1.5183 at 0.89 isentropic efficiency with intercooling back to 308.15 K, a 4.5 % pressure loss, one helium-to-helium exchanger of 50,000 m² at 1,000 W/m²K (UA 50 MW/K) in the series closure with two idle stages, a turbine at 0.93 efficiency expanding to the return pressure, a recuperator of effectiveness 0.8, and a precooler back to 308.15 K.

**Electrical balance** ('Plant Electrical Balance' with the ARIES auxiliary register): generator 0.98, motor 0.95, auxiliary heating 20 MW delivered at 0.5 wall-plug efficiency (40 MW electric), cryogenics 10 MW, fuel plant 5 MW base (the exhaust-dependent term is 0 because no fuel chain is assembled), control 5 MW, other 5 MW.

**Selected equipment and screens** ('Offered Capacity Screen' on each): compressor 3,200 MW shaft, turbine 7,000 MW shaft, generator 3,600 MW electric, heat rejection 5,000 MW thermal, helium duty package 3,500 MW thermal (the re-selected set; the ARIES-selected set is 1,600 / 3,500 / 1,800 / 2,500 / 1,500). Plus three checks: heat removal (unmet heat ≤ 1e-6 MW), net power positive, loop flow under its rated ceiling.

**Calculated outputs at the point.** Turbine inlet 677.52 K (404 °C) from a heater inlet of 423.23 K after recuperation; every one of the 3,301.21 MW accepted, 0 unmet; turbine work 3,131.99, compressor work 3 × 817.17 = 2,451.52, net shaft 680.47, gross 666.86; auxiliaries 240.28 (175.28 loop pumps + 40 heating + 10 cryo + 5 fuel + 5 control + 5 other); **net 426.58 MW**; heat rejected in the two intercoolers and the precooler 2,620.74; screen margins 748.5 (compressor), 3,868.0 (turbine), 2,933.1 (generator), 2,379.3 (rejection), 198.8 (helium duty); all 8 checks satisfied. Thermal efficiency gross/delivered is 0.202.

## 2. Why it is a useful starting point

It is the only Stellaris-loop/ARIES-cycle combination with sealed receipts that satisfies every check its assembly carries (WI-093 report § 3), so the question "which operating choices improve it" has a defined, replayable origin. Its two operating levers (cycle flow, stage ratio) are exposed chosen inputs with no calculation upstream of them, so they satisfy the study axis rule (STUDY_POLICY § 2). The prior goal already saw the objective and the heat-removal requirement move in opposite directions under one of them (L-010: ratio 1.518 → 1.35 raises net 426.58 → 575.23 and leaves 278.15 MW unremoved), which is the observation the brief asks to turn into a fair study. It is not a reconstruction of any published plant, and the brief does not ask for one.

## 3. The supplied reactor-heat boundary, and why it is adequate for a power-cycle comparison

The reactor side is a fixed-duty, fixed-temperature helium stream: 3,301.21 MW available at 773.15 K, returned to the blanket at whatever temperature the cycle leaves it (the loop model reads only the supplied duty and rise, so the return temperature is a reported cycle-side quantity, `he_return` 513.12 K here, not a feedback into the loop). Under a cycle-flow/ratio sweep nothing upstream of the exchanger moves: fusion power, blanket heat, loop flow, loop pump power and the 773 K outlet are constants. That is exactly the boundary a power-cycle operating comparison needs: the question is how much of one given heat stream becomes electricity and whether all of it is removed, and both are read on the cycle side. What the boundary does not represent, and the answer must say: the blanket outlet temperature is not coupled to duty and flow (interactions finding #7), which does not matter here because neither moves; the loop's return temperature is not checked against a blanket inlet requirement (Stellaris fixes 573.15 K as the loop inlet, and the cycle here returns the helium at 513 K, 60 K colder, so a real loop would run a bypass or a different rise, an unmodeled seam that changes no cycle result but is a limit of the comparison); and the result is specific to a 500 °C source, which is what makes the ARIES cycle's own operating point (355 → 707 °C) inapplicable and the re-selection of flow and ratio necessary.

## 4. The power boundary, resolved

Under the sweep the only terms of the electrical balance that move are the turbine work and the three compressor works (through the shaft balance and the generator), and the rejected heat on its screen. The loop pumps (175.28 MW) and the ARIES auxiliary register (60 MW electric: 40 heating, 10 cryo, 5 fuel, 5 control, 5 other) are constants. A constant subtracted from net cannot change which operating point has the highest net, nor where the heat-removal boundary lies; it moves the net-positive boundary and every absolute figure. So the mismatch the brief names, ARIES auxiliaries on a Stellaris loop, is resolved for this comparison by declaring the auxiliary register a held-equal constant and testing it as one of the declared uncertainties (the Stellaris plant's own equivalents in `baseline.json` are 100 MW heating wall-plug for 50 MW coupled, 2.14 MW cryogenic and shield, 13.02 MW cooling-water pumping, 175.28 MW circulators). One omission moves with the sweep and is disclosed: the ARIES balance carries no heat-rejection pumping term (rejection is a rating screen only), while the Stellaris cooling-water model draws 13.02 MW for 2,190 MW rejected; at the 2,000–2,700 MW this cycle rejects that is a load of order 12–16 MW that varies by a few MW across the sweep, small against the 100–200 MW swings in net; it is bounded, not modeled, in the sensitivity on auxiliaries. Generator losses (2 % of shaft) are inside the balance; heating losses (20 MW) are inside it; there is no import case at any passing point (shaft is positive wherever net is).

## 5. The cost boundary, as proposed for the comparison contract

Nothing in the starting assembly is priced. The contract proposes to price it by instantiating the ARIES definitions with the ARIES USD2004 conventions: the five screened ratings and the exchanger area through 'Selected Equipment' with the linear 'Selected Inventory Purchase' law from the ARIES reference points (compressor 1,600 MW / 78.6395 MUSD, turbine 3,500 / 125.8232, generator 1,800 / 47.1837, heat rejection 2,500 / 56.086, helium duty package 1,500 / 32.4032, exchanger 50,000 m² / 58.3257) and the fixed conversion-services budget (62.9116); the rest of the plant as a supplied held-equal capital constant; the fuel term calculated by the existing fuel chain from the supplied 2,652.6 MW fusion power under the ARIES no-credit convention (held equal because fusion power is fixed) with the initial tritium stock; replacements, O&M (70 MUSD/year), consumables (5), availability 0.85, 40 calendar years, 5 % real, six construction years, terminal and overhaul conventions as ARIES binds them; and the LCOE from 'Lifecycle Cashflow Accounts' with its eleven separate contributions. Consequence for reading the study: within one fixed inventory every numerator term is a constant of the sweep, so the conditional LCOE is a monotone function of net electricity and the operating ranking by LCOE is the ranking by net; the cost side decides the value in USD/MWh of a net gain, the comparison between inventories (a bigger compressor or exchanger bought to reach a better operating point), and the weight of the denominator effect, which is why the rest-of-plant constant and the equipment prices are declared uncertainties. Absolute LCOE is conditional on the rest-of-plant constant and the fuel convention; paired delta-LCOE at equal fuel is the comparison.
