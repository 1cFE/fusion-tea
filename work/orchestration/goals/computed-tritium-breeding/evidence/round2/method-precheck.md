## Conditions before interpreting transport results

1. **Freeze a complete material manifest.** Cite composition, isotopic abundances, densities and their temperature basis; label all mixtures and substitutions as scenario choices. Convert volume fractions through constituent number densities, preserving atomic versus mass enrichment. Verify that every material isotope has data. The initial library's absence of molybdenum matters for an ordinary SS316 recipe; either acquire required data or explicitly adopt and assess a simplified material. Do not silently renormalize omitted elements. Confirm temperature treatment; acquire thermal-scattering data if a water-containing comparison requires it.
2. **Use a physically open exterior.** The outer toroidal material surface must transmit into exterior void, with a sufficiently remote enclosing vacuum boundary. Vacuum directly on the torus would remove particles that cross the central void and re-enter another part of the assembly. Check enclosing-boundary independence and external-structure backscatter separately. Native cylindrical sampling with radial probability proportional to cylindrical radius, followed by rejection to the plasma cell, gives the required uniform-volume Jacobian; independently check sampled moments or histograms.
3. **Define the measured quantity.** Tally Li-6 and Li-7 tritium production per emitted neutron and verify reaction/yield semantics against the processed data. Keep useful production in extractable breeder separate from tritium created elsewhere, including structural boron. Do not add nonrecoverable tritium to fuel supply. Check dimensions, isotope inventories, analytical/stochastic volumes, lost-particle diagnostics, normalization and repeated-seed statistics.

These inputs need not prevent writing geometry and tally code now. They must be resolved before interpreting its output as physical evidence.
