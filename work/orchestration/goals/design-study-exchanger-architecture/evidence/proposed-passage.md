We compared series connections with a blanket-helium exchanger followed by parallel PbLi and divertor branches. Establishing the thermal requirements changed the comparison: the old high-load leaders exceeded the permitted divertor temperature, and the original exchanger inventory could not meet the adopted terminal-temperature differences. We therefore tested explicit smaller exchanger selections with stated purchase budgets. At 1,835 MW supplied fusion, the network produced 528 MW net versus 498 MW for series on the same selected inventory. Both maintained the specified primary returns and exceeded 30 K at all six actual exchanger terminals. Refining flow far below the original 100 kg/s steps retained the 31 MW advantage. The mechanism was access to a lower passing cycle flow; passing equal-flow controls produced equal electricity. Under a zero-tritium-price accounting endpoint, conditional electricity cost fell from about 96 to 90 USD2004/MWh, leaving roughly 22 million USD2004/year to cover extra network costs if extra electrical demand were zero. The conclusion remains conditional: large primary bypass fractions are required, purchase budgets are assumptions, and a tested extra network pressure loss reverses the preference. The study establishes thermally consistent operating comparisons and break-even allowances for unresolved hardware costs.
