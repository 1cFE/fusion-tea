## 1. How the source represents heat delivery to the cycle

- `[SOURCE-FIG]` Schematic (p. 565): one loop "blanket → IHX → blanket" on the primary side; on the cycle side the helium leaves the recuperator cold outlet (state 10) into the IHX and leaves the IHX at state 1 ("To") to the turbine; then recuperator hot side (2 → 3), heat-rejection HX (to Ts, state 4), three compressors with two intercoolers (4 → 9), recuperator cold side (9 → 10). One intermediate-heat-exchanger duty (sized on p. 566 as three identical 900 MW units on the same terminals `[sc]`), one primary loop, no divertor loop, no separate first-wall loop, no parallel or series branches.
- `[SOURCE]` p. 562: the study covers three areas, "Design of a self-cooled blanket concept, Performance of a closed cycle helium gas turbine, Layout of an intermediate heat exchanger"; the blanket is a self-cooled lithium/vanadium blanket in which "the total lithium flow has to cool the highly loaded First Wall (FW) before it is heated up further in the breeding and shielding zone" (p. 563), so first wall and blanket share one coolant stream.
- `[SOURCE]` p. 563–564: "With a few simplifying assumptions, the efficiency of a closed Brayton cycle can be expressed relatively simply in terms of the following parameters [8]": compressor pressure ratio r, overall pressure-loss ratio β (product of the pressure-loss ratios "including both sides of recuperator, all intercoolers, heat rejection HX, and IHX"), γ, recuperator effectiveness η_x = (T10 − T9)/(T2 − T9), turbine and compressor efficiencies, To and Ts; the closed-form expression for three compression stages is printed on p. 564. `[DERIVED, sc]` The heat source enters this expression only through To (turbine inlet) and through the IHX's share of the pressure-loss ratio β, which carries the exponent (γ − 1)/γ; the paper attributes the parameterisation to Rust 1979 (its Ref. [8]) and prints the three-stage form; "not the turbine inlet temperature To directly but the ratio of turbine inlet temperature To to helium gas temperature Ts at the heat sink influences the efficiency" (p. 564).
- `[SOURCE]` Table 1 (p. 564), "Key parameters of the power conversion system": To 923 K (650 °C), Ts 308 K (35 °C), To/Ts 3.00, r 2.0, η_x 0.96, η_c and η_t 0.92, β 1.02 (ΣΔp/p = 0.05), γ 1.66, η 46%; "slightly modified from those adopted by Wong et al. [2]". p. 565: To/Ts = 3.0 gives 46% at ΣΔp/p = 0.05 and 44% at 0.08; "A ratio of 3.0 means either a combination of a turbine inlet temperature of 650 °C with a minimum helium temperature of 35 °C or … 630/28 °C. The maximum helium temperature achievable with a self-cooled lithium blanket depends on the blanket design as well as on the layout of the intermediate heat exchanger, which is shown in Section 4."
- `[SOURCE]` Section 4 (pp. 565–566), "Lay-out of the intermediate heat exchanger": "the interface between the lithium loop of the self-cooled blanket and the helium loop of the closed cycle gas turbine"; conventional tubular design, high-pressure helium in the tubes, lithium on the shell side; helium conditions taken from Wong et al. [2]: 18 MPa, pressure drop 0.4 MPa, inlet 436 °C, outlet 650 °C; "In a first approach, the lithium temperatures at the heat exchanger inlet and outlet have been assumed to be 670 and 470 °C, respectively"; size determined "with a suitable computer code, assuming three units with a thermal power of 900 MW each": heat-transfer surface 8175 m², bundle diameter 3.6 m, height 9.0 m, vanadium tubes 0.020/0.024 m, lithium-side pressure drop 0.04 MPa; "This first layout of the heat exchanger should serve as a basis for future optimization. In particular, a detailed trade-off between size, achievable helium temperature, and required helium pressure drop has to be performed."
- `[DERIVED]` From the stated terminals the IHX is a counterflow unit with a 20 K hot-end difference (670 − 650) and a 34 K cold-end difference (470 − 436); the paper states the terminals and the sized geometry, not an approach-temperature rule or an effectiveness. Nowhere in the seven pages do a divertor, a second primary loop, per-loop duties or a network of exchangers appear; the only exchangers in the described system are the IHX (three units), the recuperator, the intercoolers and the heat-rejection HX `[sc]`.

## 2. Relation to Raffray's ARIES-CS cycle and to Q1

- `[DERIVED]` The lineage is: this paper supplies the closed-form gross-efficiency expression in To, Ts, r, β, η_x, η_c, η_t, γ for one heat source; Schleicher, Raffray and Wong 2000/2001 adopt the expressions with the heat source drawn as a boundary "to/from in-reactor components or IHX" and Tin as an input (`ref15-reading.md`); Raffray 2008 cites the latter for the ARIES-CS cycle (three compression stages, one expansion) and lists the same variables in Table III with an "HX temperature difference between hot and cold legs 30 °C" in place of Tin. In this paper the corresponding quantity is a stated pair of IHX terminals (20 K hot end, 34 K cold end) for a single lithium loop, with the turbine inlet 650 °C taken from the IHX helium outlet.
- `[DERIVED]` For a single primary loop whose whole duty passes through one counterflow IHX, setting the cycle outlet as the primary inlet minus a hot-end difference is consistent: the lithium spans 670 → 470 °C against helium 436 → 650 °C, both spans 200–214 K, so heat fractions and span fractions coincide by construction. The paper therefore shows the method being applied to one hot stream. It contains nothing about how to combine several primary loops with different temperature levels, which is where the ARIES-CS inconsistency lies (blanket helium 386 → 456 °C carrying 42% of the heat, PbLi 451 → 738 °C in Table II and 737 °C in the Fig. 12 inset, divertor 573 → 700 °C, one cycle stream 355 → 707 °C; ARIES-CS values from Raffray 2008, not from this source `[sc]`).

## 3. What this establishes and what it does not

- **Established by the source:** the ARIES lineage's cycle method, at its origin within that lineage `[sc]`, treats heat delivery as one primary loop through one intermediate heat exchanger with stated terminal temperatures and a sized geometry, and derives the cycle efficiency from To/Ts, the pressure-loss ratio and the component efficiencies. It has no representation of several primary loops at different temperature levels.
- **Bearing on the ARIES-CS inconsistency:** the paper does not address it. It neither resolves the inconsistency among the published ARIES-CS branch duties and temperature spans nor describes any per-branch or lumped combination rule for multiple loops. The source applies the method to one hot stream and says nothing about several; the Fig. 12 inset reading of ARIES-CS is consistent with it, not supported by it, and remains an inference `[owner qualification, owner-supplement-r4.md]` `[sc]`.
- **Not established:** how ARIES-CS combined its three loops in the cycle calculation; whether Ref. 13 (Wang, Malang and Raffray 2005, paywalled, queued) sizes the three exchangers; the composition of Lyon's 2916 MW.

## 4. Consequence for the answer

No model, package or study change; the current calculations are preserved. The bounded result of the citation chain (Raffray → Schleicher et al. → Malang et al.) is: the cited cycle method takes the turbine inlet temperature as an input from a single heat source or IHX and was never extended, in the obtained sources, to several primary loops at different temperature levels; the published ARIES-CS quantities cannot all hold under the stated heat-exchanger assumptions, and the obtained references neither resolve that nor state how ARIES-CS handled it.
