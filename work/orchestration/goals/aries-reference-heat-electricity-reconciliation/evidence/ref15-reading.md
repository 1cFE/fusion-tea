## 1. How the source represents heat delivery to the cycle

- `[SOURCE-FIG]` Fig. 1 (p. 2, `images/tmp4tmb42zq.pdf-0002-03.png`): the cycle helium leaves the recuperator cold side (state 10, "Tout") into one block labelled "To/from In-Reactor Components or Intermediate Heat Exchanger" and returns from it at state 1 ("Tin") to the compressor turbine. There is one heat-addition block, no branch structure, no exchanger geometry, no approach or effectiveness for that block. The rest of the cycle is drawn explicitly: pre-cooler, LP/IP/HP compressors with two intercoolers, recuperator (ε_rec), compressor turbine and generator turbine on one expansion.
- `[SOURCE]` p. 2, "The principal system parameters under the designer's control include: compressor turbine inlet temperature, Tin; recuperator effectiveness, ε_rec; turbine and compressor adiabatic efficiencies; system pressure, Pout; system ΔP/Pout." The turbine inlet temperature is an independent input of the method.
- `[SOURCE]` p. 2: "The fusion in-reactor (or intermediate heat exchanger, IHX, if the fusion in-reactor coolant is different from the power cycle He coolant) return temperature, Tout, is ideally a dependent variable that is determined by optimization of the overall compressor pressure ratio." The cycle return temperature to the heat source is an output, not a constraint from the heat source side.
- `[SOURCE]` p. 2 and Table 1: gross cycle efficiency is estimated from "four expressions derived for the cycle shown in Fig. 1" in ΔP, per-stage compression ratio r_p, T_min (lowest He temperature) and γ = 1.66, with the independent variables of Table 1 (current and near-term values). Results (p. 3, Fig. 2): with T_min 35 °C the optimum gross efficiencies are 51% (Tin 850 °C) and 64% (Tin 1200 °C) at overall compression ratios 2.38 and 2.43, with Tout 522 °C and 759 °C; "changing the turbine inlet temperature from 850 °C to 1200 °C has the major effect".
- `[SOURCE]` Conclusion (p. 5): helium closed-cycle gas turbines "can be coupled with fusion in-reactor components using the same He coolant but also using different coolants via an intermediate heat exchanger"; the compression ratio "can also be adjusted to reduce the in-reactor inlet temperature if required by material consideration".
- `[DERIVED]` Nowhere in the five pages do per-branch exchangers, branch duties, branch coolant temperatures, exchanger terminal differences or an exchanger sizing appear. The heat source enters the method only through the value chosen for Tin.

## 2. Mapping to Raffray's ARIES-CS cycle (Table III, p737)

`[DERIVED]` Raffray's Table III lists exactly the independent variables of this method: 3 compression stages, 1 expansion stage, η_C 0.89, η_T 0.93, ε_rec 0.95, cycle maximum pressure 15 MPa, total compression ratio 3.5, fractional pressure drop 0.045, lowest He temperature 35 °C, plus "HX temperature difference between hot and cold legs 30 °C", and reports gross efficiency 0.43. Under the cited method the 30 °C entry is what fixes the input Tin from the hottest coolant temperature: 738 − 30 ≈ 707 °C (Fig. 12 inset, p736, cycle He 355 → 707 °C against LiPb 737 °C). The contract § 5 `[DERIVED]` already found that our cycle relation reproduces 0.43 gross at a 707 °C turbine inlet with these parameters (heater inlet ≈ 345 °C). So the published 707 °C / 43% pair is the output of a cycle calculation whose heat-source side is a single block with Tin taken as an input.

## 3. What this establishes and what it does not

- **Established by the cited source:** the cycle methodology Raffray cites has one lumped heat-addition block with the turbine inlet temperature as an independent design input; it neither models nor checks how the in-reactor branches deliver that temperature. The lumped-heater reading of the Fig. 12 inset is therefore supported at the methodology level by the cited source `[owner qualification, owner-supplement-r4.md: not established until supported by the source — now supported for the method]`.
- **Not established:** that the ARIES-CS calculation set Tin = 707 °C without checking the three-branch heat balance is consistent with Table III and the Fig. 12 inset but is not stated in either source; whether the blanket-to-cycle coupling paper (Ref. 13, Wang, Malang, Raffray, SOFE 2005; paywalled, queued for the owner) sizes or checks the exchangers; how Lyon's 2916 MW is composed.
- **Does it resolve the inconsistency among the published branch duties and temperatures?** No. It explains where 707 °C and 43% come from (an input to a lumped-block method) and why a branch-level inconsistency would not surface in that calculation, but it does not reconcile the printed branch duties and temperature spans with the printed cycle outlet: under the stated heat-exchanger assumptions those published quantities still cannot all hold (contract § 6a). The bounded result is that the cited reference answers the methodology question and leaves the branch-level question where the round-3 review left it.
