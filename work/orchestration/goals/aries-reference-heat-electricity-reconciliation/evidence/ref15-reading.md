## 1. How the source represents heat delivery to the cycle

- `[SOURCE-FIG]` Fig. 1 (p. 2, `images/tmp4tmb42zq.pdf-0002-03.png`): the cycle helium leaves the recuperator cold side (state 10, "Tout") to a boundary labelled "To/from In-Reactor Components or Intermediate Heat Exchanger" and returns from it at state 1 ("Tin") to the compressor turbine. The heat source is drawn as that boundary label only, not as a component `[sc]`: no branch structure, no exchanger geometry, no approach or effectiveness. The rest of the cycle is drawn explicitly: pre-cooler, LP/IP/HP compressors with two intercoolers, recuperator (ε_rec), compressor turbine and generator turbine on one expansion.
- `[SOURCE]` pp. 1–2 (condensed, symbols as printed) `[sc]`: "The principal system parameters under the designers control include: Compressor turbine inlet temperature, Tin; Recuperator effectiveness, ε_rec; Turbine and compressor adiabatic efficiencies, η_T,ad and η_C,ad; System pressure, Pout; System ΔP/Pout." The turbine inlet temperature is an independent input of the method.
- `[SOURCE]` p. 2: "The fusion in-reactor (or intermediate heat exchanger, IHX, if the fusion in-reactor coolant is different from the power cycle He coolant) return temperature, Tout, is ideally a dependent variable that is determined by optimization of the overall compressor pressure ratio." The cycle return temperature to the heat source is an output of the cycle optimisation; the source adds at once that "in practical systems, Tout can be constrained by material considerations" and that the compression ratio can be adjusted to lower it `[sc]`.
- `[SOURCE]` p. 2 and Table 1: gross cycle efficiency is estimated from the two expressions (1)–(2) "derived for the cycle shown in Fig. 1", which the source attributes by citation to its Ref. [4] (Malang, Schnauder and Tillack, "Combination of a self-cooled liquid metal breeder blanket with a gas turbine power conversion system", Fusion Eng. Des. 39–40, 561, 1998, as Schleicher prints it; the captured paper is Fusion Eng. Des. 41, 561–567, 1998 `[sc]` (the extraction's "four expressions" is that superscript), in ΔP, per-stage compression ratio r_p, T_min (lowest He temperature) and γ = 1.66, with the independent variables of Table 1 (Tin, ε_rec, Pout, η_T,ad, η_C,ad, ΔP/Pout; current and near-term values). Results (p. 3, efficiency figure `images/tmp4tmb42zq.pdf-0003-01.png`; the manuscript carries two captions labelled Fig. 2 `[sc]`): with T_min 35 °C the optimum gross efficiencies are 51% (Tin 850 °C) and 64% (Tin 1200 °C) at overall compression ratios 2.38 and 2.43, with Tout 522 °C and 759 °C; the Fig. 3 discussion (p. 3) says "changing the turbine inlet temperature from 850 °C to 1200 °C has the major effect". Raffray's overall compression ratio 3.5 lies outside the source's plotted 1–3 range (optimum ≈ 2.4) `[sc]`.
- `[SOURCE]` Conclusion (p. 5): helium closed-cycle gas turbines "can be coupled with fusion in-reactor components using the same He coolant but also using different coolants via an intermediate heat exchanger"; the compression ratio "can also be adjusted to reduce the in-reactor inlet temperature if required by material consideration".
- `[DERIVED]` Nowhere in the five pages do per-branch exchangers, branch duties, branch coolant temperatures, exchanger terminal differences or an exchanger sizing appear. The heat source enters the method only through the value chosen for Tin.

## 2. Mapping to Raffray's ARIES-CS cycle (Table III, p737)

`[DERIVED]` Raffray's Table III lists all of the method's Table 1 variables except Tin (η_C 0.89, η_T 0.93, ε_rec 0.95, cycle maximum pressure 15 MPa, fractional pressure drop 0.045, lowest He temperature 35 °C), adds the stage counts (3 compression, 1 expansion) and the total compression ratio 3.5, replaces Tin by "HX temperature difference between hot and cold legs 30 °C", a terminal-difference specification the cited method does not have `[sc]`, and reports gross efficiency 0.43. Inference `[DERIVED, sc]`: under the cited method the 30 °C entry is what would fix the input Tin from the hottest coolant temperature, 737 − 30 = 707 °C (Fig. 12 inset, p736, cycle He 355 → 707 °C against LiPb 737 °C); the sources do not state this step. The contract § 5 `[DERIVED]` already found that our cycle relation reproduces 0.43 gross at a 707 °C turbine inlet with these parameters (heater inlet ≈ 345 °C). On that inference the published 707 °C / 43% pair is the output of a cycle calculation whose heat-source side is a boundary with Tin taken as an input.

## 3. What this establishes and what it does not

- **Established by the cited source:** the cycle method Raffray cites treats the heat source as a boundary with the turbine inlet temperature as an independent design input; it neither models nor checks how in-reactor branches deliver that temperature. The source never mentions ARIES-CS or Raffray's Fig. 12 `[sc]`.
- **Inferred, not established:** that Raffray applied the method to ARIES-CS by setting Tin from the hottest coolant and a 30 °C terminal difference (Table III, Fig. 12 inset). Raffray p737 says the blanket thermal-hydraulic parameters were optimised together with the cycle, which is consistent with a lumped treatment but does not state one. The lumped-heater reading of the inset therefore remains a hypothesis at the ARIES-CS level, with the cited method's lumped heat source as support for its plausibility only `[owner qualification, owner-supplement-r4.md]` `[sc]`.
- **Not established:** whether the ARIES-CS calculation checked the three-branch heat balance; whether the blanket-to-cycle coupling paper (Ref. 13, Wang, Malang, Raffray, SOFE 2005; paywalled, queued for the owner) sizes or checks the exchangers; what the expressions' origin (Ref. [4], Malang, Schnauder and Tillack 1998, a liquid-metal-blanket plus gas-turbine coupling paper) says about blanket-to-cycle heat delivery, which is a direct lead on the goal's question and is pursued next `[sc]`; how Lyon's 2916 MW is composed.
- **Does it resolve the inconsistency among the published branch duties and temperatures?** No. It explains where 707 °C and 43% come from (an input to a lumped-block method) and why a branch-level inconsistency would not surface in that calculation, but it does not reconcile the printed branch duties and temperature spans with the printed cycle outlet: under the stated heat-exchanger assumptions those published quantities still cannot all hold (contract § 6a). The bounded result is that the cited reference answers the methodology question and leaves the branch-level question where the round-3 review left it.
