## 1. The question and the short answer

**Question.** When supplied the published ARIES fusion power (2436 MW), why does the model calculate about 796 MW net instead of the reported 1000 MW, and why can it not remove all the heat?

**Answer.** Two separate things were wrong with the original case, and one thing remains unreconciled between the published description and our interpretation and implementation of it. (Revised 2026-09-25 at the owner's direction, `evidence/owner-supplement-r3.md`, and after the round-3 Q1 review.)

1. The heat could not be removed (158.7 MW left in the PbLi stage) because the inherited model connected the three primary-side exchangers in series in one cycle stream (helium, then divertor, then PbLi), so the PbLi stage received cycle helium already heated by the divertor stage, and the PbLi capacity rate (5.10 MW/K) was too small to cool the PbLi against it. The published plant (Raffray Fig. 12) puts the PbLi and divertor exchangers in parallel after the blanket-helium exchanger. Representing that network (WI-092, reviewed for MR-7 before implementation) removes this mechanism: at the source-supported inputs it takes 41.125 MW off the 151.002 MW shortfall with no hardware change. What is left (109.876 MW at the 1600 kg/s cycle-flow convention, split 53.514 MW in the helium stage and 56.362 MW in the PbLi stream) is a different mechanism: with the source's 0.95 recuperation, the whole cycle flow enters the blanket-helium stage at 309 °C and can only be heated to 452.4 °C against the 456 °C helium hot inlet, so the helium stage cannot carry its duty at that flow, and the PbLi stream, entering at that temperature with 85% of the flow, cannot carry all of its duty either; no split removes both. That residual disappears with 1700 kg/s of cycle flow (which needs a compressor rated above the assumed 1600 MW; the declared resized alternative at 1700 MW is a modeled alternative, not the reconstructed ARIES reference: the best tested steady case, not a demonstrated upper bound on achievable output, whose changed flow and compressor rating still need their cost consequences evaluated before any economic comparison; net 891.003 MW) or with the inherited 0.8 recuperation at 1600 kg/s (net 759.886 MW). Both are operating or hardware choices the sources do not state.
2. The original 796 MW was low for input-level reasons that round 1 quantified and corrected from the sources: assumed 0.8 recuperation (source 0.95), 1400 kg/s cycle flow (1600 derived from Lyon's 2916 MW over Raffray's cycle temperatures), 500 kg/s divertor flow (source 283), a deposition partition that sent about 218 MW too much heat to the divertor and 198 MW too little to the blanket circuits, and an auxiliary itemisation 20 MW below Lyon's 252 MW. Together these are worth +46.727 MW net on the series arrangement; with the network the combined correction is +83.687 MW (order interaction +7.6 MW).
3. The remaining gap to the reference is a source-internal inconsistency that survives independent review. At the best tested steady case the model is 109.987 MW gross and 108.997 MW net short, and all of it is thermal efficiency (0.3907 against the reference 0.43), which is the turbine inlet (628 °C against the published 708 °C). A fresh thermal-cycle reviewer checked the contract's Q1 against the primary diagrams, the temperature and duty definitions, the cross-paper mapping and our cycle representation (round 3, `evidence/q1-thermal-cycle-review.md`): these published quantities cannot all hold under the stated heat-exchanger assumptions (counterflow exchangers on the printed per-branch duties and temperatures): the blanket-helium loop carries 42% of the heat over 21% of the temperature span and would need more capacity rate than the whole cycle flow to deliver 707 °C in any of the arrangements checked (helium first then parallel, all parallel, helium in parallel with the others, helium last); Raffray's own Fig. 12 inset draws one lumped hot leg 385 → 737 °C with 30 K at both ends, which is consistent with a lumped-heater cycle calculation. Our per-branch model is generous rather than harsh to the source (approaches of 4–5 K against the printed 30 °C). What this establishes is that the published per-branch quantities cannot all hold under the stated heat-exchanger assumptions, and that our representation is not the cause of the residual. It does not establish what the ARIES-CS cycle calculation actually did with the exchangers or what the actual design's exchangers would achieve, so it does not decide whether the published design could achieve its stated efficiency. Round 4 obtained Raffray's cited cycle reference (Schleicher, Raffray and Wong 2000/2001, registered at `knowledge/sources/schleicher_raffray_wong_2001_an_assessment_of_the_brayton/`; reading `evidence/ref15-reading.md`, source-checked): its method treats the heat source as a boundary "to/from in-reactor components or intermediate heat exchanger" with the turbine inlet temperature as an independent design input and the return temperature as a dependent variable, and it contains no per-branch exchanger, duty or terminal difference. It therefore answers the methodology question and does not resolve the inconsistency among the published branch duties and temperature spans. That ARIES-CS set its 707 °C from the hottest coolant minus the 30 °C terminal difference of Table III is an inference consistent with the Fig. 12 inset, not a statement in either source, so the lumped-heater reading stays a hypothesis at the ARIES-CS level. The blanket-to-cycle coupling paper (Wang, Malang and Raffray 2005) is paywalled and queued for the owner; The origin of the method's expressions within the ARIES lineage (Malang, Schnauder and Tillack 1998, registered at `knowledge/sources/combination_of_a_self_cooled_liquid_metal_breeder_blanket/`; reading `evidence/malang98-reading.md`, source-checked) applies it to one lithium loop through one intermediate-heat-exchanger duty with stated terminals (lithium 670 → 470 °C against helium 436 → 650 °C) and says nothing about combining several primary loops at different temperature levels. So the obtained citation chain (Raffray → Schleicher et al. → Malang et al.) establishes that the cited method takes the turbine inlet temperature as an input from a single heat source and, in the obtained sources, was never extended to several loops; it does not resolve the inconsistency among the published ARIES-CS branch duties and temperature spans, and it does not state how ARIES-CS handled them. The lumped-heater reading of Fig. 12's inset is consistent with the method, not supported by it, and remains an inference. The review also found a mapping artefact on our side: scaling the duties to 2436 MW while holding the primary flows at Raffray's values pinches the PbLi cold end; the round-3 study puts it at ≈ 34 MW of the 56 MW PbLi shortfall at 1600 kg/s, of which 20 MW migrates to the helium stage, so the case's total shortfall falls by 14 MW (to 95.681) and it stays not steady.

**Completion condition: partially answered.** The unmet heat is traced to two mechanisms: the series-order limit is corrected by the reviewed architecture change, and the helium-stage bound is bounded by a missing-input range (a cycle flow and compressor rating, or a recuperation, that the sources do not state; 0 MW unmet across net 759.886–891.003 MW). The revised case does not reproduce the reported gross and net within the ±15 MW budget at any steady point, the shortfall is explained numerically and by subsystem within the model, and its reconciliation with the published description (Q1) stays open. So: the original case was a thermally inadequate scenario, not a steady operating point; its two causes are corrected or bounded; and the model's best tested steady result disagrees with the published 1000 MW by about 108.997 MW for a reason that is explained and bounded but cannot be settled from the retained sources. Net 1000 was never a target and is not reached at any steady point.

## 6. Remaining uncertainty

- Q1 (reviewed, round 3): the inconsistency survives checks of the primary diagrams, the definitions, the cross-paper mapping and our cycle representation and is internal to Raffray's per-branch description; what remains undetermined is how the ARIES-CS cycle calculation treated the exchangers and whether Lyon's 2916 MW includes ≈ 50 MW of balance-of-plant heat. Round 4 pursued the cycle references Raffray attaches: Ref. 15 (Schleicher, Raffray and Wong 2001) was obtained and read (a lumped heat-source boundary with the turbine inlet as an input; no per-branch exchangers; does not resolve the branch inconsistency), Ref. 13 (Wang, Malang and Raffray 2005) is paywalled and queued for the owner; the origin of the method's expressions (Malang, Schnauder and Tillack 1998) was also obtained and applies the method to one lithium loop through one intermediate-heat-exchanger duty with no rule for several loops; the earlier "p737 Ref. 7" was a misattribution, Ref. 7 being Lyon's retained systems paper, which takes 43% as a constant and has no exchanger model.
- Our exchanger approaches (4–5 K) are far tighter than the source's printed 30 °C; imposing 30 K would enlarge the shortfall, so the model's steady results are optimistic on this point.
- The cross-paper flow scaling (duties × 1.030 with Raffray's primary flows) pinches the PbLi cold end at 1600 kg/s: scaling the flows with the duties changes that case by -14.195 MW unmet and 12.757 MW net and changes no steady result (round-3 study).
- The supplied split between the parallel stages (design default 0.85, broad low 0.80–0.90 at C3) stands in for an unmodelled branch hydraulic balance; nothing redistributes flow, and the 0.85 default is not the best sampled value.
- Cycle flow (1600 kg/s) is a cross-source derived convention, not a printed value; the compressor rating (1600 MW) is an inherited assumption (A6); the resized alternative's cost consequences were not evaluated.
- Thermal composition (≈ 60 MW), the unlabelled ≈ 67 MW divertor share and the 253/252 itemisation are bounded ledger items, never tolerance.
- The model's efficiency-versus-turbine-inlet relation is its own cycle model (inherited from WI-089) and was not qualified against the source's cycle beyond the recuperation and approach values.

## 12. Plain-language explanation for the write-up

The model was handed the published ARIES fusion power and asked to work out how much electricity comes out. At first it said about 800 MW instead of the published 1000, and it also reported that it could not get all the heat out of the reactor. Investigating that turned up two different problems. The first was a plumbing mistake in the model: it had the three heat exchangers in a line, so the hot lithium-lead loop was trying to hand its heat to helium that was already hot, and could not. The published plant puts two of those exchangers side by side instead. Fixing the model to match the published layout removed about 41 MW of the 151 MW of trapped heat at the source-supported inputs. The second problem was that, with the published high-performance recuperator, the helium coming back to the reactor is already so warm that the blanket's own helium loop cannot heat it any further at the flow rate we assumed, and the lithium-lead exchanger is squeezed at its cold end for the same reason. Pushing more helium around the cycle fixes that, but it needs a bigger compressor than we assumed, and the papers do not say which choice ARIES made. With the plumbing fixed and a larger compressor, every heat balance and equipment check in the model passes at about 891 MW net; that is a modeled alternative, not the ARIES design, and the cost of the larger compressor and flow has not been evaluated. The last 109 MW comes down to one thing: the published 43% efficiency needs turbine inlet gas at 708 °C, and the printed duties and temperatures cannot all hold under the stated heat-exchanger assumptions, so our exchangers cannot deliver it. An independent thermal-cycle check confirmed this from the papers' own numbers: the blanket helium loop, which is only 456 °C hot, carries over 40% of the heat but can cover only about a fifth of the temperature climb the cycle gas needs, so with the printed numbers none of the plumbing arrangements we checked gets the gas to 708 °C. The paper's own sketch draws all the heat as one hot stream from 385 to 737 °C; that would be one way to arrive at 708 °C, but the papers do not say that is what was done. We obtained the cycle references the paper cites: their method takes the turbine inlet temperature as a given input from a single heat source and never combines several loops at different temperatures, so they neither confirm nor rule out how ARIES-CS handled its three loops. We therefore do not claim the design itself could not have reached its stated efficiency.
