# Pre-execution critique — `20260913-structural-decomposition` (T-004)

**Verdict: PROCEED WITH CHANGES** — (1) make the exporter compare the points that fail to evaluate, by state and by failure cause, and count them; (2) make channel equality NaN-aware and symmetric, remove the silent `hasattr` guard, and record the size of any difference; (3) restate § 9 to what the preflight artifact actually says (`package_clean` `fail`, overall `fail`) and carry the blob-hash comparison as the substitute evidence; (4) restate § 11 so the scan is described as one oracle module under two key maps, four of fourteen fences, and a mask that excludes nothing; (5) restate § 2 on `availability_direct` (the committed hold was reproducible in held mode; say why live mode was chosen and that per-point numbers are not comparable to the committed record).

Critic: a fresh session, no part in the design. Written 2026-09-13 before any point ran. No file edited except this one. Nothing under `knowledge/holdout/` read.

## Findings

### F1 — The exporter does not compare points that fail to evaluate, and some will. (Blocking; change 1.)

The committed `arm-fence-p100` proposed 3,751 points and evaluated 3,695. The other 56 were removed by an oracle pre-screen before execution (`20260907-minor-radius/results/excluded_points.csv`: complex `p_net` or "non-positive fuel" at the closure's validity corner, R 11.2 / n 0.6× / T 13 keV and neighbours). This study proposes all 3,751 with no pre-screen (`study.py` `proposals_old`), so those coordinates go to both sealed packages. Two committed records say what the package does there: the CAS10 land term takes `** 0.5` of a negative net electric power and the case lands as `execution_failed` (`20260823-magnet-technology-ab` finding #1; `20260829-p-pump-fence` § "The evaluability exclusion"). The runner stores such a case with `state="execution_failed"`, no evidence, and a `failure_json` carrying `phase`, `module_or_channel` and `cause` (`teax-simkit/simkit/study/runner.py` `_commit_execution_failure`).

`study.py` `export` handles these rows by writing blanks (line 154) and by not counting them anywhere: `summary["both_completed"]` counts only pairs that both completed, and `channel_differences` / `verdict_differences` are computed only inside that branch. A point that failed on one arm and completed on the other is therefore not reported as a difference. A point that failed on both is not reported as identical. And `CaseView` (`simkit/study/query.py`) does not expose `failure_json`, so nothing in the exporter can compare *why* the two arms failed even if it wanted to.

What this would make unsound: the reading "identical at every point" would rest on roughly 3,695 compared points with ~56 silently uncompared, and a lineage defect that changes *which* points fail (or how) would not be seen. Goal § Answered when (b) asks for every channel and every verdict at every point; a failed point has a state and a cause, and those are what "identical" means there.

Change: (a) add `state_differences` and `both_failed` to the summary and a `states_equal` column to `points.csv`; (b) for `execution_failed` pairs, read `failure_json` from each store directly (`SELECT candidate_id, failure_json FROM cases` — the column exists at `store.py:68`) and compare `phase` and `cause` exactly, mapping `module_or_channel` through the ledger's host paths or, if that map is not to hand, comparing `cause` and reporting `module_or_channel` on both sides without asserting equality; (c) define in § 2 or § 11, before the run, what identity means at a failed point: same state, same phase, same cause. Then the record's reading can say "identical at all 3,751 points, of which N failed identically" instead of leaving N unspoken.

### F2 — Channel equality is float `!=`, which calls NaN different from NaN, is asymmetric, and hides the size of a difference. (Should fix; change 2.)

`study.py:146` compares `e["channels"][k] != n["channels"][k]`. A NaN on both arms reads as a difference. The route's own `required_outputs` says nonfinite values are persisted as results and refused only at CSV time, so a NaN in the store is possible. Use `a == b or (isnan(a) and isnan(b))`. Line 147 reports channels present on the entering arm and absent on the restructured arm, but not the reverse; the ledger is bijective on both contracts (verified below) so the reverse set is empty today, but the check should be symmetric so a future run cannot pass with an unmapped channel. Line 107's `hasattr(c, "inputs")` guard would, if `inputs` were ever absent, collapse every row onto the key `None` and produce a one-row comparison that looks clean; `CaseView` does carry `inputs`, so remove the guard and let a missing attribute raise.

Finally, the goal contract allows exactly one kind of non-identity — floating-point reordering, which must be named. The summary records only which channels differ, not by how much. Record per differing channel the max absolute and relative difference across points, so a "differs first at …" reading can say whether it is a 1e-16 reordering or a moved number. Without that the record cannot make the distinction the contract asks for.

### F3 — § 9 says the entering cleanliness gate "did not run"; the artifact says it ran and returned `fail`. (Should fix; change 3.)

`results/arm-entering/preflight_results.json` has `package_clean: fail` with detail "git status failed over … fatal: …" and the file's overall `outcome` is `fail`. The record must state the artifact's words. The argument the record then owes is why this is not the runbook's dirty-package fail-closed condition: the tool cannot distinguish "dirty" from "not inside a git work tree", and the failure is the second. The identity gate's recomputation of the sealed fingerprint (`234d0b27…`, "every other sealed artifact matches") covers the package bytes. It does not cover the three harness files the entering arm executes through (`studies/study_route.py`, `studies/oracle_entry.py`, `studies/manifest.json`) or `verify_stellaris.py`. I hashed the scratch copy against the commit: every one of the 239 files under `generated/` and all five harness files have the same git blob hash as `16d83308` (`git hash-object` vs `git rev-parse 16d83308:<path>`). Carry that comparison in the record (§ 9 detail or § 13) as the substitute for the gate; it is cheap to rerun and it is what "clean" means for a copy that no repository watches. Also state that `package_identity.json`'s `package.path` (a `../../../../tmp/…/scratchpad/entering/…` relative path) is transient and will be dead after the session; the sealed fingerprint is the durable identity.

The entering arm is legitimate as an arm: the package is the plant-closure T-007 pin by all three fingerprints (the manifest at `16d83308` carries `234d0b27…`, `42237b2b…`, `e2b0fe39…`, matching goal.md fact 5), its baseline reproduces `224.60952472804465` with 14/14 verdicts, and its route differs from the pinned route only in key names (diff verified: `AXES`, `R_TIE`, four `CHANNELS` entries).

### F4 — § 11 describes "the two oracles" agreeing; there is one oracle module, and the scan proves the key maps agree, not the packages. (Should fix; change 4.)

`verify_stellaris.py` is unchanged between `16d83308` and HEAD (`git diff --stat` empty). Both `oracle_entry.py` copies do `import verify_stellaris as vs`; `scan.py` imports the pinned `oracle_entry` first, so `sys.modules["verify_stellaris"]` is bound once and the entering `oracle_entry` reuses it. The scan therefore feeds one math module through two entry-key maps (the pinned map re-keyed by WI-057, 208 lines changed and nothing else). Agreement at every edge is evidence that the ledger and the re-keyed oracle map are consistent — worth having — and no evidence about the two sealed packages. § 11 should say exactly that, and say that the executed arms are the only package evidence. This is the "identical by construction" item the critique brief asked about; it is not a defect in the scan, it is a wording defect in the record.

Two more § 11 restatements. `scan.py` reads four fences (wall peak, conductor peak field, sustainment, net power) of the fourteen the package asserts; "the same fences are caught" must be scoped to those four. And the validity mask `R > a + 2.25` excludes nothing on this grid (max a + 2.25 = 4.45 m against min R 11.2 m; 0 of 30 (R, a) pairs excluded, checked by running the mask), so 3,751 = 5·6·5·5·5 + 1 with no mask effect; say so, otherwise a reader assumes the mask shaped the count. "Inherited verbatim" should be qualified as "coordinates verbatim": the grids, `NE0`, `BASE`, the tie and the wall-plug are identical to `20260907-minor-radius/study.py` (verified line by line), but the held set differs on one key — F5.

### F5 — `availability_direct` 0.0 is a defensible choice, but § 2's reason for it is not quite right and its consequence is unstated. (Should fix; change 5.)

§ 2 says the committed sweep's `availability` 0.85 is "a key neither package has". True of the key. But `mfe_lifecycle.sysml` documents two modes: `availability_direct_in = 0` is LIVE (the WI-046 calendar computes availability from core life), and `availability_direct_in > 0` is HELD, "the retired chain VERBATIM". So the committed hold was reproducible on both lineages as `availability_direct` 0.85. The executor chose 0.0 instead. That is the better choice for this study — it is the pinned baseline's own value on both lineages, and it exercises the live calendar chain across the window, which the held mode would leave dormant — but the record should give that reason rather than an impossibility. It should also state the consequence plainly: with availability live, per-point LCOE and verdicts here will not equal the committed record's at the same coordinates, and this record makes no join to the committed record's values (it does not plan one; say so).

### F6 — Minor items, none blocking.

- `feasible_entering` / `feasible_restructured` (`study.py:152`) are "all fourteen verdicts satisfied"; `divertor_heat_ok` is violated by design at every point, so both columns will read `False` everywhere. Either drop them or define feasibility over the thirteen and say so, so § 4 does not report a meaningless zero.
- § 5 labels every axis `sensitivity`. STUDY_POLICY § 9 defines sensitivity as expecting a monotone response and full feasibility. The record's object is the difference channel, for which that holds trivially (identically zero); say in § 5 that the framing attaches to the difference, and that the LCOE response and its fence crossings are reported as facts of the run, not as claims. The record nearly says this; make it explicit so the post-run judgment has something exact to judge against.
- "Differs first at …" will be by sorted coordinate `(R, a, I_coil, n_e0, T_i0)`, not by execution order (`sorted(rows)`, line 142). Fine, but name the order in the record.
- The summary truncates differing channel lists to five per point (`[:5]`). Keep the full list in a sidecar if any difference appears; five is enough only when the answer is zero.
