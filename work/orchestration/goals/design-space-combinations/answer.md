## 1. The question and the short answer

**Question.** Which combinations of the choices the implemented models expose (plasma profile, coolant arrangement, conversion system, equipment selection, operating parameters) that neither Stellaris nor ARIES already uses can be assembled and evaluated from existing definitions, which of them execute and satisfy the evaluated engineering checks, which need new model behavior, and does varying the choices together reveal at least one interaction that one-at-a-time evaluation would conceal?

**Answer.** (1) *What is exposed and what is compatible.* The two live packages expose 704 (Stellaris) and 424 (ARIES) entry keys, every one classified mechanically and the designer's choices among them curated into definition-level alternatives for the five choice kinds with their interfaces, MR-7 roles and domain guards (`evidence/choice-inventory.md`). Of the 23 pairings the compatibility map classifies (`evidence/compatibility-map.md` § 2, fresh-reviewed), 12 are compatible by interface and operating range (two with a qualification: P5's range is untested, E2 holds only above the salt window), 4 are incompatible by range or interface as the bodies stand, 4 need new model behavior, and 3 are already in use by one plant. The steam/Brayton substitution the brief warned about is not promised: the two conversion systems do not share a heat-supply interface (the Brayton reads fluid-agnostic UA stages; the steam cycle reads a salt loop whose 465 → 270 °C window is a constant inside the Stellaris exchanger body), so only the ARIES divertor circuit (700 °C) could feed the steam path, and the blanket helium (456 °C), PbLi and the merged network cannot without new definitions (L-001). (2) *Which combinations execute and satisfy the checks.* Four assemblies were built from existing definitions only (WI-093: 25 distinct definitions instantiated directly in new pairings, 29 counting the four the plasma part owns; 21 reviewed bodies copied with only their import prefix changed, no new definition, no body change) and evaluated in 11 cases with receipts, all executing. **Three previously untested, compatible combinations satisfy every evaluated engineering check:** the Stellaris helium loop driving the ARIES three-stage Brayton chain at 2,500 kg/s with re-selected ratings (8 of 8 checks; 426.58 MW net from 3,301.21 MW of loop heat, none unremoved), and the Stellaris parabolic plasma feeding the ARIES deposition, branch, Brayton, electrical and fuel chain at a peak density of 4.2e20 m⁻³ or with a peaked density profile (13 of 13 checks each; 509.2 and 567.2 MW net). The six other cases of those two assemblies, the baseline included, execute with named checks failed; the lumped efficiency law executes on the three ARIES branch temperatures with its domain constraint speaking (one branch inside, two outside); the purchase law on the Stellaris circulator satisfies its one screen at 8 and 12 MW and fails it at 5 MW (§ 3). (3) *Which need new behavior.* Feeding the ARIES hollow-profile plasma to the Stellaris plant (six consumed outputs missing), any PbLi or merged-network stream to the steam path (no exchanger definition), the lumped efficiency law on three branches (no combining rule), and a Stellaris circulator law on ARIES streams above the salt window; each is named with the missing relationship (§ 4). No attempted assembly needed a definition or body change to execute. (4) *Interactions.* The 52-point sealed study on the ARIES package establishes three (§ 5): recuperation × source temperature is a limiting-check change (145.701 MW difference of differences on net at 0.95 recuperation and −60 K on the 891 MW base, from 162.118 MW of heat the bound helium stage can no longer accept), not a ranking reversal within ±60 K, and its presence at the 423 MW point depends on the turbine-efficiency assumption; the sign of the coolant-flow effect on net depends on the pump-power law; and the exchanger-arrangement ranking by net reverses between peak densities 5.5e20 and 5.75e20 m⁻³, with heat removal, not fuel processing or the compressor, the first check to bind. The round-2 assemblies add two single-evaluation observations the component calculations conceal (not established interactions under the goal's definition, § 5): lowering the Brayton stage pressure ratio from 1.518 to 1.35 raises net electricity from 426.58 to 575.23 MW while leaving 278.15 MW of a 773 K source unremoved (the objective and the heat-removal requirement move in opposite directions under one choice), and flattening the plasma temperature profile (alpha_T 1.19 → 0.5) nearly doubles fusion power to 5,047 MW and fails fuel processing and both blanket duty ratings together while the compressor never limits and net moves only 810 → 831 MW.

## 4. Characterization of the failed and the new-behavior combinations

- **Operating range, declared by a constraint**: H7 and C-4, the divertor and PbLi temperatures above the Kovari fit's 642 °C ceiling: executes, the constraint reads violated, the value is published unclamped.

## 5. Part B — interactions

**The sealed study** (`exploration/aries_integrated/studies/20260926-aries-design-choice-interactions/`, 52 points on the unchanged ARIES package, snapshot `25f6bb69…`, every point verified against the package-owned oracle, record § 1–17; reading `results/interactions.md`; fresh round-1 review FINDINGS none blocking):

- **B1 recuperation × blanket heat-source temperature.** Where every stage removes all its heat, the source temperature level (all three outlet limits ±60 K at unchanged duty and flow) is inert on net, efficiency and turbine inlet, so the preferred recuperation is 0.95 at every level on both bases. The interaction is a limiting-check change: at 0.95 recuperation and −60 K on the 891 MW base the heater inlet (541 K) rises toward the lowered helium limit and the stage can no longer accept 162.118 MW (net 745.301 instead of 891.002; difference of differences 145.701 MW), with heat removal the violated check; there is no ranking reversal within ±60 K. Assumption test: at the 423 MW point the effect is absent at turbine efficiency 0.93 and present at 0.90 (36.102 MW unremoved), and the mechanism predicts both (L-003).
- **B2 coolant flow × equipment and power demands.** Under the cubic pump proxy, more helium flow costs net everywhere and, into a bound helium stage, adds recovered friction heat the stage cannot remove (+104.555 and +160.975 MW unmet per step, net −116.004 and −178.296) until the helium duty rating fails; under the fixed pump law flow changes nothing but the pump capacity screen. The bound stage is limited by the cycle-side inlet temperature, so more primary flow recovers only 1.6 and 0.9 MW: the sign of the flow effect depends on the pump-power law, an assumption one-at-a-time evaluation of the closure would not expose (L-004).
- **B3 plasma profile × which equipment becomes limiting.** Over peak densities 4.75–5.75e20 m⁻³ at hollowness 0.66 the first check to bind as core output rises is helium-stage heat removal, never fuel processing (margin ≥ 1.363e22 atoms/s) nor the compressor; the exchanger arrangement changes where it binds, and the net ranking of the arrangements reverses between 5.5e20 (series better) and 5.75e20 (network better) (L-005).

**On the assemblies** (round 2, receipts in § 3): the pressure-ratio interaction on C-1 (net up 148.65 MW, heat removal from satisfied to 278.15 MW unmet, under one choice) and the profile interaction on C-2 (alpha_T 0.5 fails fuel processing and both blanket duty ratings together, nothing ordering them; alpha_n 1.0 and n_e0 4.2e20 clear every check; the compressor never limits because the 1,400 kg/s stream accepts only what it can carry). These are single evaluations with identities verified, not a factorial study; they are offered as what the assemblies show, not as established interactions under the goal's definition.
