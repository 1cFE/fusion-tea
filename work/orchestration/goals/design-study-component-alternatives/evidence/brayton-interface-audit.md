## Finding

[AGENT] The Stellaris primary loop is the better starting boundary: it already owns a duty, hot temperature, required return and flow relation. The existing Brayton chain can meet it at explicitly selected operating points with no bypass. That resolves the bypass-specific omission for those points, but the unchanged package still omits cooling electricity and does not qualify the recuperator or cooler equipment. Its historical net and LCOE cannot be used as complete matched-comparison results.

## Heat matching and return closure

The network closure computes counterflow effectiveness from chosen conductance and the two heat-capacity rates. It transfers `min(available heat, capability)`, then solves the recuperated cycle's operating temperature. Critically, when capability exceeds available heat, it reports an effective hot temperature `secondary_inlet + transferred/coefficient`, below the supplied hot limit, and a corresponding return. Those outputs do not demonstrate the actual fixed-temperature source loop. See `exploration/costed_loop_brayton/costed_loop_brayton_tea/handwritten/integrated_heat_electricity/network_heat_driven_closure_impl.py:28–63`, `:66–102`.

WI-095 adds the needed relationship: the source remains at its actual hot temperature; a solved primary bypass reduces exchanger flow and mixes back to the required return. It recalculates exchanger effectiveness at that reduced flow. Its numerical return tolerance is 1e-6 K, not permission for a physical deficit. Valve hardware, price and pressure loss are absent. Evidence: `models/library/analyses/loop_return_control.sysml:7–47`; implemented solve `exploration/costed_loop_brayton/bodies/loop_return_control/primary_bypass_control_impl.py:46–92`; bindings and required return `models/designs/costed_loop_brayton/costed_loop_brayton.sysml:475–500`.

Existing verified examples establish two options:

- **No bypass:** select cycle flow and explicitly locate the pressure ratio where exchanger capability equals duty. At 2,500 kg/s, stage ratio approximately 1.4273 is one such point; 2,750 kg/s / 1.3629 is another. Area remains chosen at 50,000 m², with assumed U = 1,000 W/(m² K). These points remove the entire duty and return at the required temperature. They need no bypass hardware or bypass pressure-drop model. They have no margin against heat-removal failure and are not operating recommendations.
- **Bypass:** the original 2,500 kg/s / 1.5183 point needs approximately 31.1% bypass. The 1.43 point needs approximately 1.9%. Either comparison requires explicit treatment of missing bypass hardware, cost and pressure loss; an unchanged-package claim of complete conversion cost would fail the owner's boundary. The declared maximum of 1.0 establishes no practical bypass limit.

Evidence: `work/orchestration/goals/design-study-parameters/answer.md:13–35`, `:58–68`; WI-095 preservation and adverse-case verification `work/completed/20260926_WI-095_loop-return-control/report.md:20–40`. These are prior verified results; this audit did not rerun them.

## Machines, heat sink and electricity

The cycle uses three compressors with two intercoolers, one turbine, passive recuperation and a precooler. Each cooler forces its outlet to 308.15 K and calculates signed heat. No cooling-water flow, pump demand or exchanger conductance is required by that cooler body. Cycle rejection is the negative sum of those three cooler heats. Evidence: assembly `models/designs/costed_loop_brayton/costed_loop_brayton.sysml:79–87`, `:106–153`, `:297–310`, `:431–440`; actual cooler body `exploration/costed_loop_brayton/costed_loop_brayton_tea/handwritten/ideal_gas_brayton_components/fixed_outlet_conditioning_impl.py:16–27`.

Electrical accounting is explicit: compressor demand is subtracted from turbine shaft work; the generator takes 98% of positive remaining shaft work. Negative shaft work is imported at the motor efficiency. Primary pumps and the auxiliary register are then subtracted. The inherited register is 40 MW heating wall-plug, 10 cryogenic, 5 fuel, 5 control and 5 other. These are plant loads, not an isolated conversion subsystem definition. Evidence: assembly `:312–340`; `exploration/costed_loop_brayton/costed_loop_brayton_tea/handwritten/integrated_heat_electricity/plant_electrical_balance_impl.py:8–28`. Thus the historical starting net is explained by 666.9 MW gross minus 175.3 MW primary pumping minus 65 MW register, approximately 426.6 MW; it excludes cooling pumping (`work/orchestration/goals/design-study-parameters/answer.md:7`, `:68`).

The existing `Cooling Water Rejection` definition is a candidate for adding circulation accounting: it solves flow from rejection heat and water enthalpy rise including its own pump electricity. However, its interface and approach check are condenser-specific. Applying it to Brayton's variable-temperature gas coolers requires a reviewed interpretation or small extension; renaming the input does not establish all terminal approaches or installed cooler capacity. Existing body: `exploration/stellarator_e2e/generated/handwritten/mfe_matched_steam_cycle/cooling_water_rejection_impl.py:23–58`. Its current domain is water inlet/outlet within 20–60 °C and positive heat-removal denominator; site qualification remains false.

The cycle also assumes a fixed 4.5% pressure loss, independent of flow; it is not a hydraulic prediction (`models/designs/costed_loop_brayton/costed_loop_brayton.sysml:172–179`; `models/library/analyses/ideal_gas_brayton_components.sysml:52–59`). The primary loop's loss law is separate. Matched no-bypass points avoid bypass-specific omitted losses, not these broader equipment qualifications.
