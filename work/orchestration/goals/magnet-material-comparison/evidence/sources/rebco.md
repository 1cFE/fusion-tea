# REBCO evidence note (T-002, request REQ-MMC-REBCO-01)

Scope: REBCO tape Ic(B, T) near 15–25 K over 8–20 T at the worst field orientation, tape construction, and one fusion REBCO winding with its tape-to-winding conversion. The note records evidence. It builds no model and mints no DI.

Headline: the tape side is covered at 20 K, from 5 to 24 T, for field perpendicular to the tape. The temperature law near 20 K is covered in form, and its parameter comes from different tapes. The winding side has one sourced point: the SPARC TFMC at 20 K and 20.1 T. The conversion from tape to winding-pack current density is not sourced, so winding-pack capability at a common lower field (about 12 T) cannot be computed from these sources alone.

## Equations

- **Molodyk, field law.** Jc ~ H^−α with α ≈ 0.6 at 20 K and ≈ 0.7 at 4.2 K (`output.md:93`). No prefactor and no fit interval are printed. Jc here is the layer critical current density, but the shape carries over to Ic of a given tape. The paper's own 20 K curves in Fig. 1a follow about 0.55 below 10 T and steepen to about 0.85–1.0 between 10 and 20 T. So "α ≈ 0.6" describes the low-field part of the curve, not the 8–20 T range the comparison needs.
- **Senatore eq. (1).** Jc(T, B) = Jc(T = 0, B)·e^(−T/T*), raw.pdf p.6. The rendered equation confirms the form. T* is in kelvin and depends on B and θ (Fig. 3). Jc is per REBCO-layer cross-section.
- **Senatore eq. (2).** Jc(T, B) = Jc(T = 0, B = 0)·e^(−T/T*)·B^(−α), raw.pdf p.10 (rendered). Two ambiguities. First, B^(−α) with a prefactor labelled "B = 0" is singular at zero field. The paper limits it to about 0.1 T up to about 60% of B_irr, so B must be read as B/(1 T). Second, eq. (2) is written with a single T*, but the paper's Fig. 3 shows T* changing with B, by about 7 K between 8 and 19 T for SuperOx. Using one T* across 8–20 T is an approximation. Current-density denominator: REBCO layer only. Engineering current density needs the tape's total cross-section (Table I).
- **Model context (not a source).** The current model uses Ic = 200 A per 4 mm × (B/20 T)^−0.6 at 20 K. The 200 A anchor matches the Molodyk production average (1.13 × 175 A ≈ 198 A). The −0.6 shape below 20 T is not supported by Molodyk Fig. 1a. Anchored at 20 T, it sits about 18–22% below that paper's measured 20 K curve at 8–12 T, so it is conservative for this tape. Senatore reports a constant power law with α ≈ 0.55–0.75 over 0.5–19 T at T ≤ 30 K, which would support a law of this shape, but on 2015 tapes. The two sources disagree about curvature, and this note does not resolve the disagreement (see Gaps).
- **Area bases.** Molodyk JE: whole wire, 4 mm × 56 µm. Senatore Jc: REBCO layer. TFMC 153 A/mm²: labelled "WP current density", with no stated area (whether it includes plate steel, helium channels or the case is not given). No strand, cable or conductor area is stated for the TFMC.

## Gaps

- **No sourced tape-to-winding conversion.** Tapes per turn, stack grading, and the copper, solder, Nitronic plate and helium-channel fractions of the TFMC winding pack are not in the captured overview. The same is true for VIPER. Without them the comparison cannot compute REBCO winding-pack capability at a common lower field from tape Ic. It can only quote the TFMC's own point (153 A/mm² at 20.1 T, 20 K) and the SPARC TF design point (94 A/mm² at 23 T, 8–17 K). The 0.17 ratio is illustrative and must not be used as a conversion.
- **No REBCO winding data near 12–13 T.** Both winding points are at 20–23 T. Scaling the TFMC current density down to about 12 T would require the missing conversion and an assumption about the operating fraction of Ic.
- **No-insulation versus insulated.** The TFMC current density depends on having no turn insulation and on the soldered stack-in-plate design. It does not transfer to an insulated or cable-in-conduit REBCO winding, which is the closer analogue to an Nb₃Sn winding pack. The comparison cannot claim REBCO winding-pack current density for an insulated design from these sources.
- **Field-law conflict (surfaced, not resolved).** Molodyk's stated α ≈ 0.6 has no fit interval. Its plotted 20 K curves steepen above about 10 T, while Senatore reports a constant power law up to 19 T on other tapes. The comparison cannot claim that a single −0.6 exponent represents the 2021 fusion tape across 8–20 T. Conclusions that depend on the shape of the law below 20 T should stay parked until the owner chooses a basis: the digitized Fig. 1a curve, the Senatore form, or a vendor dataset.
- **Extrapolated 20 T statistics.** Part of Molodyk's 20 K, 20 T production data were extrapolated from 0–12 T (`output.md:153`). The production-average 198 A is therefore partly extrapolated, although the three high-field samples in Fig. 1a were measured to 20–24 T.
- **Temperature near 20 K.** No captured source measures high-field Ic at 15 or 25 K on the fusion YBCO tape. The −4.5 %/K sensitivity rests on Senatore's exponential form, the Senatore T* range from 2015 tapes, and a two-temperature T* inferred from Molodyk.
- **Electric-field criterion.** Molodyk does not state the in-field Ic criterion. Its 77 K calibration uses 1 µV/cm (`output.md:135`). Senatore uses 0.1 µV/cm.
- **Degradation.** No source gives cable or winding Ic degradation relative to tape (cycling, handling, strain). Strain limits are in pre-existing sources, and irradiation is outside this request.
