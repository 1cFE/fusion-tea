## Equations

- **Green Eq. 1 (printed p2):** C(M$) ≈ 3.1 [R(kW)]^0.65, 2015 dollars, R = refrigeration at 4.5 K. The text limits it to machines >100 W, but Fig. 1 plots machines from 10 W; the line itself starts near 30 W.
- **Green Eq. 2 (printed p2):** η(%) = 15.5 [R(kW)]^0.23, R = 4.5 K refrigerator capacity. Fig. 2's caption says "4.2 K refrigerators" while its axis says 4.5 K; the text calls the same machines 4.5 K. Efficiency is plotted against refrigeration delivered, not compressor input (the wave-1 note's size-variable question). Green's introduction (printed p1) says the 1974 NBS work plotted cost against compressor input power instead; this paper does not.
- **Green Eqs. 3–4 (printed p4):** η = 100 [Q₁(300 − T₁)/T₁ (+ Q₂(300 − T₂)/T₂)] / P_COM, with P_COM the vendor-rated compressor input at 60 Hz. A two-stage cooler's first-stage duty is credited in its efficiency.
- **Green small-cooler fits (printed p5, p7):** efficiency η(%) = 3.1 + 0.91 ln R (4.2 K), 0.2 + 2.17 ln R (20 K), −2.8 + 2.95 ln R (40 K); no fit at 77 K. Cost C(k$) = 40 R^0.323 (4.2 K), 9.29 R^0.412 (20 K), 3.15 R^0.56 (40 K), 1.81 R^0.57 (77 K). R in W; the ln-fit unit is inferred from the axes.
- **Končar Eq. 1 (PDF p3):** Q_rad = σ A_c ε_r (T_h⁴ − T_c⁴), with Eq. 2 ε_r = 1/[1/ε_c + (1/ε_h − 1) A_c/A_h] and Eq. 3 for A_h ≈ A_c. No emissivity values are printed.
- **Končar Eq. 4 (PDF p4):** Q_cnd = A_cnd (1/L_cnd) ∫_{T_c}^{T_h} k(T) dT. No support geometry or k(T) data are printed.
- **Končar Eq. 5 (PDF p4):** P_i = Q_i (T_amb − T_c)/T_c with T_amb = 293 K (not 300 K as in Strobridge and Green). The printed Carnot factor 72.2 at 4 K is (293 − 4)/4 = 72.25.
- **Končar figure units:** Figs. 1–3 label the y-axes in kW but plot W (Fig. 1 reaches 70,000 while the text gives 3.3 kW at 100 K). Table 2 Case 4 prints "2.789", which from context is 2,789 kW.

## Gaps

- **Nuclear heating is not isolated.** Končar excludes it; ITER folds it into a 10.9 kW averaged pulsed load together with AC and eddy losses. The comparison cannot yet say how large the neutron load on a 4.5 K or 20 K winding is, or how it scales with shielding. This is the load most likely to differ between concepts. The open EU-DEMO paper that gives a nuclear-heat density law is queued (see Run).
- **No per-area radiation or per-support conduction values.** Only system totals (DEMO 1.3 kW radiation, 4.4 kW anchor conduction; ITER 11.8 kW static). The black-body bound shows that radiation from an 80 K shield barely changes between a 4 K and a 20 K cold mass (ratio 0.996). Conduction from 80 K into the cold mass would fall at 20 K, but no source here gives the integral of k(T) dT for support steel to size that drop.
- **Both budgets are for 4–4.5 K LTS machines.** No fusion 20 K HTS magnet heat-load budget was found. The comparison can carry the 4.5 K magnitudes and the Carnot ratio, but a 20 K budget would be assembled from those magnitudes, not sourced.
- **ITER numbers are the 2001 design, not the as-built plant** (installed 75 kW at 4.5 K per the wave-1 iter.org source). ITER's pulsed term does not transfer to a steady-state reactor.
- **Green Eq. 2 at ITER or DEMO scale is extrapolation** beyond the largest plotted machine (about 35–40 kW), and there is no large-plant law at 20 K; the 20 K fits are for coolers of at most about 400 W.
- **Joints:** one measured prototype resistance; the number of joints per coil is not sourced, so no total joint load.
