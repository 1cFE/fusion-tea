## Equations

- **Carnot specific power (Strobridge Eq. 1, printed p2):** (W_c/Q)_Carnot = (T0 − T)/T, with W_c the net input (compression minus expander output), T0 "nominally 300 K". Confirmed on the page image. Table 1 uses T = normal boiling point (4.2 K, 20.4 K), not 4.5 K or 20 K; recompute at the modeled temperature.
- **Percent Carnot (Strobridge Eq. 2, printed p4):** 100 × (W_c/Q)_Carnot / (W_c/Q)_Actual. Actual input is installed drive power, not measured motor input, so Fig. 1 efficiencies lean low where motors are oversized; for units under 10 kW the LN2 precooling power is not charged, so those lean high.
- **Size variable ambiguity.** Strobridge plots efficiency against refrigeration capacity in watts of cooling. At matched magnetic duty and a similar heat load, a 20 K plant then sits at the same point on the curve as a 4.5 K plant, but it needs about 4.7× less compressor power. If plant efficiency actually tracks compressor size (Green's later cost work plots against input power; see Green p1 introduction, triage only), the 20 K plant would sit lower on the curve. The note does not resolve which size variable governs; it matters when loads are small.
- **Current lead optimum (Ballarino slide 14):** Q_C,min = √(L0 I² (T_H² − T_C²)), so Q_C,min/I = √(L0(T_H² − T_C²)) = 47 W/kA for T_H = 300 K, T_C = 4.2 K. Per current in the lead, not per area. The heat into the cold end barely changes between 4.2 K and 20 K; the saving at 20 K is entirely in the Carnot factor.
- **Green Eq. 1–2 (not citable until re-captured):** C(M$2015) ≈ 3.1 R(kW)^0.65 for refrigerators >100 W, η(%) = 15.5 R(kW)^0.23. R is 4.5 K refrigeration in kW. Eq. 2 gives ≈30 % at 18 kW, consistent with DI-009's LHC figure; at 75 kW it gives ≈42 %, which lies above every plotted machine and should be treated as extrapolation.

## Gaps

- **No large-plant single-temperature measurement near 20 K for helium.** Strobridge's 10–30 K points at large capacity are 1960s–70s hydrogen liquefiers. The comparison can claim "percent of Carnot is not observed to rise with temperature at fixed capacity" but not a measured modern 20 K helium plant efficiency. ESS target-moderator cryoplant (30.3 kW at 15 K) was found in search but no open paper with electrical input was located.
- **No modern measured 4.5 K plant law is citable yet.** Green Eq. 2 is blocked by the capture defect; LHC measured-performance papers were not captured (capture limit). Until then the 4.5 K basis is Strobridge's curve plus ITER's plant-wide 0.24, which mixes 4.5 K and 80 K loads.
- **Magnet cold-load categories are not sourced by this run.** Nuclear heating, AC and joint losses, radiation per area to the cold mass, and support conduction magnitudes are all unavailable. Without them the comparison cannot claim how the 4.5 K and 20 K heat budgets differ in size, only how the cost per watt differs (the Carnot ratio of ≈4.7). Končar et al. (queued) gives DEMO radiation and support conduction to 4 K magnets versus shield temperature; the ITER and DEMO budget papers are queued.
- **ITER allocation basis is ambiguous.** The 45/40/15 % split does not say whether it is of 4.5 K-equivalent capacity or electrical input.
- **Current leads:** only ideal optima (47 W/kA conduction-cooled, 1.1 W/kA gas-cooled, HTS ×10 reduction into the bath). No qualified high-current fusion lead figure, and no 20 K-specific HTS lead coefficient.
