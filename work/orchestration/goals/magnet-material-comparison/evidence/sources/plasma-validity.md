# Evidence note — plasma-scaling validity at a 4–5 T HELIAS-class operating point (REQ-MMC-PLASMA-01, T-011 Worker B)

Question (request file): over what ranges is ISS04 fitted, what does `f_ren` mean off the fitted point, and what do the Stellaris paper and the stellarator-PROCESS papers say about extrapolating ISS04 and their beta limit to 4–5 T on axis instead of 9 T. The plant model holds `f_ren = 1.0` from Stellaris Tables 4/5 (`exploration/stellarator_e2e/models/designs/stellarator_09/stellarator_plant.sysml:1000`), uses ISS04 as `0.134 f_ren a^2.28 B^0.84 iota^0.41 n19^0.54 R^0.64 P^-0.61` (`models/analyses/mfe_plasma_sustainment.sysml:58-60`), imposes beta ≤ 5 % (`stellarator_plant.sysml:2136`), and declares no validity band (`plant-chain-audit.md` § 3 D8).

Short answer, stated fully in § Gaps: a 4.3–4.7 T point of this configuration with `f_ren = 1.0` is **undeclared** by every registered source and **unsupported** by the closest sourced analogues. Every HELIAS-class design point in the corpus at 4.75–5.9 T closes only with an enhancement of 1.33–1.8 over the empirical scaling and sits at or near its 4.2–5 % beta limit. No source prints a validity band for ISS04 in field; the ISS04 authors say only that the expression was chosen because a dimensionally-correct form is needed for extrapolation, and Warmer 2016 says outright that 1-D transport finds `f_ren` "quite different for W7-X and a HELIAS".

## Equations

- Stellaris eq. A.7 (raw PDF p. 32): `tau_E = 0.134 f_ren a^2.28 B^0.84 iota_2/3^0.41 n19^0.54 R^0.64 P^-0.61` [s; m, T, 10¹⁹ m⁻³, MW]. Eq. A.8 substitutes `P = W/tau_E`: `tau_E = 0.152 f_ren^2.56 a^2.72 B^2.15 iota_2/3^1.05 n19^-0.18 R^0.08 T_keV^-1.56`. The model's closed form `(C W^-0.61)^(1/0.39)` is the same substitution (`mfe_plasma_sustainment.sysml:58-60`); its field response `B^(0.84/0.39) = B^2.15` matches A.8.
- Lion 2021 eq. (9) and thesis eq. (1.26): density exponent **0.52**, prefactor 0.134 (n in 10¹⁹) or 0.444 (n in 10²⁰). Ambiguity: 0.52 (PROCESS papers) vs 0.54 (Stellaris, model). The 2004 preliminary fit has 0.55. Resolution needs the queued journal paper; effect at the Stellaris density ≈ 7 % in `tau_E` (derived above), which is small against the factor 1.4–1.8 question below but should be settled.
- Yamada 2004 eq. (3), dimensionless form: `tau/tau_Bohm ∝ rho*^-0.90 beta^-0.14 nu_b*^-0.01`. The fitted beta dependence is weak and slightly negative; the database's beta coverage is not tabulated in this version.
- Warmer eqs. (2)–(4), HELIAS-line transformations (preprint p. 6): `rho* ~ B*^-0.8104 P*^0.1934 n*^-0.2302`, `nu* ~ B*^0.2418 P*^-0.7737 n*^1.9207`, `beta* ~ B*^-0.6209 P*^0.3868 n*^0.5397`. No current density enters any equation in this note (plasma scalings only).

## Gaps

1. **ISS04 fitted ranges in B, n, a, R, beta, P are unavailable in the repository.** The 2004 preprint gives device names, LHD size (R 3.9 m, a 0.6 m, so B ≲ 3 T), iota 1.3–2.2 from TJ-II and a rho*–nu_b* figure, not a range table; the journal paper that prints it is queued. Consequence: no source-backed statement that either 9 T or 4.5 T is "inside" the fit; both are far above every database device's field and size, so the comparison must call both points extrapolations of the same law and cannot claim one is more inside than the other on field alone.
2. **No source states a validity band for ISS04 or `f_ren` in field.** Stellaris, Lion 2021, the thesis and Warmer all use ISS04 with a free or capped `f_ren` and never print a band. The model's D8 "undeclared" stands as the correct reading of the sources.
3. **`f_ren = 1.0` at 4.3–4.7 T on this configuration is unsupported by every sourced analogue.** HELIAS-class points at 4.75–5.9 T close only with enhancement 1.33–1.8 over the empirical scaling (thesis Table 4.3: 1.38 and 1.77 at 5.8 T; Beidler: 1.69 and 1.33 over ISS95 at 4.75–5.0 T; Warmer caps 1.5–1.8 at 4–5.6 T) and every one sits at or near a 4.2–5 % beta limit. Stellaris closes at 9 T with 1.0 and 3 % beta. The audit's `[AGENT]` estimate (beta ≈ 11 % at 4.34 T at fixed pressure; ≈ 4.8× heating at fixed W) is consistent with these sources: the thesis says lower field "would require a higher β limit". The comparison cannot present an Nb₃Sn point at `f_ren = 1.0`, 4.3–4.7 T as a supported plant design point; it is an unsupported extrapolation of the model's own anchor.
4. **The beta limit is not field-dependent in any source** (a fixed 5 % input in PROCESS; 4.5 % linear / 5–6 % predicted in Warmer; 3 % design with surfaces intact to 4 % in Stellaris). So a low-field point is limited by beta ∝ B⁻² at fixed pressure, not by a moving limit; the model's 5 % is a PROCESS input, not a Stellaris statement.
5. **What would make a 4.3–4.7 T point supported:** (a) a re-chosen operating point (density, temperature, and size) at ⟨β⟩ ≤ 5 % with `f_ren` supplied and labelled as an assumption in the sourced 1.4–1.8 range (thesis Table 4.6, Warmer Table 2), carrying the W7-X evidence that 1.4 is the best transient value; or (b) a sourced HELIAS design point taken whole (thesis Table 4.3 conservative: 17.1 m, 5.81 T, f_ren 1.38, beta 5 %, Nb₃Sn 11.6 T on coils) as a second plasma anchor, which is a different configuration and coil set (Worker C's domain). Neither option keeps `f_ren = 1.0` fixed across 9 T and 4.5 T, so the comparison must state that the two materials are compared under different confinement assumptions.
6. **Dinklage 2007 (configuration factor vs effective ripple) is queued**; it would give a physics basis for `f_ren` per configuration, not a field band. Its absence does not change gaps 1–5.
