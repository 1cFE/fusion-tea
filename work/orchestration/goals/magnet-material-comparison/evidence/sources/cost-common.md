# Evidence note — cost-common (REQ-MMC-COST-02)

Request: `knowledge/research/requests/REQ-MMC-COST-02.json`. Worker brief: `evidence/briefs/t002-research-common.md` with the wave-2 changes and the `cost-common` section of `evidence/briefs/t003-research-wave2.md`.

Bottom line: Chislett-McDonald et al. 2022 prices both conductors at one common reference, 6 T and 4.2 K, in USD/kA·m. The prices are Nb₃Sn 8.0, REBCO ≈80 today, 30 near-term and 10 at volume, and Nb-Ti 1.7. The paper scales these to operating conditions by the inverse of whole-strand or whole-tape Jc (its Eq 9). The prices are secondary and cover strand or tape only. The REBCO numbers are Cooley & Pong's 20 T, 4.2 K prices re-referenced to 6 T. A PROCESS-independent cross-check (Bottura & Bordini, CERN orders, normalised at 12 T and 4.2 K) puts REBCO at about 2–4× Nb₃Sn per kA·m in 2023–24, in arbitrary units only.

## Equations

- **C-M Eq 8** (PDF p17, image inspected): Jc(B,T,ε) = A* [T*c (1 − t²)]² [B*c2]^(n−3) b^(p−1) (1 − b)^q, with b = B/B*c2 and t = T/T*c. Current density is per whole strand or whole tape (engineering Je, p17 top), not non-Cu and not cable. Strain is fixed at −0.5 % applied (−1.0 % intrinsic) for all conductors. Eq 8 has no explicit strain function. Table 7 (p54) nonetheless prints strain parameters u, w, c2, c3, c4 and εm, and repeats the v and s columns. It is ambiguous whether the printed T*c and B*c2(0) are strain-free or at −0.5 %. B*c2(T) = B*c2(0)(1 − t^ν) for LTS and B*c2(0)(1 − t)^s for REBCO. Table 7 values: Nb₃Sn A* = 2.45×10⁷ A m⁻² K⁻² T^(3−n), T*c = 16.89 K, B*c2(0) = 28.54 T, p = 0.47, q = 1.95, n = 2.34, ν = 1.45. REBCO A* = 1.24×10³, T*c = 184.98 K, B*c2(0) = 138.97 T, p = 0.45, q = 1.44, n = 3.33, s = 5.27 (fit parameters, not physical Tc or Bc2). Nb-Ti A* = 3.42×10⁸, T*c = 9.04 K, B*c2(0) = 14.86 T, p = 0.49, q = 0.56, n = 1.83, ν = 1.42. My evaluation reproduces the paper's own “≈1.7× lower at 20 K” (1.719). That supports this reading of the table.
- **C-M Eq 9** (PDF p17): Cost(B,T) = Cost(Bref,Tref) × Jc(Bref,Tref)/Jc(B,T), with Bref = 6 T and Tref = 4.2 K for all three materials. It assumes manufacturing cost per metre is independent of Jc.
- **C-M cable assumptions** (PDF p17): LTS cable conductor is 69 % Cu and 31 % superconductor, with 33 % helium void. REBCO uses stacked tapes with 20 % void. Operating current is 100 kA, limited to 50 % of cable Ic. These set PROCESS cable sizing and are not part of the $/kA·m price.
- **Unit and currency ambiguity.** The prices are stated “in 2021 costs” (p17), but Table 2 states “All costs are in 1990 M$” (p49). The paper does not say whether the 2021 prices were deflated by 2.13 before entering PROCESS. So the TF cable line (130 vs 98 M$₁₉₉₀) cannot be converted back to $/kA·m with confidence.

## Gaps

- **No primary, currency-dated absolute price at the goal's matched point.** Both C-M prices are secondary and sit at 6 T, 4.2 K. Values at 12–13 T and at 20 K for REBCO come only from C-M's own Jc law, and the REBCO fit's measured domain is not printed in C-M. This prevents claiming an absolute strand or tape cost at matched duty tighter than the spread of the REBCO price scenarios. That spread is a factor of 8 (10 to 80 at 6 T), and the ratio to Nb₃Sn at 12 T runs from 0.6× to 5× at 4.5 K and from 1.1× to 8.5× at 20 K.
- **Nb₃Sn 8.0 USD/kA·m is untraced.** Its origin, Lee et al. 2015 FED, is a Durham/CCFE paper with overlapping authors (Surrey, Hampshire). Its open copy is behind a bot check (queued), so its basis (field, temperature, year, supplier quotes vs model) is unknown. Independence from the Durham group is not established.
- **Independence from PROCESS defaults (answer).** The conductor prices are external inputs (Lee 2015; Cooley & Pong 2016; IEA 2015), not PROCESS defaults. C-M replaced PROCESS's native $/kg model with $/kA·m and report magnet costs within 20 % of the $/kg model (p18). The Jc laws are Durham measurement fits. Every plant-level cost, including the cryoplant 88 vs 95 M$ and the linear cryoplant scaling, is a PROCESS cost-model output in 1990 USD, so none of those is independent. The REBCO price and the pre-existing C&P source share one origin, so they are not two independent confirmations.
- **REBCO reference temperature (answer).** C-M references REBCO to 4.2 K, not 77 K and not 20 K. Its upstream source (C&P) is also at 4.2 K, at 20 T. C-M costs no plant at 20 K in its tables; the 20 K case is prose only (p6–7).
- **Scope stops at strand or tape.** There is no $/kA·m for cabling, jacket, insulation or winding for either material. The comparison cannot claim conductor-level or winding-pack-level cost parity from these sources.
- **Cryoplant relation is weak.** “Approximately linear with cooling power” is asserted without data or fit, and “cooling power” is undefined. It is not reconciled with the sub-linear refrigerator cost laws being extracted by the cryo-loads class (Strobridge 1974; Green 2015). The comparison should not use C-M's 88 → 20 M$ as a cryoplant cost law.
- **B&B is in arbitrary units.** It supports only a REBCO/Nb₃Sn ratio band of ≈2–4× at 12 T, 4.2 K (2023–24, HEP R&D order volumes, REBCO worst orientation). It gives no absolute price and nothing at 20 K. Its 150–200 USD/kA·m REBCO figure has no stated conditions.
- **No DI proposed here.** A candidate insight, “REBCO strand cost per kA·m at 12 T is ≈2–4× Nb₃Sn in 2023–24 procurements; C-M's scenario prices bracket this”, would go through the owner's `/research` approval flow.
