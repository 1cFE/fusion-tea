Please proceed to Round 2. The question is:

Can REBCO’s additional field or winding-space capability improve the plant enough to offset its higher magnet cost, measured in LCOE?

Round 1 established the comparison at matched magnetic duty. Now pursue the consequences of letting each material support a different plant design.

### Scope and approach

Investigate the chain conductor choice → achievable field and winding geometry → plant performance and equipment → LCOE.

Start with the Round 1 finding that conductor requirements grow differently with field. Determine whether REBCO can support higher field, a smaller winding, or a smaller plant under credible construction assumptions.

Select one coherent plant model and explain why it supports this comparison. Model updates and source acquisition are authorized where needed.

Let each material have explicit design choices suited to it. Preserve the distinction between a supplied design and calculated requirements; no silent resizing to make constraints pass.

Resolve the known field/geometry limitations wherever the result depends on them. Do not infer plant benefits from a conductor field limit alone.

### All accounting must return to LCOE

Use consistent whole-plant accounting for both alternatives. Carry through changes in magnet purchases, construction, refrigeration, electrical consumption, affected plant equipment, replacements, and net electricity.

Do not add Round 1’s annualized subsystem cost to a plant model that already includes those accounts. Reconcile the accounting boundaries first.

Show:

A matched-duty comparison that connects to Round 1.
The best supported plant design points found for each material.
An LCOE breakdown explaining how the changed design improves or worsens the result.
The REBCO price at which the whole-plant preference changes, where a supported crossing exists.

### Pursue the interaction, not just another price sensitivity

Evaluate combinations of consequential choices and assumptions. In particular, test whether higher field or reduced size changes the value of lower REBCO prices, and whether construction limits or additional plant costs erase that benefit.

Choose a bounded search with justified ranges. A few well-explained, verified design points are more useful than a large sweep with weak physical support. Retain failed and unsupported cases and distinguish them from economically unattractive designs.

### Standard for the result

We want directional TEA evidence, not a fully qualified reactor or a forced REBCO win. A supported finding that the anticipated benefit disappears is useful.

However, subsystem savings alone do not answer this round. If a credible LCOE comparison cannot be established, identify the missing relationship and report partial completion rather than substituting an unsupported plant benefit.

Follow the native goal workflow and obtain focused independent review of the new physical relationships, comparison assumptions, and integrated accounting. Fix the missing package oracle_entry and retire the bogus source registration as part of the necessary cleanup.
