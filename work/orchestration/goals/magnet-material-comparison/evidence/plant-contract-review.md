# Review of the plant comparison contract r1 (T-012)

Fresh reviewer, brief `evidence/briefs/t012-plant-contract-review.md`. Artifact: `evidence/plant-contract.md` (r1). I did none of the work. Every judgment below is `[AGENT]`; quoted contract text is in quotation marks; model facts were checked at the cited file:line on the working tree (branch `goal/magnet-material-comparison`, 2026-09-30). ## Verdict: REVISE

The contract makes the owner's map executable in outline, and its accounting rule (§ 6) and package route (§ 9) follow the audit correctly. It cannot be released as r1 because three things in it would decide the map for reasons that are not the question: the held operating point turns most REBCO high-field designs into burn-hold failures (finding 1); the plant's ~35 captured equipment ratings are held while the design's thermal power moves (finding 2); and the "matched-duty connection" compares the two materials at different fields, so no plant-level Round 1 pair exists (finding 3). Each is a contract revision, not an owner ruling. The must-fix findings are labelling and placement corrections that the owner's question ("are those conditions supported by evidence?") depends on.

## Blocking findings

### 1. The held operating point makes enhanced-confinement and high-field REBCO designs "failed (burn hold)" by construction

- Contract: "`T_i0` 14.63 keV held; `n_e0` set by the policy to the matched fusion power 2,653 MW" (§ 5); statuses: "failed: any magnet, plasma, power-balance or wall/divertor check is violated" (§ 7). No mention of ignition or burn hold anywhere in §§ 5, 7, 8.
- Evidence: the plant's `burn_hold_ok` requires `p_aux_required ≥ 0`, and the model doc states that ignited points "read VIOLATED here … they are points this heating system cannot hold" (`exploration/stellarator_e2e/models/designs/stellarator_09/stellarator_plant.sysml:2407-2425`). The reference design sits 49.08 MW above ignition with `W_th` 519.9 MJ and `tau_E` 1.557 s (recorded at `exploration/stellarator_e2e/studies/20260907-burn-control/results/points.csv`, case `c3694`), so its loss power is 334 MW against about 285 MW of net alpha heating. The ISS04 closed form makes `tau_E ∝ f_ren^(1/0.39) = f_ren^2.56` and `∝ B^2.15` at fixed stored energy (`exploration/stellarator_e2e/models/analyses/mfe_plasma_sustainment.sysml:58-60`; `plasma-validity.md` § Equations, eq. A.8). At the reference design, `f_ren` 1.4 gives `p_aux_required ≈ −144 MW` and 1.8 gives `≈ −211 MW`: ignited, hence failed. At the held operating point the driven condition needs `B_axis ≲ 6.5 T` (≈ 18 T peak under the anchored ratio) at `f_ren` 1.4 and `≲ 4.8 T` (≈ 13 T peak) at 1.8; in the HELIAS-class cell the 24.9 T REBCO point is 11.7 T on axis and is ignited even at `f_ren` 1.0. Bigger plants at matched power are more ignited, not less (loss power falls faster than stored energy grows with `a`).
- Consequence: as written, the REBCO grid (18–24.9 T peak) has no supported design in roughly seven of nine cells, and the map would read "REBCO's field capability does not help" when the model is actually saying "a 2.65 GW plant at that field and confinement needs no heating and this heating system cannot hold it". That is an operating-point convention, not a material result. It also contradicts the brief's "Do not infer plant benefits [or penalties] from a conductor field limit alone" in spirit: the penalty is inferred from a held plasma temperature.
- Fix: (a) give the operating-point policy a second lever, `T_i0` from a declared ladder (e.g. 11, 13, 14.63, 16, 18 keV) chosen with `n_e0` so that `0 ≤ p_aux_required ≤ installed` at matched power, and record which lever value each design carries; (b) where no driven point exists at matched power, report the design as `ignited` (a distinct status or flag, not `failed`), because it says the coherent design at that confinement is smaller or lower-field, which is the owner's "smaller plant" lever (finding 7); (c) state in § 3.1 that the cell's `f_ren` is applied to designs spanning 4–12 T on axis although the sources say `f_ren` changes with transport regime (Warmer: "quite different for W7-X and a HELIAS"; `plasma-validity.md` row "`f_ren` definition (Warmer)"), so the uniform application is `[U]` away from each analogue's field.

### 2. The plant's captured equipment ratings are held while the design's thermal power moves, so most non-reference designs fail on package screens

- Contract: "Everything else (blanket, vessel, buildings, heat transport, conversion, heating capital, fuel cycle, O&M, replacements, availability, DCF): the plant's, unchanged, for both materials" (§ 6); "fusion power is the plant's supplied power class (turbine, BOP, heat transport all supplied at the reference class), so matched power keeps the non-magnet equipment comparable" (§ 5). § 7 names only four reference-common screens.
- Evidence: the plant carries about 35 offered-capacity screens (`stellarator_plant.sysml:2203-2339`: turbine gross, HP/LP flow and shaft, main/reheat UA, condenser rejection, condensate and feedwater flow/pressure/electric, water rejection/flow/head/electric, helium and salt loops, IHX, electric gross, magnet TF/PF electric). Their ratings are captured supplied values, e.g. `selected_gross_MWe` 1219.998, `rated_rejection_MW` 2109.62, `rated_water_electric_MW` 13.023, with `purchase_cost_per_module` "independent of subsequent operating demand" (`stellarator_plant.sysml:743-770, 845-870`). `water_electric_capacity_ok` is already violated at the reference (replay `s0-published`; audit § 5), so the captured ratings sit at or below zero margin. Thermal power is `p_th = mn·p_neutron + p_alpha + p_input + q_recovered` (audit § 2e), so matched fusion power does not hold `p_th`: a low-field, `f_ren` 1.0 design with `p_aux_required` several hundred MW raises `p_th` by that amount and flips the turbine, condenser and heat-rejection screens; a high-field or enhanced-confinement design lowers it and passes them with margin.
- Consequence: heavy-heating designs (every Nb₃Sn design in the `f_ren` 1.0 cells) are downgraded to `failed` by a captured turbine or cooling-water rating, not by physics; the asymmetry favours REBCO systematically and is invisible in the LCOE decomposition because the package purchase costs do not move.
- Fix: extend the § 5 policy rule already used for heating and cryo to every offered-capacity package: supply each rating at the design's computed demand × a declared margin, with the package purchase re-supplied on the plant's existing power-class scaling (audit § 2d: `(supplied power class/ref)^α`; WI-079 bases), and say so in § 6; or, if the reference package is deliberately held, add a status `capacity-limited (reference package)` distinct from `failed` and exclude those screens from "supported" with their margins reported. The same rule must cover `power_short` designs, which as written pay for a 2.65 GW conversion class while producing a fraction of it (§ 5 "otherwise `n_e0` at `beta = beta_limit` and the case carries `power_short = 1`"): state that the conversion class is held, or re-supply it at the design's power, and label the LCOE accordingly.

### 3. The "matched-duty connection" is not matched duty, and the REBCO grid has no point at the Nb₃Sn fields

- Contract: "Round 1's anchor-S pairs are the same duty on a fixed envelope. The plant-level comparison at that duty, with the Nb₃Sn winding at the highest field it supports on the same coil set, is reported first" (§ 1); REBCO grid "`B_peak` 18, 20, 22, 24.9 T"; Nb₃Sn "10, 11, 12 T (13 T edge)" (§ 5).
- Evidence: Round 1's matched points are "8, 9, 10, 11, 12 T; edge point 13 T; REBCO-only extension 14, 16, 18, 20 T" and anchor S's pairs are rankable at "8–13 T on the Stellaris envelope under common-C" (`comparison-contract.md` § 2; `answer.md` § What was compared and § Results at reference offers, "Winding fit"). No Round 1 pair exists at 24.9 T; Nb₃Sn is unsupported there. The owner asks for "A matched-duty comparison that connects to Round 1" (`owner-brief-round2.md`).
- Consequence: the first shown item compares REBCO at 24.9 T with Nb₃Sn at 12 T on the same coil set. That is a useful comparison ("each material at its own highest supported field on the reference coil set"), but it is not the matched-duty comparison, and nowhere in the grid do the two materials share a field, so the map cannot separate the magnet-account difference at equal duty from the plant response to a different duty. That separation is the owner's interaction question.
- Fix: add REBCO points at `B_peak` 10, 11, 12 T (the same supplied ampere-turns as the Nb₃Sn designs, each material's own supplied pack) to every cell, report the equal-duty pair per cell as the Round 1 connection (its LCOE difference is the magnet, cryo and structure accounts only), and rename the § 1 comparison to what it is. Cost: about 100 more designs.

## Must-fix findings

### 4. The evidence label is per cell, but the labels the contract itself assigns depend on the field and pack where each material's design sits

- Contract: "`f_ren` 1.0 — [S] at 9.0 T … at fields below 9 T this is [U]" (§ 3.1); "`peak_ratio` 2.7667 … [S] at the Stellaris pack (0.1296 m²), [U] at any other pack area" (§ 3.2); the map carries "the evidence label of the cell ([S]/[A]/[D]/[U] of its two assumptions)" (§ 8).
- Conflict: in the anchored × 1.0 cell the REBCO reference design is [S]×[S] while the Nb₃Sn design at 4.3–4.7 T with its own pack is [U]×[U] by the contract's own rules; one cell label misstates one side. Amendment 2 asks "what evidence supports that cell's assumptions" (goal.md:77) for the conditions under which each material wins. Above 9 T (HELIAS-class REBCO at 11.7 T axis) `f_ren` 1.0 is equally [U]; the contract only says "below".
- Fix: label per best supported design (the label at its field and pack), make the cell's comparison label the weaker of the two, and add the policy assumptions the LCOE depends on ([U] structure-mass scaling, [U] heating rule, [U] uniform `f_ren`, D7 field-blind equipment) as a third column so an [S]×[S] cell cannot read as fully supported.

### 5. The HELIAS-class cell is a hybrid coil set, and its label overstates the support

- Contract: "`peak_ratio` 2.12 — [A] … every HELIAS-line and ITER/DEMO design sits at 2.0–2.2 … applied here to the Stellaris plant as an explicit assumption that a coil set with that ratio exists for this configuration" (§ 3.2); held facts "`k_link` 0.7731, `n_coils` 48 … all Stellaris-anchored" (§ 2).
- Conflict: `nb3sn-stellarator.md` § Gaps: "Using it means re-anchoring the plant to HELIAS 5-B, not correcting Stellaris's ratio", with `k_link` 0.92–0.97 for every HELIAS-line set against Stellaris 0.773 (row "Axis-linkage check") and about 25 held constants to re-anchor. Ampere-turns per axis tesla scale as `1/k_link` (`mfe_magnet_field.sysml:57`), so holding 0.773 with a 2.12 ratio charges both materials about 20 % more conductor and stored energy than a HELIAS-consistent set; the conductor account is the material-dependent one, so the break-even price moves with it. Also, "every … sits at 2.0–2.2" omits the same note's code-sized Helias 3/4 and W7-X-upscale rows at 2.3–2.7.
- Fix: label the cell's geometry [U] (the ratio value alone [A]); add a `k_link` 0.95 variant on the three HELIAS-class cells and report the break-even price under both; correct the "every" sentence.

### 6. The pack-size arm has no stated domain, and the grid leaves the fit's span by a factor of two

- Contract: "pack-size arm: `peak_ratio(A_wp) = 2.7667 + 0.064 × (R/√A_wp − 35.3)` — [D]" (§ 3.2); R grid to 22 m (§ 5).
- Conflict: the three-point fit spans `R/√A_wp` 28.9–31.5 only, is "poorly conditioned" and "may mix coil sets", and its intercept is "a 10× extrapolation" (`field-term.md` row "Helias-5 peak/axis ratio vs R/√A_wp"; § Gaps). Stellaris sits at 35.3, already outside the span; a REBCO design at R 22 with a 0.36 m pack sits at 61, giving a ratio near 4.4. The slope was derived under PROCESS's constant-coil-radius scaling (bore held), while the model multiplies its own bore factor on top; the contract does not say whether the arm and the bore factor double count.
- Fix: state a domain for the arm (report `R/√A_wp` per case; flag cases outside, say, 25–40 as `arm_extrapolated`), state that the arm replaces `peak_ratio` and the bore factor is retained unchanged with the reason, and add the field-term note's reminder that a Stellaris filament computation (appendix A) is the sourced repair. The Ampère floor is stated correctly (`B_peak ≥ μ0 I_coil/(4√A_wp)`, coil current, square pack); under the arm it never binds (floor slope 0.042 per unit against the arm's 0.064 with a positive intercept), so say it is a check on the anchored cells, where it can bind at large R and small pack.

### 7. The R grid has no plant smaller than the reference

- Contract: "(12.7, 1.3) reference; (15.0, 1.5); (18.0, 1.8); (22.0, 2.2)" (§ 5).
- Conflict: the owner's brief: "Determine whether REBCO can support higher field, a smaller winding, or a smaller plant". Under enhanced confinement (finding 1) the coherent REBCO design at matched power is smaller, and the beta, wall-load, divertor and stress checks are the ones that should decide whether it exists. The row also says "fixed aspect ratio ≈ 9.8–10 except the HELIAS point" but lists (22.0, 2.2), aspect 10, not HELIAS 5-B's (22, 1.8).
- Fix: add (10.0, 1.0) and (11.0, 1.1) for both materials, and either add (22, 1.8) or delete the "except the HELIAS point" clause.

### 8. The four "reference-common" screens respond to the design, and other building screens are not addressed

- Contract: "the four reference-common screens `facility_occupancy_ok`, `water_electric_capacity_ok`, `tbr_ok`, `divertor_heat_ok`, which fail at the published reference on the current model for reasons unrelated to the magnet … a design that fails one of them by more than the reference does is downgraded to failed" (§ 7).
- Conflict: `facility_occupancy_ok` is a building-layout margin on `plasma.R` and `rb.outer_radius` (`exploration/stellarator_e2e/models/designs/generic_mfe/mfe_plant.sysml:77`; audit § 2d "buildings"), so every R > 12.7 m design is "worse than the reference" and downgraded by a site screen, and the bigger Nb₃Sn pack raises `coil_t`, which failed `facility_geometry_ok` at 0.60 m in replay s2 (audit § 2d); `water_electric_capacity_ok` follows the heat-rejection demand, so it tracks `p_th` (finding 2); `divertor_heat_ok` scales the source shadow with R and load. Only `tbr_ok` (held achieved TBR) is truly design-independent. "Unrelated to the magnet" is true at the reference and false across the grid. Ruling asked for by the contract: the "held common with margins, downgrade if worse" treatment is defensible only for `tbr_ok`; the other three must be either re-supplied as installed choices per design (site area, building envelope, cooling-water capacity, with cost following) under MR-7, or reported with margins as a separate `reference-screen` column outside the supported/failed decision, stated in every LCOE quoted. Statuses: say that `unsupported` takes precedence over `failed` (a design can be both) so the three are exclusive and complete; "the REBCO band is `extrapolated` above 25 T" (§ 7) should read `unsupported` above 25 T per § 4.

### 9. `B_max` per material is framed as the material's field limit

- Contract: "`B_max` (the plant's ceiling check) is set per material to the material's law limit (§ 4), not to Stellaris's 24.9 T design envelope, because the map asks what each material supports" (§ 3.2); "13.0 T (edge admitted, flagged)" (§ 4).
- Conflict: goal.md § Invariants: "no field value in the brief is treated as a universal material limit"; Round 1's answer: "No Nb₃Sn field limit … The 13 T 'edge' and 14 T 'law-only' labels are validity flags of the fitted law, not performance limits" (`answer.md` § What the comparison does and does not establish, "No Nb₃Sn field limit"); the plant's `peak_field_ok` is the owner-ruled zero-margin design envelope (audit D3; `stellarator_plant.sysml:431-436`). The conductor `supported` status already carries the evidence claim, so the ceiling duplicates it under a different name and turns a validity flag into a failed physical constraint, which MR-7 forbids ("An unsupported conductor field … is neither a failed physical constraint nor permission to extrapolate").
- Fix: call `B_max` a supplied per-material design envelope chosen at the support-band edge, report it as an envelope flag (as § 3.2 already does for 24.9 T on REBCO), and let the conductor status alone decide `unsupported`.

### 10. The REBCO extrapolated band extends past both declared law extents, and the contract does not say so

### 12. No bridge from the pinned reference to the material instances, and the money-year statement understates the bias

- Contract: "The untouched reference instance keeps every plant basis, so its outputs must reproduce today's pin bit-for-bit" (§ 6); "conductor prices are USD2021 by declaration; the plant's other accounts are its mixed-year basis (stated limit, common to both materials)" (§ 6).
- Conflict: the REBCO-material instance at the reference design will not reproduce the reference LCOE (Green laws replace the 0.20-Carnot chain and 31.48 M$ package; a supplied count at 80 USD/m of 4 mm tape replaces 36.6 M tape-metres at 20 $/m of 6 mm), so the map's REBCO reference point and the pin are two different numbers with no reported reconciliation. - Fix: require a reported basis bridge (reference instance → REBCO-material instance at the same supplied design, by account) before the map; state the direction of the money-year bias and add a CPI 2021→2026 variant on conductor prices (a post-processing scalar).

### 13. Variant placement: beta limit, structure mass and construction pairing

- Contract: "a 0.045 variant on the two anchored cells only" (§ 3.1) against "two beta-limit variants on the anchored-geometry cells" (§ 3.2), where there are three anchored-geometry cells; structure-mass "variants ×0.5 and ×2 on the two anchored cells" (§ 5); "common-P as a variant on two cells" (§ 4).
- Conflict: `beta_limit` is a threshold on a computed `beta`, so the 0.045 verdict costs nothing and matters most where Nb₃Sn's best design sits near 5 % — the HELIAS-class cells (5.7 T axis at R 15 gives ≈ 5.0 % at matched power) and the anchored cells at R 22 (≈ 4.8 %), not the anchored cells at R 12.7 (≈ 11 %, already `power_short`). Construction C lists no insulation while P carries 23.7 %, so the native pairing gives REBCO a smaller pack partly by convention.
- Fix: report both beta verdicts on every design in every cell; put the common-P variant on all three arm cells; resolve "two" vs "three" anchored cells; place the structure-mass variants on the cells where the two best designs differ most in `W_mag` (data-driven, stated after the first pass).

### 14. The policy's closure loops and tolerances are undeclared, and the `power_short` fallback sits on the constraint boundary

- Contract: "`n_e0` set by the policy to the matched fusion power … otherwise `n_e0` at `beta = beta_limit`" (§ 5); "`p_wallplug_heat = max(100 MW, 1.1 × p_aux_required / 0.5)`" (§ 5); "Pack side `wp_side` … `coil_t` … moves the bore factor" (§ 5); "Target axis field … the values that put `B_peak` at 10, 11, 12 T under the cell's ratio and the design's bore factor" (§ 5).
- Conflict: `p_fus`, `p_aux_required` and `beta` come out of the ash fixed point (`mfe_plasma_sustainment.sysml:40-80`), and `B_peak` depends on `coil_t`, which depends on `wp_side`, which depends on turns, which depend on the target `B_axis`; so the policy either replicates the plasma chain or iterates the plant. MR-7 permits a declared search, but a design placed exactly at `beta = beta_limit` passes only if the policy's beta equals the model's to the ulp, and a design "matched" to 2,653 MW needs a tolerance. STUDY_POLICY § 2 requires the swept quantities to be the causal levers (turns, current, `n_e0`, `T_i0`, `wp_side`), with `B_peak` and `p_fus` as computed targets of the policy, and the record must carry the attribute → entry-key expansion.
- Fix: declare the mechanism (propose → evaluate → re-supply → evaluate, with the evaluation count per design), the tolerances (matched power ±0.5 %, `B_peak` target ±0.1 T after turn rounding), a margin for the fallback (`beta = 0.95 × beta_limit`), and add policy acceptance tests to § 9 (the supplied design re-evaluated without the policy reproduces the recorded `p_fus`, `p_aux_required` and `B_peak` within tolerance).

### Amendment 1 — least-required-heating ladder rule (§ 5, Q1): sound

- The r4 "lowest ladder temperature" rule was defective as the policy author says: installed heating is re-supplied at `1.1 × p_aux_required/0.5`, so the upper bound never binds and the rule took the lowest temperature with any non-negative heating, which in this plant is the highest-heating point (reference design: 229 MW at 11 keV against 52.7 MW at 14.63 keV, Q1). That was my r2 caveat on F1 ("a selection convention that minimises the heating draw"), which the r4 wording did not implement.
- Least required heating is the right convention for the owner's question: one MW of coupled heating costs about 2 MW of wall-plug draw and returns about 0.4 MW of gross power through `p_th` (`mfe_power_balance.sysml:141-172`; audit § 2e), plus ECRH capital, plus larger re-supplied packages, so the least-heating ladder value is also the LCOE-favourable one for both materials alike, and it is the value nearest the divertor threshold (amendment 2). Ties to 14.63 keV are fine. The bound `beta ≤ 0.95 × beta_limit` still applies at the chosen value, and wall load does not depend on the ladder at matched power.
- Two consequences to state, not defects: the chosen point is by construction the one nearest ignition, so the recorded `p_aux_required` is the burn-hold margin and should be read as such (falling-branch driven points need feedback, `20260907-burn-control` § 3); and the policy author's Q2 rule for `power_short` designs (the ladder value with the largest admissible fusion power, which produced a 765 MW-heating Nb₃Sn point) is not in the contract. Either adopt Q2 in § 5 or align it with amendment 1 (largest admissible fusion power among ladder values whose required heating is at most the design's installed rule bound); as it stands the two rules pull in opposite directions.
