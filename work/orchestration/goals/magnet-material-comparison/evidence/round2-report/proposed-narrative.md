# Proposed narrative — Round 2

The subsystem study showed why cheaper refrigeration alone was not enough to justify REBCO at the supplied tape price. We then asked whether its field or winding-space capability could improve the rest of the plant enough to pay for the conductor. The plant study made confinement and coil geometry explicit assumptions and gave each material its own supplied design. It retained 2,921 cases, including failures and domain refusals, and checked the computed results against an independently assembled oracle.

At 80 USD2021 per metre of REBCO tape, Nb₃Sn had the lower modeled LCOE in every cell where both materials passed the declared ranking checks. Reducing the tape price changed the preference: the break-even price varied from 5.7 to 37.7 USD/m across the eight comparable cells. Higher field did not consistently increase that price. Additional conductor and plant costs could erase its benefit. With the Stellaris field ratio and confinement multiplier 1.0, no tested Nb₃Sn design passed the ranking checks, so that cell gave no material comparison.

The map also showed the limit of the result. Source-supported confinement values and coil ratios belonged to particular configurations; transferring them to the tested plants remained an assumption. Every comparative cell carried that limitation. Breeding failed everywhere, divertor checks remained open, and some re-supplied capacities had no cost response. The model therefore gave useful conditional price thresholds and accounted for consequences beyond the magnet, but it did not establish a material choice for a qualified plant.
