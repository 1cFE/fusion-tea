## 2. Question 1 — what is the 2.87 an average over?

**The source does not say.** No sentence in § 2.3 or § 2.8 defines "Av. neutron wall power"; it appears only as a Table 5 row of the 0.5-D operating-point model, beside "Av. photon wall power" and "P/S_LCFS".

**Under the source's own definition of wall load it cannot be the average over the Fig. 33 wall.** [240] defines the average NWL as neutron power over the first-wall area. The neutron power is 0.8 × 2700 = 2160 MW. Over the printed 940 m² plasma surface that is 2.30 MW/m²; the first wall stands at least 100 mm outside that surface (averaged 16.6 cm, Fig. 34) and has a larger area, so the true wall average is at most about 2.3. The source's own damage map says the same thing independently: peak/mean DPA on the wall is 3.9/2.2 = 1.77 (Fig. 38), and if the wall load peaks like the damage ([240] line 366) the wall's average NWL is 4.05/1.77 ≈ 2.28, implying a wall area of about 945 m² — the printed LCFS plus a small standoff. A 2.87 average would need a 753 m² surface, **smaller than the plasma itself**.

**What reproduces 2.87 from printed numbers.** Exactly one expression built from figures the paper prints: fusion power over the plasma surface area, 2700 / 940 = 2.872. The round-1 reviewer's reading — neutron power over a circular torus at R 12.7 m, r 1.5 m (2160 / 752.06 = 2.872) — also reproduces the number, but **the 1.5 m radius is not printed anywhere**: it came from the extraction's rewritten Table 2 row (line 227, "1.5"), and the image prints 1.3. No printed gap makes 1.5 either: the printed plasma radius plus the minimum standoff gives 1.40 m (3.31 MW/m²) and plus the averaged standoff 1.466 m (2.94 MW/m²). At the Table 5 radius 12.74 the torus reading gives 2.863, which would print as 2.86. Nor does the 0.5-D code's own surface carry it (2.87 × 327 = 939 MW is no power in the design). The two candidate readings coincide because 0.8 × 940 = 752.0 and 4π²·12.7·1.5 = 752.06 — a numerical accident, not a relation the text states.

**Conclusion.** The 2.87 is a systems-level figure whose basis the source leaves unstated; numerically it is fusion power per unit of printed plasma surface, and by the source's own wall-load definition it is not an average over the source's first wall. **It is not on any basis the model's circular-torus average can be put on by a reference-radius correction**, because the only printed radius is the plasma's and the printed standoffs (0.10 m minimum, 0.166 m averaged) do not reach it. It belongs in the model as a **reported comparand with its basis marked "unstated in the source"** — never as the fence, and never as the denominator of a peaking factor: 4.05 / 2.87 = 1.41 is a ratio of two quantities on different bases and is not the wall's peaking factor.

## 3. Question 2 — the two printed peaks

**There is one printed peak, 4.05 MW/m².** It is Table 2's headline "Peak neutron wall load" (image-confirmed) and it is the design value the model already carries as `wall_load_limit`. Fig. 33's colour scale runs 0–4 MW/m² with the map's maxima at the top of the scale, consistent with 4.05 and not with a 4.95 peak, which would sit off the scale.

**The 4.95 does not exist in the source.** The Table 5 image has no peak row at all; its wall rows are the 2.87 neutron average and the 0.70 / 0.72 photon average. The extracted row (line 748) is an artifact of the page rewrite recorded in `stellaris-design-details/decisions.json` (page 9, `claude_replace`), as is the extracted Table 6 row "Peak neutron wall loading at FW (W/m²) ~1.5" (page 20, `claude_replace`; the image's rows are TBR, power multiplication, coil fluxes and lifetime, peak first-wall DPA, first-wall lifetime, nuclear heating). The WI-022 doc comment at `stellarator_plant.sysml:1140` already recorded the 4.95 as an extraction artifact; this reading confirms it from the image and extends it to the Table 6 row.

**What the 4.05 is.** A 3D value on the design's own CAD first wall — the one first-wall geometry the paper uses for its divertor, neutronics and thermal work (lines 1304–1306), held at least 100 mm from the plasma (line 1295) — at 2700 MW, from the § 2.3 profile-based neutron source, computed with [240]'s deterministic method (line 1606–1607). One attribution in the round-1 review is corrected by this: the wall-load map is [240]'s method on the CAD wall, not an OpenMC tally; OpenMC supplies the TBR, coil fluxes, nuclear heating and DPA (§ 2.8's five evaluation steps, lines 1608–1648).

## 4. Question 3 — what sets in-vessel lifetime

**The peak, in the source and in its cited method.** Table 6 prints the first-wall lifetime (~4–6 FPY) from the **peak** first-wall DPA (10.7 DPA/FPY); the text puts it at about 4 FPY at 2700 MW, when the cumulative peak damage reaches 42.8 DPA (lines 1763–1766, 1811–1813); Fig. 38's caption locates that peak on the inboard mid-plane, with the wall-integrated mean at 2.2 against a peak of 3.9 DPA/GWf/yr. [240] states the rule directly: "the lifetime of the blanket is determined by the peak loads" (line 88), and it is why that paper optimises the wall for a low peaking factor at all.

**What that means for the model's chain.** `'Levelized Replacement Cost'` (`mfe_account_costs.sysml:796-803`) sets `core_lifetime_FPY = fluence_limit / q_n` with `q_n = p_neutron / firstwall_area` — today the circular-torus average. With the pinned 18 MW·yr/m² limit, the source's peak gives 18 / 4.05 = 4.44 FPY, **inside the source's own ~4–6 FPY band and beside its ~4 FPY estimate**; the model's current average gives 5.80 FPY, at the top of the band; the 2.87 would give 6.27, outside it. So the peak operand is both what the source says and what reproduces the source's lifetime. The move at the baseline, computed from the calc's own equations with the pinned constants (i 0.07, N 30 yr, availability 0.85, cost per event 828.545 M$): core life 5.797 → 4.404 FPY (calendar 6.82 → 5.18 yr), scheduled replacements 4 → 5, **CAS72 95.898 → 131.464 M$ (+35.566 M$, +37%)** at a peak operand of 4.087. This is the comparison-meaning change strategy assumption (3) anticipated; committed studies are restated before the regeneration commit (constraint carried forward from the round-1 review).

## 5. What this does to the strategy

**Assumption (2) does not survive in letter.** The strategy assumed the model's circular-torus average "can be put on the source's basis by a stated reference-radius correction alone, once that basis is established". The basis cannot be established as a radius: the source states none, the only printed radius is the plasma's 1.3 m, and the one printed-number reproduction of 2.87 is a power-over-plasma-surface figure, not a wall quantity. Nor is the fallback the strategy wrote for this case triggered: the basis is not a shaped wall either, so T-001's shaped-wall route is not what the evidence calls for, and no bounded negative arises, because **the increment never needed the 2.87**.

**What the evidence does support, fully sourced.** The source's own 3D peak on its own wall (4.05 at 2700 MW) is the limit, and it is also the calibration: the multiplier that carries the model's own circular-torus average to the source's peak *at the source's power and geometry* is N = 4.05 × 701.926 / 2160 = **1.3161** — every factor printed or already in the model (the peak, Table 2; the neutron share, `physics.py`; the model's own `wall_area` at the source's R, a and minimum standoff). It is an identity at the design point (an SV row: reproduce 4.05 at 2700 MW, R 12.7, a 1.3) and it extrapolates over the study's levers through the model's own `wall_area` ∝ R·(a + 0.10) under one stated assumption — that the wall's peaking factor and shape factor do not change over the sweep — which is the assumption every candidate form makes and which the study can carry as a shadow column. At the baseline it reads 3.10527 × 1.3161 = **4.087 against 4.05: violated, by the model's 0.94% fusion-power excess over the source's 2700 MW** (2725.4 MW), exactly as the strategy expected and as `goal.md` § Invariants requires be disclosed and never tuned.

**Its decomposition is a cross-check, not a definition.** N = p_f × (A_torus / A_FW). With the source's own damage peaking, p_f ≈ 1.77 (Fig. 38, on [240]'s relative-indicator statement), A_FW ≈ 701.926 × 1.77 / 1.316 ≈ 945 m² — the printed 940 m² plasma surface plus a small standoff, which is what the wall is. With the round-1 external band p_f = 1.5–2.1, A_FW spans about 800–1120 m². Both brackets contain a wall the source describes; neither is needed to define N. The reviewer's decomposition (1.316 = 1.411 × 0.933, a "reference-radius correction" 701.926 / 752 times the ratio 4.05 / 2.87) gives the same number for the reason in § 2 and should not be carried into the model text, because both of its factors rest on the unprinted 1.5 m.

**Two alternatives the model task may weigh, recorded so the choice is visible** (this task does not decide the form — scope exclusion): (a) carry the decomposition explicitly, p_f = 1.77 from Fig. 38 and A_FW = 945 m² as a *derived* quantity, so the model text names a wall area — at the cost of stating a DPA-to-NWL transfer the source only calls "relative"; (b) carry N alone as a named source-anchored peak calibration with its identity SV, and record the decomposition and the external band in the doc text as the plausibility bounds the strategy asked for. Either is sourced; (b) puts fewer inferred numbers into the model.

**What the model text must say about the 2.87.** The WI-022 cross-check comment (`stellarator_plant.sysml:1137-1140`: "the model's flat-wall average vs the source's shaped-wall average") is wrong in its claim, not just unsourced: the source states no basis, and its figure is not a wall average by its own definition. The corrected text reports 2.87 as the source's systems-level average of unstated basis, equal to fusion power over the printed 940 m² plasma surface to three figures, on a basis the model's 3.105 does not share; and it says the source's peak is 4.05 on the source's own wall. Also to correct at the same regeneration: the two archived-path doc comments at `stellarator_plant.sysml:700,703` (trail § Amendment 2026-09-04) and the EI-5 library doc line.

**The standoff, precisely.** The model's `vacuum_t = 0.10 m` is the source's *minimum* plasma-to-wall distance (line 1295); the source's *averaged* SOL gap is 16.6 cm (Fig. 34). So the model's wall radius sits at the source's closest approach, and its circular-torus area (701.9 m²) is below both a torus at the averaged gap (735 m²) and the real wall (~945 m²). N absorbs all of that at the design point; the doc text should say which standoff the 0.10 m is.
