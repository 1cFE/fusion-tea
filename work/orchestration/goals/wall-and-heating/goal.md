## Question

> Can the two fences that actually bound the machine at the printed 50 MW of installed heating — neutron wall load and sustained heating — be made honest, so that the wall-load check measures what it claims to measure, the heating system carries real structure, and the model charges truthfully for escaping from between them?

`[AGENT] — proposed wording.` The owner's direction: *"we need to create a new goal for the wall-load and the heating structure"* `[OWNER-VERBATIM 2026-09-03, relayed by the round-1 handoff at work/orchestration/goals/priced-levers/evidence/round1_handoff_20260903.md]`. Singular "goal", two subjects. The proposed question binds the two subjects with the fact that made them one: at the printed power the machine's deadlock is between them.

The deadlock is measured, not assumed (`20260903-priced-levers` § 15 finding #1): with the field lever priced and temperature swept, the machine **can** sustain itself below the conductor ceiling at 50 MW — and then the first wall cannot take the neutron load. 27 of 240 points at 50 MW are blocked by `wall_load_ok` alone; 6 by the conductor ceiling alone; `wall_load_ok` is violated at 264 of 439 points across the study. The two fences that bound the printed operating point are the ones this goal names, and both are thin in the model today:

- **The wall-load check compares an average to a peak.** The operand is a flat-wall average — fusion power over `wall_area = kappa·4π²·R·vacuum_or` (`models/library/analyses/mfe_plasma_scaling.sysml:52`) — and the model's own cross-check comment says so ("the model's flat-wall average vs the source's shaped-wall average", `models/designs/stellarator_09/stellarator_plant.sysml:1071-1075`). The limit it is compared against is the source's printed **peak** design value (`wall_load_limit = 4.05`, doc comment at `:1079-1081`: "peak neutron wall load 4.05 MW/m²"). The source prints both an average (2.87) and a peak (4.05) for the same design; the pinned baseline row reads 3.105 (`20260903-priced-levers/results/points.csv` `c0064`; the WI-022 doc comment's cross-check says 3.13 at its profile-integrated `p_fus`). Whether the honest form is a computed peak against the peak limit or an average against an average limit is the round's to decide from sources; **the fence as bound measures neither**. `[AGENT] arithmetic, for scale and not as a claim: the source's own peak-to-average ratio is 1.41; applied to the baseline's 3.105 average it would read 4.38 against 4.05 — a violated baseline. The ratio may not transfer (the source's average is over a shaped wall, the model's over a circular torus), which is exactly why it is a research question and not a fix.`
- **Heating is structureless.** The heating account is one linear line at $5,282,900/MW installed (`stellarator_plant.sysml:842`), `eta_pin = 0.5` is held (`:648`), and the only heating arithmetic in the model is that held ratio dividing one held constant inside the recirculating sum (`models/library/analyses/mfe_power_balance.sysml:117-121,136`). No heating-chain element exists as a model object; R4.P grades 1 against a written target of 2 (`grading.md@fc80e5b2` cell R4.P and grader note G1). `[INHERITED: priced-levers goal.md § Question]`

The work is making both honest. What the model then says about which escape is cheaper — a smaller or hotter or denser machine under the wall, or more and better-structured heating — is a **result**, not the goal.
