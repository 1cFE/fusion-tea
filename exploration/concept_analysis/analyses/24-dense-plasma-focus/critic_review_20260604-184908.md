

# Critic Review — 24-dense-plasma-focus

## Headline issues

1. **CAS22 at 1 GWe ($426M) is dominated by C220200 ($208M) and C220700 ($89M), which are pure library defaults for a concept where the library archetype is acknowledged as poorly fitting.** The analysis explicitly grades archetype-fit as Low, meaning the DENSE_PLASMA_FOCUS library defaults were "calibrated with generic DPF defaults (5 Hz rep rate, DT-scale geometry)" — yet C220200 (power injection) and C220700 (instrumentation & control) carry through to the 1 GWe projection unmodified. At 1 GWe, these two accounts alone constitute 70% of the CAS22 total. The analyst considered overrides for C220104 (driver) and C220109 (DEC) but declined for lack of company-grounded data, which is disciplined — but the result is that the single largest cost account in the entire model (CAS22 at 1 GWe) is driven by library defaults the analyst has already told us are unreliable for this concept. The analysis should flag this as a structural limitation of the output, not just as a data gap.

2. **The recirculating-power paradox (Gap #10) is identified in the analysis but never resolved, and its implications for the model's energy balance are unquantified.** At 200 Hz with a 115 kJ bank, continuous recharge power is 23 MW — 4.6× the 5 MWe net output. The analyst notes the numbers may be "not self-consistent" and proposes possible resolutions (smaller bank, higher conversion efficiency, recharge embedded in gross-to-net), but the model_setup.py accepts the library's default `q_eng = 3.0` without testing whether this value is compatible with any of those resolutions. If the actual recirculating power fraction is much higher than what `q_eng = 3.0` implies, the native LCOE is understated. This is a load-bearing assumption hiding inside a library default.

3. **Seven overrides apply multiplicative fractions (3–10%) to generic values that are themselves unvalidated for this archetype, creating compounded uncertainty with no error bounds.** Every enabled override except CAS23 (zero) is expressed as a small percentage of the generic cost for that account. But the generic values are computed from a Low-fit archetype — the base being multiplied is itself unreliable. A 5% fraction of a number that could be wrong by 3× is not a "conservative" estimate; it is an estimate with unknown error. The analysis would benefit from at least one sanity-check anchoring these to absolute dollar values (e.g., "5% of generic CAS21 = $6.9M at native scale — is a $6.9M building plausible for a 4m×4m room housing a 3-tonne device?"). At native scale, CAS21 = $6.9M for what is described as a small industrial enclosure; that figure seems high and suggests the fraction-of-generic approach may not be compressing costs as much as the analyst intends.

4. **CAS70 and CAS80 overrides are disabled due to a framework limitation, and the analysis correctly notes this — but the LCOE headline number silently carries full generic O&M and fuel costs for a concept where both are structurally different.** The native LCOE of 154.5 $/MWh includes generic CAS70 ($2.4M annualized) for a device whose O&M is monthly electrode swaps on a 3-tonne machine, and generic CAS80 for a fuel that costs $30k/year at mass-production prices. The 1 GWe projection inherits this distortion. Any reader comparing the headline LCOE to other concepts should be warned that the operating-cost side is known to be overstated but uncorrectable within the current framework.

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

All four deterministic checks returned `status: ok`. The P_native is coherent at 5 MWe across the design-point table, analysis.md, and model_setup.py. The contract is satisfied via the helper form. The override count of 7 is within the Low-fit band of 6–12. The sanity check shows all 38 accounts with `no_data` flags, meaning no comparables exist to flag outliers against — the sanity check is structurally vacuous for this concept. This is expected given the empty comparables list, but it means the entire numerical output has no peer-concept cross-check. The deterministic layer provides structural assurance (nothing is broken) but zero content assurance (nothing is validated against a reference).

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes one named plant: the "Focus Fusion commercial generator (Lerner et al. 2023)." However, there is mild stitching: the design-point parameters table in Section 5 mixes FF-2B *demonstrated* values (anode radius 2.8 cm, peak current ~1 MA, plasmoid radius ~250 μm) with *commercial target* values (rep rate 200 Hz, fusion yield 60 kJ, net electric 25 kJ). The analysis is transparent about this — the "Critical note on design-point coherence" paragraph at the end of Section 5 explicitly flags the gap. The model_setup.py spec dictionary uses the commercial targets (f_rep=200.0), not the demonstrated values, which is correct for modeling the design point. The thickness parameters (blanket_t=0.10, ht_shield_t=0.05, structure_t=0.05, vessel_t=0.05) are analyst-assigned order-of-magnitude values for a concept that has no published structural dimensions beyond the electrode geometry. These are reasonable given the device description but are not traceable to any source.

One coherence concern: the analysis states the FF-2B capacitor bank is "max 115 kJ stored energy" and model_setup.py's comment says "~115 kJ," but the commercial device's bank energy is never separately specified. The model accepts the library default for driver energy (back-solved from q_eng and P_native), which may or may not correspond to 115 kJ. The recirculating-power paradox (Gap #10) suggests it cannot be 115 kJ at 200 Hz, but this inconsistency is not resolved in model_setup.py.

### Override discipline (judgment)

**C220105 (3% of generic) — provenance: derived.** The 1000× mass reduction rationale is reasonable as a direction but the 3% fraction is not derived from any published cost data. At native scale, generic C220105 rounds to ~$0.03M and 3% of that is ~$0.001M ($1,000). That seems implausibly low even for a small steel frame. At 1 GWe scale the override value is also near-zero. The override is doing real work (eliminating structural cost), but the specific fraction is arbitrary. This is within acceptable bounds for a Low-fit concept with no cost data.

**C220110 (5% of generic) — provenance: derived.** The argument is structurally sound: aneutronic fuel eliminates the need for rad-hardened remote handling. The 5% residual for "basic lifting/tooling" is a reasonable judgment call. At native scale, generic C220110 = $0.9M and the override takes it to ~$0.05M. This is doing real work.

**CAS21 (5% of generic) — provenance: derived.** At native scale, 5% of $138.4M = $6.9M. For a 4m×4m room housing a 3-tonne device, $6.9M for "buildings and site structures" seems generous. A small industrial building with a concrete pad, electrical room, and control area might cost $500k–$2M. The 5% fraction may be too high at native scale, but the 1 GWe projection carries the same $6.9M (it doesn't scale further), which seems more reasonable for a facility housing 200 units. The analyst should note the native-scale implausibility.

**CAS23 (zero) — provenance: direct.** Correct. No thermal cycle, no turbine plant. Structurally zero.

**CAS24 (10% of generic) — provenance: derived.** At native scale, 10% of $0.6M = $0.06M ($60k) for switchyard and plant electrical at 5 MWe. This is plausible for small industrial-scale electrical distribution. At 1 GWe, $12.6M is the output — this is the scaled value and seems reasonable for grid connection at that scale.

**CAS26 (5% of generic) — provenance: derived.** No thermal cycle means no condenser/cooling tower. Electrode cooling and electronics cooling at 5 MWe is indeed small. At native scale, 5% of $0.6M = $0.03M ($30k), which seems low but defensible for a compact cooling loop. At 1 GWe, $6.4M for heat rejection across 200 units is plausible.

**CAS27 (10% of generic) — provenance: derived.** At native scale, generic CAS27 rounds to ~$0.04M and 10% is ~$0.004M. The analysis argues initial materials are negligible (Be electrodes + initial fuel charge). This is consistent. The override is doing minimal work because the generic value is already small.

Overall, the overrides are disciplined in that none claim `provenance: direct` except CAS23 (which is genuinely direct — zero by architecture). The fraction-of-generic approach is the only option given no published cost decomposition, and the analyst is transparent about this. The concern is not individual overrides but the compounded reliance on an unreliable base.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The 1 GWe NOAK projection shows LCOE of 8.5 $/MWh and overnight cost of 808 $/kW. The CAS22 account at 1 GWe is $426M — dominated by C220200 ($208M, power injection) and C220700 ($89M, I&C). These are library defaults scaled to 200 modules (1 GWe ÷ 5 MWe = 200 units).

The C220200 (power injection) value of $208M at 1 GWe is architecturally suspect. For Focus Fusion, "power injection" is a capacitor bank — the simplest possible driver architecture. The library default likely prices this based on generic DPF or MFE power injection assumptions. At $208M for 200 capacitor banks (200 × 115 kJ = 23 MJ total stored energy), the implied cost is ~$1M per bank or ~$9/J stored — high for commercial capacitor technology but not implausible for pulsed-power-grade capacitors with fast switching.

C220700 (I&C) at $89M for 200 units ($445k/unit) seems high for a device with no complex plasma control (the pulse is ~10 ns — there is no feedback control loop during the fusion event). DPF I&C consists of bank charge/discharge timing, vacuum monitoring, and diagnostics. This is another case where the library default is architecturally mismatched.

The CAS30 jump from $3.9M (native) to $106.8M (1 GWe) reflects the multi-module scaling of indirect costs. CAS60 similarly jumps from $7.1M to $130.3M. These are standard two-knob projections and not architecturally implausible — project management and financial costs scale with total capital.

### Family delta vs comparables

No comparables were assigned. The sanity check is therefore structurally vacuous — every account shows `no_data`. The analysis's Section 7 provides qualitative positioning against MFE, IFE/MIF, and other p-B11 concepts, which is informative but cannot substitute for quantitative delta analysis. The qualitative discussion is well-reasoned and correctly identifies the concept's structural uniqueness (no magnets, no thermal cycle, no tritium, distributed-generation scale). The absence of comparables is appropriate — there is genuinely no peer concept in the corpus for a 5 MWe pulsed DPF with direct conversion.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

The two assumptions that would most change the LCOE conclusion:

1. **q_eng (engineering Q).** The model accepts the library default of 3.0. If the recirculating-power paradox (Gap #10) is real and q_eng is actually much lower (e.g., 1.5–2.0 due to capacitor bank recharge requirements), the native LCOE would increase substantially because P_net would require more gross fusion power. The analysis identifies this concern in Gap #10 but does not propagate it into a sensitivity bound. This is the single most load-bearing unvalidated parameter.

2. **The archetype's per-MWe scaling behavior at 200× module count.** The 1 GWe projection assumes 200 identical 5 MWe units. The library's multi-module scaling (learning curves, shared infrastructure) may not apply to a mass-manufactured device that LPPFusion envisions as produced "like automobiles." If manufacturing learning curves are steeper than the library assumes, the 1 GWe LCOE could be lower; if module-count scaling doesn't apply (each unit is independently sited), infrastructure sharing doesn't occur and the projection is optimistic for shared-cost accounts. The analysis notes the "fundamentally different deployment model" in Section 2 but does not flag it as a sensitivity driver.

Both gaps are identified somewhere in analysis.md but neither is called out as the load-bearing assumption it is. The data gap inventory (Section 6) correctly lists 12 gaps, but the prioritization focuses on physics feasibility (Gaps #1–5, all "blocking") rather than on modeling sensitivity. From a cost-model perspective, Gaps #10 (recirculating power) and #11 (capacity factor) are more consequential than Gaps #1–5, which are binary feasibility gates — if any of Gaps #1–5 are real, there is no cost model to build at all.

## What I deliberately did not say

- **Whether LPPFusion's physics claims (QMFE, yield scaling, p-B11 feasibility) are credible.** The analysis treats these as blocking data gaps and models the design point as-claimed. This is the correct approach for cost modeling — the model answers "if the physics works as claimed, what does the economics look like?" Evaluating the physics credibility is upstream of model_critic scope.

- **Whether the 5 MWe design point is the right one to model.** The design-point selection is a fixed input from the upstream table. The analyst chose the only published design with a stated net electrical output. This is out of scope for model_critic but I note that the FF-2B experimental device (the only hardware that exists) produces 0.2 J per shot, not 60 kJ — the design point being modeled has never existed in any form. This is a research-stage concern about grounding confidence, not a model_critic concern.

- **Whether the sources (primarily Lerner's own papers and LPPFusion website) are adequate.** The analysis relies almost entirely on company-authored materials. The absence of independent engineering assessments or cost studies is noted in Section 1. Source selection and quality evaluation belong to research-stage review. The analyst has correctly used what is available and flagged the limitations.

- **The absolute plausibility of the 1 GWe LCOE of 8.5 $/MWh.** This is extremely low by any fusion standard, but it is a mechanical output of scaling a concept with (a) very low native capital cost (most accounts overridden to 3–10% of generic), (b) 200× module replication with learning curves, and (c) generic O&M/fuel that are themselves unvalidated. The number is what the model produces; whether it is believable requires evaluating the physics feasibility, which is out of scope.
