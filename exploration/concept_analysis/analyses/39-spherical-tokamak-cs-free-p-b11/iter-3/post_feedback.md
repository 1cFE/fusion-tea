### F-1: DEC failure scenario implements the misleading scale-up variant, not the constant-fusion-power variant called for in H1
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Hypotheses, H1) and model_setup.py (DEC_failure scenario)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis explicitly identifies two DEC failure variants in H1 and labels the scale-up variant "operationally misleading" — it holds `net_electric_mw=500` fixed and scales fusion power upward to compensate. The analysis names the constant-fusion-power variant as "the single most informative output this model can produce" and "the primary demonstration of the DEC go/no-go condition," projecting LCOE ~230+ $/MWh. But the implemented `result_dec_failure` scenario is exactly the misleading variant: `model.forward(net_electric_mw=NET_ELECTRIC_MW, eta_de=0.0)` holds output fixed and scales fusion power from 2031 MW to 3270 MW (+61%). The published result (+4% LCOE, 99.9 $/MWh) directly contradicts the analysis narrative and fails to demonstrate why DEC is a go/no-go economic condition rather than a sensitivity parameter.
- **Recommendation:** Add a second DEC failure run that fixes fusion power at the base-case value (2031 MW) and lets net electric output fall to whatever the framework computes with `eta_de=0.0` and all alpha power routed through `eta_th=0.35`. Use `override_reference_mw` or equivalent mechanism to pin fusion power rather than net output. Report this result as the primary H1 scenario; demote the current `result_dec_failure` to the "scale-up (operationally misleading)" label. The constant-fusion-power result is the number that demonstrates whether p-B11 + DEC is a go/no-go condition or a tunable efficiency parameter.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: H4 (hot ion mode heating burden) scenario is called for in the analysis but not implemented
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Hypotheses, H4) and model_setup.py
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** H4 explicitly states: "The model should evaluate this via a scenario flag `hot_ion_mode_heating_multiplier` (range 1–20, base case = 1 representing 'physics solved') that scales a plasma-state-maintenance power term. At multiplier ≥ 2, net electrical output from DEC becomes negative regardless of η_de or p_input — the boundary value defines the conceptual go/no-go gate." No such scenario exists in model_setup.py. This is the scenario that operationalizes the Li (2024) finding — that maintaining Ti/Te = 4 via external heating requires ~20× the fusion power output — and it is structurally independent of the ECRH current drive sweep in H2. Without it, the model has no output that tests the fundamental physics gate from the only independent critique of ENN's roadmap.
- **Recommendation:** Add a `hot_ion_mode_heating_multiplier` sweep (values: 1, 2, 5, 10, 20) that adds a plasma-state-maintenance power term (`p_hot_ion = (multiplier - 1) * p_fus * some_fraction`) to the parasitic load. The exact parameterization should follow from the H4 description: at multiplier=20, total auxiliary power collapses net output below zero. Report the crossover multiplier where net output first goes negative — this is the conceptual boundary between "physics might work" and "physics cannot work at any engineering scale."
- **Priority:** important
