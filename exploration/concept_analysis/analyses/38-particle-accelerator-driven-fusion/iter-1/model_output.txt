======================================================================
KEY BINDING CONSTRAINTS — Top 3 LCOE Drivers
======================================================================

1. FUNDAMENTAL PHYSICS CEILING ON q_eff (ELIMINATES VIABILITY)
   Current q_eff = 0.0020  |  Break-even q_eff ≈ 2.14  |  Gap = 1071×

   In beam-target D-T at 300 keV, each deuteron must be given ~300 keV of kinetic
   energy. The D-T reaction releases 17.59 MeV (a 59:1 energy ratio per reaction),
   but only a tiny fraction of beam deuterons undergo fusion before losing their
   energy by ionization and elastic scattering in the target gas. The resulting
   effective Q (q_eff ≈ 0.001–0.01) is set by the D-T fusion cross section
   integrated over the stopping range — a property of atomic physics, not technology.

   Breaking even electrically requires q_eff > (1 - η_th) / [η_th × M_eff] ≈ 2.1,
   where η_th is thermal efficiency and M_eff is the effective blanket multiplier.
   This is ~210× above the physics ceiling. No accelerator improvement, no
   engineering optimization, and no amount of capital can overcome this constraint
   without a fundamental change in the fusion geometry (i.e., plasma confinement).

   SHINE's solution is correct: use the neutrons for high-value applications
   (medical isotopes, materials testing) where the economic metric is $/Ci or
   $/neutron, not $/kWh.

2. ACCELERATOR CAPITAL SCALES WITH MODULE COUNT (PROHIBITIVE AT POWER-PLANT SCALE)
   At baseline: 10,000 modules × $0.50M/mod = $5B accelerator capital.
   CAS22 total: $8.1B  |  Total capital: $11.5B

   Because p_fus per module = 141 W, any scale of power requires
   a proportionally large number of modules. The capital cost scales as n_mod,
   growing linearly with plant size while net power remains negative. Even at
   $10,000/module (well below any realistic accelerator cost), 10,000 modules
   would cost $100M for accelerators alone — and still produce only 1.4 MW fusion.

3. TRITIUM COST SCALES LINEARLY WITH PLANT SIZE (SIGNIFICANT OPEX FLOOR)
   At baseline: 10,000 mod × 8mg/yr × $35,000/g = $2.8M/yr

   SHINE's current 8 mg/yr tritium consumption is operationally trivial (~$280/yr).
   At power-plant scale (10,000 modules), tritium cost becomes a significant
   operating line. This is manageable but highlights that beam-target D-T without
   breeding has an open tritium supply chain — unlike power-plant concepts with
   on-site tritium breeding blankets.
