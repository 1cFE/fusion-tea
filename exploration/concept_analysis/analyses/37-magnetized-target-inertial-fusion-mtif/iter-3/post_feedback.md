### F-1: Override count below Med-fit band without adequate justification
- **Target:** Section 5b (Override Candidates Registry)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The concept has Med archetype-fit (expected 3–8 enabled overrides) but enables only 1 override (C220107 capacitor bank). The coherence flag reports this as a potential library-default trust issue. While the analysis acknowledges the data opacity and states "the count (1) is below the expected band but reflects the extreme opacity of NearStar's public materials," the per-account walkthrough identifies several additional accounts where analogy-based overrides are feasible. Specifically: (1) C220104 (railgun driver) has historical analogy data (Z-IFE $372M, Navy railgun $50–500M) that could bound the cost despite NearStar opacity — the walkthrough rejects this as "speculative" but the same reasoning applies to the enabled C220107 override; (2) C220102 (radiation shield) has a concrete physical rationale (D-D neutrons at 2.45 MeV vs. D-T at 14.1 MeV) that affects shielding mass, yet the analysis defers to "library default should account for this"; (3) CAS70 (O&M) has component-lifetime data from railgun literature (10^5–10^6 shots) that could constrain replacement OPEX, yet it's dismissed as "data gap too severe." The Med fit signals that the MAG_TARGET archetype is only moderately aligned with NearStar's hypervelocity-projectile + D-D architecture, meaning more library corrections should be expected.
- **Recommendation:** Add 2–3 additional derived overrides with explicit uncertainty bands to bring the count into the Med-fit band (3–8). Candidates: (1) C220104 railgun driver, using Z-IFE and Navy railgun bounds to establish a plausible range (e.g., midpoint $150M ± factor of 3, derived from $50–500M Navy + $372M Z-IFE analogy, with rationale explaining why railgun-specific data is more credible than Z-pinch default for this concept); (2) C220102 radiation shield, with a physics-based reduction factor (e.g., 0.6–0.8× library default) reflecting 2.45 MeV vs. 14.1 MeV neutron energy difference and molten lead self-shielding; (3) CAS70 O&M, with a component-replacement adder derived from rail lifetime bounds (10^5–10^6 shots at 1 Hz → weeks to months replacement interval, $50k–500k per event from Section 4 lines 283–286, yielding $1.5–150M/year annualized OPEX range, use midpoint with ±factor-of-5 uncertainty). Mark each as `derived` with explicit uncertainty acknowledgment in `rationale`. The goal is not to fabricate precision but to encode the architectural deltas (railgun vs. Z-pinch, D-D vs. D-T, high component-replacement frequency) that the Med fit signals the library does not adequately capture.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Model-output plausibility caveat is correctly stated but the 1 GWe LCOE is unexplained
- **Target:** Section 5 (Model Output Plausibility Caveat, lines 336–352)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly warns that the 1 GWe LCOE (~60.2 $/MWh from model output) is "implausibly optimistic" and should "NOT be interpreted as a credible estimate" until NearStar publishes target gain, driver cost, and component lifetime. It lists three physical reasons: (1) D-D reactivity penalty not reflected, (2) railgun component lifetime unknown, (3) TRL-1 concept with no experimental validation. However, the caveat does not explain why the model produces a competitive LCOE despite these gaps, or what library-default assumptions are being inherited that create this optimism. The model output shows the 1 GWe CAS22 ($2.94B) is 17.6× the native CAS22 ($167.4M) for a 20× power scale, which is roughly linear — yet the analysis Section 2 identifies railgun component lifetime (rail erosion every 10^5–10^6 shots) as a "BLOCKING" gap that should drive high OPEX at scale. The CAS70 (O&M) values in the model output are 13.2M$ (native) → 25.0M$ (1 GWe), only 1.9× increase for 20× power, which does not reflect the analysis's concern about frequent rail replacement. The plausibility caveat needs to explicitly connect the model's outputs to the archetype defaults it inherits and explain which defaults are non-physical for NearStar's architecture.
- **Recommendation:** Expand the plausibility caveat (Section 5, lines 336–352) to explicitly decode the model output: "The 1 GWe LCOE of 60.2 $/MWh reflects MAG_TARGET archetype defaults that assume [list 2–3 specific inherited assumptions from the library that are unlikely for this concept — e.g., 'target gain Q~20–50 typical for D-T MagLIF concepts, not validated for D-D', 'driver cost scaling consistent with Z-pinch pulsed-power where component lifetime is >10^7 shots, not railgun rail erosion at 10^5–10^6 shots', 'CAS70 O&M scaling that does not account for weekly-to-monthly rail replacement intervals at 1 Hz']. The model's low LCOE is a consequence of trusting these defaults; it is NOT a projection of NearStar's actual cost until the company discloses [list 2–3 blocking parameters — target gain, railgun component lifetime, driver cost breakdown]. A sensitivity analysis sweeping [name the same parameters] would show the LCOE range under pessimistic vs. optimistic assumptions, but such analysis is beyond this D1+ scope." This makes the warning actionable — it tells the reader exactly what the model is assuming and what data gaps undermine those assumptions.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: P_native coherence is clean but the Design Point block does not explain the 50 MWe selection
- **Target:** Section 0 (Design Point block, lines 19–29)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The coherence flag confirms P_native is identical across the Design Point block (50 MWe), Section 5 table (50 MWe), and model_setup.py (P_native = 50) — no drift detected. However, the Design Point block states "50 MW to 1 GW+ scalability range" and selects "50 MW — lower bound" without explaining whether this is thermal or electric. The analysis assumes MWe ("assumed MWe for consistency with P_native" in Section 5 line 315), but the source (nearstar-mtif-technical-overview.md) does not distinguish. The Design Point block should clarify this ambiguity explicitly: is 50 MW the company's stated net-electric output, or is it a marketing figure that could be thermal? The analysis Section 1 notes "the '50 MW' figure from marketing materials is not distinguished as thermal vs. electric" (line 49) — this uncertainty should be flagged in the Design Point block, not just in Section 5.
- **Recommendation:** Add a sentence to the Design Point block (after line 24) clarifying the thermal-vs-electric ambiguity: "The 50 MW figure is not explicitly distinguished as thermal or electric in source materials (nearstar-mtif-technical-overview.md, nearstar-website-summary.md). It is assumed to be net-electric (MWe) for model consistency, but this is an interpretation, not a company-grounded specification. If the figure is gross-thermal, the net-electric output would be ~17.5 MWe at 35% thermal efficiency, changing the power scale by a factor of 2.9×." This makes the grounding-confidence rating (`low`) more transparent by exposing a concrete ambiguity in the design-point specification.
- **Priority:** minor
