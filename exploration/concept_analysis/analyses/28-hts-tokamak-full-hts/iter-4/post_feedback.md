VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Interest rate scenarios not implemented despite explicit analysis recommendation
- **Target:** Section 2 (Key LCOE sensitivity parameters) and model scenario table
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** Section 2 explicitly identifies cost of capital as "the primary financial lever" and states it "should be the axis for scenario sweeps." The model output shows interest_rate elasticity of +0.66 — the second-largest LCOE lever after availability, exceeding thermal efficiency (+0.15), construction time (+0.27 elasticity), and all engineering parameters except availability. Despite this call-out, the model runs only a single fixed interest rate (7% DEFAULT) with no scenario sweep. The analysis's own language sets an expectation the model doesn't fulfill, and for a concept with a China-domestic financing context (which could plausibly range from 4–5% for state-adjacent projects to 9–10% for private commercial terms), the interest rate assumption carries as much uncertainty as any engineering parameter.
- **Recommendation:** Add an interest rate sensitivity sweep (e.g., 5%, 7%, 10%) to the scenario table alongside Scenarios A–D, or add an explicit note in the model output explaining why 7% was fixed rather than swept. The analysis's own framing makes this the natural Scenario E. This directly fulfills the stated intent in Section 2 without requiring new analysis work.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Disruption cost pathway is absent while disruption risk is explicitly emphasized
- **Target:** Model CAS22 (C220109) and sensitivity analysis; Section 2 (AI control technical bet)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The model sets C220109 (DEC Equipment) to $0.0, and all disruption parameters (disruption_rate_base, disruption_steepness, disruption_damage, disruption_downtime) show exactly zero elasticity — meaning disruption events have no cost representation in the model. Scenario B (AI Control Underperforms) captures the availability impact of disruption-limited operation, but not the direct cost of disruption events: first-wall and divertor damage, inspection downtime, repair labor, and emergency energy dump equipment. The analysis frames AI plasma control and disruption frequency as the second-ranked technical bet and explicitly identifies disruption mitigation as the primary novel capability claim. Having zero-cost disruption parameters while these parameters appear in the sensitivity sweep creates a misleading picture — they look like active model levers but are dead weight. For a concept where the core value proposition is AI-enabled disruption reduction, the model should either connect disruption parameters to CAS70 O&M costs, or remove them from the sensitivity sweep and add an explicit statement that disruption costs are captured only through availability.
- **Recommendation:** Either activate the disruption cost pathway (connect disruption_rate_base and disruption_damage to an O&M cost increment in CAS70, even with a rough per-event cost like $0.5–2M for first-wall inspection and repair) or remove dead disruption parameters from the sensitivity output and add a footnote to Scenario B explaining that disruption repair costs are not separately modeled. The former is preferred given the analysis emphasis.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Approved peer concepts 29 and 33 absent from cross-concept comparison
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** Section 7 compares Energy Singularity against the conventional large-aspect-ratio ITER baseline, with 21-spherical-tokamak-hts as the approved nearest neighbor and 01-hts-compact-tokamak as a proxy reference. Two approved concepts in the landscape table are direct peers in the compact HTS tokamak family but are not referenced: 29-negative-triangularity-tokamak (Firefly — same HTS wound magnet type, same compact MFE-tokamak family, different plasma shaping; the NT plasma shape confers a potential confinement quality advantage that is TEA-relevant) and 33-state-backed-tokamak-best (Neo Fusion — standard D-shaped tokamak with LTS+HTS hybrid magnets, providing the natural contrast for the full-HTS vs. partial-HTS cost structure). Omitting 33 in particular weakens the "full HTS premium vs. TF-only HTS" framing in the differentiator table: the cost contrast is described qualitatively but no approved concept is cited as a concrete anchor for what the LTS+HTS baseline looks like at similar topology and scale. Without this, the hts_full_coil_premium range (×1.1–×1.3) floats without triangulation against a modeled peer.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief cross-reference to 29 and 33 in Section 7. For 33, note the CAS22 magnet cost difference from the LTS+HTS baseline as a concrete data point for bounding the hts_full_coil_premium range. For 29, note whether the NT plasma shaping confers any LCOE-relevant advantage over the D-shaped design used by Energy Singularity, or flag it as unknown.
- **Priority:** important
