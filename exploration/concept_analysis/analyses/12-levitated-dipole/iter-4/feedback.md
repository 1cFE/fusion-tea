### F-1: Structural nearest neighbor (concept 19) absent from cross-concept notes
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes — nearest-neighbor selection)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The cross-concept comparison names ST-HTS (21) as the technology comparator and FRC (08) as the physics comparator, but omits concept 19 (Orbital Levitated Dipole, D-He3), which shares the identical confinement topology: a levitated HTS dipole coil, no wall-connected field lines, steady-state operation, and direct energy capture. This is the only other levitated dipole in the 37-concept landscape. Comparing concept 12 against concept 19 would directly isolate what fraction of the cost structure is topology-driven (shared: levitated coil, concrete vessel, no divertor) vs. fuel-driven (divergent: D-He3 eliminates the tritium breeding blanket and enables direct energy conversion, removing two major capex categories entirely). Without this comparison, the cross-concept framing cannot distinguish which cost penalties and advantages are inherent to the dipole topology vs. specific to D-T fuel.
- **Recommendation:** Add a comparison paragraph in Section 7 for concept 19 (Orbital Levitated Dipole, D-He3). Note what is shared (levitated coil architecture, no divertor, concrete vessel, HTS flux pump) and what diverges (D-He3 eliminates the tritium breeding system and thermal conversion cycle; direct energy capture changes the BOP cost structure fundamentally). Flag concept 19 as the structural nearest neighbor and explain why ST-HTS and FRC are used as TEA comparators despite their topological distance.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: ICRH→ECRH sensitivity sweep range mismatches analysis narrative
- **Target:** Model sensitivity analysis (ICRH wall-plug efficiency sweep)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The model sweeps heating wall-plug efficiency from 0.50 to 0.75, labeling η=0.50–0.55 as "ECRH fallback." But the analysis explicitly states ECRH wall-plug efficiency is 30–40% (§2.2.7 of the source paper), and the analysis narrative quantifies the ECRH fallback as cutting net output from 208 MWe to ~123–160 MWe (a 23–41% reduction). The model sweep at η=0.50 produces only 183 MWe — a 9–13% reduction, far short of the analysis-computed impact. The actual ECRH scenario (η=0.30–0.40) is never tested despite being the explicitly identified fallback path. The model mislabels 0.50 as "ECRH fallback" when 0.50 is neither achievable for gyrotrons (0.30–0.40) nor an intermediate technology — the label creates false confidence that the ECRH scenario has been modeled.
- **Recommendation:** Extend the ICRH efficiency sensitivity sweep to η=0.30–0.40 to represent actual ECRH performance. Label the sweep points correctly: 0.30–0.40 = ECRH (demonstrated on LDX/RT-1), 0.65–0.70 = ICRH baseline (undemonstrated in dipole geometry). Add a dedicated ECRH scenario row to the scenario comparison table alongside the existing "ECRH fallback" row, ensuring the net power calculation matches the ~123–160 MWe range stated in the analysis.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Blanket replacement schedule is unanchored and not swept
- **Target:** Model CAS72 (scheduled replacements) and Section 5 (missing parameters)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The model assigns 6 blanket replacements over the 40-year plant life (every ~6.7 years), contributing $48.6M/yr — the second-largest scheduled replacement cost after the sacrificial coil. This schedule is not sourced in the model output, the analysis, or the Simpson et al. paper. Section 3 lists "blanket panel replacement schedule" as "Missing at scale," and Section 5 acknowledges first-wall lifetime as truly-unknown/important. Over the plant life, the 6× blanket replacement adds ~$1.9B in O&M (vs. $349M initial capital), making total blanket cost ~$2.3B — larger than the magnet system. Despite this magnitude, the model does not sweep blanket replacement rate as a sensitivity parameter; the only blanket sensitivity shown is unit cost ($/kg). If the replacement period is actually 10 years (4 replacements) vs. 6.7 years (6 replacements), LCOE changes by ~$8–12/MWh — comparable to the thermal efficiency sensitivity.
- **Recommendation:** Add a blanket replacement rate sensitivity sweep (e.g., 3, 6, 10, 15 replacements over 40 years) alongside the existing Li₂O unit cost sweep. Label the baseline 6-replacement assumption as analyst-assumed (no published source) and flag it as a testable hypothesis comparable to the first-wall replacement treatment. If the Simpson et al. paper provides any blanket fluence limit or replacement schedule guidance, cite it; otherwise note the gap explicitly in the model output.
- **Priority:** important
