VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Two differentiators in Section 7 lack stated cost implications
- **Target:** Section 7 (Key Differentiators vs. Conventional Tokamak), differentiators 1 and 2
- **Finding:** Four of the five listed differentiators have explicit cost directions (advantage, penalty, or unknown-with-reasoning). Two do not: (1) "Confinement mechanism" is described as a physics challenge with no scaling database, but no cost implication is stated — is successful scaling cost-neutral? Is failure a binary viability threshold rather than a continuous penalty? (2) "Coil topology" notes that cost is "concentrated in one component that must be periodically replaced," but gives no cost direction — the Section 2 analysis makes clear this is a penalty risk (novel O&M category with no published cost), yet Section 7 presents it as a neutral structural observation. Goal 3 requires every differentiator to carry a stated cost implication.
- **Recommendation:** For the confinement mechanism differentiator, add a sentence stating the cost direction: confinement scaling is a binary threshold risk (if Tahi validates Bohm-like scaling, the concept is viable at the modeled cost; if not, the reactor requires a larger magnet — raising overnight capital — or is nonviable as designed, not merely more expensive). For the coil topology differentiator, state explicitly that cost concentration in a single periodically-replaced component is a penalty risk relative to distributed fixed external coils: it adds a recurring O&M capital line with no analogue, and the magnitude is unquantified.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Section 5 has no parameter row for the disruption-free operation advantage
- **Target:** Section 5 (LCOE-Relevant Parameters), Available and Missing Parameters tables
- **Finding:** Section 7 explicitly identifies disruption-free operation as a cost advantage: no disruption mitigation system, no thermal energy storage for grid coupling, no first-wall fatigue from disruption energy deposition. This is stated as a genuine LCOE advantage vs. inductive tokamaks. However, Section 5 contains no parameter row capturing this advantage — no "disruption-driven first-wall replacement rate (avoided)" or "thermal energy storage capital cost (avoided)," and no note explaining why this advantage is not parameterizable. The cost model cannot capture a stated advantage that has no parameter hook. Goal 3 and Goal 4 both require that cost-relevant differentiators be reflected in the parameter table.
- **Recommendation:** Add one or two rows to the Section 5 Missing Parameters table (gap type: not-yet-sourced) for the disruption-free advantage: e.g., "First wall disruption replacement schedule (avoided) — no tokamak comparator figure exists to quantify the savings; flag as qualitative advantage unless reference tokamak disruption cost data is sourced." This closes the loop between Section 7's stated cost advantage and the modeling inputs.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Nearest-neighbor framing in Section 7 is implicit rather than explicit
- **Target:** Section 7 opening, concept positioning (Goal 1)
- **Finding:** The analysis compares the levitated dipole to ST-HTS and the FRC, and the FRC comparison labels it the "second nearest neighbor in the high-beta compact MFE space." But the analysis never names the nearest-neighbor set explicitly or explains why ST-HTS is the primary comparator (it's chosen for commercial/technology similarity — shared REBCO — not for confinement physics proximity). A reader must infer which concept is the first nearest neighbor and on what basis. Goal 1 requires the 2-3 nearest neighbors to be named.
- **Recommendation:** Add a one-sentence positioning statement at the top of Section 7 naming the two primary comparators and their comparison basis: e.g., "Nearest neighbors: spherical tokamak HTS (technology comparator — shared REBCO supply chain and HTS magnet challenges) and field-reversed configuration (physics comparator — high-beta compact MFE with similar confinement uncertainty profile)."
- **Priority:** minor
