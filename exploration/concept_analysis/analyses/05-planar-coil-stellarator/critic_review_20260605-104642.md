# Critic Review — 05-planar-coil-stellarator

## Headline issues

1. **C220104 outlier (heating at $12.5M) is architecturally correct but the analysis doesn't explain it to the reader.** The sanity check flags C220104 at 0.12× the comparable median ($100M). This is a genuine architectural feature: Helios operates ignited with only 2.5 MW ECRH wallplug, so the library correctly prices it at 5.0 M$/MW × 2.5 MW = $12.5M — an order of magnitude below stellarators that need 20–75 MW of heating. The analysis notes the low heating power in Section 5b (C220104 walkthrough) and Section 2 (Challenge #3), but never explicitly connects this to the cost consequence or flags it as an expected outlier against comparables. A one-sentence note in Section 7 or Section 5b acknowledging that the ignited regime drives C220104 to ~1/8 of the family median would close this accountability gap.

2. **CAS27 outlier ($5M vs $15M median) receives no architectural explanation anywhere.** The sanity check flags CAS27 at 0.33× the comparable median. The Section 5b walkthrough for CAS27 discusses tritium inventory and Pb-17Li fill but concludes "no override" without noting that the library default for a 390 MWe plant will inherently produce a smaller CAS27 than the three comparables at 1,000 MWe (09, 10, 20b), which all show $15M. The $5M value at 1 GWe projection (model_output.txt line 15) suggests a floor or fixed component that doesn't scale. The analysis should explain whether this is simply a scaling artifact or whether there is an architectural reason CAS27 stays low at 1 GWe. Without that explanation, a reader cannot distinguish "library quirk" from "concept feature."

3. **Zero overrides for a concept with clear qualitative cost differentiators leaves money on the table in both directions.** The analyst correctly identifies that Thea published no dollar figures. But the analysis also identifies multiple architectural features that directionally differ from the library default: (a) 336 planar coils vs. the library's implicit coil model (potential manufacturing advantage), (b) V-4Cr-4Ti first wall with an immature supply chain (potential cost penalty on C220101), (c) an exceptionally low 2.5 MW heating requirement (already captured by `p_input`, not an override concern), and (d) COTS 150A power supplies for 324 shaping coils vs. the library's generic magnet power supply costing (potential C220107 advantage). The decision to use zero overrides is defensible under the "no published cost data" rationale, but the analysis should explicitly state the *direction of known bias* introduced by accepting every library default. The reader should know: "the library probably overcharges on C220107 (power supplies) and undercharges on C220101 (first wall material maturity) for this concept."

4. **C220107 outlier ($40.9M vs $84.2M median) is unexplained and may be a legitimate library-scaling artifact, not architecture.** At 1 GWe, C220107 is 0.49× the median. The comparables cluster around $84–92M at 1,000 MWe. The 05 concept at native 390 MWe has C220107 = $45.2M, scaling to $40.9M at 1 GWe. The analysis's Section 5b walkthrough for C220107 notes the COTS relay approach for shaping coils but does not explain why the 1 GWe-projected value ends up half the family. This could be an artifact of how the library scales power supply costs with the two-knob projection for a small-native-scale concept, or it could reflect an actual architectural difference. Either way, it needs a sentence.

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

**dpc (ok):** P_native is coherent at 390 MWe across all three legs — no action needed.

**contract (ok):** `model_setup.py` satisfies the helper-form contract. The `generic` = `native` identity (both $233.6/MWh, both $7,748.8M total) correctly reflects zero overrides: with no overrides, the two forwards must be identical. This is internally consistent.

**count_smell (ok):** Zero overrides against a High archetype-fit grade is within the 0–4 band. The rationale — no published cost data — is articulated in Section 5b.

**sanity (flagged — 4 outlier accounts):** C220104 at 0.12× median, C220106_pump at 0.36× median, C220107 at 0.49× median, and CAS27 at 0.33× median. All four are low-side outliers. C220104 is architecturally explained (ignited plasma, trivially low heating power) but the explanation is dispersed across sections rather than being stated as an expected outlier. C220106_pump is a sub-$1M line item in all comparables (range $0.1–0.8M) — the absolute magnitude is too small to carry LCOE weight and the 0.36× ratio is noise on a near-zero denominator. C220107 and CAS27 are not architecturally attributed in the analysis; both need explanation (see headline issues #2 and #4).

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes ONE named plant throughout: the Helios preconceptual design (Swanson et al. 2025). There is no stitching across published designs. Every parameter in the Section 5 table cites the same arXiv paper (2512.08027), with two exceptions: (a) elongation is inferred from figure inspection (appropriately flagged as low-confidence), and (b) neutron wall loading is analyst-derived from published fusion power and geometry (appropriately flagged as low-confidence and not used as a spec key). The `p_input = 2.5 MW` in `model_setup.py` corresponds to the "2.5 MW budgeted" steady-state auxiliary power from §4.4 of the paper, not the 10 MW startup figure — this is the correct choice for steady-state cost modeling and is documented in the code comment. The `plasma_volume = 500 m³` is passed as a spec key, which is unusual — this parameter presumably helps the library size non-geometric subsystems. It matches Table 1 of the paper.

One minor coherence note: the analysis references a 40.2% thermal conversion efficiency (§4.4 of the paper) but correctly does not pass `eta_th` as a spec key, deferring to the library's Rankine cycle enum. If the library's default Rankine efficiency differs materially from 40.2%, this would silently shift the P_native ↔ thermal power relationship. The analyst noted this as a commented-out line in `model_setup.py:37` — acceptable as documentation but worth flagging if the library default is, say, 33% or 45%.

### Override discipline (judgment)

With zero overrides, there are no entries to evaluate for provenance accuracy, arithmetic, or noise. The judgment question is instead: *should* any of the no-override decisions have gone the other way?

The strongest candidate for an override is **C220104** — the library's ECRH cost rate ($5M/MW) applied to 2.5 MW correctly produces $12.5M, but this result depends entirely on `p_input` flowing through as a spec key. This is handled correctly: the low cost is a natural consequence of the ignited regime, not an override. No override is needed here.

The second-strongest candidate is **C220107** (power supplies). The analysis notes that 324 shaping coils operate at 150 A using "inexpensive COTS relays." The library presumably costs magnet power supplies based on total stored energy or total current capacity, not on per-coil architecture. If the library's default power supply cost model is calibrated to high-current (50 kA) systems, the COTS-relay approach for the shaping coils could be significantly cheaper. However, without a published unit cost from Thea, the analyst cannot quantify this. The no-override decision is defensible but the direction of expected bias should be stated.

The weakest no-override decision is **C220101** (first wall / blanket). V-4Cr-4Ti is a material with no industrial supply chain, explicitly acknowledged as immature by the authors. The library default for a liquid-metal blanket almost certainly uses EUROFER97 or generic RAFM steel pricing. A V-4Cr-4Ti first wall should be *more* expensive than the library default (material novelty premium, bespoke fabrication), but no cost data exists to quantify the premium. The analyst's reasoning is sound — you can't override without data — but the absence of an upward adjustment on C220101 is a known downward bias in the LCOE estimate.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The `result_1gw` column shows the expected pattern for a 390 MWe → 1 GWe scaling: CAS22 scales up from $4,599M to $13,307M (2.89×), while non-reactor-island accounts (CAS23–CAS26, CAS30, CAS50, CAS60, CAS70) scale up by proportions consistent with multi-module or capacity scaling. Within CAS22 sub-accounts, C220103 (magnets at $3,098M) is invariant from native to 1 GWe — this is expected for a stellarator where the magnet cost is dominated by ampere-meters and field strength rather than plant power output. The magnet system is the single largest cost item and doesn't benefit from the two-knob scaling.

The 1 GWe LCOE of $236.6/MWh vs. native LCOE of $233.6/MWh shows a *slight increase* under projection, which is unusual (most concepts benefit from scaling to larger size). This makes sense architecturally: Helios at 390 MWe is already an optimized design point, and the two-knob projection to 1 GWe introduces multi-module costs and scaling penalties on non-reactor accounts without proportional benefit from the reactor island (since the magnet system doesn't scale down per-MWe). This is plausible and internally consistent.

No per-account values appear implausible for architectural reasons. The C220103 invariance is the most notable feature and is expected.

Only 20b (Renaissance, 5 MW) is comparably low at $25M. - **C220107** ($40.9M vs. $84.2M median): The analysis identifies the COTS relay approach for shaping coils but does not connect this to the cost outcome. The comparable values at 1 GWe cluster at $84–92M. ### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

The two assumptions that would most change the LCOE conclusion if wrong:

1. **The library's ampere-meter-based coil cost model is representative of planar HTS coils.** C220103 is $3,098M — by far the largest single sub-account, representing 23% of the 1 GWe overnight cost. The library computes this from B (6 T) and R0 (8 m), presumably via a generic HTS coil cost-per-ampere-meter curve. The planar coil architecture's central claim is that manufacturing simplification reduces cost per ampere-meter relative to 3D coils, but the library cannot capture this without an override. If planar coils are genuinely 30% cheaper per ampere-meter (a plausible but unquantified claim), C220103 drops by ~$900M, LCOE drops by ~$20/MWh. Conversely, if the 336-coil control system, quench protection, and integration complexity adds cost, C220103 could be higher. The analysis identifies this as the primary cost uncertainty (Section 2, Challenge #2; Section 5b, C220103) and Gap #7. This is appropriately flagged.

2. **The ignited regime (p_input = 2.5 MW) holds.** If confinement falls to H_ISS04 = 1.0 (instead of 1.4), the required heating power could increase to 50–100 MW, making C220104 jump from $12.5M to $250–500M and increasing recirculating power enough to reduce net electric output below 390 MWe. This cascading failure is identified in Section 2 (Challenge #3) as a high-impact risk. The analysis correctly flags this. However, the LCOE model as constructed *assumes* the ignited regime works — there is no sensitivity case. This is appropriate for a point-estimate model but worth noting as the single largest downside risk.
