### F-1: Modeling approach not justified for IFE cost structure
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Recommendations) — missing subsection
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis never states whether the costingfe framework is appropriate for laser ICF or explains what adaptations were required. IFE has a structurally different cost profile from any MFE concept: no magnets (C220103 = $0), a laser driver as the single dominant capital item (C220107 = $3,000M), a novel target factory account (C220600), and pulsed availability dynamics driven by chamber clearing rather than plasma stability. The model clearly adapts costingfe via LASER_COST_PER_J and NOAK overrides, but the analysis gives no justification for why this framework was chosen over free-form modeling, nor does it explain what the costingfe framework cannot capture (rep-rate-coupled availability degradation, per-shot optics damage accumulation) and how those gaps are handled. A future analyst cannot assess framework fitness without this context. This directly violates Goal 4 (checklist: "The analysis states whether 1costingfe or free-form modeling is appropriate and why").
- **Recommendation:** Add a paragraph to Section 2 that: (1) states costingfe is used with IFE-specific overrides at C220107 and C220600, (2) explains the structural adaptation (laser driver replaces magnets as the dominant capital account; pulsed chamber dynamics captured through scenario branching on availability rather than a pulsed-operation module), and (3) notes what the framework cannot capture and how those gaps are addressed (availability scenarios, sensitivity sweeps on LASER_COST_PER_J).
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Dominant uncertainties not stated as testable propositions
- **Target:** Section 2 (Dominant LCOE Uncertainties)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The seven dominant uncertainties are described well, and the model output includes explicit scenario tables for laser cost, availability, and target factory. However, the analysis never synthesizes these into explicit testable propositions — statements of the form "IF parameter X achieves threshold Y, THEN LCOE reaches Z and viability is confirmed/denied." The model demonstrates exactly this logic: at availability 88% and laser cost $140/J, LCOE = $79.7/MWh; at availability 58% and laser cost $850/J, LCOE = ~$165/MWh. The commercial viability envelope is fully defined by these two parameters together, but the analysis text frames everything as open uncertainty rather than hypotheses the model tests. This directly violates Goal 4 (checklist: "Key hypotheses are stated as testable propositions, not just open questions").
- **Recommendation:** Add a "Key Model Hypotheses" paragraph at the end of Section 2 with 2–3 explicit propositions. Example framing: (H1) "Viability hypothesis: if availability reaches 80%+ (liquid-wall architecture) AND laser driver achieves ≤$140/J NOAK, LCOE falls to ~$80/MWh — competitive with advanced fission at current projected costs." (H2) "Failure mode hypothesis: if availability is capped at ~68% (OSIRIS historical anchor) and laser cost remains at $300/J, LCOE exceeds $107/MWh regardless of target factory or thermal efficiency optimization." Reference the model scenario tables for the supporting numbers.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: CAS-level cost structure divergence from reference concept not quantified
- **Target:** Section 7 (Key Divergences from Tokamak Baseline)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** Section 7 notes conceptually that "laser driver replaces magnet system as dominant capital cost item" and "pulsed operation introduces target injection, ash clearing" — but gives no magnitudes. The model establishes: C220107 (laser driver) = $3,000M vs. C220103 (magnets) = $0, with laser alone representing 43% of total overnight capital ($6,927M) and 57% of CAS22. No MFE concept in the landscape approaches this concentration in a single account. C220108 (ash removal / target injection) = $396M — comparable in scale to the turbine plant ($395M). These proportions define the structural story of laser ICF economics, but without numbers the narrative assertion is qualitative only. The checklist requires that "CAS-level cost structure differences from the reference concept are noted" — the current text does not satisfy this at the required specificity level.
- **Recommendation:** Add a 3–4 sentence CAS-level quantification to the existing Section 7 divergence bullets: state C220107 at the model's central case ($3,000M at $300/J, 43% of overnight capital), contrast with C220103 = $0 (vs. 20–30% of CAS22 in typical HTS tokamak), and call out C220108 as the IFE-unique pulsed-operations account at turbine-plant scale. This requires no new analysis — it surfaces what the model already computes.
- **Priority:** important
