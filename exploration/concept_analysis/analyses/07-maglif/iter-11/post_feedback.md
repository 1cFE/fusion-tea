### F-1: "96% of plant capital" driver cost claim is a misstatement
- **Target:** Key Differentiators section (Novel driver capital cost category bullet)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The differentiators section states "Driver capital is estimated at 96% of plant capital in the Z-IFE reference (LTD architecture)." This is factually inconsistent with the rest of the analysis. The model output shows driver ($372M) is 8.9% of total plant capital ($4,167M). The 96% figure appears later in Section 2 with correct context: "LTD cavities (12,600 units at ~$28k each) comprising 96% of the total" — meaning 96% of the $372M driver cost is cavities, not 96% of plant capital. The driver cost sweep confirms the misstatement: a 10× driver cost reduction changes LCOE only 10% (77.7 → 69.9 $/MWh), consistent with driver being ~9% of capital, not 96%. The misstatement overstates driver cost's leverage on total LCOE and could mislead readers about which interventions matter most.
- **Recommendation:** Correct the differentiators bullet to read something like: "The pulsed power driver represents the novel dominant direct capital account — LTD cavities alone account for 96% of the $372M driver cost, and the driver is the largest single direct capital item at the component level. However, total plant capital per the Z-IFE cost structure (including 93.6% indirect costs, IDC, O&M, and BOP) is substantially larger than direct driver cost alone." Optionally add the driver's share of total plant capital (roughly 9–15%) to calibrate reader expectations against the scenario sweep results.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: f_rep elasticity in sensitivity table (-0.075) is inconsistent with scenario sweep results
- **Target:** Model output — sensitivity table and REP RATE SCENARIO SWEEP
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The parametric sensitivity table shows `f_rep` with elasticity = -0.075, implying a 10% increase in rep rate reduces LCOE by only 0.75%. But the scenario sweep demonstrates approximately 1/rep_rate LCOE scaling: 0.5 Hz → 77.7 $/MWh, 1.0 Hz → 38.9 $/MWh (50% reduction for 2× rep rate increase, elasticity ≈ -1.0). These two results are inconsistent by more than an order of magnitude. The analysis narrative correctly identifies rep rate as the dominant LCOE lever, and the scenario sweep confirms this — but the sensitivity table gives the opposite impression. If `f_rep` in the CostingFE parameter set represents a recirculating power correction rather than the fundamental Hz value driving P_net = yield × rep_rate × η_th, this should be explicitly noted so readers do not interpret the -0.075 elasticity as capturing rep rate's true LCOE leverage.
- **Recommendation:** Add a note directly below or adjacent to the sensitivity table explaining that `f_rep` in the standard parametric sensitivity does not capture rep rate's dominant LCOE role because net power is computed via an explicit scenario parameterization (yield × Hz × efficiency) rather than through the standard sensitivity perturbation chain. Direct readers to the REP RATE SCENARIO SWEEP for the correct rep rate elasticity. Alternatively, if the model architecture allows it, restructure so the Hz value appears with near-unit elasticity in the sensitivity table.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: No quantified 250 MWe scenario despite Pacific Fusion's commercial target being 4× smaller than the reference plant
- **Target:** Section 2 (Scale note) and model LCOE sweeps
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly flags that Pacific Fusion's 250 MWe commercial target is 4× smaller than the Z-IFE 1000 MWe reference and states "all COE figures should be treated as lower bounds." The Z-IFE data point for 500 MWe (>10 ¢/kWeh at 0.5 Hz) is cited. However, no model scenario quantifies the 250 MWe LCOE, leaving the commercial viability gap unquantified. For a concept where "500 MWe is already >10 ¢/kWeh," the 250 MWe LCOE likely exceeds 15–20 ¢/kWeh — materially above even the pessimistic 0.1 Hz large-plant scenario (~20 ¢/kWeh). This is a directly testable scenario using existing model infrastructure (scale capital by economy-of-scale from Z-IFE data) and would directly answer whether Pacific Fusion's commercial architecture has a viable LCOE path under any achievable rep rate.
- **Recommendation:** Add a 250 MWe scenario to the rep rate sweep (alongside the existing 1000 MWe scenarios), scaling capital using Z-IFE's economy-of-scale relationship (Z-IFE explicitly provides 500 MWe and 2000 MWe data points from which a scale exponent can be estimated). This directly quantifies the LCOE risk for Pacific Fusion's stated commercial design point. A result showing 250 MWe LCOE exceeds 15 ¢/kWeh even at 1.0 Hz would be a significant analysis conclusion — it would establish that competitive LCOE at Pacific Fusion's target scale depends entirely on the IMG driver cost reduction claim and potentially requires a larger first plant than stated.
- **Priority:** important
