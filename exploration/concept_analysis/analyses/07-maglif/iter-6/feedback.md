VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Nearest-neighbor concepts not explicitly designated
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and Key Differentiators framing
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies the MIF confinement family correctly and names Helion and HTS tokamaks as prior cross-reference analyses in Section 7. However, no concept is designated as a *nearest neighbor*, and the comparison set is incomplete. General Fusion's Magnetized Target Fusion (MTF) — pneumatic compression of a magnetized plasma target — is the closest structural analog to MagLIF in the MIF family (both use an imploding liner/target geometry, both destroy a compression element each shot, both face the rep rate problem as the central LCOE lever), yet it appears nowhere in the analysis. The framing treats Helion as the MIF peer, but Helion uses magnetic compression of merging FRC plasmoids, which is structurally less similar to pulsed-power metal-liner implosion than MTF is. Goal 1 (concept positioning via nearest-neighbor comparison) is not fully met.
- **Recommendation:** Add an explicit nearest-neighbor designation — ideally in the Key Differentiators section header or in Section 7 — identifying the 2–3 concepts most similar to MagLIF and the single key distinguishing feature for each. Candidates: (1) MTF/General Fusion — same liner-compression MIF family, different driver (pneumatic vs. pulsed power); (2) Helion — same pulsed MIF rep-rate economics, different compression mechanism and fuel; (3) laser ICF (NIF/IFE) — same IFE consumable-target economics, different driver capital category. The comparison with Helion in Section 7 is already strong; extend it with MTF and summarize the three-way comparison upfront.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Testable hypotheses not evaluated; rep rate sweep absent from model
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Recommendation, Hypotheses 1–3) and model output
- **Finding:** Section 2 states three testable hypotheses (rep rate break-even, target cost tolerance, driver cost cliff) and correctly identifies rep rate as the dominant LCOE lever. The model output explicitly disclaims that its sensitivity table "does NOT capture the dominant MagLIF LCOE drivers" and identifies rep rate, target cost per shot, driver $/J, and plant scale as the four real levers. Yet no rep rate sweep or parametric table is provided anywhere. The Z-IFE data in Section 5 is sufficient to directly evaluate Hypothesis 1: COE as a function of rep rate can be read from the cited Z-IFE table (0.1 Hz → ~20 ¢/kWh; 0.5 Hz → 7.0 ¢/kWh; optimal 1.0–1.8 Hz → ~5–6 ¢/kWh), and this forms the answer to the break-even question for the LTD architecture. The analysis contains all the data required to answer Hypothesis 1 but does not assemble the answer. For a decision-maker using this analysis, the model headline figures (6.75 and 12.55 ¢/kWh) stand without the rep rate context that would show how sensitive they are to the most critical assumption.
- **Recommendation:** Add a simple rep rate sensitivity table — derivable from the Z-IFE data already in Section 5 — showing LCOE vs. rep rate across the 0.1–1.8 Hz range for the Z-IFE LTD architecture at both the 1000 MWe and 250 MWe design points. This directly answers Hypothesis 1 and makes the model output interpretable in its correct context. The table does not require new sources: it is a synthesis of data already present. Place it in Section 2 immediately after the hypothesis list, labeled as "Hypothesis 1 evaluation (Z-IFE LTD architecture, indicative only)."
- **Priority:** important
