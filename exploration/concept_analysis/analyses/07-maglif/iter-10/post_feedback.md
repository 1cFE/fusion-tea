VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Analysis text overstates driver cost reduction as a sufficient path to competitive LCOE
- **Target:** Section 2 (Hypothesis 1 evaluation table and interpretation paragraph)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The Hypothesis 1 interpretation paragraph states that break-even at 1000 MWe scale requires "either rep rates above 0.5 Hz (beyond the RTL technology) **or** the IMG architecture's claimed 5–10× driver cost reduction." But the model's own Hypothesis 3 sweep (DRIVER COST SWEEP in model_output.txt) shows that a 10× driver cost reduction at 0.5 Hz only reduces LCOE from 68.8 to 61.8 $/MWh — still well above the 40–60 $/MWh competitive threshold. Driver cost reduction alone at 0.5 Hz is a minor lever (~10% LCOE improvement), not a sufficient condition for break-even. The "or" framing is internally inconsistent with the model result, and risks overstating the commercial impact of the IMG driver cost advantage (Goal 3 — TEA implication of differentiator is mischaracterized).
- **Recommendation:** Revise the Hypothesis 1 interpretation to reflect the model result: driver cost reduction alone cannot achieve competitive LCOE at 0.5 Hz — break-even requires rep rate ≥ 0.57 Hz (at $372M driver) or a combination of rep rate improvement AND driver cost reduction. Reframe driver cost reduction as a secondary lever that shifts the required break-even rep rate, not an independent path to competitiveness. A sentence like "IMG's claimed 5–10× driver cost reduction narrows the required rep rate threshold slightly, but the model shows rep rate ≥ ~0.5 Hz is required regardless of driver cost" would correctly represent the result.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: No combined rep-rate × driver-cost scenario matrix
- **Target:** Model sweeps (model_setup.py / model_output.txt)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The model separately sweeps rep rate (REP RATE SWEEP) and driver cost (DRIVER COST SWEEP), but never varies them jointly. The central commercial viability question for MagLIF is: "At what (rep_rate, driver_cost) combination does LCOE reach competitive levels, and how much does the IMG driver cost reduction lower the required break-even rep rate?" From the two independent sweeps, the model shows competitive LCOE requires rep rate ≥ ~0.57 Hz at $372M driver — but it doesn't show how the break-even rep rate shifts as driver cost drops (e.g., at $75M driver, does break-even drop to 0.45 Hz?). This 2D interaction is the most policy-relevant output the model can produce (Goal 4 — modeling the hypotheses that have the most leverage), and its absence means the three hypotheses are evaluated in isolation rather than as a system.
- **Recommendation:** Add a 2D scenario matrix sweeping rep rate (0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0 Hz) × driver cost ($372M, $186M, $75M, $37M), reporting LCOE at each cell. This directly answers whether the IMG driver cost improvement changes the rep rate break-even threshold and whether there is a (rep_rate, driver_cost) combination achievable with current technology trajectory that reaches 40–60 $/MWh. A 4×4 table is sufficient.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: No explicit capacity factor / availability scenario sweep despite highest standard sensitivity
- **Target:** Model sweeps (model_setup.py / model_output.txt)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The standard sensitivity analysis shows availability with elasticity −0.98 — by far the highest of any engineering parameter — yet no explicit availability scenario sweep is included alongside the rep rate and driver cost sweeps. The analysis text (Section 5 parameter table) flags two distinct scenarios: thick liquid wall success (85–90% CF, low confidence) vs. failure (60–75% CF, inferred), with a full Z-IFE gap (Gap 5) on chamber lifetime. The capacity factor assumption drives almost ±1:1 proportional LCOE change, which means the 85% assumption — cited as low confidence — carries more LCOE sensitivity than the entire driver capital account. Without a sweep, the model cannot quantify the downside from liquid wall underperformance, which is the primary scenario branch for the MagLIF-specific first-wall risk (Goal 5 — capturing key risks in the TEA).
- **Recommendation:** Add an availability scenario sweep at 60%, 70%, 75%, 85%, and 90%, clearly labeled as (liquid wall failure, pessimistic, conservative, Z-IFE assumed, liquid wall success). Report LCOE at 0.5 Hz baseline for each. This converts the Section 5 "low confidence" flag on capacity factor into a concrete LCOE range that downstream comparison work can use.
- **Priority:** important
