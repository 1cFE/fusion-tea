VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Per-shot consumable O&M absent from model despite analysis identifying it as potentially the binding LCOE constraint
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 2) and model output CAS70/CAS220600
- **Finding:** Section 2 quantifies that cryo target consumables at $10/shot and 1 Hz would produce $300M+/yr in operating cost — "comparable to or larger than annual capital amortization on the driver" — and identifies the commercial viability threshold as $1–2/shot with no demonstrated path there. Yet the model shows CAS220600 (target factory) at $11.5M capital and CAS70 (O&M) at $99.6M/yr with no per-shot variable component. The LCOE result of 82.8 $/MWh is therefore missing the potentially dominant operating cost term. The analysis correctly characterizes the risk but the model is inconsistent with the analysis narrative's emphasis on consumables as a primary LCOE driver.
- **Recommendation:** Add a per-shot consumable O&M parameter to the model output (target $/shot × rep rate × operating hours/yr = annual consumable cost), and show how the LCOE shifts under the $1/shot (competitive) and $10/shot (failure) scenarios. Alternatively, add an explicit note in the model output calibrating the LCOE impact of consumable cost scenarios, so readers do not interpret 82.8 $/MWh as a complete estimate that includes the consumable risk.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Rep rate sensitivity absent from model, breaking the primary modeling recommendation
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Recommendation, Hypothesis 1) and model output sensitivity table
- **Finding:** Section 2 states that rep rate is "the primary LCOE lever" (10× rep rate → 10× LCOE) and recommends testing the rep rate break-even hypothesis as the first of three explicit testable propositions. The model output's sensitivity table explicitly acknowledges: "LCOE sensitivity to rep rate is approximately -1.0 (10x rep rate → 10x LCOE). This elasticity is NOT captured in the framework sensitivity above." The model provides a single point at 0.5 Hz with no rep rate sweep, leaving the analysis's primary testable hypothesis untested. The framework sensitivity table therefore shows availability (-0.957) and interest rate (+0.825) as the apparent top drivers — an artifact of fixing rep rate — without clarifying that this ranking only holds within a given rep rate scenario.
- **Recommendation:** Add a rep rate scenario comparison to the model output showing LCOE at the 0.1 Hz Z-IFE baseline (~20 ¢/kWeh from source), the 0.5 Hz current model, and the 1 Hz commercial target. This directly tests Hypothesis 1 from Section 2 and makes clear that the framework sensitivity ranking is conditional on a fixed rep rate assumption, not a statement about primary commercial levers.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Buildings ($896M, 18% of overnight capital) unexplained as a major cost center
- **Target:** Section 2 (CAS-level cost structure table) and model output CAS21
- **Finding:** The model shows CAS21 Buildings at $896M — larger than the driver override ($372M) and larger than IDC ($878M), comprising ~18% of overnight capital. For reference, advanced tokamak plant studies (ARIES-AT class) typically show buildings at $150–250M for comparable net electric output. The analysis's CAS structure table focuses on driver, consumables, and magnet differences but does not address Buildings at all. For a concept the analysis frames as benefiting from "no superconducting magnets" as a first-order cost advantage, a buildings cost 3–5× higher than tokamak analogues is counterintuitive and warrants acknowledgment. This may correctly reflect the large pulsed power driver hall footprint (Pacific Fusion DS: 73m × 80m for a non-commercial machine) and blast-hardened chamber construction, but the analysis should state whether large civil construction is a structural cost penalty that partially offsets the SC magnet elimination advantage.
- **Recommendation:** Add a sentence or row in the Section 2 CAS table noting that MagLIF's large-footprint driver hall and blast-hardened chamber infrastructure likely create a substantial civil construction cost premium relative to compact tokamaks, and whether this is reflected in the Z-IFE reference cost or is a separate modeling uncertainty.
- **Priority:** minor
