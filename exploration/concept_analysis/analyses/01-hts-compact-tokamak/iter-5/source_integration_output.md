VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: REBCO tape pricing in the analysis is anchored to 2014 figures that a 2025 source now updates
- **Target:** Section 2 (Hypothesis 1), Section 4 (REBCO Supply Chain), Section 5 (Parameters Table — REBCO rows)
- **Finding:** The ScienceDirect 2025 source (sciencedirect-science-article-pii-s2772830725000390.md) reports that leading PLD-REBCO manufacturers are currently selling tape at $20/m, with top producers collectively supplying >3,000 km-12mm annually (>50% of global HTS wire production). The analysis consistently uses 2014 prices ($36–198/m) as its REBCO cost baseline and states Hypothesis 1 as "At current 2014 tape prices." The $20/m 2025 market price sits below the entire 2014 range — including the low end ($36/m). In $/kA-m terms (the economically relevant metric), $20/m at ~200 A/4mm tape is approximately $100/kA-m, still ~10× above the $10/kA-m commercial target. However, the implied trajectory — from $144–792/kA-m (2014) to ~$100/kA-m (2025) — represents substantial convergence that the analysis does not reflect. The current framing presents REBCO cost as a larger obstacle than the updated data supports.
- **Recommendation:** (1) In Section 4, replace "REBCO tape pricing in 2014 ranged from $36 to $198/m" with a sentence acknowledging the 2014 baseline and noting the 2025 market price of ~$20/m from industrialized PLD manufacturers. (2) Add a row to the Section 5 parameters table: "REBCO tape price (current, ~2025) | ~$20/m | sciencedirect-science-article-pii-s2772830725000390.md | medium | PLD-REBCO from leading manufacturers; production >3,000 km-12mm/yr from top producers alone." (3) Update Hypothesis 1 in Section 2 to replace "At current 2014 tape prices ($36–198/m, equivalent to ~$144–792/kA-m)" with current prices, and note that the $10/kA-m target remains ~10× below the 2025 market price but is now ~1.5–8× closer than the 2014 figures implied — reducing (but not eliminating) the REBCO cost risk.
- **Priority:** important
