### F-1: Hypothesis 2 CF claim is mathematically inconsistent with the model's capital structure
- **Target:** Section 2 (Hypothesis 2) and Section 5 (Modeling Approach)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** Hypothesis 2 states "the concept achieves sub-$100/MWh LCOE only if capacity factor exceeds approximately 70–80%." This claim is incompatible with the model's own cost structure. At $12,578M total capital for 261 MWe, even at 100% capacity factor the capital-only contribution to LCOE is ~$440/MWh ($12,578M × 8% FCR ÷ 2.286 TWh/yr). The model correctly produces 571 $/MWh at 85% CF; the analysis's narrative claim is off by roughly 6×. The CATF IWG 60–100 $/MWh NOAK range cited in Hypothesis 4 applies to larger, more cost-optimized NOAK plants (~500–1000 MWe at lower $/kWe) — not to ARC at 261 MWe with a $6.9B HTS magnet system. The analysis never reconciles this gap, leaving the cost competitiveness framing unsupported.
- **Recommendation:** Remove or replace the "sub-$100/MWh at 70–80% CF" conditional in Hypothesis 2. Reframe the LCOE trajectory correctly: at the 2015 design point (261 MWe, NOAK HTS magnets), the model-derived NOAK LCOE is ~570 $/MWh — well above the generic fusion competitive range. State what trajectory would be required to close the gap: primarily (a) REBCO tape cost reduction to ~$10/kA-m (reduces C220103 by ~10×, approximately halving total capital), (b) output scaling to the 400+ MWe CFS commercial target, and (c) both together. The CATF NOAK range should be cited as a future-fleet target, not the current concept's expected performance. This reframing also corrects the Section 5 capacity factor discussion, which currently treats CF as the primary LCOE lever — at current capital costs, CF variation produces a proportional LCOE swing (~2× for 50% vs. 90%) on top of an already uncompetitive baseline.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: REBCO cost sensitivity absent from sensitivity output
- **Target:** Section 5 (Modeling Approach) and model sensitivity table
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** REBCO tape cost (C220103) is identified throughout the analysis as the primary LCOE sensitivity — Section 2 calls for "at least a 5× range," Section 5 calls for "at least 10× the $10/kA-m commercial target," and assumption #3 in model_output.txt states "vary C220103 by ±50%." Yet C220103 does not appear in the sensitivity table at all. The table is dominated by `availability` (−0.95) and `construction_time_yr` (+0.29). At $6.9B out of $12.6B total capital (~55%), a ±50% C220103 variation should produce LCOE elasticity of roughly ±0.27 — comparable to construction time. Whether the sweep was run but not reported, or never implemented, the absence directly contradicts the analysis's primary stated lever. The ±50% declared range also understates the declared uncertainty (analysis calls for 5–10× span, not 2×).
- **Recommendation:** Add C220103 to the sensitivity sweep and report its elasticity in the output table. Widen the sweep range to bracket the full REBCO uncertainty: a 10× cost reduction scenario (C220103 × 0.1, representing the $10/kA-m commercial target) and a 3× increase scenario (representing near-term FOAK manufacturing uncertainty) alongside the ±50% central sweep. Report all three as named scenarios rather than a single elasticity figure, since the distribution is not symmetric around the central value.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: I-mode failure scenario branch absent from model
- **Target:** Section 2 (Hypothesis 3) and model scenario branches
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** Hypothesis 3 explicitly categorizes I-mode accessibility as a "necessary condition for economic viability" and states that I-mode failure reduces net output from ~261 MWe to ~80–100 MWe while holding capital cost fixed — producing a 2.5–3× LCOE increase. The analysis states this should be modeled as a discrete scenario branch. The current model output contains one scenario branch (FOAK at 2× C220103), but no I-mode failure branch. This leaves the most significant physics-driven economic risk unquantified in the model output, despite Hypothesis 3 explicitly framing it as a testable discrete case.
- **Recommendation:** Add a discrete I-mode failure scenario alongside the FOAK case. Use P_net = 90 MWe (midpoint of the 80–100 MWe H-mode fallback per arc-reactor-specifications.md §3.5), same capital cost as NOAK central case, same availability. Report resulting LCOE (expected ~1500–1600 $/MWh from ~2.7× output reduction). Label clearly as "H-mode fallback (I-mode inaccessible at ARC operating point)" so it reads as a physics scenario rather than an operating choice. This directly operationalizes Hypothesis 3 as a quantified risk case.
- **Priority:** important
