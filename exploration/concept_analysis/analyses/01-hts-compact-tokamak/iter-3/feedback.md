VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Parameter table missing rows for two cost-relevant differentiators
- **Target:** Section 5 (LCOE-Relevant Parameters) and Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes)
- **Finding:** Section 7 identifies demountable TF coil joints and I-mode confinement as novel differentiators with direct cost implications — respectively, higher joint fabrication cost offset by maintenance access improvements, and reduced first-wall/divertor replacement frequency vs. H-mode ELMy operation. Both cost effects are stated in Section 7 but have no corresponding parameter rows in Section 5. The parameter table covers REBCO cost, FLiBe cost, and capacity factor, but does not carry the demountable joint fabrication premium or the first-wall/divertor replacement frequency (and its sensitivity to confinement regime) into the model parameter inventory. This means both cost lines would be silently dropped when the model is built.
- **Recommendation:** Add placeholder rows to the Section 5 parameter table for: (a) demountable coil joint fabrication cost premium vs. welded joints (marked not-yet-sourced, with a note to estimate from ITER coil joint cost data or ARC §4.2 coil structure estimates); and (b) divertor/first-wall replacement frequency (marked truly-unknown, with a range spanning I-mode success vs. H-mode fallback, cross-referencing the Section 2 Hypothesis 3 failure mode). These can carry low-confidence estimates or data-gap flags — the goal is that the model builder sees these as parameters to populate, not lines to omit.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: No CAS-level cost account mapping
- **Target:** Section 5 (Modeling Approach) and Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes)
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly recommends free-form parametric scaling rather than a full CAS-structured model, and the reasoning is sound given available data. However, the project's cross-concept comparison axis is CAS10-LCOE — the analysis needs to establish where ARC's costs fall in that taxonomy, even if the model doesn't use the CAS framework internally. The analysis discusses magnet cost dominance (~92% of fabricated cost), blanket cost, and BOP fractions, but never maps these to CAS account numbers (e.g., CAS22 for magnets, CAS26 for breeding blanket, CAS23 for heating). Without this mapping, the cross-concept comparison in Section 7 and the `Reuses` relationships cannot be anchored to a common cost structure.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief CAS account sketch to the Section 5 modeling approach — even a three-row table listing the dominant CAS accounts for ARC, whether they are novel vs. analogued to conventional tokamak, and whether they are covered by the existing $5.56B cost basis or require BOP analogue estimation. The goal is to give the cross-concept comparator a CAS-to-ARC mapping, not to restructure the model.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: FLiBe blanket failure mode not formulated as a TEA consequence
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges) and Key Testable Hypotheses
- **Finding:** The FLiBe blanket is the lowest-TRL subsystem (TRL 3–4) and is rated "high impact" in Section 2. Three concrete failure modes are identified: tritium extraction rate insufficient, MHD flow disruption, and radiation-accelerated Inconel corrosion. However, unlike the REBCO, capacity factor, and I-mode hypotheses, the FLiBe section does not state what happens to the cost model if the blanket fails. Blanket failure would likely force a redesign toward a solid ceramic breeder module approach (ITER/ARIES-AT style), which changes the BOP cost structure substantially: eliminates the integrated FLiBe coolant/breeder function, adds solid module remote handling, and requires separate cooling circuits. This is a scenario branch with direct LCOE implications, but the analysis treats it only as a technical risk, not a cost-model branch.
- **Recommendation:** Add a fourth testable hypothesis to Section 2 covering the FLiBe blanket: state the condition for blanket success (tritium extraction demonstrated at kg/day rates, MHD effects bounded to within design tolerance), and state the cost consequence of failure (blanket redesign to solid ceramic modules increases blanket fabrication cost toward ITER TBM-class cost, eliminates the integrated coolant advantage, and adds BOP complexity). Frame this as a scenario branch in the modeling approach: the model should include a "FLiBe failure / solid blanket fallback" scenario alongside the base case.
- **Priority:** important
