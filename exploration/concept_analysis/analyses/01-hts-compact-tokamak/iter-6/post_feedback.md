VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Model LCOE result not reconciled against competitive benchmark
- **Target:** Section 2 (Key Testable Hypotheses) and Section 5 (Modeling Approach)
- **Finding:** The model produces a NOAK LCOE of 640 $/MWh. The analysis cites the CATF IWG NOAK benchmark of 60–100 $/MWh (Hypothesis 4) — a 6–10× gap. The model output notes this comparison without explanation. The analysis lists sensitivity parameters (REBCO price, capacity factor, regulatory multiplier) but never connects any combination of them to a viable LCOE trajectory. A sensitivity sweep of availability from 75% → 90% reduces LCOE by ~15% (to ~545 $/MWh); the interest rate from 8% → 5% reduces it further but still leaves a >5× gap to 100 $/MWh. The TEA's core function is to assess whether economic viability is achievable under any realistic parameter combination — without this reconciliation, the analysis cannot answer Goal 3 (TEA implications) for this concept.
- **Recommendation:** Add a "viability pathway" paragraph at the end of Section 5 Modeling Approach. State explicitly: at the modeled NOAK parameter set (75% availability, current REBCO-based magnet cost, 261 MWe), the central LCOE estimate is ~640 $/MWh — far above grid competitiveness. Identify what combination of scale-up (e.g., 400 MWe design), magnet fabrication cost reduction, capacity factor, and/or REBCO price would bring LCOE into the 60–100 $/MWh range (or state that no plausible combination does so, which would itself be a key TEA finding). If the answer requires a 10× reduction in manufacturing cost per tonne — not just tape price — say so explicitly, since that changes the interpretation of Hypothesis 1.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: REBCO tape price conflated with magnet fabrication cost as primary uncertainty
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 1 and Hypothesis 1) and Section 5 (Primary Scaling Axes)
- **Finding:** Section 2 (Challenge 1) correctly states that REBCO materials account for only $160–260M of the $5.1–5.2B magnet/structure cost — roughly 3–5% materials, 95–97% fabrication labor and tooling. Yet Hypothesis 1 frames "REBCO tape cost falling to ≤$10/kA-m" as the necessary condition for cost competitiveness, and Section 5 lists "REBCO tape cost [$/kA-m]" as driving "80–90% of the nuclear island cost uncertainty." At 3–5% materials fraction, a 10× tape price reduction saves approximately $135–180M on a $12.5B plant — a ~1–1.5% LCOE reduction. This is not the path from 640 to 60 $/MWh. The fabrication cost assumption ($1.06M/tonne, implicit NOAK/learning, from ARIES/FIRE analogues) carries far more LCOE leverage but is treated as a fixed input rather than a primary uncertainty. The tape price uncertainty is real but second-order in magnitude; conflating it with the fabrication cost trajectory obscures the actual dominant hypothesis the model should test.
- **Recommendation:** Revise Hypothesis 1 and the Section 5 primary axis description to distinguish two separable claims: (a) REBCO tape price uncertainty brackets the materials cost component (genuine, small in magnitude), and (b) NOAK fabrication cost per tonne — the $1.06M/tonne assumption — is the dominant leverage point for cost reduction (requires manufacturing scale-up and automation, not tape price). State the fabrication cost scaling as the primary hypothesis: "ARC's magnet fabrication cost becomes cost-competitive only if the $/tonne rate falls toward ARIES-class levels through manufacturing maturation, independent of tape price." The tape price sweep remains valid for bracketing materials uncertainty but should be presented as secondary to the fabrication cost trajectory.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Sensitivity table sweeps near-zero parameters; primary axes from analysis are absent
- **Target:** Section 5 (Modeling Approach, Primary Scaling Axes) and model_output.txt sensitivity table
- **Finding:** The model sensitivity output lists ~30 engineering parameters, of which 28 have LCOE elasticity below 0.015 (essentially zero). The three parameters the analysis identifies as primary scaling axes — REBCO tape cost, regulatory cost multiplier, and implicitly magnet fabrication cost — do not appear in the sensitivity table. Note #3 in the model output mentions a ±50% C220103 sensitivity sweep but does not show the result. The two highest-elasticity entries (availability at −0.96 and interest_rate at +0.77) are consistent with the analysis, but the table as presented is dominated by parameters (plasma_t, blanket_t, vessel_t, etc.) that contribute negligibly to LCOE for this CAPEX-dominated concept. This makes the sensitivity output directly inconsistent with the analysis's stated modeling recommendations.
- **Recommendation:** Replace the low-elasticity engineering parameter rows with the sweeps the analysis explicitly requires: (a) C220103 ±50% (and ideally ×0.1 to ×2.0 to bracket REBCO/fabrication uncertainty), (b) regulatory multiplier as a discrete scenario column (1.0× vs. 2.2×), and (c) plant scale as a discrete scenario (261 MWe vs. 400 MWe). Retain availability and interest_rate as the top two continuous sensitivities. Parameters with |elasticity| < 0.01 should be dropped or moved to a secondary table — they communicate that the model is insensitive to plasma geometry, which is correct, but burying the primary axes under 28 irrelevant rows obscures the model's actual message.
- **Priority:** important
