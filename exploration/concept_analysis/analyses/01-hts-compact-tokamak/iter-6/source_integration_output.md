### F-1: O&M cost anchor from FECONS — updates gap #7 from truly-unknown
- **Target:** Section 5 (LCOE-Relevant Parameters — Operating Cost rows) and Section 6 gap #7
- **Finding:** The analysis currently lists annual O&M as "truly-unknown" with a placeholder of "$50–100M/yr [estimated from fission BoP analogue]." The FECONS/pyFECONs framework paper (arxiv-2601-21724, Woodruff et al.) provides a fusion-specific O&M rate of **$60/kWe-yr**, yielding $15.7M/yr at ARC's 2015 design point (261 MWe) or $24M/yr at the updated 400 MWe target — roughly 2–4× lower than the fission-BoP analogue in the analysis. This is a material downward revision to O&M uncertainty, since O&M affects LCOE additively. The source also provides an illustrative LCOE of 55.1 $/MWh at 0.9 availability / 636.75 MWe — a fusion-specific contemporary reference absent from the analysis (which relies on ARIES-AT at 5¢/kWh in 2000–2003 USD as its only LCOE benchmark).
- **Recommendation:** In Section 5, add a new O&M parameter row citing FECONS $60/kWe-yr as a fusion-specific anchor (source: arxiv-2601-21724), alongside the existing fission-BoP analogue. Update the confidence from "—" to "low" and note the FECONS figure is a framework reference, not ARC-specific. Add the FECONS illustrative LCOE (55.1 $/MWh, 636.75 MWe, 0.9 availability) as a second LCOE benchmark row alongside ARIES-AT. In Section 6, update gap #7 status from "truly-unknown" to "not-yet-sourced" and add arxiv-2601-21724 to the source recommendation.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Indirect cost fraction quantified — refines the BOP modeling approach
- **Target:** Section 5 (Modeling Approach) and Section 6 gap #1
- **Finding:** The analysis's modeling approach states that BOP, indirect, and financing costs "can double-to-triple component cost" (referencing ITER analogues) but does not give a concrete fraction for the indirect cost component. The FECONS framework paper (arxiv-2601-21724) quantifies indirect/owner/supplementary/financial costs at **29% of total capital cost (TCC)** in its reference design, with direct costs at 71% of TCC. This gives a structural split: for a $7–9B ARC total direct cost estimate (nuclear island + BOP), indirect costs would add ~$3–4B, putting total plant cost closer to $10–13B rather than "double-to-triple the $5.56B fabricated figure." The current framing ("double-to-triple $5.56B") suggests $11–17B, which overstates the upper bound relative to the FECONS reference case if BOP is treated correctly. The FECONS framework also decomposes direct costs into nuclear island (~Account 22, ~44% of direct) and conventional BOP accounts (structures/site ~17%, turbine/electrical/heat rejection ~20% combined) — a breakdown the analysis could use to bound gap #1 without requiring ARC-specific BOP data.
- **Recommendation:** In Section 5 Modeling Approach paragraph (a), replace the open-ended "double-to-triple component cost" framing with the FECONS reference split: direct costs ~71% of TCC, indirect/owner/financial ~29% of TCC (cite arxiv-2601-21724). Note that this implies a total plant cost roughly 1.4× the direct cost (nuclear island + BOP), not 2–3× the nuclear island alone. Update gap #1 in Section 6 to add arxiv-2601-21724 as a partial-address source for indirect cost fractions.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: FOAK vs. NOAK distinction absent from cost estimate confidence
- **Target:** Section 5 (Capital Cost parameter table) and Section 2 (Challenges)
- **Finding:** The CATF International Working Group methodology paper (arxiv-2602-19389) explicitly frames fusion cost estimates on the AACE classification scale (Class 1–5, ±10% to ±50% accuracy) and discusses the FOAK (First-of-a-Kind) vs. NOAK (Nth-of-a-Kind) cost gap as a primary source of uncertainty for first commercial plants — with FOAK premiums typical in advanced nuclear on the order of 2–3× NOAK. The analysis assigns "medium" confidence to ARC's $5.56B fabricated component cost without flagging whether this is a FOAK or NOAK basis. The ARC 2015 paper's costing methodology (mass-proportional scaling at $1.06M/tonne, benchmarked against ARIES/FIRE conceptual designs) implicitly reflects NOAK learning assumptions, meaning the first physical ARC plant would very likely cost substantially more. This gap is not called out anywhere in Section 2 (Challenges) or Section 6 (gap inventory), and it affects the plausibility assessment of the "$29,200/kWe" inferred figure in Section 5 — which is already labeled low confidence but without explanation of why it might be systematically low.
- **Recommendation:** In Section 5, add a table note to the Total fabricated component cost row clarifying that the $5.56B figure reflects NOAK/learning-assumed scaling, and that FOAK costs for the first ARC plant would be expected to be substantially higher (cite AACE classification framework from arxiv-2602-19389). In Section 2 or as a new Hypothesis 4, add an explicit FOAK-to-NOAK cost trajectory as a discrete scenario dimension: the analysis currently models REBCO cost and capacity factor as continuous sensitivity parameters, but the FOAK cost premium is a step-function risk that applies specifically to the first 1–3 plants and disappears after fleet learning — it should be named as a scenario branch alongside the regulatory multiplier.
- **Priority:** important
