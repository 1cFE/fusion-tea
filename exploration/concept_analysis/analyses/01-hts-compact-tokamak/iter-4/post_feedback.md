VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Modeling approach text contradicts the model's actual cost methodology
- **Target:** Section 5 (Modeling Approach)
- **Finding:** The Modeling Approach states "use ARC's $5.56B as the direct nuclear island cost basis" but the model uses a bottom-up NOAK REBCO tape calculation ($50/kAm × 5,730 km × 8× markup = $573M for coils; CAS22 total = $1,428M; total capital = $3,325M). These two approaches are irreconcilable — applying the $5.56B nuclear island basis would produce an LCOE roughly 3–4× higher than the 172 $/MWh shown in the model output. The discrepancy is not a calculation error: the 2015 paper's $5.56B is a FOAK fabricated-component cost using $1.06M/tonne mass-based scaling, while the model uses NOAK materials pricing. Both are legitimate modeling choices, but the written guidance says one thing and the model does another. Goal 4 requires the modeling approach to correctly describe what the model does and why. (Goal 4)
- **Recommendation:** Replace the "use ARC's $5.56B as the direct nuclear island cost basis" instruction with an explicit description of the NOAK bottom-up approach: REBCO tape quantity × price per kAm × markup factor for the magnet accounts, with ARIES-AT fractions for BOP accounts. If FOAK is also worth modeling, frame it explicitly as a scenario branch (FOAK vs. NOAK) with separate LCOE outputs rather than conflating the two in a single guidance paragraph.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: I-mode scenario branch is identified in Section 2 but absent from Section 5 modeling structure
- **Target:** Section 2 (Hypothesis 3) and Section 5 (Modeling Approach / Parameters)
- **Finding:** Hypothesis 3 correctly identifies I-mode inaccessibility as a binary structural outcome: I-mode accessible → ~261 MWe; I-mode inaccessible → ~80–100 MWe, a 2.5–3× increase in $/kWe. This is a larger potential LCOE swing than the REBCO price uncertainty at NOAK pricing. However, the Section 5 Modeling Approach lists only three primary scaling axes (REBCO cost, capacity factor, regulatory multiplier) and does not include I-mode regime as a scenario branch. The parameter table has no scenario flag or parameter row for I-mode availability, leaving Hypothesis 3 documented but disconnected from the cost model structure. Goal 4 requires that key hypotheses are expressed in the model, not just stated as analysis prose. (Goals 4, 5)
- **Recommendation:** Add I-mode accessibility as a fourth scenario dimension in the Section 5 Modeling Approach. At minimum, state explicitly: "I-mode failure is a discrete scenario branch, not a sensitivity parameter — model it as a separate run with net electric output reduced to 80–100 MWe and all capital accounts held constant." Add a one-row parameter entry in Section 5 for "Net electric output (I-mode inaccessible scenario)" with value "80–100 MWe" and confidence "uncertain." This gives the cost model a clear handle on what the analysis identifies as a high-impact risk.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Demountable TF coil joints identified as cost-relevant differentiator but absent from parameter table and gap inventory
- **Target:** Section 7 (Differentiator Table) and Section 5 (Parameters / Gap Inventory)
- **Finding:** The Section 7 differentiator table correctly identifies demountable TF coil joints as a novel feature with a cost implication: "higher joint fabrication cost; but enables in-situ blanket/vessel maintenance without full reactor disassembly, reducing lifecycle maintenance downtime." This is a two-sided cost item — a capital cost penalty (joint fabrication and tooling) offset by an O&M benefit (reduced downtime during blanket/vessel maintenance). Neither side is parameterized in the Section 5 table, and no gap entry exists in Section 6 for joint fabrication cost or the maintenance availability benefit. For a differentiator that the analysis labels as cost-relevant, the lack of any parameter or gap entry means the model silently omits it. (Goals 3, 4)
- **Recommendation:** Add two entries to Section 6: (a) "Demountable TF coil joint incremental capital cost vs. welded coil design" — gap type not-yet-sourced, criticality important; (b) "Availability benefit from demountable coils: reduction in scheduled outage duration for blanket/vessel maintenance" — gap type not-yet-sourced, criticality important. Add corresponding parameter rows in Section 5 with values marked unknown and a note pointing to Section 6. This closes the loop between the differentiator claim and the cost model structure.
- **Priority:** important
