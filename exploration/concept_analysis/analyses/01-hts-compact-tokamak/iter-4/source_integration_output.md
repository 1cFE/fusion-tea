### F-1: ARIES-AT benchmark data available but not incorporated despite being the recommended analogue
- **Target:** Section 5 (LCOE-Relevant Parameters table and Modeling Approach) and Section 6 (Gap #1)
- **Finding:** The analysis explicitly recommends ARIES-AT CAS fractions as the primary BOP cost analogue and flags this as a blocking gap (Gap #1), but the Section 5 parameter table contains no ARIES-AT reference data — not the LCOE, not the net power, not the thermal efficiency, not the recirculating power fraction. The new source is the ARIES-AT paper itself and directly provides these figures. The gap is now addressable from this source, but the analysis does not yet reflect that. This affects analysis goal 4 (Modeling Approach): the recommended modeling strategy cannot be executed without knowing the actual ARIES-AT values it is grounded in.
- **Recommendation:** Add an ARIES-AT reference row block to the Section 5 parameter table (LCOE, net electric output, thermal efficiency, recirculating power fraction, on-axis field, plasma bootstrap fraction) marked as "analogue benchmark." Update the modeling approach paragraph to note that Gap #1 is now partially addressable from this source. Update Gap #1 status from "not-yet-sourced" to reflect the source is ingested.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: BOP analogue mismatch from thermal efficiency gap is unacknowledged
- **Target:** Section 5 (Modeling Approach, ARIES-AT analogue discussion)
- **Finding:** The analysis proposes applying ARIES-AT CAS fractions to ARC's BOP without noting that ARIES-AT's thermal cycle (59% SiC/Brayton) differs structurally from ARC's recommended cycle (46% supercritical Rankine). A 13-percentage-point efficiency gap reflects a fundamentally different power conversion architecture — SiC composite blanket at ~1,100°C coolant outlet vs. FLiBe at lower outlet temperatures. ARIES-AT's BOP cost fractions for power conversion equipment are calibrated to a different hardware configuration and cannot be transferred to ARC without scoping which CAS accounts are architecture-independent (buildings, site, electrical systems) vs. architecture-specific (turbomachinery, heat exchangers, hot-side piping). Applying ARIES-AT fractions uncritically would embed a systematic mismatch in ARC's BOP estimate. This is a structural framing issue affecting analysis goal 4 (Modeling Approach) and goal 5 (Risks and Assumptions).
- **Recommendation:** In the Section 5 modeling approach, add a paragraph scoping which ARIES-AT CAS accounts transfer cleanly to ARC (cycle-independent: buildings CAS-21, site CAS-20, electrical systems CAS-24) vs. which require independent treatment (power conversion CAS-22, which reflects ARIES-AT's SiC/Brayton hardware). Flag the efficiency gap explicitly as a calibration caveat on the analogue.
- **Priority:** important
