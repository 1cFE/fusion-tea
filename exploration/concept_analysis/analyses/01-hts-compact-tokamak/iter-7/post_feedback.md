### F-1: Model LCOE inconsistent with narrative cost-trajectory claims — no bridging scenario provided
- **Target:** Model output vs. Section 2 (Hypothesis 1 and 2) and Section 5 (Modeling Approach)
- **Finding:** The model outputs 640 $/MWh NOAK and 1,203 $/MWh FOAK. The analysis narrative cites the CATF IWG NOAK target range of 60–100 $/MWh and states (Hypothesis 2) that "the concept achieves sub-$100/MWh LCOE only if capacity factor exceeds approximately 70–80%." These claims are mutually inconsistent with the model: the sensitivity table shows availability elasticity of −0.96, so even at 90% availability the LCOE would be ≈530 $/MWh — still 5× above the cited NOAK range. Hypothesis 1 identifies REBCO cost reaching $10/kA-m as a necessary condition for competitiveness, but the model has no scenario at commercial REBCO pricing to show what LCOE would result. The CATF IWG range appears in a footnote without explanation of why the model's NOAK estimate departs from it by 6–10×. The analysis therefore neither demonstrates the concept's economic viability nor explains why the model baseline is so far from the aspirational range — which is the core TEA question.
- **Recommendation:** Add a "REBCO-at-commercial-target" scenario: re-run the model with C220103 recalculated at $10/kA-m tape cost (≈10× reduction from current), holding all other parameters constant, and report the resulting LCOE. If this scenario still does not reach 60–100 $/MWh, identify what additional changes (plant scale, availability, regulatory outcome) are required — and state them explicitly in the model output narrative. The goal is a scenario tree that connects the current 640 $/MWh baseline to the CATF range with a legible set of assumptions.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: REBCO cost absent from sensitivity table despite being identified as the primary uncertainty
- **Target:** Model output (sensitivity table) vs. Section 2 Challenge 1 and Section 5 Modeling Approach (c)
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies REBCO tape cost as "the single largest input uncertainty" (Section 2 Challenge 1), recommends spanning "at least 10× the $10/kA-m commercial target in sensitivity analysis" (Section 5), and key assumption #3 in the model output says "Sensitivity sweep: vary C220103 by ±50%." However, neither C220103 nor any REBCO price parameter appears in the sensitivity table — which instead shows 30+ plasma and geometry parameters, most with near-zero elasticity. The dominant cost driver is absent from the sensitivity analysis while non-drivers (T_e, n_e, Z_eff with elasticity ≈0.000) are included. This is a direct internal inconsistency: the analysis text and the model output disagree on what matters.
- **Recommendation:** Add C220103 (coil cost) or an equivalent REBCO $/kA-m parameter to the sensitivity sweep, spanning from current market price (~$100/kA-m, i.e., roughly the modeled baseline) down to the commercial target ($10/kA-m, ≈10× reduction). Report the resulting LCOE elasticity alongside the existing engineering levers. This single addition would make the sensitivity table consistent with the analysis narrative and provide the quantitative backbone for F-1's bridging scenario.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-3: FLiBe blanket failure has no stated cost consequence
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 3) and Section 7 (differentiator table row for liquid FLiBe blanket)
- **Finding:** Challenge 3 identifies three unresolved FLiBe failure modes (tritium extraction timescales, MHD heat transfer disruption, Inconel corrosion) and states they "could force blanket redesign." But unlike I-mode failure (Hypothesis 3: 2.5–3× $/kWe increase) and REBCO failure (Hypothesis 1: cost-competitiveness condition formalized), the FLiBe failure has no stated cost consequence — not even an order-of-magnitude range. The Section 7 differentiator table row for the FLiBe blanket lists the cost implication as "Adds chemical processing plant and introduces uncharacterized MHD/corrosion risk" but does not state what blanket redesign would cost relative to the current $260M fabricated blanket line item. Given that FLiBe is simultaneously the largest novel differentiator and the item with the most open R&D gaps (TRL 3–4), the absence of a failure-cost framing is a material gap in Goal 5.
- **Recommendation:** Add a "blanket redesign" failure scenario to Section 2 Challenge 3 or Section 7, structured as a conditional: "If FLiBe is found incompatible with ARC operating conditions (MHD, corrosion, or tritium extraction), the likely substitution is [solid ceramic breeder + separate He coolant], which would increase the blanket line item from $260M to approximately $X–Y and add/remove the following BOP accounts." Even a qualitative framing ("would eliminate the FLiBe chemistry plant cost but require a solid module handling system comparable to ITER HCPB") is sufficient — the goal is to bound the downside so it can be captured as a scenario branch in the TEA.
- **Priority:** important
