VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Capacity factor modeling framing overstates LCOE penalty
- **Target:** Section 2 (Hypothesis 2) and Section 5 (Modeling Approach, bullet b — "Capacity factor [%]")
- **Finding:** Schwartz et al. (2024, arXiv:2405.01514, PPPL/Princeton Andlinger Center) — a grid-capacity-expansion study that explicitly cites the ARC design philosophy as motivation — quantifies that naive inverse-availability LCOE scaling overstates the economic penalty for lower availability by up to 15%. At 80% availability with strategic maintenance scheduling in low-electricity-price windows, a fusion plant retains 91% of the value of a maintenance-free plant (not 80%); at 65% availability, it retains 80% (not 65%). The paper also shows that shorter, more frequent maintenance blocks frequently outperform longer, infrequent ones at the same average availability. Hypothesis 2 currently states LCOE scales "approximately inversely with capacity factor" and that a 2× swing (50% → 85%) "dominates LCOE more than any other parameter." Both formulations are overstated under grid-value-weighted accounting. The paper additionally provides two quantitative anchors absent from the analysis: VO&M = $2.07/MWh_net ($1.74/MWh_gross), and a parasitic power breakdown of 5% active (plasma heating, magnets, pumps) + 10% passive (cryogenics, vacuum, tritium handling) — with the passive load persisting during maintenance outages.
- **Recommendation:** Revise Hypothesis 2 to replace "LCOE scales approximately inversely with capacity factor" with language distinguishing simple energy-revenue scaling (approximately inverse) from grid-value-weighted availability (up to 15% less penalized than the naive estimate when maintenance is scheduled in low-price windows). Add a sentence noting that maintenance scheduling strategy — block duration, frequency, and seasonal timing — is a second-order but non-negligible parameter in the capacity factor sensitivity. In Section 5, add the Schwartz et al. VO&M anchor ($2.07/MWh_net) to the Operating Cost rows and note the 5%/10% active/passive parasitic breakdown. Update the modeling approach note for capacity factor to flag that the simple inverse relationship is an upper-bound penalty, not a central estimate.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Order-of-magnitude cost anchor now available for FLiBe chemistry/tritium processing plant (Gap #15)
- **Target:** Section 6, Gap #15 (FLiBe chemistry plant and tritium extraction system — flagged as "truly-unknown")
- **Finding:** The Woodruff Scientific (2020) ARPA-E ALPHA re-costing study (OSTI:1820946) provides a full CAS breakdown for four compact MIF/Z-pinch fusion concepts at ~500 MWe scale. The Fuel Processing line (CAS22.5) averages $124M per plant across the four concepts, with Power Supplies (CAS22.1.7) averaging $55.8M and blanket/first wall (CAS22.1.1) averaging $57M with a very wide spread ($3.6M–$116.5M). This is the closest available published CAS-structured analogue for ARC's FLiBe chemistry and tritium extraction plant. The study's cross-concept NOAK LCOE reference — $43/MWh at 90% availability, 3-year construction, zero contingency, for non-HTS compact fusion — also provides a lower-bound reference point not currently in the analysis. The existing analysis lists Gap #15 as "truly-unknown" with no analogue estimate and notes that ARIES CAS fractions cannot be applied due to missing FLiBe-specific plant scope; the ALPHA re-costing fills this gap at the order-of-magnitude level.
- **Recommendation:** Update Section 6, Gap #15 to add: "The Woodruff Scientific (2020) ARPA-E ALPHA re-costing study (OSTI:1820946) provides a rough floor for this line item: Fuel Processing (CAS22.5) averages $124M per plant at ~500 MWe for compact MIF/Z-pinch concepts. These concepts differ from ARC in blanket chemistry (no FLiBe), fuel cycle (no in-blanket tritium breeding), and confinement architecture, so the analogy is structural, not direct — treat $100–200M as an order-of-magnitude anchor, not a point estimate." Also add the ARPA-E cross-concept NOAK LCOE ($43/MWh, ~$2.4/W) to the benchmark rows in Section 5, with the caveat that it applies to non-HTS compact fusion with no superconductor cost and zero contingency.
- **Priority:** important
