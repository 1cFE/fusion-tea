# Critic Review — 01-hts-compact-tokamak

## Headline issues

1. **Section 7 labels the no-override native as "native design point" — it is the library baseline, not an ARC estimate** — model_output.txt is explicit: "Native LCOE = 199.0 $/MWh (P_native reference, n_mod=1, no overrides)." Section 7 presents this as "native design point: ≈ 199 $/MWh at 16,092 $/kW," which a reader will interpret as the ARC estimate at its design scale. It is not — it is the library's prediction for a generic 233 MWe HTS tokamak before the concept-specific overrides fire. If the C220103 override were applied at native scale (Sorbom's $6.9B magnet replacing the library's $989M), native overnight would be roughly $9.7B and LCOE roughly $510/MWh — close to the 1 GWe figure, not 2.7× below it. The 2.7× gap between $199 and $544 is therefore override-activation timing, not scale effects. Relabel as "library baseline at P_native (no overrides)" everywhere it appears, or add a native-with-overrides run so the two figures bracket the design-point estimate honestly.

2. **C220101 outlier_low (5× below median) has no cross-account reconciliation in Section 7** — §5b correctly explains that the $143.8M covers only the structural blanket/first-wall/multiplier mass from Sorbom's thin-wall liquid-immersion design, with FLiBe fill separated into CAS27 ($196.2M) and the primary cooling loop presumably in C220200 ($208.3M at 1 GWe). Adding those three: $143.8M + $196.2M + $208.3M = $548M — approaching the $644M median for C220101 alone in the solid-blanket comparables. Section 7 does not show this summation, leaving the C220101 outlier_low unexplained to a reader who looks at the sanity table without reading §5b. Additionally, §5b states "Be neutron multiplier (3.82 t → $4.1M)" — implying $1,073/kg fabricated — while §4 cites beryllium at "$257/kg" from the same Table 10. The distinction (raw material vs. machined tiles) is plausible but unstated; if the $257/kg is raw and the $4.1M embeds fabrication cost, say so explicitly. If they conflict, the override arithmetic is wrong by ~4×.

3. **Availability elasticity (−0.92) is the dominant LCOE lever, but the analysis frames it as one of several challenges** — the sensitivity output places availability first by a large margin (+0.72 for r_coil is second; +0.36 for b_max is third). An elasticity of −0.92 means 10 percentage points of availability — say 85% to 75% — raises LCOE by ~9%, erasing roughly half the gap between ARC's $544/MWh and ARIES-AT's ~$50/MWh. This is more impactful than any engineering override in the analysis. Section 2 challenge #3 rates availability "Impact: High" and Section 6 gap #5 marks it "important," but neither the challenge narrative nor the gap table synthesizes the quantitative weight. The sensitivity section of model_output.txt is available and should be cited directly: "availability elasticity −0.92 makes this the single largest engineering LCOE driver, exceeding the magnet ($r_{coil}$, $b_{max}$) by 2× in elasticity and making blanket replacement cadence the analysis's highest-priority unresolved gap."

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

**dpc ok, contract ok, count_smell ok** — no interpretation needed; the mechanical plumbing is clean. P_native at 233 MWe is coherent across the design-point table, analysis.md §Design Point block, and the `P_native = 233.0` line in model_setup.py. The three-enabled-override count sits inside the High archetype band. The helper-form call is structurally valid.

**sanity flagged — 15 outliers** — the count sounds alarming but is structurally dominated by one driver: C220103 ($6,916M) inflates CAS22, which then cascades into every percentage-markup account. CAS22 itself ($33,794M, ratio 9.46×) and CAS30 ($6,986M, ratio 5.85×), CAS50 ($1,566M, ratio 4.02×), CAS60 ($8,365M, ratio 5.00×), CAS90 ($3,892M, ratio 5.99×) are all proportional to total capital cost or direct cost. At native scale, CAS30/total ≈ 13.2%; at 1 GWe, CAS30/total ≈ 13.5% — essentially constant, confirming these are fixed-fraction markups on a high-cost direct. The 15 outliers are therefore not 15 independent problems; they are three independent signals (CAS22/C220103 magnet, CAS27 FLiBe, C220101 blanket structure) plus ~12 derivative echoes. The analysis does not state this explicitly, leaving the sanity table without interpretive guidance.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes a single named plant throughout: ARC 2015 Conservative Pilot phase (Sorbom et al.), 233 MWe. The Colliva et al. BOP work models the FNSF phase, which shares identical machine hardware with the Conservative Pilot — the distinction is blanket outlet temperature only. Using Colliva for the thermal power figure (~645 MWth, marked "medium confidence" in Section 5's table) is acceptable; it is not stitching because the machine is the same, and the confidence rating acknowledges the phase difference. The Lin/Wukitch ICRF source is used only for maturity assessment (Section 3) and does not appear in design-point parameters or overrides — correct scoping. Section 2 challenge #2 explicitly puts the 400 MWe commercial ARC out of scope. No stitching detected.

### Override discipline (judgment)

**C220103 (enabled, derived)** — The strongest and best-argued entry. The provenance is "derived" because the Sorbom figure is an aggregate subtotal (magnets + tension ring + steel base), not a line-item from a cost table. The rationale explains the structural-steel-dominated cost physics clearly. The explicit note that the subtotal bundles items a finer breakdown would put in C220105 is honest. One weakness: the override uses the "upper estimate" ($5,200M) of the $5.1–5.2B range without documenting why the upper bound is the conservative choice; this is defensible (conservative for a cost analysis) but should be one sentence in the rationale.

**C220101 (enabled, derived)** — The Be arithmetic issue in Headline #2 applies here. The Inconel 718 cost ($100M for 97.1t → ~$1,030/kg fabricated) is plausible for precision-machined fusion-grade structure, but should note the raw vs. fabricated price distinction to prevent confusion with the $56/kg raw Inconel figure a reader might look up.

**CAS27 (enabled, derived)** — The rationale correctly identifies the quantity (958t from Table 11) and the unit price ($154/kg from Table 10) as direct published figures, making the FY2014 total direct and only the CPI escalation derived. The classification is accurate. One note: the native CAS27 in model_output.txt is $3.5M (library default, no override at native scale). This confirms the override is active only at 1 GWe. Readers should not interpret the $3.5M native as the ARC design-point FLiBe cost.

**C220108 (disabled)** — Correctly disabled. The reasoning (override would depart from library in the non-conservative direction on an explicitly incomplete placeholder, while the narrative rates divertor difficulty as "between ITER and reactor designs") is sound override discipline. The disabled entry's documentation is the right artifact to leave in place.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The contract check confirms the helper-form call is valid. The per-account values at 1 GWe are structurally consistent with the override logic: C220103 ($6,916M), C220101 ($143.8M), and CAS27 ($196.2M) each equal their override values exactly. Non-override sub-accounts (CAS23–CAS26) scale linearly by ~4.3× from native, consistent with n_mod applied to those accounts. The large "unlisted" portion of CAS22 (~$25.6B beyond the listed sub-accounts) is producing most of the $33.8B CAS22 total — this is the library's physics-based magnet scaling for a 1 GWe machine and is not visible in the printout. The override replaces one sub-account ($6,916M for C220103) within this larger structure; the magnitude is not implausible given the library's non-linear magnet scaling at higher power. The only account where the 1 GWe value looks potentially undercounted is CAS27: if the override represents one ARC unit's FLiBe inventory applied as the 1 GWe anchor without n_mod multiplication, it would undercount by ~4×. At $196M vs. a corrected ~$843M, the effect on total overnight ($51.9B) is ~1.3% — small enough not to change the LCOE conclusion, but worth verifying against the helper contract.

### Family delta vs comparables

**CAS22/C220103 high outlier** — attributed to architecture in Section 7 and §2 challenge #1. The reconciliation of "compactness advantage per fusion power vs. high $/kWe" in Section 7's closing paragraph is the strongest qualitative piece in the analysis, and it is correct.

**CAS27 high outlier (13×)** — architecturally attributed in §5b and noted in Section 7 as an "ARC-specific cost signal." However, Section 7 does not quantify the delta against any comparable's blanket fill cost. Since none of the four comparables uses a FLiBe liquid-immersion blanket, the 13× ratio is expected (their CAS27 is near-zero or uses a different material). A one-line statement to this effect in Section 7 would close the loop.

**C220101 low outlier (5×) and CAS21 low outlier (2.1×)** — C220101 is discussed in §5b but not in Section 7 family delta. CAS21 ($304M flat at both native and 1 GWe, vs. $656M median) is not discussed anywhere. The flat scaling of CAS21 suggests the model treats buildings as a fixed-site cost independent of module count; if the comparables are larger machines with proportionally larger buildings, ARC's compact footprint would genuinely produce a lower CAS21. This is an unsupported inference — neither the analysis nor the model_output.txt explains it.

**C220105/C220106/C220107 low outliers** — C220105 (primary structure, $10.5M, 3.4× below median) is explained by the C220103 bundling decision (§5b). C220106 (vacuum vessel, $50.2M, 3.7× below median) is architecturally plausible given ARC's ~85t solid vessel vs. multi-thousand-tonne ITER-class structures; §5b notes the figure was considered but left to the library to avoid double-counting with C220101. C220107 (power supplies, $38.5M, 2.3× below median) is unexplained and small enough not to matter.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

**Assumption 1: availability at ~85% (library default)** — elasticity −0.92 makes this the most sensitive parameter in the model, ahead of r_coil (+0.73) and interest_rate (+0.78). A 10-point drop in availability (85% → 75%) raises LCOE by ~9%, widening the gap to ARIES-AT by another ~50 $/MWh. The blanket replacement interval and outage duration are unpublished for ARC specifically (Gap 5). This is the assumption that, if wrong, most changes the LCOE headline — and the analysis should say so explicitly rather than treating it as one of eleven listed gaps.

**Assumption 2: C220103 = $6,916M correctly represents the ARC magnet account** — the cost is structure-dominated, grounded in a published peer-reviewed table, and the largest single account in the model. If the actual fabricated structural-steel cost scales differently than Sorbom's volume-and-materials estimate (e.g., due to non-recurring tooling, modular construction savings, or a redesigned cage at commercial scale), the LCOE would shift proportionally. C220103 / total overnight ≈ 13.3%. A 50% reduction in magnet cost would reduce LCOE by ~7%. The analysis identifies this correctly as the "central modeling hazard" (§2 challenge #1) but does not cite the sensitivity's quantitative weight to reinforce the claim.

**Thermal efficiency (Gap 3) is not a load-bearing assumption** — the eta_th sweep confirms this: $199.0/MWh at 40% vs. $192.2/MWh at 50% (elasticity −0.16, range 3.5%). The "aspirational 1100 K materials" risk is real for net power output but irrelevant to LCOE because the capital cost dominates. The analysis lists Gap 3 alongside availability and component replacement as equally "important" — it is not. Correcting this priority ordering would sharpen the gap inventory.

## What I deliberately did not say

**I did not assess source quality or provenance trustworthiness for Sorbom 2015.** The observation that the $4.6B structural-steel cost is "rough scaling based on volumes and materials prices" (quoted in §1) means the published figure is an estimate, not a fabricator's quote. Whether this rough estimate is reliable for a techno-economic anchor is a research-stage judgment that belongs upstream. I note it only because the override's claim of `provenance: derived` may be understating the epistemic uncertainty — the FY2014 figure itself is a rough estimate, not a measured cost — but routing this concern to research-stage review is correct per the contract.

**I did not evaluate whether the CAS account structure correctly separates blanket structural mass (C220101) from cooling system (C220200) for a liquid-immersion design.** The $208.3M at 1 GWe in C220200 might double-count some equipment also priced in C220101, or might miss the molten-salt energy-storage buffer that Colliva et al. identify as required. This is a library-mapping question that requires reading the `costingfe` account definitions, which are not part of the artifacts under review.

**I did not challenge the design-point selection** — the 233 MWe ARC 2015 Conservative Pilot is fixed upstream. The 400 MWe mismatch is correctly scoped out in Section 2 and requires no further action here.
