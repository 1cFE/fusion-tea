# Critic Review — 29-negative-triangularity-tokamak

## Headline issues

1. **CAS22 (Tokamak costs) explodes to $27.1B at 1 GWe while native is $2.5B — 10.8× scale factor for 11.1× power scale is mechanically correct but hides that CAS22 dominates the 1 GWe LCOE at 64% of overnight cost** — The sanity check correctly flags CAS22 as `outlier_high` (ratio 4.19 vs. comparables median $6.5B). The $1.5B TF override at native scale becomes the primary cost driver when scaled to 1 GWe. This is architecturally defensible (REBCO procurement scales with device size, MANTA's 7-year TF replacement strategy carries forward), but the analysis should explicitly note that NT's LCOE competitiveness hinges entirely on REBCO cost trajectory. If REBCO stays expensive, NT loses to lower-field larger designs. The override is grounded, but the 1 GWe projection is sensitive to an assumption (REBCO $/kA-m) that MANTA itself flags as ±50% uncertain.

2. **CAS30 (capitalized replacements) at $5.7B for 1 GWe is 3.77× comparables median, driven by the 7-year TF replacement schedule, but the analysis doesn't quantify the LCOE penalty from this cyclic capital outlay** — The sanity check flags CAS30 as `outlier_high`. MANTA states TF lifetime is 3100 ± 400 MW-yr (~7 years at 450 MW fusion), and the analysis correctly notes this is extrapolated from fission neutron data, not fusion-validated. However, `analysis.md` §3 (Maturity subsection on REBCO TF lifetime, TRL 4–5) and §6 (Gap #2) both emphasize the neutron damage uncertainty but don't translate it into an LCOE sensitivity. The CAS30 outlier is expected given the replacement schedule, but the reader has no quantitative sense of "if REBCO lasts 5 years instead of 7, LCOE increases by X%." The TF replacement frequency is a first-order cost driver for NT and should have a sensitivity band in the final LCOE, not just a qualitative gap flag.

3. **Multiple sub-accounts (C220101, C220102, C220105–C220108, C220110–C220111) flag as `outlier_low` at 1 GWe scale, suggesting the library's 1 GWe projection for non-overridden accounts is systematically underscaling relative to comparables** — The sanity check shows 8 accounts at 0.29–0.61× comparables median. These are *not* overridden, so the divergence comes from the library's scaling behavior. The analysis doesn't discuss this — it's silent on whether NT's conventional aspect ratio (A = 3.79 vs. spherical or ultra-compact comparables) should produce cheaper blanket/shield/structure per unit power, or whether the library is mis-scaling. The deterministic layer caught the outliers; the judgment layer should say whether the library's answer is plausible for NT geometry or whether these low values are a red flag that the 1 GWe projection is unrealistic.

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

**dpc: ok** — The three-leg coherence check (design-point table P_native 90 MWe = `analysis.md` frontmatter P-Native 90 = `model_setup.py` P_native 90.0) passes cleanly. No disagreement between artifacts on what plant is being modeled or at what scale.

**contract: ok** — `model_setup.py` uses the helper-form two-knob pattern (`run_native_and_1gw`), emits `result_1gw`, and has no syntax errors. The module-level contract is satisfied. No structural misunderstanding of the library interface.

**count_smell: ok** — 2 enabled overrides fall within the 0–4 band expected for High archetype fit. The count is consistent with the stated archetype grade. The third override (C220104 ohmic-only heating elimination) is disabled and explicitly marked as a sensitivity case, which is appropriate handling of an unvalidated physics pathway. No over-reach or under-reach signal from the count alone.

**sanity: flagged** — 16 accounts are outliers (12 low, 4 high). The high outliers (CAS22, CAS30, CAS50, CAS60, CAS90) cluster around tokamak cost, capitalized replacements, and backend cost accounts — all tied to the $1.5B TF override and the 7-year replacement schedule. These are *expected* outliers given MANTA's cost structure and are attributed in the analysis to REBCO procurement and neutron lifetime limits (§2 challenges #2, §3 REBCO TF maturity, §4 REBCO supply chain, §7 family-delta vs. comparables). The divergence is architectural and explained.

The low outliers (C220101 first wall/blanket, C220102 shield, C220105 primary structure, C220106 vacuum system, C220107 power supplies, C220108 divertor, CAS27 special materials, CAS40 turbine, CAS21 buildings) are *not* attributed anywhere in the analysis. The divertor (C220108) has an override applying a 30% reduction, which partially explains why it's low, but that override is at native scale — the 1 GWe value ($39.3M) is still flagged as `outlier_low` (0.49× comparables median $79.7M). The analysis argues NT's divertor advantage should reduce cost (§2 challenge #5, §7 family-delta divertor row), so a low divertor cost is expected, but the sanity check is saying the 1 GWe projection is *too* low even relative to that expectation (it's outside the 2σ band). Either the library is underscaling non-tokamak accounts for this geometry, or NT's conventional aspect ratio genuinely produces cheaper BOP, but the analysis doesn't say which.

**No drift block present** — The live re-import of `model_setup.py` succeeded and presumably agrees with `model_output.txt` (the absence of a `### drift` block means no discrepancy was detected). No headline issue here.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes **one named plant consistently**: MANTA NT Fusion Pilot Plant (Rutherford et al. 2024), 90 MWe, R₀ = 4.55 m, B = 11 T, P_fus = 450 MW, 15-min quasi-steady pulses with ICRF heating. The design-point block (§5) cites every parameter to the same source (manta-reference-design.md), and the spec keys in `model_setup.py` match those values (R0=4.55, plasma_t=1.2, elon=1.8, B=11.0, p_input=40.0, plasma_volume=155.0).

**No stitching detected.** The analysis mentions Firefly Fusion's stated parameters (R = 2–2.5 m, B = 10–12 T, P_fus = 50–100 MW) as context in §1 and §5, but explicitly notes these are "for reference, not the fixed design point" and that "Firefly has not published sufficient detail to construct a complete parameter set." The LCOE parameters section (§5) describes MANTA exclusively. The model is not conflating two published designs.

One minor coherence concern: the analysis states (§2 challenge #1) that the ohmic-only scenario "could reach Q ≈ 500 with ohmic heating alone, compared to Q = 11.5 with 40 MW ICRF," but the disabled C220104 override rationale says "If enabled, also adjust p_input spec to 0 MW and expect fusion power and Q to increase substantially." This is correct (the override is disabled, so p_input remains 40 MW in the spec), but it would be clearer if the analysis explicitly noted that the baseline model uses the MANTA ICRF case (Q = 11.5), not the Ball et al. ohmic case (Q = 500). The distinction is there, but a reader skimming §2 might think both scenarios are being modeled simultaneously.

### Override discipline (judgment)

**C220104 (auxiliary heating) = $0, disabled, provenance: derived**
- **Is `provenance: derived` correct?** Yes. Ball et al. project that MANTA-class devices *could* achieve burning plasma with zero auxiliary heating, but this is a theoretical extrapolation (0D power balance, confinement enhancement H_NA ≈ 2 from limited experimental data), not a published cost figure from MANTA or Firefly. The override is derived from the physics analysis, not a direct cost statement.
- **Does the rationale accord with the source?** Yes. ball-balestri-ohmic-nt-paper.md §Conclusions states "Ohmically heating a negative triangularity power plant is an attractive option meriting further investigation" and projects Q ≈ 500 for high-field NT designs. The rationale correctly notes this is "not yet validated at reactor scale" and is "disabled by default" as a sensitivity case.
- **Is the override doing real work?** Not in the baseline (it's disabled). If enabled, it would eliminate $282.6M (native scale) or $282.6M (1 GWe scale, oddly flat — the library may treat auxiliary heating as a fixed-size system regardless of plant power?) from C220104. The note "Currently blocked by library issue 1cFE/1costingfe#25" explains why it's disabled — the library doesn't yet support zero-heating physics modes. This is appropriate override discipline: the physics case exists, the cost implication is clear, but it's not production-ready, so it's left as a documented sensitivity path.

**C220103 (magnets) = $1500.0M, enabled, provenance: direct**
- **Is `provenance: direct` correct?** Yes. manta-reference-design.md §7 explicitly states "TF coil cost at US$1.5 billion" as a bottom-up NOAK estimate. This is a direct published figure, not arithmetic from unit costs.
- **Does the value accord with the source?** Yes. The rationale cites the exact statement from §7 and notes the REBCO specifications (24 coils, 11 T, 20 K, 1000 A/mm² SuperOx tape).
- **Is the override doing real work?** Absolutely. The generic magnet cost at native scale is $808.3M; the override raises it to $1500.0M, a 1.86× multiplier. At 1 GWe scale, the override holds at $1500.0M (flat, not scaled — this is unusual and suggests the override is being interpreted as an absolute dollar figure rather than a relative adjustment). The CAS22 explosion at 1 GWe is driven by *other* accounts scaling up while the magnet override stays fixed. This seems mechanically wrong — if the 1 GWe design has R₀ scaled up by ~2× (to maintain similar B and power density), the TF coils should also scale in cost, not stay at $1.5B. However, the helper (`run_native_and_1gw`) may be applying the override as written (a fixed dollar value) rather than re-deriving it at 1 GWe scale. This is a subtlety of the override system, not the analysis, but it means the $1.5B TF cost is only grounded at native 90 MWe scale, and the 1 GWe projection may be underestimating magnet cost.

**C220108 (divertor) = $30.2M, enabled, provenance: derived**
- **Is `provenance: derived` correct?** Yes. The rationale applies a 30% cost reduction relative to the library's default divertor cost, justified by MANTA's quantitative divertor metrics (M₁, M₂) being 4–10× better than PT comparables. This is arithmetic from a physics advantage, not a direct cost figure.
- **Does the value accord with the source?** The source (manta-reference-design.md §3, Table 2) provides the M₁ and M₂ metrics, which the analysis correctly cites in §2 challenge #5 and §7 family-delta divertor row. The 30% reduction is a judgment call (the rationale says "Could be refined with more detailed divertor engineering cost breakdown. Conservative relative to the physics advantage"). The native-scale instantiation ($30.2M = 0.70 × $43.2M generic) is correct arithmetic.
- **Is the override doing real work?** At native scale, yes — $43.2M → $30.2M, a $13M (30%) reduction. At 1 GWe scale, the override produces $39.3M, which is flagged as `outlier_low` (0.49× comparables median $79.7M). The 1 GWe value is *not* 0.70 × generic (which would be 0.70 × [some larger number]), it's close to the native override value, suggesting the divertor is being treated as a fixed-size component rather than scaling with plant power. This is plausible for a divertor (it's sized by plasma surface area and heat flux, not net electric power), but it means the 1 GWe plant is getting an even larger relative divertor cost benefit (49% of comparables) than the 30% reduction the override intended. The analysis should note this — the low divertor cost at 1 GWe is a compound effect of (a) the 30% physics-based reduction and (b) the divertor not scaling strongly with plant power.

**Overall override discipline:** Two enabled overrides are well-grounded in MANTA's published data. The disabled ohmic-only override is appropriately flagged as speculative. The override count (2) is appropriate for High archetype fit. The rationales are detailed and cite sources correctly. The one judgment concern is whether the 1 GWe scaling behavior (magnet override flat, divertor override nearly flat) is what the analyst intended, or an artifact of the helper's interpretation of absolute-dollar-value overrides.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The contract validator confirms `result_1gw` exists and is structurally legal. The per-account 1 GWe values in `model_output.txt` show expected patterns: CAS22 (tokamak) scales strongly (1.86× native → 27.1B at 1 GWe), BOP accounts (CAS23 turbine, CAS24 electrical, CAS26 heat rejection) scale ~11× (matching the 11.1× power scale), indirect costs (CAS50 owner's, CAS60 total capital, CAS90 total overnight) scale proportionally.

**Implausible values for architectural reasons?**

1. **C220103 (magnets) = $1500.0M at both native and 1 GWe** — This is implausible if the 1 GWe design is geometrically larger (R₀ scaled up to maintain similar field and power density). A 1 GWe NT tokamak at B = 11 T would have R₀ ≈ 9–10 m (roughly 2× MANTA's 4.55 m), requiring 2× the REBCO tape length and ~4× the structural mass (scales with R²). The magnet cost should scale to $3–6B, not stay flat at $1.5B. The helper may be applying the override as a fixed value rather than re-computing the magnet cost for the 1 GWe geometry. This is a library/helper behavior issue, not an analysis error, but it means the 1 GWe magnet cost is likely underestimated.

2. **C220108 (divertor) = $39.3M at 1 GWe (vs. $30.2M at native, $43.2M generic native)** — The divertor cost barely increases from native to 1 GWe, which is plausible *if* divertor cost scales with plasma surface area (which scales as R₀^2 × kappa, growing slower than volume or power) rather than net electric power. However, the comparables median is $79.7M, so NT's divertor is 49% of comparables at 1 GWe — a larger relative advantage than the 30% reduction the override intended. This could be architectural (NT's divertor geometry fundamentally requires less material and remote handling equipment per unit power) or a scaling artifact. The analysis should say which.

3. **CAS30 (capitalized replacements) = $5661.3M at 1 GWe** — This is 3.77× comparables median. If comparables assume LTS magnets with >30-year lifetime (no replacement) or HTS magnets with longer lifetime (e.g., ARC may project >10 years if neutron shielding is thicker), then NT's 7-year TF replacement cycle produces a structural cost penalty. The analysis explains this (§3 REBCO TF maturity, §4 REBCO supply chain, §7 family-delta TF row), so the outlier is expected. However, the analysis doesn't quantify: "What fraction of LCOE is TF replacement?" or "If TF lifetime increases from 7 to 10 years, LCOE drops by X%." The CAS30 outlier is the clearest signal that NT's LCOE is dominated by a single lifecycle cost driver, but the analysis stops short of quantifying the sensitivity.

4. **Multiple BOP accounts flagged low (C220101, C220102, C220105–C220107)** — These are not overridden, so the low values come from the library's scaling. The analysis doesn't discuss whether NT's conventional aspect ratio (A = 3.79, vs. spherical comparables at A < 2 or ultra-compact comparables at A ≈ 3) should produce cheaper blanket/shield/structure. Conventional tokamaks have more generous geometry (easier remote handling access, lower neutron flux on inner components), which could justify lower costs, but the analysis is silent on this. The sanity check flagged it; the judgment layer should interpret it.

### Family delta vs comparables

The analysis provides a detailed family-delta comparison in §7 (vs. each comparable individually, then a summary table). The divergences are mostly architectural and explained:

- **Divertor cost advantage (20–30% reduction)** — Attributed to NT's larger divertor area and lower peak heat flux (M₁ metric 57.3 vs. ARC 263). The sanity check flags C220108 as `outlier_low` at 1 GWe, which is consistent with this explanation. The analysis could strengthen this by noting that the 1 GWe divertor cost is 49% of comparables (not just 30% lower than generic), reinforcing that the NT advantage compounds at larger scale.

- **TF replacement penalty (7-year cycle vs. potential lifetime LTS)** — Attributed to REBCO neutron damage limits. The sanity check flags CAS30 as `outlier_high` (3.77× comparables). The analysis explains the cause (§3 REBCO TF maturity, §4 REBCO supply chain) but doesn't quantify the LCOE impact. The family-delta table (§7 summary) says "Ongoing replacement cost; neutral vs. HTS comparables (ARC, ST) with same issue," but the sanity check disagrees — NT's CAS30 is 3.77× comparables median, suggesting the comparables *don't* have the same issue (or project longer lifetimes). Either the comparables are underestimating TF replacement cost, or NT's 7-year interval is conservative. The analysis should say which.

- **Ohmic-only heating (potential $200–400M savings)** — Described as "Advantage (if physics works)" with magnitude estimate. This is in the family-delta table but not quantified in the baseline LCOE (the C220104 override is disabled). The analysis should note that the baseline LCOE *does not* include this advantage — it's a sensitivity case, not the central estimate.

- **Pulsed operation (88.2% duty cycle)** — The analysis notes this in §2 challenge #3 and §7 family-delta row "Pulsed operation," but the capacity factor is not visible in `model_output.txt` (the library likely embeds it in the LCOE calculation). The family-delta table says "Mixed" (avoids steady-state CD hardware but accepts lower duty cycle), but there's no quantitative comparison: "NT's 88.2% duty cycle costs X% in LCOE vs. a steady-state comparable's 100%."

**Unjustified divergences?** The low BOP accounts (C220101, C220102, C220105–C220107, CAS27, CAS40) are not attributed to NT architecture in the family-delta section. The analysis doesn't say "NT's conventional aspect ratio enables cheaper blanket/structure because [reason]" or "The library is underscaling these accounts and the low values are suspect." This is the largest gap in the family-delta reasoning — half the outlier accounts have no architectural explanation.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

The analysis provides a detailed gap inventory (§6, 13 gaps) with criticality ratings. The two most load-bearing assumptions for the LCOE conclusion are:

1. **REBCO lifetime = 7 years (Gap #2, "truly-unknown," "important")** — The analysis states "3×10²² n/m² fluence limit is extrapolated from fission reactor NbTi/Nb₃Sn data, not fusion-spectrum neutron irradiation of REBCO" (§3 REBCO TF maturity). If REBCO degrades faster under 14 MeV neutrons, the 7-year replacement interval shortens, increasing CAS30 and LCOE. The analysis flags this as a gap but doesn't quantify: "If TF lifetime drops to 5 years, CAS30 increases by X%, LCOE increases by Y%." Given that CAS30 is already 3.77× comparables and is the second-largest cost driver after CAS22, this sensitivity should be quantified.

2. **Ohmic-only confinement (Gap #1, "truly-unknown," "blocking")** — The analysis notes "H_NA ≈ 2 for NT burning plasma from extrapolation of limited experimental data (DIII-D, TCV). No reactor-scale validation exists" (§6 gap #1). If the ohmic-only pathway proves viable, C220104 drops to $0 (saving $282.6M at native scale, unclear at 1 GWe due to flat scaling) and Q increases from 11.5 to ~500 (reducing recirculating power fraction, increasing net electric output). The analysis flags this as "blocking" criticality, which seems too strong — the baseline model doesn't depend on ohmic-only (it includes ICRF), so this gap blocks the *optimistic sensitivity case*, not the baseline. The criticality should be "important" (affects cost by >10% if true) rather than "blocking" (baseline model cannot be built without it).

The two assumptions that would flip the LCOE conclusion most are (1) REBCO cost trajectory (MANTA applies ±50% sensitivity) and (2) TF replacement interval (currently 7 years, flagged as uncertain). Both are noted in the analysis but not quantified in LCOE sensitivity bands.

## What I deliberately did not say

- **Firefly Fusion's actual design may differ substantially from MANTA** — The analysis correctly uses MANTA as the fixed design point (it's the only published NT reactor design at pilot-plant scale), but Firefly's stated parameters (R = 2–2.5 m, P_fus = 50–100 MW) are 2–5× smaller than MANTA. If Firefly's commercial design is fundamentally different (e.g., uses ECRH instead of ICRF, or targets higher field at smaller radius), the MANTA-based model may not represent NT economics correctly. However, this is a design-point-selection concern (upstream from `model_critic`), not an artifact-coherence issue. The analysis is transparent about the proxy substitution (§1 "MANTA serves as a design-point proxy," §5 "Firefly has not published sufficient detail"), so the reader knows the model is MANTA, not Firefly. Routing to `research`-stage review: Is MANTA the right NT proxy, or should the dossier wait for Firefly to publish?

- **The 1 GWe divertor cost ($39.3M) being flat relative to native scale ($30.2M) seems too good to be true** — A 1 GWe NT tokamak with R₀ ≈ 9–10 m (2× MANTA) should have ~4× the plasma surface area and divertor target area, requiring more tungsten monoblocks, more remote handling equipment, and more cassette replacements. A divertor cost that barely increases from 90 MWe to 1 GWe (30% increase for 11× power) is *extremely* favorable scaling. The analysis argues NT's divertor advantage is architectural (lower heat flux per unit area), but it doesn't say "NT divertors scale sub-linearly with power because [physics reason]." This could be correct — if NT spreads heat so effectively that a 1 GWe NT divertor has similar heat flux density to a 90 MWe NT divertor, the $/m² cost might be flat and the total cost scales only with area (which grows slower than power). But this reasoning is absent. I suspect the library is treating divertor cost as weakly dependent on net electric power (scales with geometry, not power), which is defensible but should be stated.

- **The magnet override ($1.5B) being flat at 1 GWe likely underestimates the true 1 GWe magnet cost** — MANTA's $1.5B is for 24 TF coils at R₀ = 4.55 m, B = 11 T. A geometrically scaled 1 GWe design (R₀ ≈ 9 m, same B) would have TF coils with 2× the bend radius, requiring ~2× the REBCO tape length per coil and 2–4× the structural mass (scales with R² or R³ depending on stress geometry). The magnet cost should scale to $3–6B, not stay at $1.5B. However, the override is written as an absolute dollar value ($1500.0M), and the helper may be interpreting it as "always use this value regardless of scale" rather than "re-compute the magnet cost for the 1 GWe geometry using MANTA's REBCO unit cost and fabrication multiplier." This is a tooling issue (how do we write scale-invariant overrides?), not an analysis error. The 1 GWe LCOE may be optimistically low because of this underestimation, but I can't prove it without seeing the helper's scaling logic.

- **MANTA's $3.4B overnight cost is not broken down by CAS account in the published paper** — The analysis notes (§6 gap #10) that MANTA provides a total overnight cost but not an LCOE or detailed CAS breakdown. This means the $1.5B TF override is the *only* account-level cost figure grounded in MANTA; all other accounts are library defaults. The analysis could strengthen its transparency by explicitly noting: "Of the three overrides, only one (C220103 magnets) is a direct MANTA cost figure. The other two (C220104 ohmic-only, C220108 divertor) are derived from physics analysis or judgment calls. The remaining ~$2.9B of native overnight cost comes from the library's generic tokamak cost model." This would clarify that the model is 44% MANTA-grounded (magnet cost) and 56% library-projected.
