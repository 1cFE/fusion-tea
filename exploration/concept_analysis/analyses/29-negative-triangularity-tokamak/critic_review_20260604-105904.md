# Critic Review — 29-negative-triangularity-tokamak

## Headline issues

1. **CAS22 (Fusion Island) 6.2× outlier-high is a methodological artifact, not a real cost divergence** — The deterministic sanity check flagged CAS22 at $28.8B (ratio=6.20 vs. median $4.6B), but this is the 1 GWe NOAK projection column being compared against comparables' *native-scale* values. The native-scale CAS22 is $2.6B, ratio=0.57 vs. median, which is actually *below* comparables. All the outlier-high flags (CAS22, CAS30, CAS50, CAS60, CAS70, CAS90) are scale-projection artifacts. The real accountable comparison is native-to-native: MANTA at 90 MWe costs $4.5B overnight ($50k/kW), vs. comparables (ARC 525 MW, TE ST40 100 MW, Energy Singularity HH70 unknown scale, Neo Fusion BEST 1 GW). The deterministic check should compare apples-to-apples (native-to-native or projected-to-projected), not mixed columns.

2. **C220108 (divertor) override is transcription-correct but the cost delta vs. library doesn't support the "explicitly stated" rationale** — Override sets C220108=$150M for the double-null tungsten monoblock divertor. Generic library value is $43.2M at native scale. The rationale claims "capital cost is explicitly stated" as justification for overriding, but doesn't explain *why MANTA's divertor costs 3.5× the library default* despite having lower heat load (P_SOL=23.5 MW vs. ~80 MW). If NT geometry reduces thermal stress, why is the capital cost higher? The analysis.md §2 claims NT divertor operates "in a far less challenging environment," which should reduce cost, not increase it. Either the library default is systematically low for all tokamaks (in which case this isn't an NT-specific override), or MANTA's $150M includes something beyond monoblock tiles (remote handling? cassette structure?). The override is numerically correct per the source, but the *accountability chain* (why this concept departs from library) is incomplete.

3. **CAS27 (special materials) override at $28.6M for FLiBe is arithmetically correct but labeled "material-only" while the rationale conflates material vs. fabrication cost allocation** — The override computes 169 t × $169/kg = $28.6M and states "this is material-only cost; fabrication/structure in C220101." But C220101 (blanket and first wall) shows generic=$144.7M, native=$144.7M (no override), which means the library's *fabrication cost* for a FLiBe liquid immersion blanket is being trusted at face value. The analysis.md §3 rates the FLiBe blanket at TRL 2-3 ("on paper only") and notes the toroidally continuous tank architecture is "unconventional for tokamaks." If the blanket architecture is novel (TRL 2-3), why is the library's fabrication cost (which presumably assumes modular solid breeder TBMs) appropriate? Either (a) C220101 should also be overridden to capture FLiBe tank fabrication cost, or (b) the $28.6M override is incomplete because it's only swapping material unit cost while leaving fabrication assumptions unchanged. The override is arithmetically sound but *conceptually incomplete* for an unconventional blanket design.

4. **Outlier-low flags (C220106_pump, C220107, CAS40) are not addressed in analysis.md family-delta section** — Deterministic sanity flagged C220106_pump (vacuum pumping) at ratio=0.31, C220107 (power supplies) at ratio=0.42, CAS40 (turbine plant) at ratio=0.43 vs. comparables. The family-delta section (analysis.md §7) discusses divertor heat load, ohmic heating, demountable TF coils, and aspect ratio, but does *not* explain why MANTA's vacuum pumping, power supplies, or turbine plant would cost less than comparables. For C220107 specifically, the analysis.md §5b (accounts considered but not overridden) mentions "TF power supplies at $0.5M each (18 coils = $9M) and resistive leads at $2M each ($36M total) reported" — this is $45M, but the native C220107 is $26.7M. Either the power supply cost is *understated* in the model (library default is too low and should be overridden upward to $45M), or the MANTA Table C1 figure is an incremental cost and the library's $26.7M already includes baseline power supplies. The outlier-low flags suggest potential *missing overrides* rather than real cost advantages.

5. **Ohmic-only heating fork (±$370M) is documented as high-uncertainty in analysis.md but not propagated to the model as a scenario** — Analysis.md §2 and §7 identify ohmic-only operation as a "binary fork" with "large potential advantage" ($370M capex + reduced opex if heating eliminated), but model_setup.py retains p_input=40 MW and does not override C220104 (heating cost). The comment at model_setup.py:26-28 acknowledges "Ball et al. claim ohmic-only (0 MW) viable at Q=500, but MANTA reference retains 40 MW ICRF." This is a *load-bearing fork* (could change LCOE by ~11% per analysis.md §7), but the model presents only one branch (auxiliary-heated case). The deterministic contract check can't catch this because the model is structurally valid — it's a *scenario-coverage gap*. Either (a) run two models (ohmic-only vs. auxiliary-heated), or (b) explicitly state in analysis.md that the cost estimate is for the *conservative* (auxiliary-heated) case and ohmic-only would reduce LCOE by ~$XX/MWh.

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

**`### sanity`: 12 outlier accounts** — The outlier-high accounts (C220000, CAS22, CAS27, CAS30, CAS40, CAS50, CAS60, CAS70, CAS90) all appear in the "1 GWe" column at ratios >3×. However, the deterministic check is comparing the *1 GWe NOAK projection* for MANTA against the *native-scale* median of comparables. This is methodologically broken. MANTA native CAS22 is $2.6B; 1 GWe projection is $28.8B. The 6.2× ratio is scale-driven, not a real cost divergence. The two outlier-low accounts (C220106_pump at 0.31×, C220107 at 0.42×) and the single outlier-high sub-account at native scale (C220108 at 2.42× even at native) are the *real* flags that need explanation. CAS27 at 42.37× is also 1 GWe-projection artifact ($317.8M projected from $28.6M native vs. $7.50 median — but the median is probably zero for most comparables because they don't publish FLiBe costs).

**`### dpc`, `### contract`, `### count_smell`**: All status=ok. P_native coherence is clean at 90 MWe across table/analysis/model_setup. Contract is satisfied (result_1gw via helper form). Override count=4 is within the High archetype-fit band (0-4). No interpretation needed beyond confirming these are not blocking.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The design point is **one named plant**: MANTA NT Fusion Pilot Plant (Rutherford et al. 2024). Every parameter in analysis.md §5 traces to manta-reference-design.md Table 1 or §2. There is **no stitching** across published designs. The analysis.md §1 acknowledges Firefly Fusion as the commercial entity but explicitly uses MANTA as the design-point proxy due to absence of Firefly technical publications. The analysis.md §5 "Notes on Design Point Selection" states: "MANTA represents the closest published NT tokamak reference design at pilot-plant scale and is used as the design-point proxy."

However, there is a **tension between analysis scope and model scope**: analysis.md discusses Firefly's commercial plant (R=2-2.5 m, 50-100 MW P_fus) alongside MANTA (R=4.55 m, 450 MW P_fus, 90 MWe), but the model_setup.py only implements MANTA. The analysis correctly identifies this as "academic study, not commercial plant design," but the archetype-fit grade is "High" and the comparables include commercial concepts (CFS, Tokamak Energy). If MANTA is an academic paper-concept and Firefly is the commercial entity, should the archetype-fit be "Medium" (commercial concept with no published design) rather than "High" (published reactor design)?

### Override discipline (judgment)

**C220103 (TF coils) = $1,500M** — Provenance is "direct" and source is Table C1. Rationale cites REBCO tape at $40/kA·m, Inconel 718 structure, 5× fabrication factor, and notes demountable joint costs. Spot-check: MANTA Table C1 line item "Superconducting Magnets: $1,500M" matches override exactly. The override is doing real work: generic=$808.3M, native=$1,500M (1.85× library default). The rationale explains *why* (demountable joints, window-pane geometry), not just *what*. **Override discipline: good.**

**C220108 (divertor) = $150M** — Provenance "direct," source Table C1. Spot-check: MANTA Table C1 "Divertor: $150M" matches. However, the rationale says "capital cost is explicitly stated" but doesn't explain why MANTA's divertor costs 3.5× the library default ($43.2M) despite lower P_SOL. The analysis.md §2 lists divertor as "Low-Medium Impact, Low-Medium Uncertainty for NT specifically" and §3 rates it TRL 6-7 (mature technology). If the divertor is *less challenging* for NT (P_SOL=23.5 MW vs. 80 MW for PT), why is it more expensive? Possible explanations: (1) MANTA's $150M includes remote handling infrastructure not in library's monoblock cost, (2) library default is systematically low for all tokamaks, (3) NT-specific double-null geometry increases fabrication cost despite lower heat load. The override is numerically correct but **rationale is incomplete** — it states "capital cost is explicitly stated" (which is always true for a direct override) but doesn't explain the *departure from library*.

**CAS27 (special materials) = $28.6M** — Provenance "direct," arithmetic is 169 t × $169/kg = $28.561M. Source is Table C4 (FLiBe inventory) and Table 6 (material costs). The rationale states "this is material-only cost; fabrication/structure in C220101," but C220101 is *not overridden* (generic=$144.7M = native=$144.7M). If the FLiBe liquid immersion blanket is TRL 2-3 ("on paper only," §3) and "unconventional for tokamaks" (§5), why is the library's blanket fabrication cost (C220101) appropriate? The library presumably assumes modular solid breeder TBMs (ITER baseline). A toroidally continuous FLiBe tank with external plumbing and V-4Cr-4Ti vacuum vessel integration is a different fabrication problem. Either (a) the override is incomplete (should also override C220101 for tank fabrication), or (b) the $28.6M is the total *incremental* cost of choosing FLiBe over solid breeder (in which case the rationale should say "material cost delta" not "material-only cost"). **Override discipline: arithmetically correct, conceptually ambiguous.**

**CAS70 (O&M) = 0.70 × generic.costs.cas70** — Provenance "derived," source Table C5. The rationale cites "~1 person/MWe staffing at $150k/employee-year = ~$15M/yr for 90 MWe plant" as "~30% lower than library default." Spot-check: generic CAS70=$48.7M/yr, native CAS70=$63.5M/yr (wait — this is *higher* than generic, not lower). The 0.70× multiplier should *reduce* CAS70, but the native value increased. Re-reading model_output.txt: generic=$48.7M at 90 MWe, native=$63.5M at 90 MWe. The 0.70× override is being applied, but the *absolute* value went up. This suggests the library's CAS70 scales with something other than P_electric (perhaps plasma volume? fusion power?), and MANTA's 0.70× staffing intensity still results in higher absolute cost because the *denominator* (plant size, fusion power, something) is larger than the library's default tokamak at 90 MWe. The override rationale is **directionally confused** — it claims MANTA has lower staffing intensity (0.70×) but doesn't explain why the native cost is 1.3× generic. **Override discipline: directionally unclear, needs re-examination.**

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The 1 GWe NOAK projection produces LCOE=$527/MWh, overnight=$45k/kW. Native LCOE=$599/MWh, overnight=$50k/kW. The projection *reduces* LCOE and overnight $/kW as the plant scales up, which is the expected direction (economies of scale, learning curve).

**Architectural plausibility check**: MANTA is a 90 MWe pilot plant. Scaling to 1 GWe (~11× electric power) requires either (a) 11 modules of the same size, or (b) a larger single unit (higher R0, higher B, higher P_fus). The 1 GWe projection assumes *replication* (n_mod scaling), not a larger single unit. For a tokamak, replication makes sense (modular plants are the commercial deployment model per CFS/Tokamak Energy). The per-account 1 GWe values show CAS22 (fusion island) scaling to $28.8B for 1 GWe, which is ~$29k/kW — this is *lower* than native $/kW ($2.6B / 90 MW = $29k/kW for fusion island alone), suggesting the library is applying learning-curve cost reductions as the fleet scales. **No architectural implausibility detected.**

However, the *absolute* LCOE of $527/MWh at 1 GWe NOAK is **2.6× the MANTA study's published LCOE of $396/MWh for a 550 MW scaled plant** (manta-reference-design.md §7.2). The discrepancy could be due to: (1) different scaling assumptions (MANTA's $396/MWh is for a *larger single unit* at 550 MW, not replicated 90 MWe modules), (2) different cost account mappings, (3) different amortization period or discount rate. The analysis.md should acknowledge this 1.3× divergence between the model's 1 GWe projection and MANTA's published 550 MW LCOE.

### Family delta vs comparables

The analysis.md §7 articulates six deltas: divertor P_SOL reduction (advantage), ohmic-only heating (large potential advantage if validated), NT confinement scaling (high uncertainty), ELM elimination (small advantage), demountable TF coils (small net penalty+advantage), conventional aspect ratio vs. spherical (medium advantage in TBR, penalty in size). The deterministic sanity check flagged C220108 (divertor) as 2.42× outlier-high at native scale, but the family-delta section explains this is an *override* (MANTA publishes $150M, library default is $43M). The delta rationale (§7: "Capital cost is explicitly stated") is weak — it doesn't explain *why* NT divertor costs more despite lower heat load.

The **two outlier-low accounts** (C220106_pump at 0.31×, C220107 at 0.42×) are **not explained** in the family-delta section. C220107 (power supplies) is mentioned in analysis.md §5b as "TF power supplies at $0.5M each + resistive leads at $2M each = $45M total reported," but this is *higher* than the model's native C220107=$26.7M. If MANTA reports $45M and the model shows $26.7M, either (a) the model is understating power supply cost and C220107 should be overridden *upward* to $45M, or (b) the $45M is an incremental cost and the library's $26.7M already includes baseline supplies. The analysis does not resolve this. **Family-delta coverage is incomplete for outlier-low accounts.**

CAS27 (special materials) at 42.37× is a projection artifact (1 GWe=$317.8M from native=$28.6M; comparables median=$7.50 likely reflects solid breeder costs). At native scale, CAS27=$28.6M vs. generic=$1.3M (22× library default) is explained by the FLiBe override. The family-delta section does not discuss CAS27 specifically, but analysis.md §4 covers FLiBe supply chain (beryllium constraint, 300 t/year global production vs. 169 t for one plant). **Delta is architecturally justified** (liquid vs. solid breeder).

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

Analysis.md §6 lists 11 gaps, ranked by criticality. The top three blocking gaps are: (1) NT confinement scaling validation at reactor parameters, (2) ohmic-only operation feasibility, (3) FLiBe liquid immersion blanket TBR and tritium extraction at kg/day rates. The analysis.md §2 ranks challenges with "confinement scaling uncertainty" as #2 (high impact, high uncertainty) and notes:

> "If NT confinement does not scale as predicted, the design point may require larger size (higher R0), higher magnetic field (higher magnet cost), or external heating systems (negating the cost advantage)."

The **two load-bearing assumptions that would flip the LCOE conclusion**:

1. **H_98y2 = 1.44 confinement factor** — If NT confinement at reactor scale is *worse* than extrapolated from DIII-D (e.g., H_98y2 drops to 1.0), MANTA would need either 1.44× larger R0 (capital cost scales ~R0^3), 1.44× higher B (magnet cost scales >linearly with B), or 1.44× higher auxiliary heating (higher p_input, lower Q, higher LCOE). **Magnitude: ±50% in overnight cost, ±30% in LCOE** (rough estimate based on tokamak cost scaling laws).

2. **Ohmic-only operation eliminates $370M heating capex** — If Ball et al.'s Q=500 ohmic-only claim validates, heating cost drops from $370M (model currently assumes p_input=40 MW, library will cost this) to ~$0. This is 11% of overnight cost ($370M / $3.4B). The model currently represents the *conservative* case (auxiliary heating retained). **Magnitude: -$50-70/MWh in LCOE if ohmic-only validates** (rough estimate from 11% capex reduction + reduced recirculating power).

The analysis correctly identifies these as the highest-uncertainty forks, but the model only implements one branch (auxiliary-heated). A sensitivity case with p_input=0 (ohmic-only) would bound the range.

## What I deliberately did not say

**Half-formed concerns flagged but not fully backed by artifacts:**

1. **CAS70 override rationale claims 30% lower staffing but native CAS70 is 1.3× generic** — I flagged this as "directionally confused" above, but I cannot definitively say the override is *wrong* without seeing the library's internal CAS70 scaling formula. It's possible the library scales O&M with plasma volume or fusion power (not just P_electric), and MANTA's large plasma volume (155 m³) drives absolute cost up even with lower per-MWe intensity. But the rationale should explain this explicitly if that's the case.

2. **MANTA $3.4B overnight cost vs. model's $4.5B native overnight** — The model's native overnight is $4.5B ($50k/kW × 90 MW), vs. MANTA's published $3.4B. This is a 1.3× divergence. Possible explanations: (1) MANTA's $3.4B is NOAK with aggressive learning-curve assumptions, (2) CAS account mapping differences (MANTA may exclude some accounts the library includes), (3) the four enabled overrides don't fully capture MANTA's cost structure. I did not make this a headline because the design-point table says the MANTA study is "NOAK assumptions" (per analysis.md §5), so a 1.3× divergence between academic-study NOAK and library-default NOAK could be within model uncertainty. But it's worth noting.

3. **Source-quality concern (out of scope for model_critic)** — The FLiBe blanket is TRL 2-3 ("on paper only"), yet the cost model uses a direct override from the MANTA study's Table C4 ($169/kg × 169 t = $28.6M). The MANTA study itself sources FLiBe cost from Araiinejad & Shirvan (2025), which is a *modeling paper*, not a supplier quote. The $169/kg figure is an *academic estimate* of NOAK FLiBe cost with 20% learning rate, not a demonstrated production cost. Should a TRL 2-3 technology with no industrial supply chain (beryllium global production is 300 t/year, one MANTA plant needs 169 t) be costed at NOAK learning-curve assumptions? This is a **source-quality / grounding-confidence concern**, not a model-contract issue, so it belongs in upstream (research-stage) review, not here. But I'm flagging it as a "thing I thought about but deliberately did not include in headline issues because it's out of scope."

4. **Design-point selection concern (out of scope for model_critic)** — The design point is "MANTA NT Fusion Pilot Plant (Rutherford et al. 2024)," but the concept is "Negative-Triangularity Tokamak (Firefly Fusion)." Firefly has disclosed R=2-2.5 m, 50-100 MW P_fus; MANTA is R=4.55 m, 450 MW P_fus. These are *different plants*. The analysis.md correctly identifies this ("MANTA is used as design-point proxy due to absence of Firefly technical publications"), but the upstream design-point selection is **weak** — we're costing an academic paper-concept and calling it a commercial company's concept. The archetype-fit grade is "High," suggesting the TOKAMAK archetype is a good match, but the *concept-to-design-point* mapping is strained. This is an **upstream design-point table concern**, not a model-artifact concern, so I'm noting it here as "deliberately out of scope" but worth escalating to design-point review.
