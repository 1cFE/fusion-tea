# Critic Review — 15-sheared-flow-stabilized-z-pinch

## Headline issues

1. **The library's q_eng = 3.0 is doing most of the economic work, and it has no published basis.** The analysis explicitly notes that published Q > 10 is Q_sci (P_fus/P_input), not q_eng (P_et/P_recirc). The model_setup.py comments confirm this distinction (line 33) and deliberately accepts the YAML default of 3.0 rather than passing a value. But the analysis never discusses *why* q_eng = 3.0 is appropriate for this concept, nor does Section 5 or 5b flag it as a load-bearing assumption. At q_eng = 3.0, a full third of gross electric is recirculated — a fraction that dominates the native LCOE of 156.6 $/MWh. This is the single most consequential number flowing through the model, it is entirely library-supplied, and the analysis treats it as invisible. Section 6 (Data Gap Inventory) lists the missing power balance as Gap #1 (blocking), but never connects that gap to the fact that the model is currently governed by an ungrounded q_eng default.

2. **CAS22 at 1 GWe ($1,389M) is 41% of overnight cost, driven entirely by n_mod = 20 replication of unvalidated library defaults for pulsed-power capital.** At native scale (50 MWe), CAS22 is $93.4M — modest. But the 1 GWe projection uses n_mod = ceil(1000/50) = 20 modules, and several sub-accounts scale linearly with module count. C220200 (driver/heat systems) jumps from $16.4M to $208.2M; C220500 (power conditioning) from $14.7M to $120.0M; C220700 (instrumentation/control) from $12.7M to $88.9M. These are all library defaults — the analysis identified C220104 and C220107 as the canonical pulsed-power accounts but found no published data to override them. The resulting $1,389M CAS22 at 1 GWe is the load-bearing number behind the headline 41.3 $/MWh LCOE projection, and it rests entirely on the library's generic $/MJ and $/J cost coefficients for a concept whose dominant capital subsystem (pulsed power) has no published cost data at any scale.

3. **The 1 GWe projection LCOE of 41.3 $/MWh is aspirational, and the analysis doesn't flag the scale of the extrapolation.** The model goes from 50 MWe single-module to 1 GWe 20-module plant, a 20× replication. The analysis discusses multi-module architecture in passing (Section 5, line 171) but never interrogates what 20-module shared-infrastructure assumptions the library makes. CAS21 (buildings) stays flat at $198M from native to 1 GWe — implying complete building-cost sharing across 20 modules. Whether a 20-module z-pinch plant actually shares a single building or requires 20 separate containment cells is an architectural question the analysis doesn't address.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The native-to-1GWe scaling shows several accounts with large jumps that warrant architectural scrutiny:

- **CAS22 total:** $93.4M → $1,389.3M (14.9×). This is driven by 20-module replication. The sub-accounts that scale roughly 13–15× (C220200, C220500, C220700) are doing so because they are per-module costs that replicate linearly. This is architecturally expected for a modular concept — each 50 MWe module needs its own driver, power conditioning, and I&C. The scaling is internally consistent with n_mod=20.

- **CAS23 (Turbine plant):** $14.8M → $296.5M (20×). This implies the library treats each module's thermal conversion as independent — no sharing of steam turbine infrastructure across modules. For a multi-module plant with 20 cores feeding LiPb heat into a common thermal circuit, this may overstate BOP costs. Alternatively, if each 200 MWth core has its own steam turbine, 20× scaling is correct. The analysis doesn't specify.

- **CAS21 (Buildings):** $198.0M → $198.0M (1.0×). Complete sharing — one building houses all 20 modules. For 3m-diameter cores, this may be physically plausible, but 20 fusion cores with flowing LiPb and pulsed power equipment in a single building is a nontrivial layout assumption.

- **CAS30 (Indirect costs):** $65.7M → $444.2M (6.8×). This scales sub-linearly with direct costs, which is the standard NOAK learning-curve treatment.

The headline LCOE of 41.3 $/MWh at 1 GWe depends on the 20-module plant being a reasonable economic unit. The analysis notes this is not parameterized (Section 5, line 171) but doesn't flag the 20× replication as a modeling assumption with its own uncertainty.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

The two assumptions that would most change the LCOE conclusion:

1. **q_eng (engineering Q).** The YAML default of 3.0 means 33% of gross electric is recirculated. If q_eng were 5.0, the native LCOE would drop substantially (less recirculating power → more net electric per MWth). If q_eng were 2.0, native LCOE would rise sharply. The analysis identifies the missing power balance as Gap #1 (blocking) but never connects the gap to the q_eng parameter that the model is actually using. This is the most load-bearing assumption in the entire model run and it is invisible in the artifacts.

2. **Pulsed-power capital cost coefficients ($/MJ for C220104, $/J for C220107).** These library constants determine what fraction of CAS22 goes to the driver system. At 20× replication, even a 2× change in the driver cost coefficient moves the 1 GWe LCOE by tens of $/MWh. The analysis identifies pulsed-power cost as Gap #4 (important) but cannot ground it.

The analysis correctly identifies its three blocking gaps (power balance, rep rate, cathode lifetime) in Section 6. The rep-rate gap (#2) and cathode-lifetime gap (#3) are physics/engineering risks that the cost model cannot capture — they are availability and capacity-factor risks, not capital-cost risks. The power-balance gap (#1) is the one that directly governs the model through q_eng, and it is the one most worth surfacing as a headline concern.
