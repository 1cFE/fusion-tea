========================================================================
KEY BINDING CONSTRAINTS — Top LCOE Drivers
========================================================================

  1. PHYSICS Q (Q > 10 never demonstrated) — CRITICAL

     Q is the single most leveraged parameter: halving Q roughly doubles
     driver recirculating power as a fraction of gross electric.
     At Q = 5, driver wall-plug power ≈ 54 MW/module; gross electric at 33%
     efficiency ≈ 66 MWe/module → net output collapses to ~4 MWe/module.
     The model becomes net-negative at Q < 4.5 (at 33% thermal efficiency).
     STATUS: Calculated at 200 µs, 1.35 MA. FuZE demonstrated 20–40 µs.
     A 5–10× pinch lifetime extension is required before Q can be measured.

  2. REPETITION RATE (0.2 Hz demonstrated → 10 Hz required) — CRITICAL

     Rep rate scales net output proportionally (at fixed Q and efficiency).
     A 50× gap from current Century capability to commercial target means
     electrode thermal loading, liquid-metal dynamics, gas injection timing,
     and pulsed-power component heat rejection must all work at 50× higher
     duty cycle simultaneously. No published failure-mode characterization
     for this regime. LCOE at 5 Hz is roughly 2× that at 10 Hz.

  3. PULSED POWER DRIVER COST (no published estimate exists) — HIGH IMPACT

     The driver capital cost is the largest single CAS22 line item, replacing
     the magnet system from tokamak economics. At $3 M/MJ stored energy,
     driver cost ≈ $8.1 M/module; at $10 M/MJ, ≈ $27 M/module.
     The OSTI 2025 pulsed power report documents a supply chain problem:
     10,000–216,000 capacitors/plant, 4–6 yr delivery lead times,
     and capacitor lifetimes 4–6 orders of magnitude short of commercial
     requirements. This is a program-level constraint, not a cost-curve issue.

  NOTE: Elimination of HTS magnets and cryogenic plant is a genuine
  structural advantage vs. compact tokamak designs — these would add
  $500M–$1B to the capital cost of a comparable spherical tokamak plant.
  The pulsed power driver is a cost substitute, not just an addition.

========================================================================
UNCERTAINTY SUMMARY
========================================================================

  This model carries HIGH aggregate uncertainty because its two largest
  cost drivers (driver capital, driver recirculating power) both depend
  on parameters that have never been demonstrated at commercial scale:

  • Q > 10: calculated, not measured (5–10× pinch lifetime extrapolation)
  • 10 Hz rep rate: 50× beyond current Century capability
  • Driver cost: no published estimate; 10× range in industrial analogues

  The LCOE range across scenarios above spans roughly 3–10×, primarily
  driven by these three parameters. Until FuZE-A or Century demonstrates
  Q ≥ 1 and rep rates above 1 Hz, the baseline scenario is an extrapolation
  of a calculated design point, not an engineering projection.

  All estimates follow CAS structure from 1costingfe costing_constants.yaml.
  Standard accounts use documented scaling laws. Overrides are marked [OVERRIDE].
