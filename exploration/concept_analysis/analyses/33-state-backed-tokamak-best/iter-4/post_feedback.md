### F-1: Q sweep holds p_net fixed while recirculating power varies — LCOE at low Q severely understated
- **Target:** model_setup.py Q sensitivity sweep
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The Q sweep reports p_net = 1000 MW at every Q value from 5 to 15, while P_in_elec ranges from 1224 MWe (Q=5) to 408 MWe (Q=15). With P_fus fixed at 3673 MW, P_gross_elec ≈ P_fus × mn × eta_th ≈ 1400 MWe. At Q=5, recirculating H&CD power alone (1224 MWe) plus auxiliary loads (~50 MWe) leaves roughly 126 MWe net — not 1000 MWe. The LCOE computation at Q=5 (185.1 $/MWh) appears to use 1000 MWe in the denominator when the correct net output is ~126 MWe, understating LCOE by a factor of ~8. Separately, the Key Assumptions block states "Q value implied: ~10 (drives p_input = 200 MW)" but P_fus/Q = 3673/10 = 367 MW thermal (the Q sweep's Q=10 row correctly shows 367 MW, making the 200 MW annotation stale). The Q sensitivity as implemented does not capture the cliff-edge in net output that the analysis narrative correctly identifies as the key risk at low Q.
- **Recommendation:** Redesign the Q sweep so that at each Q, the model either (a) holds p_net fixed and adjusts P_fus upward (so P_gross grows enough to cover the higher P_in_elec while delivering 1000 MWe net) and recomputes LCOE at each resulting P_fus and cost scale, or (b) holds P_fus fixed and propagates the actual P_net at each Q into the LCOE denominator. Either approach will reveal the true shape of the LCOE–Q curve and the cliff-edge near Q~4 where net output approaches zero. Also update the Key Assumptions annotation to state p_in_th = 367 MW at Q=10 (not 200 MW).
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Key differentiators from conventional tokamak not explicitly listed
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and overall framing
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** BEST is a conventional-aspect-ratio D-T tokamak (A ≈ 3.3), so its TEA differentiators are relative to the mainstream MFE tokamak family — not to an exotic concept. The analysis covers these differentiators in narrative across multiple sections but never consolidates them as an explicit list. A reader cannot identify at a glance what distinguishes BEST's PFPP from, say, 01-hts-compact-tokamak or 28-hts-tokamak-full-hts at the structural level. The five key differentiators that drive TEA divergence — (1) LTS Nb3Sn/NbTi magnet technology vs. full-HTS, requiring larger machine volume at lower field; (2) experimental-to-commercial extrapolation chain unique among analyzed concepts (BEST → CFEDR → PFPP); (3) Chinese state construction economics (2–4× cost discount hypothesis); (4) four-method H&CD portfolio vs. single-technology approaches; (5) blanket technology undecided among three competing TBM concepts — each have different cost implications but are embedded in challenges, cross-concept notes, and gap tables rather than presented as a unified comparison.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short explicit differentiator table or bulleted list (preferably near the start of Section 7) that names each key structural difference from the conventional D-T tokamak reference, labels it as novel / borrowed / shared, and states whether it creates a cost advantage, penalty, or uncertainty. This does not require new content — it reorganizes what is already in the analysis into a scannable format that directly serves Goals 2 and 3.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Modeling approach (1costingfe vs. free-form) not stated or justified
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Approach) or Section 7
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis does not state which modeling approach is used for the PFPP commercial analogue or explain why it is appropriate. The model uses 1costingfe with ARIES-ACT1 as the geometry analogue — a reasonable choice given the conventional aspect-ratio topology — but this choice carries significant implications for how the output should be interpreted: 1costingfe applies a Western parametric cost structure to a concept whose central LCOE hypothesis (H1) is that Chinese construction economics fundamentally reduce that cost structure by 2–4×. The gap between the model's Western ARIES baseline and the Chinese context it is trying to capture is wider than for any other concept in the pipeline, making the modeling approach justification more important here, not less. Without an explicit statement of the approach and its limitations, readers may misinterpret the 150.8 $/MWh base case as a meaningful estimate rather than a Western upper-bound proxy.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief statement in Section 2 or Section 7 that identifies the modeling approach as 1costingfe applied with ARIES-ACT1 as the geometry proxy, explains why this is appropriate for a conventional-AR D-T tokamak topology, and explicitly frames the base case output as a Western NOAK upper bound that should be read in conjunction with the Chinese discount scenarios (82.7 and 48.6 $/MWh). This positions the model output correctly relative to the analysis's central uncertainty.
- **Priority:** important
