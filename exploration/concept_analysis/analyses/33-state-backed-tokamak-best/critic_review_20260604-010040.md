# Critic Review — 33-state-backed-tokamak-best

## Headline issues

1. **18 outlier flags concentrated in CAS22 subsystems but analysis does not justify the divergences** — The sanity check flagged C220101 (first wall/blanket), C220102 (shield), C220105 (structure), C220106 (vacuum), C220107 (power supplies), C220108 (divertor), C220110 (RH), C220111 (instrumentation) all as high outliers, plus CAS22 aggregate as 0.26× comparables median (outlier low). The analysis extensively documents why ACT1's divertor/blanket/shield *should* cost more (5 FPY replacement, 180 dpa RAFM, SiC composites, W-alloy divertor, 13.7 MW/m² heat flux, 90% radiation fraction) but then presents model outputs showing C220108 (divertor) at 2.1× comparables and total C220000 at 0.26× comparables. This is incoherent. Either the library is systematically underpricing ACT1's reactor equipment because the archetype calibration doesn't capture ARIES advanced-technology assumptions, or the comparables (which are all HTS compact designs) have fundamentally different cost structures that make ratio-based sanity checks uninformative for a large-scale LTS machine. Section 7 argues the latter (HTS enables compactness but costs 3-5× per kg; ACT1 is 3× larger major radius) but does not explicitly tie this to the C220000 outlier-low flag. The reader is left wondering whether ACT1's CAS22 is genuinely cheap (economies of scale at 6.25 m radius, mature Nb3Sn supply chain) or whether the model is missing cost drivers the analysis identified.

2. **Zero overrides despite extensive identification of cost-driving unknowns creates accountability gap** — The override walkthrough in §5b correctly notes that ARIES-ACT is a paper study without company-grounded cost data and therefore no direct-provenance overrides are justified. However, §4 identifies multiple *derived* cost drivers with quantified ranges that materially impact LCOE: (a) SiC composites at $500-2000/kg current vs. $100-300/kg at scale (§4), with failure forcing fallback to RAFM/DCLL reducing thermal efficiency 58%→45% (+30% fusion power, +10% major radius per §2); (b) RAFM steel at $10-15/kg current vs. $5-8/kg at scale, with 500-800 t per reactor and 180 dpa design limit vs. 50 dpa experimental database (§4); (c) enriched PbLi inventory ~$30-60M one-time cost at $30-50/kg (§4, §5b C220107 entry notes this but dismisses as "analyst-derived estimate, not company-published"). The analysis documents these but the model ingests none of them. The result is that `model_output.txt` shows generic=native=1GWe (all three forwards identical) despite the analysis arguing ACT1 has higher component costs than tokamak baseline (advanced materials) *and* higher economies of scale than compact designs (6.25 m vs. 1.85 m). If the library already prices SiC at commercial scale and RAFM at 180 dpa and enriched PbLi inventory, state this in §5b. If it does not, the zero-override posture leaves LCOE ungrounded.

3. **CAS21 (buildings) flagged as outlier-high (2.23×) contradicts §7 assertion that larger size drives higher building costs for ACT1** — The family-delta reasoning in §7 repeatedly states that ACT1's 6.25 m major radius (3× SPARC's 1.85 m) should *reduce* $/kWe for buildings and non-magnet CAS22 via economies of scale, offsetting HTS compactness advantage. But the model outputs show CAS21 at $620.8M (2.23× comparables median $278.86M). If ACT1 is genuinely 3× larger by volume than SPARC, a 2.23× building cost is plausible (volume scales ~R³, so 3³=27× volume but shared facilities and height limits compress the multiplier). The analysis does not reconcile this. Either (a) ACT1's larger size *does* drive higher absolute building costs and the §7 framing is wrong, or (b) the library is overpricing CAS21 for large-radius tokamaks relative to compact HTS machines, and the comparables set (four HTS compacts) is a poor reference for a 6.25 m conventional machine. The outlier-high flag suggests (a); §7 argues (b) implicitly. Which is it?

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

**DPC status: ok** — P_native coherence across table/analysis/model_setup is confirmed at 1000 MWe. No interpretation needed.

**Contract status: ok** — The module uses the `run_native_and_1gw` helper form (all three forwards via shared orchestration), which is the current-generation contract shape. The `spec` dict at model_setup.py:30-45 correctly excludes physics parameters (delta, plasma_current, beta_n, h_factor) that the analysis documents but the library doesn't consume, with an explanatory comment at lines 37-45 noting these are "not costing model inputs." This is correct discipline — the library's TOKAMAK archetype takes geometry + power and solves inverse power balance internally, so intermediate plasma physics from the ARIES systems code is out of scope for costingfe. No interpretation needed beyond noting the comment demonstrates understanding of the library's interface contract.

**Count_smell status: ok** — Zero enabled overrides for High archetype-fit falls in the expected band (0-4). The analysis in §5b argues this is justified because ARIES-ACT1 is a paper concept matching the library's default ARIES-class tokamak calibration exactly. The count alone is not a smell. However, the *reasoning* in §5b creates tension (see headline issue #2) — the analysis extensively documents cost drivers (SiC composites, RAFM radiation limits, enriched PbLi, divertor lifetime) that are material to LCOE but then declines to encode any of them as overrides because they lack "direct company sourcing." This is a judgment call: is "analyst-derived estimate from published unit costs × design-point quantities" a valid override provenance tier, or must overrides be "company published this exact $ figure"? The zero-override outcome is consistent with the strict interpretation (direct only), but leaves the model ungrounded if the library doesn't already price these drivers at ACT1's assumed maturity levels.

**Sanity status: flagged (18 outliers)** — This is the core signal. The flags cluster in two patterns:

1. **CAS22 subsystems all outlier-high** (C220101 first wall/blanket 6.01×, C220102 shield 5.33×, C220105 structure 4.72×, C220106 vacuum 5.30×, C220107 power supplies 2.65×, C220108 divertor 2.10×, C220110 RH 2.02×, C220111 instrumentation 2.59×) but **C220000 aggregate outlier-low** (0.26×). This is arithmetically possible if the comparables have vastly different internal CAS22 cost distributions (e.g., magnets dominating their C220103 but ACT1's Nb3Sn being cheaper per kg, pushing other accounts up in relative terms), but the analysis does not explain this structure. The reader sees "every reactor subsystem costs more than comparables" but "total reactor equipment costs 74% less than comparables" and cannot reconcile it.

2. **Top-level CAS rollups outlier-low** (CAS22 0.26×, CAS30 capitalized spares 0.31×, CAS60 pre-ops 0.32×, CAS90 cooldown/cleanup 0.32×) with **CAS21 buildings outlier-high** (2.23×) and **CAS27 special materials outlier-high** (5.87×). The CAS27 flag is trivial (absolute magnitude $15M, comparables median $2.56M — rounding-level noise). The CAS21 and CAS22 flags are load-bearing: if ACT1's buildings genuinely cost 2.23× comparables but reactor equipment costs 0.26× comparables, the implication is either (a) ACT1 is a physically larger but cheaper-per-kg machine (plausible for Nb3Sn LTS vs. REBCO HTS), or (b) the library's volume scaling for buildings is overpricing ACT1 while the archetype calibration for CAS22 is underpricing it, and the two errors cancel at the LCOE level (dangerous). The analysis in §7 leans toward (a) but does not make it explicit.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes ONE named plant: ARIES-ACT1 reference case per Kessel et al. 2015, Table I. Every parameter in the design-point table (§5, lines 94-124) cites the same source (osti-servlets-purl-1178069.md, which is the ARIES-ACT paper extraction). There is no stitching across published designs. The model_setup.py spec (lines 30-45) encodes the subset of those parameters that the library consumes (R0, plasma_t, elon, B, p_input), all traceable to Table I, with P_native=1000 matching the design point's net electric output.

**However**, the analysis front-matter (lines 15-27) lists 11 "alternatives_considered" including ARIES-ACT2/ACT3/ACT4, CFEDR, CFETR Phase I/II, BEST, PFPP, EU-DEMO, JA-DEMO, K-DEMO. The selection_rationale (lines 24-25) correctly states "ARIES-ACT1 is the only design in this concept's corpus with a published net electric power figure (1000 MWe) attached to a fully specified engineering design point." This is clean. But then §1 (data availability) and §3 (maturity) extensively cite the BEST Research Plan v1.1 for component specifications (hybrid Nb3Sn/YBCO magnets "~2000 tonnes total" at line 54-55; 50 MW H/CD at line 56; TBM program at line 57; tritium inventory 110g at lines 58, 168) and §4 (materials) references BEST blanket structures requiring "the same structural and functional material with CFEDR blankets" (lines 220-221). The question: are these BEST references *context* (illustrating state-backed tokamak programs generically) or *parameter stitching* (importing BEST magnet mass or tritium inventory into the ACT1 cost model)?

Reading model_setup.py confirms no stitching — the spec uses only ARIES-ACT1 Table I values. The BEST references in the analysis are contextual: they document the state of the art for subsystems (magnets, TBMs, tritium handling, remote handling) that ACT1 assumes but ARIES did not detail at component level. This is appropriate use of cross-concept sourcing to fill maturity gaps without conflating design points. **Verdict: coherent.** One named plant (ACT1), cross-references used for context not parameter substitution.

### Override discipline (judgment)

The override registry (model_setup.py lines 48-51, analysis §5b lines 169-228) contains zero enabled overrides with a four-paragraph walkthrough justifying the null set.

**Per-entry judgment** (walking the CAS22 accounts mentioned in §5b):

- **C220101 (first wall/blanket)** — Analysis states "ARIES reports 5 FPY lifetime and SiC/PbLi configuration but no dollar figure." The sanity check shows C220101 at 6.01× comparables median. The analysis in §2 (challenges) and §3 (maturity) identifies SiC composites as TRL 2-3 ("on paper only" for structural-grade at 100+ dpa, line 91-98) and the cost driver (current $500-2000/kg vs. $100-300/kg at scale, §4 line 215-217). The question: does the library price C220101 assuming SiC maturity or RAFM fallback? If SiC, does it use $500/kg (current) or $100/kg (future)? If RAFM, why is ACT1's C220101 6× higher than HTS compact tokamaks (which also use RAFM blankets)? **The override walkthrough does not address this.** It correctly notes "no company override" but does not state what the library baseline assumption is, leaving the 6.01× outlier unexplained.

- **C220103 (magnets)** — Analysis states "BEST uses hybrid Nb3Sn/YBCO (~2000 tonnes total) but this is experimental scale. ACT1 uses Nb3Sn at 11.8 T (ITER-class). No published cost." The sanity check shows C220103 at 1.08× comparables (in range). This is the one CAS22 subsystem that is *not* an outlier. The analysis in §7 argues ACT1's Nb3Sn magnets should be cheaper per kA-m than comparables' REBCO ($10-20/kA-m vs. $30-100/kA-m, line 277-278) but ACT1's larger size (6.25 m vs. 1.85 m) means more total conductor. The net effect is "uncertain — SPARC wins on building costs, ACT1 wins on magnet $/kg, total capital cost is trade-off between volume and unit cost" (§7 line 282-283). The C220103 near-parity (1.08×) is consistent with this "uncertain trade-off" framing. **This is the most coherent override entry**: the analysis predicts offsetting effects, the model shows near-median cost, the walkthrough declines to override. Good.

- **C220104 (heating)** — Analysis states "ACT1 requires 42.7 MW H/CD at 0.4 wall-plug efficiency. BEST deploys 50 MW... but these are R&D system costs, not commercial unit costs. No override." The sanity check shows C220104 at 1.09× (in range). The analysis in §2 identifies H/CD efficiency as medium-criticality gap (0.25-0.5 range vs. 0.4 assumed, each 0.05 reduction adds ~10 MW recirc, +2% LCOE, line 123-128). The analysis in §3 rates H/CD as TRL 6-8 ("demonstrated MW-class... BEST deploying 50 MW... not demonstrated multi-year continuous operation," line 181-186). **Why no override?** The walkthrough reasoning is "R&D system costs, not commercial unit costs" but ACT1 is also not a commercial plant (it's a paper study). If the gap is ±0.1 efficiency (0.3 to 0.5) and each 0.05 step is 2% LCOE, that's ±4% LCOE uncertainty from this parameter alone. The override discipline here is: "we have quantified uncertainty but no company-published resolution, therefore no override." This is internally consistent with the strict direct-provenance standard but leaves the model exposed to the unquantified gap.

- **C220108 (divertor)** — Analysis states "W-alloy + ODS RAFM steel, 13.7 MW/m² peak heat flux baseline. 5 FPY lifetime stated but no cost. No override." The sanity check shows C220108 at 2.10× comparables. The analysis in §2 identifies divertor as high-criticality gap (ELM damage forcing 2-3 FPY replacement drops availability 5 ppt and increases levelized replacement cost 50-100%, line 166-168). The analysis in §3 rates divertor as TRL 4-5 with critical unknowns (90% radiation fraction, 100 million ELMs over 5 FPY, redeposition property degradation, line 148-168). **Why is C220108 2.1× comparables if ACT1's divertor lifetime (5 FPY) is stated as longer or equal to baseline tokamak assumptions?** Possible explanations: (a) ACT1's peak heat flux (13.7 MW/m²) is higher than comparables, driving more cooling/armor cost even at same lifetime; (b) the library prices W-alloy + ODS RAFM as more expensive than standard W monoblocks; (c) the comparables (HTS compact tokamaks) have lower absolute divertor cost because smaller machines have smaller divertor surface area, so the 2.1× ratio is a size effect not a $/m² effect. The override walkthrough does not distinguish these. **Judgment: the 2.1× ratio is plausibly architectural (size effect) but the analysis does not say so.**

- **C220110 (remote handling)** — Analysis states "BEST documents remote handling requirements but no cost estimate. ARIES does not provide RH cost separately. No override." The sanity check shows C220110 at 2.02× comparables. The analysis in §2 identifies RH availability impact as medium-criticality gap (reliability forcing annual shutdowns or +50% outage duration drops CF from 80% to 70%, +14% LCOE, line 176-178). The analysis in §3 rates RH as TRL 5-6 ("ITER prototypes... BEST planning... missing at scale: reliable high-availability remote maintenance," line 171-179). The analysis in §7 vs. ST80 notes "ST80's demountable magnets simplify maintenance (contact maintenance feasible for some components, reducing RH capital cost ~20-30%)" (line 313-315), implying ACT1's welded Nb3Sn TF coils require *more* RH than demountable-HTS designs. **The 2.02× outlier is consistent with this architectural difference** (ACT1 needs full remote handling; some comparables have partial contact-maintenance access). But the override walkthrough does not state this, leaving the flag unexplained.

**Summary judgment on override discipline**: The walkthrough is internally consistent (strict direct-provenance standard applied uniformly) but does not reconcile the zero-override posture with the 18 sanity flags. Every outlier-high subsystem (C220101, C220102, C220105, C220106, C220107, C220108, C220110, C220111) is dismissed with "no company cost data, no override" but the analysis provides architectural reasoning (SiC composites, larger size, advanced divertor, full remote handling) that *predicts* higher costs. The reader cannot tell whether the library already prices these drivers (in which case the outliers are explained and no overrides needed) or whether the library uses generic tokamak defaults (in which case the zero-override posture is under-grounding the model).

**The cleanest fix**: Add one sentence per dismissed override in §5b stating what the library baseline assumption is. Example: "C220101: ARIES reports SiC/PbLi but no dollar figure. The library prices first wall/blanket using ARIES-AT RAFM/DCLL scaling at [X $/m² or Y% of C220000], which is appropriate for ACT1's fallback case (if SiC fails to mature per §2). If SiC matures, C220101 could be 20-30% lower due to reduced mass. No override because the library default represents the conservative (RAFM) baseline and the SiC case lacks company-grounded cost data."

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The `result_1gw` forward is structurally identical to `native` (both at 1000 MWe, so n_mod=1 and no NOAK learning). This is correct for a P_native=1000 design point. The two-knob projection questions are:

1. **Do the per-account `result_1gw` values look implausible relative to `native` for any architectural reason?** No — they are identical by construction (n_mod=1). The library's NOAK learning curve applies at n_mod>1; for a 1 GWe comparison at 1 GWe native scale, there is no projection, only a consistency check.

2. **Are there architectural reasons ACT1 should scale differently than the library's standard projection?** Potentially yes — the analysis in §2 identifies that "power scrape-off width λq is the dominant sizing constraint" (line 72-75) and "if scrape-off widths prove narrower than predicted, ACT1 would require R > 7 m to maintain tolerable divertor loading. This single parameter can shift overnight capital by 20-30%" (line 77-79). This is a *native-scale uncertainty*, not a 1GWe projection issue. But if the library's divertor heat flux model uses an older λq formulation (pre-Eich scaling), the entire CAS22 cost for ACT1 could be underestimated by 20-30%. The analysis does not state which λq model the ARIES-ACT study used or whether it matches the library's internal model. This is a gap in the traceability chain, not an implausible two-knob value per se.

3. **Is the LCOE=117.1 $/MWh outcome plausible relative to the ARIES-ACT COE=64.3 stated at line 124?** The analysis notes "units not specified; likely mills/kWh per ARIES convention" (line 124). If ARIES COE is 64.3 mills/kWh = 64.3 $/MWh, then the library's 117.1 $/MWh is 1.82× higher. Is this plausible? The analysis does not compare. Possible explanations: (a) ARIES used lower financing assumptions (5% real discount rate) vs. library default (8-10%?); (b) ARIES used higher capacity factor (85%?) vs. library default (80% or lower?); (c) ARIES costing basis is 2015 dollars, library is 2024 dollars, and 1.82× is within inflation+learning uncertainty; (d) the library is systematically overpricing tokamaks. Without knowing the ARIES financial assumptions and the library's, the 1.82× ratio is un-interpretable. **This should have been a paragraph in the analysis** — either reconciling the library LCOE with the ARIES COE or explicitly noting that the ARIES COE lacks sufficient documentation (financial assumptions, dollar year) to serve as a validation anchor.

**Summary judgment on two-knob projection**: The projection mechanics are correct (n_mod=1 produces identity). The architectural question is whether the library's *native-scale* divertor and blanket costing captures ACT1's assumptions (λq, SiC composites, 5 FPY lifetime), which the analysis documents extensively but does not tie back to the model's internal physics. The LCOE comparison to ARIES COE is missing entirely, which is a gap — if ARIES published COE=64.3 and the library produces LCOE=117.1, that 82% delta is either explainable (financing, CF, $/year) or a red flag (model miscalibration), and the analysis should say which.

### Family delta vs comparables

The analysis in §7 provides four pairwise comparisons (ACT1 vs. SPARC, ST80, HH70, Firefly) with qualitative cost drivers identified for each. The sanity check flagged 18 outliers. The question: do the outliers correspond to the architectural deltas the analysis identified?

**CAS21 buildings outlier-high (2.23×)**: Section 7 vs. SPARC states "ACT1 major radius is 6.25 m — more than 3× larger than SPARC" (line 264) and "SPARC's HTS magnets enable 3× smaller major radius, reducing CAS21 (buildings)" (line 276-277). This predicts ACT1 buildings should be higher than SPARC. The 2.23× ratio is consistent with this prediction (not a 27× ratio because volume scales R³ but building cost scales less than linearly with volume due to shared facilities). **Outlier explained by architecture: yes.**

**CAS22 aggregate outlier-low (0.26×)**: Section 7 vs. SPARC states "SPARC wins on building costs, ACT1 wins on magnet $/kg, total capital cost is trade-off between volume and unit cost" (line 282-283). If ACT1's magnets are cheaper per kg (Nb3Sn $10-20/kA-m vs. REBCO $30-100/kA-m, line 277-278) but the machine is 3× larger radius (implying ~10× larger volume for coils at fixed aspect ratio and field), the total magnet cost is uncertain. The analysis argues "net effect: uncertain" but the model shows C220103 at 1.08× (near-median), suggesting the two effects cancel. Then why is C220000 aggregate at 0.26×? The analysis does not explain. Possible explanations: (a) the comparables are dominated by magnet costs (C220103 is 60-70% of their C220000) whereas ACT1's magnets are 30% of C220000 (line 252: C220103=1368.8, C220000=4648.6, ratio 29%), so ACT1's non-magnet subsystems appear cheaper in relative terms even if absolute costs are similar; (b) the comparables have not yet built hardware and their CAS22 estimates are conservative/padded, whereas the ARIES-ACT study used mature costing methods; (c) the library is systematically underpricing large-radius tokamaks because the archetype calibration is anchored to compact designs. **The analysis hints at (a)** in the "uncertain trade-off" framing but does not explicitly compute the magnet-vs-total-CAS22 ratio for ACT1 vs. comparables. **Outlier explained by architecture: partially** (the analysis provides ingredients but does not complete the math).

**CAS22 subsystems all outlier-high (C220101 6.01×, C220102 5.33×, C220105 4.72×, C220106 5.30×, C220107 2.65×, C220108 2.10×, C220110 2.02×, C220111 2.59×)**: Section 7 identifies multiple architectural differences predicting higher subsystem costs for ACT1:
- First wall/blanket (C220101): "ACT1 uses SiC/PbLi self-cooled blanket achieving 58% thermal efficiency vs. SPARC baseline (likely DCLL or water-cooled with ~40-45% efficiency)" (line 286-288). SiC composites are currently $500-2000/kg (§4 line 215-217), which is 10-50× commodity steel. If the library prices C220101 using SiC at current cost, 6.01× is plausible. But the analysis in §5b states "no override because it lacks direct company sourcing" and does not say whether the library uses SiC or RAFM for ACT1. **Outlier explained by architecture: potentially, but the analysis doesn't confirm the library's assumption.**
- Divertor (C220108): ACT1 peak heat flux 13.7 MW/m² (line 121) vs. comparables likely 10-15 MW/m² (SPARC/ST80 are smaller machines with lower total fusion power but potentially similar or higher specific heat flux due to geometric constraints). The analysis in §7 vs. ST80 states "Spherical tokamaks have unfavorable divertor geometry — tight radial build at inboard, limited space for divertor cooling. ST80 will face higher peak heat flux per MW-fusion than ACT1" (line 311-313). If ST80's divertor is more expensive than ACT1's, why is ACT1's C220108 at 2.10× comparables median? Either (a) the comparables median is dominated by SPARC and HH70 (conventional-aspect tokamaks with lower heat flux than ACT1), and ST80 is an outlier within the comparables set, or (b) the analysis's claim that ACT1's divertor is less challenging than ST80's is wrong. **Outlier explained by architecture: unclear** — the analysis argues both ways (ACT1's divertor is more challenging than SPARC's but less challenging than ST80's) without resolving which dominates the median.
- Remote handling (C220110): Section 7 vs. ST80 states "ST80's demountable magnets simplify maintenance (contact maintenance feasible for some components, reducing RH capital cost ~20-30%)" (line 313-315), implying ACT1's welded coils require more RH. The 2.02× ratio is consistent with this (ACT1 needs full remote sector-based handling; some comparables have partial demountable access). **Outlier explained by architecture: yes.**

**Summary judgment on family delta**: The analysis identifies architectural deltas (size, magnet technology, blanket technology, divertor heat flux, maintenance access) that qualitatively predict the outlier patterns (higher CAS21 for ACT1 due to size; higher CAS22 subsystems due to SiC/advanced materials; lower CAS22 aggregate due to cheaper Nb3Sn magnets per kg). But it does not quantitatively reconcile the outlier magnitudes (why 6.01× for C220101 specifically? why 0.26× for C220000 aggregate when subsystems are 2-6× higher?). The most likely explanation is that ACT1's C220103 (magnets) is a *much* smaller fraction of total C220000 than comparables (29% for ACT1 vs. 60-70% for HTS compacts), so the subsystem ratios are inflated in relative terms even though absolute costs are architectural. **But the analysis does not say this.** It should have included a table comparing C220103/C220000 ratios for ACT1 vs. each comparable to show the cost-structure difference.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

The analysis in §6 lists 18 gaps with criticality ratings. The "blocking" gaps (per §6 lines 373-382) are:
- #5: Power scrape-off width λq (dominant sizing constraint, ±50% uncertainty → 20-30% capital cost swing)
- #6: Advanced physics performance (H98=1.65 at Greenwald density, βN=5.75 — if H98 degrades to 1.3, major radius +15% and COE rises proportionally)
- #9: RAFM radiation tolerance (5 FPY lifetime assumes 180 dpa, database ends at 50 dpa — if limit is 100 dpa, replacement intervals halve and availability drops 5-10 ppt)
- #11: Divertor lifetime under neutron + ELM + erosion (5 FPY assumed, if 2-3 FPY, availability drops 5 ppt and replacement cost +50-100%)
- #12: ELM suppression (without mitigation, W-divertor fails)

**Which one or two would flip the LCOE conclusion?** The analysis identifies #5 (λq) and #6 (advanced physics) as the two highest-impact parameters (each individually capable of +15-30% capital cost). These are load-bearing. The analysis in §2 prioritizes them as #1 and #2 in the challenges ranking (lines 70-110). **This is coherent.**

**Are those flagged as gaps in `analysis.md`?** Yes — §6 gap inventory line 375 "#5: Power scrape-off width λq experimental database and uncertainty quantification" and line 376 "#6: Advanced physics performance database: H98 = 1.65 at n/nGr = 1.0 with βN = 5.75 and wall stabilization" both rated "truly-unknown" and "blocking."

**Does the model encode these uncertainties?** No. The `spec` dict in model_setup.py encodes `R0=6.25` (which implicitly bakes in the λq assumption that led ARIES to size the machine at 6.25 m rather than 5.5 m or 7.0 m) and `p_input=42.7` (which bakes in the H98=1.65 assumption that allows 42.7 MW H/CD to sustain the plasma). The model produces a point estimate (LCOE=117.1 $/MWh) with no sensitivity bounds. **The gaps are documented but not quantified in the model outputs.** This is a limitation of the deterministic cost model (it doesn't do Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation), not a flaw in the analysis per se, but it means the LCOE=117.1 should be interpreted as "conditional on λq and H98 assumptions holding, 117.1 $/MWh; if either degrades, +20-30%." The analysis should state this explicitly in a "confidence in LCOE" section, which is absent.

## What I deliberately did not say

1. **Whether the ARIES-ACT source extraction is complete** — The analysis cites osti-servlets-purl-1178069.md for all Table I parameters and COE=64.3, but notes "units not specified" for COE and "detailed cost account breakdown not provided in available extraction" (§1 line 68-69). I cannot assess whether the full ARIES-ACT paper contains a CAS-level cost table that would justify overrides, or whether the extraction is lossy. This is a source-quality concern (was the right information extracted?) which is upstream of `model_critic` scope, but I flag it here because if the full paper has CAS-level costs and they were not extracted, the zero-override posture is incomplete rather than correct. Route to `research`-stage review.

2. **Whether the comparables set is appropriate** — All four comparables (01, 21, 28, 29) are HTS-magnet compact tokamaks (or spherical tokamak or negative-triangularity, all with R < 3 m and REBCO coils). ACT1 is a 6.25 m LTS-magnet conventional tokamak. The sanity check compares ACT1 to this set and finds 18 outliers, but the analysis in §7 argues the cost structures are fundamentally different (HTS compact vs. LTS large-scale). Is the comparables set serving as a useful reference, or is it comparing apples (ACT1 large-scale state-backed ARIES-class) to oranges (HTS compact private concepts)? The analysis treats the comparables as valid but notes multiple times that "advantage: uncertain" or "net effect: trade-off." I suspect the comparables set is weakly informative for ACT1, and a better reference would be other ARIES-class designs (ARIES-AT, ARIES-RS, EU-DEMO, K-DEMO) if those were modeled in the library. But comparables selection is a pipeline-design question (upstream of this stage), not an artifact-level critique. I note it here as a half-formed concern: **the 18 outliers may indicate comparables-set mismatch rather than ACT1 anomaly.**

3. **Whether SiC composites maturity is a show-stopper** — The analysis rates SiC as TRL 2-3 ("on paper only" for structural-grade, §3 line 91-98) and identifies it as the critical enabler for ACT1's 58% thermal efficiency advantage (§2 line 93-95, §3 line 97-100, §7 line 286-288). If SiC fails, fallback to RAFM drops efficiency to 45%, increasing fusion power +30% and major radius +10%.
