# Critic Review — 31-laser-icf-oec-architecture

## Headline issues

1. **Provenance disagreement (C220108, CAS23) between model_setup.py and analysis.md prevents accountability** — DPC validator flags that `model_setup.py` marks both C220108 and CAS23 as `provenance: derived` while `analysis.md` Section 5b Override Candidates labels them `provenance: direct`. The model_setup.py provenance is correct (both are analyst-assembled estimates, not company-published figures). The analysis.md provenance is wrong. This is a transcription error, not a substantive disagreement, but it breaks the deterministic provenance-coherence check and must be fixed. Corrective action: change both entries in `analysis.md` Section 5b from `provenance: direct` to `provenance: derived` to match `model_setup.py` and reflect reality (neither override is a direct company figure).

2. **Override count (3 enabled) is half the Low archetype-fit rubric floor (6-12), and the under-reach is unjustified by the analysis** — The count_smell validator correctly flags this. Analysis.md Section 5b acknowledges the gap ("This count (3 enabled) remains below the 6–12 rubric band, reflecting **data scarcity across most accounts**") but does not explain why the library default is trusted for the *other* 9+ accounts where the Low archetype-fit grade says it shouldn't be. The analysis identifies qualitative architectural deltas in first-wall/blanket (tungsten-RAFM vs. library baseline), chamber geometry (9 m radius vs. library 4 m), repetition rate (10 Hz design point vs. library 10 Hz default — no delta), target gain (160 vs. library assumption), and DEC subsystem (addressed in CAS23 override), but does not translate most of these into overrides. The under-reach suggests either (a) the archetype-fit grade is wrong (should be Medium or High, not Low), or (b) the analyst is trusting the library default where it misrepresents BLF's cost structure. Given that the Optics Express paper provides zero cost data, (b) is unavoidable — but the analysis should explicitly say "we lack data to override X, Y, Z accounts where the archetype delta likely matters, therefore the native LCOE is biased toward the library baseline and may not reflect BLF's actual cost structure." As written, the analysis handwaves the under-reach without flagging the cost-modeling consequence.

3. **Six sanity-flagged outlier accounts (C220101, C220102, C220104, C220105, C220106, C220106_vessel) diverge from comparables by 3–4×, but only C220104 is explained in analysis.md** — The sanity validator flags: C220101 (first-wall/blanket) 4.32× median, C220102 (radiation shield) 3.55× median, C220104 (laser driver) 0.05× median (outlier_low — BLF's $90/J override is 20× cheaper than the generic), C220105 (primary structure) 3.66× median, C220106 (vacuum system) 3.49× median. Analysis.md Section 5b Override Candidates walks C220104 in detail (the $90/J laser driver override, which *is* the architectural delta and explains the outlier_low flag). But the other five outliers are not discussed. The analysis says "No override" for C220101, C220102, C220105, C220106 in the per-account walkthrough, claiming "no cost data" — but if the library default yields a 3–4× divergence from laser ICF comparables (concepts with published blanket, shield, structure, vacuum costs), that divergence itself is diagnostic. Either (a) BLF's chamber geometry (9 m radius, 10 Hz pulsed loading, embedded magnetic fields for alpha deflection) drives real cost increases in these accounts (architectural, expected), or (b) the library's laser ICF baseline for these accounts is calibrated to a different chamber size or rep rate and does not generalize to BLF (unjustified divergence). The analysis does not say which. If (a), the divergence should be explained in Section 7 Family-Delta as a genuine cost penalty for BLF's larger chamber or pulsed-loading fatigue requirements. If (b), the library default is wrong and an override (or at least a flagged uncertainty) is needed. As written, the analysis is silent on why these accounts are 3–4× higher than comparables.

4. **C220108 target factory override uses incorrect library baseline fraction (0.087 of CAS22) — the actual generic C220108 is 8.7% of generic CAS22, but the override rationale and model_setup.py both hardcode 0.087 as if it's a universal constant** — Analysis.md Section 5b states "The library's IFE target factory baseline (for a mixed direct/indirect portfolio) prices C220108 at ~8.7% of CAS22 for LASER_IFE at this scale." Model_setup.py computes `value: 0.85 * 0.087 * float(generic.costs.cas22)`. Checking: generic C220108 = 616.6 M$, generic CAS22 = 7052.8 M$, ratio = 616.6 / 7052.8 = 0.0874 = 8.74%. The 0.087 hardcoded fraction happens to be correct for this concept's generic forward, but it is *not* a library constant — it's a derived ratio that depends on the concept's P_native, chamber geometry, and rep rate. If the override is ported to a different scale or different concept, the 0.087 will be wrong. The override should either (a) reference `generic.costs.c220108` directly and multiply by 0.85 (the claimed direct-drive advantage), or (b) recompute the fraction dynamically. As written, the override is fragile and non-portable. This is a technical debt issue, not a LCOE-conclusion-changing error, but it reflects imprecise override construction.

5. **CAS23 DEC override rationale claims a 12% capital increment for adding DEC electrodes to handle "~1000 MWe" at 10 Hz, but the model_output.txt shows native CAS23 = 832.3 M$ and generic CAS23 = 743.1 M$, a 12% increase — yet the design point's DEC channel should handle 0.3 × 3520 MWe gross electric = 1056 MWe, not ~1000 MWe, and the override does not explain why adding a 1000+ MWe power-handling subsystem costs only 12% more than the library's thermal-only turbine plant** — The 1.12× multiplier implies the DEC electrode subsystem (high-voltage collection, power conditioning, electrodes along magnetic field lines for ~1 GW of charged-particle kinetic energy conversion) adds only ~$89 M$ to the turbine plant capital (832.3 - 743.1 = 89.2 M$). For context, CAS24 (electric plant equipment, which handles generator step-up transformers and switchgear) is 316.5 M$ in the generic. A 1 GW DEC subsystem is plausibly comparable in capital intensity to a 1 GW generator + switchgear, yet the override prices it at 0.28× that cost. The rationale cites no cost anchors (magnetic mirror experiments in the 1970s had no cost data; Rax et al. 2025 is theory). The 12% increment appears to be a guess. If the DEC subsystem is genuinely novel (no hardware demo, uncharacterized capital cost), the override should flag a wider uncertainty range (e.g., 1.05× to 1.30×) rather than pinning it at 1.12× with no sensitivity discussion. As written, the override understates DEC capital cost uncertainty and may be systematically low.

Coherence is clean.

However, one **latent coherence issue** is flagged in analysis.md Section 5 "Note on auxiliary power (p_input)": the design point specifies 600 MW total auxiliary power (laser 500 MW + facility 100 MW), but the spec dict does not pass `p_input` to the library. The note says "The library's pulsed IFE model back-solves recirculating power from net_electric_mw and q_eng (engineering gain), rather than accepting p_input as a direct input. ... If the library's power balance back-solves to a different recirculating load (say, 700 MW instead of 600 MW), the model is not faithfully representing the design point's power balance. The analyst notes this as a future validation task but does not check it. Reading `model_output.txt`, there is no power-balance printout (only CAS cost breakdown), so we cannot verify whether the library's back-solved auxiliary power matches 600 MW. This is a **unresolved coherence concern** that the analyst flagged but did not close out. It should be validated before the concept is marked as approved.

1. **Target gain G = 160 (Gap #1, blocking, truly-unknown).** If achievable gain is G = 80 (half the assumption), fusion power drops by 2×, and to maintain P_native = 2820 MWe, the laser energy or rep rate must double. If laser energy doubles (5 MJ → 10 MJ), the driver cost doubles (assuming linear scaling with energy), CAS22 increases by ~$450M (or ~$90M if the C220104 override is taken at face value, but see Override Discipline section), overnight capital increases by ~7–13%, and LCOE increases proportionally. If rep rate doubles (10 Hz → 20 Hz), chamber clearing and target injection become even more aggressive (50 ms between shots), and component survivability constraints may force a redesign or higher replacement costs (CAS70 O&M increases). **Net effect: if G is wrong by 2×, LCOE increases by ~10–20%.** This is the single most load-bearing assumption.

2. **Laser driver $/J (Gap #3, blocking, not-yet-sourced).** The C220104 override at $90/J (or $450M total, after fixing the unit error) is derived via comparative triangulation and carries high uncertainty. If BLF's actual driver cost is 2× higher ($180/J or $900M total, still cheaper than DPSSL's $700–1000/J but at the upper end of the Xcimer range), CAS22 increases by $450–810M (depending on whether the current override value is 90M or 450M — see Override Discipline section), overnight capital increases by ~8–15%, and LCOE increases proportionally. If BLF's driver cost is no better than DPSSL ($700/J or $3.5B total), the concept loses its claimed cost advantage and becomes uncompetitive. **Net effect: if $/J is wrong by 2×, LCOE increases by ~10–25%.** This is the second most load-bearing assumption.

3. **DEC efficiency η = 0.44 (Gap #2, blocking, truly-unknown).** If DEC efficiency drops to 0.30 (per the analysis's "if DEC proves infeasible" scenario), the overall fusion-to-electric efficiency falls from 0.44 to ~0.35 (per Section 2 Challenges). Gross electric output drops by ~20%, net electric drops by ~20%, and to maintain P_native = 2820 MWe, fusion power must increase by ~25%, which requires higher laser energy, higher rep rate, or higher target gain. If laser energy increases, driver cost increases (see #2 above). **Net effect: if DEC efficiency is wrong by 30%, LCOE increases by ~15–25%.** This is the third most load-bearing assumption.

**All three gaps are flagged as blocking in Section 6, and all three are unresolved.** The analysis correctly identifies them as critical uncertainties. The LCOE result (native 65.8 $/MWh, 1 GWe NOAK 89.1 $/MWh) is contingent on G = 160, $/J = $90 (or $18 if the override value is taken literally), and η_DEC = 0.44. If any one of these is wrong by 2×, LCOE increases by 10–25%. If all three are wrong in the unfavorable direction (G = 80, $/J = $180, η_DEC = 0.30), LCOE could increase by 30–60%, making the concept uncompetitive with the laser ICF comparables or with fission baseload. The analysis does not quantify this sensitivity, but it should.
