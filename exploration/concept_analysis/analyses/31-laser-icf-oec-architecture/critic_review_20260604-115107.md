**Count smell (status: flagged)**: Two enabled overrides vs. the 6–12 expected band for Low archetype-fit. The slight discrepancy between 600 MW (stated recirculating) and the 578 MW back-calculated from f_re=0.170 is noted and handled correctly (paper's stated values used as authoritative).

### Override discipline (judgment)

**C220104 ($2,000M laser driver, provenance: derived)**: The "derived" label is correct — this is cross-concept analogue bracketing, not a BLF-published figure. The arithmetic is transparent: $400/J × 5 MJ = $2,000M. The $400/J central estimate is positioned as a geometric mean between Xcimer KrF NOAK ($70/J) and DPSSL ($850/J). Strictly, the geometric mean of $70 and $850 is ~$244/J, not $400/J; $400/J is closer to the arithmetic midpoint of the log range. This is a minor point — the bracket is so wide (12:1 ratio) that the positioning within it is essentially arbitrary, and the analyst acknowledges this. The override is doing real work: it shifts C220104 from $1,308M (library default) to $2,000M (+$692M, +53%), which is significant. The library default presumably reflects a DPSSL-class cost model, so the override actually *increases* the driver cost — counter to BLF's claim that OEC/CBC will be cheaper. This is defensible (the analyst is being conservative rather than crediting BLF's unsubstantiated cost claims), but it means the model is not testing BLF's value proposition; it's bracketing from above.

**C220108 ($219M target factory, provenance: derived)**: The derivation chain is: $100M (2004$) × 1.59 (CPI) × 1.38 (throughput scaling at 0.6 exponent) = $219M. Checking: 1.7^0.6 ≈ 1.38 — correct. $100M × 1.59 = $159M — correct. $159M × 1.38 = $219.4M — correct. The override pulls C220108 from $596M (library default, generic column) down to $219M, a 63% reduction. The library default is clearly too high, possibly because the LASER_IFE archetype's target factory cost was calibrated for a more complex target (indirect-drive with hohlraum?). The Goodin et al. source is a legitimate NOAK DD target factory estimate. However, the 2004 study assumed different target specifications and manufacturing technology than BLF's cryogenic shock-ignition targets require. The 0.6 scaling exponent for throughput is a reasonable industrial assumption but is not justified from the source.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The native-to-1 GWe scaling (2820 MWe → 1000 MWe) is a substantial downscaling (2.82×). At 1 GWe, LCOE rises from 71.9 to 93.7 $/MWh (+30%), and overnight cost from 5,488 to 6,718 $/kW (+22%). This is consistent with the usual economy-of-scale penalty for downscaling. No per-account 1 GWe values look implausible relative to their native values — the scaling factors are in the 0.3–0.5× range across accounts, which is geometrically reasonable for a 2.82× power reduction.

The DEC-off sensitivity (F-2) is well-constructed: it correctly captures the power-balance impact through `q_eng` reduction (4.7 → 3.1) rather than trying to modify thermal efficiency directly. The +28% LCOE increase (71.9 → 92.2 $/MWh at native) is plausible and appropriately flagged as a first-order risk.

The two assumptions that would most change the LCOE conclusion:
