### F-1: eta_de near-zero sensitivity contradicts 30% DEC power share
- **Target:** Model — DEC channel wiring in net power calculation
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The DEC efficiency sensitivity table shows only ±0.3 $/MWh LCOE change when η_DEC swings from 0.20 to 0.55 — a factor of 2.75× change in a channel the analysis says handles 30% of fusion power. Correct wiring (P_net = P_fus × (0.70 × η_th + 0.30 × η_de)) would show ~12–14 $/MWh LCOE increase when η_DEC drops from 0.44 to 0.20 (≈16% net output loss, same capital base). The observed +0.3 $/MWh is ~40× smaller. This makes the engineering sensitivity row for eta_dec (+0.0000) consistent with a near-dead wiring path, not a 30% power share. The analysis explicitly flags TRL 1–2 DEC as a major risk and says the 30% DEC contribution represents ~840 MWe at the 10 Hz design point — a claim the model cannot demonstrate. The model comment "non-zero variation confirms eta_de is a live input" is technically true but misleading given the magnitude.
- **Recommendation:** Diagnose and fix the DEC channel wiring. The hybrid net power formula should use `P_net = P_fus × (f_neutron × eta_th + f_dec × eta_de) - P_recirc`; verify f_dec = 0.30 is applied as a multiplier on eta_de, not as a coefficient that already collapses into the eta_th path. After fixing, re-run the eta_de sensitivity sweep and confirm the LCOE shifts by ~10–15 $/MWh across the η_DEC = 0.20 → 0.55 range. If sensitivity remains near-zero, escalate to the framework — the pulsed_thermal_forward pathway may be passing eta_de to a route that is not subtracted from the thermal output.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: C220109 DEC capital baseline internally inconsistent
- **Target:** Model — CAS22 breakdown vs. DEC capital cost scenario table
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The CAS22 breakdown shows C220109 Direct Energy Converter = $72.2M at the baseline LCOE of 48.2 $/MWh. The model notes state "C220109 DEC cost: NOT MODELED — TRULY UNKNOWN; TRL 1–2; no prototype." The DEC Capital Cost Scenarios table then shows "Base case (DEC omitted): 0 M$ → 48.2 $/MWh." This is contradictory: the base LCOE (48.2) is reached at both $72.2M (CAS table) and $0 (scenario table). Either (a) the DEC scenarios layer on top of a $72.2M framework default that is counted as "baseline," in which case "DEC omitted" means "no additional DEC cost beyond default," and the true baseline DEC capital is $72.2M not $0; or (b) the $72.2M entry in the CAS table is mis-labeled and does not represent DEC hardware at all. Both interpretations are consistent with LCOE = 48.2, but they produce different interpretations of the scenario analysis and different OPEX commentary downstream.
- **Recommendation:** Identify the source of the $72.2M C220109 entry. If it comes from a framework default formula (e.g., a $/kWe scaling that isn't DEC-specific), relabel the CAS row to reflect what it actually captures and add a note that explicit DEC cost is overlaid separately in scenarios. If it is genuinely a placeholder for DEC hardware, set it to $0 in the base model (make "not modeled" mean $0 in the CAS output), and restate the scenario table baseline accordingly. Either way, ensure the CAS table and scenario commentary agree on what the base case assumes.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Section 2 misstates gain G as highest-leverage LCOE parameter
- **Target:** Section 2 (narrative framing of gain uncertainty) vs. model sensitivity output and Section 7
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** Section 2 concludes: "Gain uncertainty is therefore the single highest-leverage parameter in the LCOE model." The model's engineering sensitivity table contradicts this: availability has elasticity −0.9574, eta_th −0.2406, and q_eng (the gain proxy) only −0.1953. At the 2800 MWe design point, availability dominates LCOE by a factor of ~5× over gain-driven power output. Section 7 correctly distinguishes these two regimes — gain G determines the viability cliff (whether the plant is economically viable at all), while availability dominates standard LCOE elasticity once above the cliff. Section 2's unqualified claim that gain is "single highest-leverage" collapses this distinction and will mislead a reader trying to understand where to focus sensitivity analysis.
- **Recommendation:** Revise the conclusion of Section 2, Challenge 1 to distinguish the two leverage regimes: gain G is the highest-leverage parameter for viability (below the cliff, a 2× gain reduction can make the concept non-viable), while availability is the highest-leverage parameter for LCOE optimization at the design point. The Section 7 formulation — "viability boundary vs. LCOE elasticity" — is the correct framing; Section 2 should reference or mirror it rather than contradicting it.
- **Priority:** important
