VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: No modeling approach recommendation and no testable hypotheses
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges) — Goal 4 (Modeling Approach)
- **Finding:** The analysis never states whether 1costingfe or free-form modeling is appropriate for this concept, nor why. More significantly, the analysis identifies the correct economic master lever (magnetic energy recovery efficiency, Section 2.2) but stops short of framing any key hypotheses as testable propositions. The closest it gets is the RLC circuit inequality in Section 2.2, but this is presented as explanatory narrative, not as a sensitivity-testable claim. There are no statements of the form "H1: If η_recovery is X%, then LCOE is Y c/kWh; if it drops to X-10%, LCOE becomes Z c/kWh." The Section 5 parameter table has the inputs but the analysis never connects them to a modeling strategy.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short modeling approach paragraph to Section 2 (or a dedicated Section 2.8) that: (1) states whether 1costingfe or free-form modeling is appropriate and why (this concept has no conventional thermal cycle, making free-form likely necessary); (2) identifies the 2-3 parameters with highest LCOE sensitivity (η_recovery and rep rate are clearly the top two — state this explicitly); (3) reframes at least the top 2-3 economic challenges as testable propositions, e.g., "H1: Net electricity is achievable at Q~1.2 if and only if η_recovery ≥ 0.95 — a 5pp drop to 0.90 eliminates net output at the ARPA-E design point."
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: CAS-level cost structure not mapped
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and Section 5 (Parameters) — Goal 3 (TEA Implications)
- **Finding:** The analysis discusses cost advantages and penalties thoroughly in prose (no REBCO, no steam cycle, capacitor bank as uncertain cost, He3 breeding as cost risk), but never maps these to CAS accounts. The checklist requires "CAS-level cost structure differences from the reference concept." The Section 7 differentiator table lists architectural differences but not their CAS implications — e.g., which accounts are eliminated (no breeding blanket → CAS22X absent), which are novel and need free-form treatment (capacitor bank + pulsed coils → CAS22 substitute), and which carry the dominant uncertainty. Without this, a modeler reading the analysis cannot derive a CAS skeleton for Orion.
- **Recommendation:** Add a CAS mapping column to the Section 7 comparison table, or add a short paragraph after it, identifying: (a) which standard CAS accounts are eliminated (breeding blanket, steam generator/turbines, cryogenic plant); (b) which require novel treatment (capacitor bank as primary energy storage and conversion hardware — no CAS analogue); (c) which are retained approximately (balance of plant for 5% neutron thermal capture, site costs, O&M structure). This does not require numerical values — shape is sufficient.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Technical bets lack failure-consequence framing
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges) — Goal 5 (Risks and Assumptions)
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies six major technical bets (Q achievement, D-He3 temperature threshold, rep rate scaling, 40 T magnets, He3 self-breeding, direct conversion efficiency at scale) with solid descriptive detail, but does not state what happens to the TEA if any individual bet fails. For example: if D-He3 operation cannot be achieved (temperature gap not bridged), what is the fallback? The concept would revert to D-T, eliminating the primary cost advantage (no breeding blanket) and restoring the neutron problem — but this is never stated. Similarly, if He3 self-breeding fails, the concept has no viable fuel path at commercial scale, making the LCOE model undefined. The Section 6 gap table marks items as "blocking" but blocking for data collection, not for the concept's commercial viability.
- **Recommendation:** For each of the top 3-4 technical bets, add a one-sentence failure consequence: "If X fails, then Y (TEA implication)." These can be added inline in Section 2 after the risk description, or collected in a brief "failure modes" table. The key distinction is concept-specific failures (e.g., D-He3 temperature gap) from shared fusion assumptions (e.g., regulatory costs) — flag which is which so the model can scope sensitivity correctly.
- **Priority:** important
