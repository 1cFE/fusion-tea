VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Concept family not classified; nearest neighbors limited to one comparison
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and overall framing
- **Finding:** The analysis never places this concept in the study taxonomy. Helion's FRC with pulsed compression is not straightforwardly MFE or IFE — it is often classified as MIF (Magneto-Inertial Fusion) or MTF (Magnetized Target Fusion), a hybrid category that matters for cross-concept comparison. This distinction is never stated. Goal 1 asks what family the concept belongs to and who the nearest neighbors are. Section 7 names only one reference concept (ST-HTS), chosen for architectural contrast. The nearest pulsed-compression and FRC neighbors (General Fusion's MTF, TAE Technologies' FRC, and MagLIF as a pulsed liner analog) appear at most in passing; none are compared systematically. A reader cannot tell where Helion sits in the study's comparative framework.
- **Recommendation:** Add a concept family statement at the top of Section 7 (or Section 2 preamble) that classifies this concept in the MFE/IFE/MIF taxonomy and explains why the classification is non-obvious. Add a row or short paragraph naming the 2-3 nearest-neighbor concepts (by confinement family and energy conversion approach) and noting the key differentiator from each — even a sentence per neighbor is enough. The ST-HTS comparison can remain as the structural contrast; the neighbors should complement it.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: H2 cost viability anchors absent from Section 5 parameter table
- **Target:** Section 5 (LCOE-Relevant Parameters) and Section 2, hypothesis H2
- **Finding:** Section 2 H2 identifies two quantified cost anchors for the capacitor bank: $5/J as the current pulse power industry baseline and $0.50/J as the viability threshold below which LCOE can reach 50 c/kWh. These are the most actionable cost figures in the analysis and directly drive a testable hypothesis. Neither appears in the Section 5 parameter table — not in the Available Parameters (even at low confidence with a derived basis), and not explicitly in the Missing Parameters table (which lists "Capacitor bank capital cost ($/J or $/MWe)" as a blocking gap without the viability range). Goal 3 requires each cost-relevant differentiator to have stated cost implications captured in Section 5. A parameter that anchors a blocking hypothesis should be in the table.
- **Recommendation:** Add two rows to the Section 5 Available Parameters table: (1) Capacitor bank cost — pulse power baseline: ~$5/J, source: H2 derivation from pulsed power literature, confidence: low; (2) Capacitor bank cost — LCOE viability threshold: ~$0.50/J at the 50 MW / 2 Hz design point, source: inferred from H2 RLC balance, confidence: low. Both should note they are inferred estimates, not published figures. This brings H2's hypothesis inputs into Section 5 where they belong.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: D-T fallback scenario recommended but not structured for the cost model
- **Target:** Section 2.4 and Section 2 Modeling Approach
- **Finding:** Section 2.6 (He3 breeding) explicitly recommends that "the model should include a D-T fallback scenario rather than treating D-He3 as the single modeling path," and H3 quantifies some of the capital cost delta. However, the Modeling Approach subsection (end of Section 2) does not reflect this recommendation — it specifies the free-form model structure only for the D-He3 path, with no indication of how the D-T branch differs structurally or what additional parameters it requires. Goal 4 asks how differences should be modeled; Goal 5 asks how to capture risks as sensitivity parameters, scenario branches, or flags. The D-T fallback is the most consequential scenario branch for this concept (given D-He3 is TRL 2-3 with no demonstration), but its model implications are left to the reader to infer.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short paragraph in the Modeling Approach subsection specifying that the D-T fallback requires a structurally different model: breeding blanket is reinstated (CAS22), 80% neutron loading applies, direct conversion capture applies only to the ~20% charged-particle fraction, and He3 breeding economics drop out entirely. Note the 3-4 parameters that differ from the D-He3 path (neutron energy fraction, blanket capital cost, tritium supply rate, direct conversion yield). This does not require full modeling — just enough structure that a future modeler knows what to build for the D-T branch.
- **Priority:** minor
