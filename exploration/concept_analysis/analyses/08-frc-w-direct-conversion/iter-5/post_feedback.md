### F-1: He3 fuel cost (CAS80) is anomalously large — likely a unit or scaling bug
- **Target:** Model CAS80 Fuel line and model_setup.py He3 consumption calculation
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** CAS80 Fuel is $586.8M/yr annualized, which implies ~293 kg of He3 consumed per year at the stated $2M/kg unit cost. A 1000 MWe D-He3 plant with ~180 MW fusion power and D-He3 yield of 18.3 MeV per reaction requires on the order of tens of grams of He3 per year, not hundreds of kilograms — a discrepancy of roughly 3–4 orders of magnitude. At $586.8M/yr, fuel alone contributes ~$79/MWh to the $127.6/MWh LCOE (62% of total), directly contradicting the analysis's framing of D-He3 as a low fuel-cost concept. The analysis (Sections 2.4 and 4) explicitly identifies reduced fuel cost as a structural advantage of D-He3. The model inverts this: fuel becomes the second-largest LCOE driver after CAS22 capital. A unit mismatch is the most likely explanation — check whether `u_he3` in `costing_constants.yaml` is applied in $/kg but the consumption calculation returns grams, or vice versa. Fix the unit handling and rerun; the corrected fuel cost should be negligible relative to capital.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: eta_pin dominates sensitivity at +16 elasticity while eta_dec (the stated #1 lever) is only −0.49
- **Target:** Section 2.8 (Top 2 LCOE sensitivity parameters) and model sensitivity sweep
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis explicitly identifies η_recovery (mapped to `eta_dec`) as the #1 LCOE sensitivity parameter, and rep rate (`f_rep`) as #2. The model sensitivity output contradicts this: `eta_pin` (pulsed power wall-plug efficiency, 95%) has elasticity +16.1, which is 33× larger in absolute value than `eta_dec` at −0.49, and 100× larger than `f_rep` at −0.16. The analysis never mentions `eta_pin` as a significant lever. An elasticity of +16 for a parameter fixed at 95% is anomalous — it suggests the model is near a recirculating power cliff where small changes in driver efficiency have outsized LCOE impact, but this behavior is not described or anticipated in the analysis. The discrepancy could have two causes: (a) the model's `eta_pin` enters the recirculating power formula with unintended leverage at the current operating point (Q_sci = 0.5, 33% recirculating fraction), making the model near a breakeven the analysis didn't identify; or (b) a structural error in how `eta_pin` is applied in the DEC recirculating power calculation. Investigate which lever actually dominates the model, confirm whether the +16 elasticity is physically correct, and update Section 2.8 to reflect what the model actually shows as the top sensitivity parameters.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-3: C220109 Direct Energy Converter ($1,795M, 72% of CAS22) is unacknowledged as a major cost driver
- **Target:** Section 2.8 (Modeling Approach) and Section 7 (CAS Account Mapping)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis frames "the capacitor bank + pulsed coil system" as the primary capital item (Section 2.8), and Section 4 extensively discusses the cap bank cost uncertainty ($5/J today vs. $0.50/J NOAK target). The model contradicts this: C220109 (Direct Energy Converter) is $1,795M — 72% of CAS22 and ~44% of total overnight capital — while the cap bank (C220104, $200M) + coils (C220103, $100M) together are only $300M (12% of CAS22), 6× smaller than the DEC. The Section 7 CAS mapping notes the DEC as "Novel" but gives it no cost estimate. The DEC parametric cost of $89.8M per 50 MWe module ($1.8/W of power electronics) may be reasonable but its derivation is never explained. If the DEC cost is produced by the framework's parametric formula, the analysis should explain what drives it to $90M/module and whether that is a major uncertainty comparable to the cap bank. If it is a hardcoded placeholder, that needs to be documented. Either way, the analysis's characterization of cap bank + coils as "the primary capital item" is inconsistent with the model, and the Section 7 CAS mapping should flag the DEC as a major uncharacterized cost driver.
- **Priority:** important
