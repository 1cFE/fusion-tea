VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Nearest-neighbor concepts not explicitly named as positioning anchors
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and overall concept framing
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies the concept family (MFE — Stellarator (QI)) and compares to exactly one reference concept (01-hts-compact-tokamak / CFS ARC). The Helios QI stellarator (Thea Energy) is used extensively as a data analogue throughout but is never framed as a nearest-neighbor concept — it appears only as a source of proxy parameters, not as a positioning reference. No within-family comparison is structured. The checklist criterion (Goal 1) requires naming 2-3 nearest-neighbor concepts; the analysis provides one explicit comparator and an implicit second that is never framed as such.
- **Recommendation:** Add a 3-5 sentence positioning statement (either as a preamble to Section 7 or a standalone subsection) that explicitly names the 2-3 nearest neighbors: (1) Helios / Thea Energy as the nearest within-family neighbor — another private-sector QI stellarator commercial plant study using the same optimization approach — and (2) the EUROfusion HELIAS concept or W7-X → DEMO path as the large-device public-sector comparator. For each, state in one sentence what the key similarity and key difference is relative to Stellaris.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: H2 (capacity factor advantage) is not testable as stated, and the ranked top-3 LCOE sensitivity list is incomplete
- **Target:** Section 2 (Recommended Modeling Approach and Key Hypotheses)
- **Finding:** The modeling approach correctly identifies the coil cost multiplier as "the primary sensitivity axis" (Goal 4). H2 names capacity factor as the second LCOE lever, framing it as a trade against the low-beta scale penalty. However, H2 is not testable as written: "sufficient to offset" is not a threshold. No capacity factor range is given — the "e.g., 90%+" figure is an un-sourced illustrative value, not a plausible range anchored in any stellarator operational data (e.g., W7-X availability, HELIAS design targets). Without a range, neither the magnitude of this LCOE lever nor the breakeven condition can be assessed. Additionally, the modeling approach names two sensitivity parameters (coil cost, capacity factor) but not a third — the low-beta machine scale penalty (plasma volume drives CAPEX, linked to Challenge 2) is identified as a cost issue but not surfaced as a ranked sensitivity parameter with a plausible range.
- **Recommendation:** Revise H2 to include a testable threshold: state a specific capacity factor advantage (e.g., ≥3–5 percentage points over the tokamak reference) that would offset the beta-driven scale penalty at a given coil cost multiplier, and identify the source or reasoning behind the proposed advantage range. Then add a third named sensitivity parameter to the modeling approach — plasma volume or major radius (driven by beta = 2.76% relative to compact tokamak ~5–8%) — with a rough estimation approach (e.g., from the Helios analogue or W7-X scaling to 2.7 GW).
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Burning plasma assumption is central to the H&CD cost advantage but is not stated as a technical bet with a failure mode
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 4 and Key Hypotheses) and Section 7 (CAS22 H&CD delta)
- **Finding:** The CAS-Level Cost Delta table assigns a "-50% to -80%" cost advantage to the H&CD account on the basis that ECRH ignited-phase power is ~1 MW (Helios analogue). This large negative delta is the main partial offset to the coil cost premium. But this advantage depends entirely on the assumption that Stellaris achieves ignition (alpha self-heating dominates, external heating drops to ~1 MW steady-state). The analysis notes "burning plasma inferred from 2.7 GW fusion target" as medium confidence (Section 1), but never frames the ignition assumption as a technical bet with a stated failure mode for LCOE purposes. No Q>1 stellarator has been demonstrated. If Stellaris requires sustained external heating at tens of MW (rather than ~1 MW post-ignition), the H&CD cost advantage largely disappears — and the case for competitiveness against HTS tokamaks weakens further. This is an assumption unique to the cost advantage framing (Goal 5) that is not captured in H1–H3.
- **Recommendation:** Add a fourth hypothesis (H4): state the ignition assumption explicitly as a testable bet — e.g., "Stellaris achieves alpha self-heating such that steady-state ECRH requirement is ≤~5 MW. If false, the H&CD account reverts to a cost penalty rather than an advantage, and the net directional assessment in Section 7 changes sign on the H&CD row." This does not require resolving the physics — it requires framing the risk so the cost model can branch on it.
- **Priority:** important
