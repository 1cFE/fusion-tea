### F-1: DEFAULT base scenario stacks two simultaneous lower bounds without a comparison-ready combined estimate
- **Target:** Model output (coil multiplier sweep / O&M multiplier sweep) and Section 2 (Recommended Modeling Approach)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The DEFAULT scenario uses the wound-coil C220103 calibration (acknowledged lower bound for coil costs) AND the framework CAS70 default (acknowledged lower bound for O&M). Both are individually flagged as lower bounds with separate sweeps, but no scenario combines a non-trivial coil multiplier with an O&M uplift into a single "comparison-ready" central estimate. The analysis's own H1 framing labels 1.5× coil multiplier as "optimistic" — meaning the DEFAULT is below even the optimistic case on the primary uncertainty axis. A reader using the DEFAULT replacement-inclusive LCOE (110.8 $/MWh) for cross-concept comparison is benchmarking against two simultaneously optimistic assumptions. At 1.5× coil + 1.5× O&M, the combined replacement-inclusive LCOE can be computed analytically from the existing sweeps (~129 $/MWh), but this combined figure is not surfaced anywhere in the model output.
- **Recommendation:** Add a combined "comparison-ready" scenario to the model output — e.g., 1.5× coil multiplier + 1.5× O&M — with its replacement-inclusive LCOE computed and labeled as the cross-concept comparison reference. Call it out explicitly as distinct from the lower-bound DEFAULT. The individual sweeps are valuable for bracketing; the combined scenario is what a cross-concept comparison table should cite.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Section 7 net directional assessment leaves competitiveness question as "uncertain" without conditional thresholds
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes, net directional assessment row)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The net directional assessment row correctly notes that competitiveness "depends entirely on whether the 3D coil manufacturing premium is more than offset by the H&CD saving + CF advantage." But the modeled LCOE figures are never converted into conditional competitiveness propositions. The analysis has all the pieces: replacement-inclusive LCOE by scenario (110.8 at DEFAULT, 117.6 at 1.5×, 131.1 at 2.5×, 165.0 at 5×), plus the O&M multiplier sweep, plus the H2 caveat that the CF advantage may be only 0–1 percentage points. These can be combined into statements of the form: "Stellaris is competitive on LCOE only if the HTS compact tokamak reference exceeds [X] $/MWh at [coil scenario, O&M scenario]." Without this framing, the "uncertain" verdict cannot be updated as reference concept data becomes available. Goal 3 requires stating the cost implication — "uncertain" is a placeholder, not a TEA conclusion.
- **Recommendation:** Add a breakeven table or paragraph to the Section 7 net directional assessment. For each of three modeled scenarios (DEFAULT, 1.5× coil + DEFAULT O&M, 1.5× coil + 1.5× O&M), state the replacement-inclusive LCOE and the implied ceiling on the HTS compact tokamak reference LCOE below which Stellaris is uncompetitive on LCOE alone. Frame the H2 CF advantage caveat quantitatively: "Even a 1 pp CF advantage over the HTS tokamak reference at DEFAULT coil cost reduces LCOE by [X] $/MWh — insufficient to offset the 1.5× coil premium of [Y] $/MWh at replacement-inclusive cost." This converts the "uncertain" row into conditional propositions the reader can evaluate when the 01 analysis is complete.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Coil replacement comparison basis assumes asymmetry that may not hold
- **Target:** Section 7 (net directional assessment row, final footnote) and model output Key Assumptions #9
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis states "this cost [coil replacement] is not shared by the compact HTS tokamak reference, making the raw $106/MWh figure a stellarator-specific lower bound." ARC-class REBCO tokamak magnets face the same neutron fluence constraint (REBCO fluence limit ~3×10²² m⁻²). Whether the compact HTS tokamak reference avoids coil replacement over a 30-year plant life depends on its fusion power density and neutron wall load — which are higher per unit machine volume than Stellaris due to compactness. If the HTS compact tokamak reference also requires magnet replacement (at potentially shorter intervals given higher neutron flux per coil volume), the replacement-inclusive comparison basis changes and Stellaris's $4.5–22/MWh replacement penalty may be partly or fully matched by the reference. The claim of asymmetry is plausible — larger machine volume may shield coils better — but it is an assumption, not a verified finding.
- **Recommendation:** Add a note in Section 7 (near the coil replacement footnote) and Key Assumptions #9 clarifying the basis for claiming asymmetry: specifically, that the compact HTS tokamak reference's coil fluence per FPY is expected to be lower than Stellaris's (due to more shielding mass per coil volume at larger R0) OR that its lifetime exceeds 30 years before the fluence limit is reached. If this cannot be verified from available sources, soften the claim to "likely asymmetric, pending confirmation from the 01 analysis."
- **Priority:** minor
