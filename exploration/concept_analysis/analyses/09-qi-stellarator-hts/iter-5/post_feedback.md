### F-1: Magnet replacement lifecycle cost unmodeled — LCOE is a systematic lower bound
- **Target:** Model output (Key Assumption #9) and Section 7 cross-concept comparison
- **Finding:** The model explicitly flags that magnet replacement lifecycle cost is "NOT modeled" and identifies it as "a deferred CAPEX component unique among the concepts analyzed." However, the LCOE of $106/MWh is presented without a quantitative estimate of this gap or a flagged adjustment to the cross-concept comparison. With REBCO fluence limited to ~10 full-power years at 2.7 GW, at least two full coil replacements are required in a 30-year plant life. At the C220103 lower bound ($516M), two replacements add ~$1B in deferred capital — roughly $33M/yr annualized, or ~$4/MWh at 88% CF. At the optimistic 1.5× coil multiplier ($774M/replacement), the impact is ~$6/MWh; at 5× it exceeds $20/MWh. This cost is not shared by the compact HTS tokamak reference (which has no equivalent coil fluence replacement schedule on the same timeline), making its omission directly bias the Section 7 directional comparison. The current LCOE is a lower bound with a stellarator-specific unmodeled term that, at the optimistic coil cost scenario, is material relative to the full $106/MWh figure.
- **Recommendation:** Add a magnet replacement scenario to the model output: compute annualized coil replacement cost as a function of the coil multiplier (two replacements over 30 years, each at the same C220103 unit cost as the initial build) and incorporate it into a third LCOE row. Flag the base $106/MWh as "excluding periodic magnet replacement" and provide the replacement-inclusive LCOE for the 1.5× and 2.5× coil multiplier cases. This turns the currently unquantified gap into a bounded scenario and makes the cross-concept comparison in Section 7 valid.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: CAS account labels in Section 7 don't map to the model's actual CAS structure
- **Target:** Section 7 (CAS-Level Cost Delta table) and model output
- **Finding:** Section 7's cost delta table uses CAS21 = "Magnet System" and CAS22 = "Heating & Current Drive" — but the model output uses CAS21 = Buildings ($930M) and CAS22 = Reactor Plant Equipment ($3,142M, containing all nuclear island sub-accounts including C220103 coils and C220104 heating). The model's Key Assumption #10 tries to bridge both conventions in a single list, citing "CAS21 (magnet system)" then switching to "C220104 (H&CD)" — mixing the Section 7 narrative convention with the model's actual sub-account structure in the same paragraph. A reader using Section 7 as a guide for where to apply coil cost multipliers would target the wrong account (model CAS21 = Buildings, not magnets). The magnet cost lives at C220103 under CAS22 in the model.
- **Recommendation:** Revise the Section 7 table to use the model's actual account identifiers: replace "CAS21: Magnet System" with "C220103 (under CAS22): Coils" and "CAS22: H&CD" with "C220104 (under CAS22): Heating System." Add a one-sentence note explaining that the model uses the standard ARIES/fusion CAS20X convention (CAS22 = Reactor Plant Equipment as a super-account) rather than PROCESS-style per-subsystem numbering. This makes Section 7 directly usable for model parameter targeting.
- **Priority:** important
