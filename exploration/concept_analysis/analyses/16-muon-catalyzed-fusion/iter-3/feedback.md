### F-1: 47% recirculating power fraction claim not closed as testable hypotheses
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 1 / Critical Risk Framing)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly identifies that Acceleron's 47% recirculating power fraction claim is inconsistent with the energy-sink result at stated parameters (E_mu=2.5 GeV, N_fus=200), and proposes three possible explanations: undisclosed conversion efficiency gains, more favorable physics, or heat sales revenue. But these are listed as alternatives without ranking or closing. The mathematics makes the ranking deterministic: at E_mu=2.5 GeV, N_fus=200, η_th=50%, M=1.10 → Q_sci=1.41, gross/driver=0.78 → P_gross < P_beam, so ε > 100% by definition. Achieving ε=47% with net-positive electricity would require η_th > 86% (impossible for any thermal cycle) or N_fus ≥ 550 at 2.5 GeV. The only mathematically viable reconciliation is that Acceleron's "recirculating fraction" denominator includes heat output, not just electrical output — which is consistent with their LCOE model explicitly including heat sales revenue. The analysis identifies the right candidates but does not state which one is the only physically possible path, leaving the 47% claim as an unexplained anomaly rather than a resolved risk.
- **Recommendation:** Update the Critical Risk Framing paragraph to close the reconciliation: "Of the three reconciliation hypotheses, only one is mathematically consistent with the energy balance: Acceleron's 47% figure must use thermal output (not electrical output) as the denominator, which is coherent because the plant does produce net thermal energy at Q_sci=1.41 (thermal gain = 1.55× beam power). The 47% recirculating fraction is physically meaningful as P_beam / P_thermal_gross, not P_beam / P_electrical_gross. This means Acceleron's LCOE model is a heat-product model, not a pure-electricity model, and the $0.025/kWh target cannot be evaluated without knowing the assumed heat price." This frames the discrepancy as a specific model assumption gap (Goal 5) rather than a vague inconsistency.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Sensitivity model sweeps N_fus independently despite analysis recommending T-φ coupling
- **Target:** Model sensitivity sweeps — model_setup.py
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** Section 2 (Modeling Approach) explicitly states: "the model should treat N_fus as a function of T and φ rather than a free parameter independent of operating conditions." The analysis identifies that N_fus and η_th are jointly determined by operating temperature — increasing T simultaneously improves both, and Acceleron's 800–1300 K operating range is in the frontier where N_fus is projected to exceed cold-target baselines. But the sensitivity model sweeps N_fus from 100 to 400 and η_th from 0.35 to 0.60 as orthogonal independent parameters, implying scenarios like N_fus=400 at η_th=0.35 (high N_fus at low temperature) that are physically incoherent. The key engineering hypothesis — that Acceleron's high-temperature operating approach provides a joint lever on both parameters — is never tested in the model, despite being identified as a core differentiator in the analysis.
- **Recommendation:** Add a coupled temperature scenario to the sensitivity table, parameterizing N_fus(T) with a two-point linear interpolation anchored at (T=500K, N_fus=150) from the LAMPF result and (T=1200K, N_fus=250) from the Yamashita et al. kinetics projection, with η_th(T) from Brayton efficiency at the same temperature. Show at least three operating points: cold-target baseline (~500K: N_fus≈150, η_th≈35%), Acceleron low-end (~800K: N_fus≈180, η_th≈42%), and Acceleron target (~1200K: N_fus≈250, η_th≈50%). Label this "Temperature-coupled scenario." This is the only sweep that directly tests the concept's specific claimed differentiator and connects the operating temperature to both physics and BOP performance simultaneously.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: No heat-sales scenario despite Acceleron's LCOE model depending on it
- **Target:** Model scenario comparison table
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** Acceleron's $0.025/kWh LCOE target explicitly includes revenue from heat sales as a contributor (flagged in Section 5 parameter table notes and Section 2 Critical Risk Framing). The scenario comparison table contains five electricity-only scenarios, of which three are SINK and two show LCOE of 208.7 and 24.1 ¢/kWh. At E_mu=2.5 GeV (Acceleron's stated target), the plant is a net electricity sink but produces net thermal energy: Q_sci=1.41, M=1.10 → thermal gain 1.55× beam power, meaning 55 MW net thermal per 100 MW beam. At 500–1000°C operating temperature, this heat has market value for industrial process heat or district heating. The model never tests whether heat sales revenue can make the concept commercially viable even at net-negative electricity — which is the only scenario in which Acceleron's $0.025/kWh target is possibly achievable at stated physics parameters. Without this scenario, the model cannot evaluate Acceleron's actual commercial case.
- **Recommendation:** Add a heat-revenue scenario to the scenario comparison table. At E_mu=2.5 GeV, N_fus=200: compute net thermal output (positive), apply heat credit at two price points ($20/MWh_th conservative and $50/MWh_th optimistic for industrial process heat), and report an effective blended LCOE accounting for heat revenue offsetting the electrical deficit. If even the optimistic heat-sales scenario does not approach $0.10/kWh, state this explicitly as a model-supported conclusion: Acceleron's stated physics parameters cannot reach their cost target even with full heat monetization at realistic prices. This closes the analysis's open framing ("or on substantial heat sales revenue offsetting the electrical deficit") with a quantitative answer.
- **Priority:** important
