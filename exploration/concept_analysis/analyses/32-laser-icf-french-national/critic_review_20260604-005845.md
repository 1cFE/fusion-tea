### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The contract check confirmed the call shape is structurally legal (`run_native_and_1gw` helper form). The two-knob projection is trivial here: P_native = 1000 MWe, so the 1 GWe NOAK projection is an identity (no scaling, same spec, overrides apply identically). The `model_output.txt` shows `generic = native = result_1gw` for all accounts—LCOEs all $88.9/MWh, overnight all $6511/kW, every CAS and sub-account identical across the three columns. This is expected when P_native = projection target.

The judgment question is: do the per-account `result_1gw` values look implausible for any architectural reason specific to GenF? The sanity check already flagged five structural accounts as 3× median comparables, which is the primary implausibility signal. Beyond that:

- **C220104 (laser) = $567.5M/GWe**: For a 3 MJ (assumed), 10 Hz laser, this is $567.5M / (3 MJ × 10 Hz) = $18,917 per Joule delivered per second... wait, that's not the right unit. Let me recalculate: laser capital cost per shot is tied to total laser energy, not per-second power. If the laser is 3 MJ/shot, then $567.5M / 3 MJ ≈ $189/J. This is within the range of published IFE laser cost estimates (Xcimer KrF: $100-120/J FOAK, $60-80/J NOAK; Inertia claims $700-1000/J for DPSSL, likely inflated). $189/J for DPSSL at 10 Hz, MJ-class is plausible if the library assumes moderate DPSSL cost (midpoint between Xcimer's cheap KrF and Inertia's expensive DPSSL claim). However, the analysis never validates this—Section 5b says "library's default laser cost formula (per archetype) is unknown to me." Without knowing whether the library is using $100/J, $200/J, or $500/J, we can't say if $567.5M is too high or too low for GenF.

- **C220108 (target factory) = $298.4M/GWe**: At 10 Hz, 90% availability, this is 86,400 targets/day × 365 days = 31,536,000 targets/year. Amortizing $298.4M over 30 years (standard plant life) = $9.95M/year capital recovery (simplified, ignoring interest). This is $0.32/target in capital amortization alone, which seems low if target consumable cost (CAS80) must also be <$1-3/target for viability (per analysis Section 4). But CAS80 = $0.7M/year in the model output, which is $0.02/target—way below the Inertia $1/target goal or the Goodin et al. <$1-3 threshold. This suggests the library is either assuming very cheap targets (consumable cost $0.02/target) or the CAS80 accounting is incomplete (maybe target consumables are buried elsewhere, or deuterium/tritium procurement dominates CAS80 and capsule shells are in C220108). This isn't obviously *wrong*, but it's opaque and inconsistent with the analysis's discussion in Section 4 ("target cost must be <$1-3/target to be economically viable").

- **C220101 (first wall/blanket) = $700.8M/GWe**: This is 23.7% of CAS22, the single largest sub-account. For a liquid-lithium blanket in an 8 m radius chamber, this could reflect large lithium inventory cost (hundreds of tonnes at $15k-300k/tonne for enriched Li-6, per analysis Section 4), plus pumping infrastructure, plus corrosion-resistant containment. Or it could be library over-estimation. The analysis doesn't break this down, so we can't judge plausibility from first principles. But 3.47× comparables (sanity flag) suggests this is either architecturally distinct (GenF's liquid-Li is more expensive than comparables' dry walls or FLiBe) or library error.

None of these are *obviously* implausible in the sense of "violates physics" or "off by orders of magnitude," but the opacity (library formulas unknown, no GenF validation) and the 3× structural cost divergence (sanity flags) mean the projection is untrustworthy even though it's arithmetically legal.

### Family delta vs comparables

The sanity check flagged five accounts as outliers high (2.9-3.5× median of comparables). Analysis Section 7 provides detailed qualitative comparison to the five comparables (17b fast ignition, 26 indirect drive, 30 NIF commercialization, 31 OEC architecture, 17a hybrid drive) but focuses *exclusively* on laser and target cost deltas—coupling efficiency (direct drive 40-60% vs indirect 10-15%), laser energy (GenF 3 MJ vs Inertia 10 MJ), target complexity (spherical capsule vs cone-in-shell vs hohlraum), gain uncertainty (GenF's simulated G=120 vs NIF-validated G=1.5-3). Section 7 concludes "GenF's central trade is direct-drive coupling efficiency vs indirect-drive symmetry robustness" and ranks concepts by laser energy/cost, with GenF "middle of the pack."

However, the sanity-flagged accounts (C220101 blanket, C220102 shield, C220105 structure, C220106 vacuum) are *not* laser or target—they're chamber/vessel/blanket systems. Section 7 never explains why GenF's blanket costs 3.47× comparables' blankets, or why the radiation shield is 2.91× median. The analysis mentions in passing (Section 2, Section 5) that GenF uses "liquid lithium blankets" and "chamber radius ~8 m," and Section 4 discusses lithium inventory cost as a potential CAS27 driver (special materials), but there's no connection drawn to the flagged accounts.

Possible architectural explanations (not stated in analysis):

1. **Liquid lithium vs alternatives**: If comparables use dry walls (tungsten first wall, no flowing blanket) or FLiBe blankets (lithium fluoride-beryllium fluoride molten salt), GenF's pure liquid lithium may require different (more expensive?) pumping, containment, or corrosion-resistant materials. Liquid lithium is chemically aggressive (reacts with air, water, many alloys), requiring inert-atmosphere handling and corrosion-resistant steel (e.g., V-Cr-Ti alloys, or thick stainless with coatings). This could drive up C220101 (blanket) and C220105 (primary structure).

2. **8 m chamber radius vs comparables**: The analysis states GenF's chamber is ~8 m radius (Ribeyre §III). Are comparables smaller? Xcimer uses 1 GJ/shot at 0.25-1 Hz (much higher yield per shot), which might allow smaller chamber (less frequent neutron pulses, easier clearing). Inertia's chamber size is not stated in the exemplar. If GenF's 8 m is large relative to comparables' 5-6 m (or smaller for high-yield/low-rep-rate designs), the larger radius → more surface area → more blanket volume → more lithium inventory and structural steel. This could explain 3× blanket cost.

3. **Library modeling artifact**: If the library's LASER_IFE archetype was tuned on Xcimer-like hybrid-drive or Inertia-like indirect-drive designs, and GenF's liquid-lithium direct-drive at 8 m radius is an outlier within the archetype, the library's blanket/shield/structure scaling might be mis-calibrated. The comparables are all laser IFE, so they should share the archetype, but sub-architectural differences (blanket chemistry, chamber size, pulsed neutron flux vs continuous) could cause scatter.

None of these explanations appear in Section 7. The section evaluates "cost-relevant differences" for each comparable but lists only laser/target items, not chamber/blanket. For example, "vs 26. Laser ICF Indirect Drive (Inertia)" discusses coupling efficiency, laser $/J, target complexity, and gain—but not chamber size or blanket type. The summary ("GenF has major potential advantage in laser cost... contingent on direct-drive gain validation") is about C220104 (laser), not the flagged structural accounts.

**Conclusion**: The flagged divergences are *not* explained by architecture in the analysis. Either (a) the divergences are real and architectural (liquid-Li blanket is genuinely 3× more expensive than dry-wall or FLiBe blankets, or 8 m chamber is large for 10 Hz IFE), in which case the analysis should articulate this in Section 7 or a new subsection, or (b) the divergences are library modeling artifacts (comparables are structurally similar to GenF, and the 3× scatter is noise or calibration error), in which case the analysis should flag this as "unexplained—requires library source inspection." The current analysis is silent, which is a gap.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

Analysis Section 6 (data gap inventory) lists 12 gaps and ranks three as "blocking" (TBR tritium breeding, laser architecture, target gain validation). The summary says "blocking gaps cannot be filled from public GenF sources" and recommends using Ribeyre reference case as baseline with wide sensitivity analysis. This correctly identifies the two most load-bearing assumptions:

1. **Target gain G=120 vs actual G=50-80**: If GenF's real gain is 2× lower than Ribeyre's simulated G=120 (due to LPI effects not included in sims, or instability quenching), required laser energy doubles from 3 MJ to 6 MJ to maintain fusion power, doubling laser capital cost (C220104). Since C220104 = $567.5M/GWe in the model output (19.2% of CAS22, 8.7% of total overnight), doubling this adds ~$570M/GWe, increasing total overnight from $6511/kW to ~$7100/kW (+9%), increasing LCOE from $88.9/MWh to ~$97/MWh (+9%). This is first-order linear (no compounding effects from larger laser → larger building, etc., which would amplify). This is a major swing, and the analysis correctly flags it (Section 2: "±50% gain uncertainty → ±100% laser energy uncertainty → ±50% laser CAPEX uncertainty").

2. **Tritium breeding TBR>1.0**: If breeding is not demonstrated, the concept cannot operate (global tritium supply <2 kg/year vs 115 kg/year consumption, per Section 4). This is existential, not just a cost driver—the LCOE is meaningless if the reactor can't run. The analysis correctly labels this "blocking" (Section 6, Gap #1: "Without TBR>1, fuel cycle cannot close and concept is not viable").

The third "blocking" gap (laser architecture—DPSSL vs KrF) affects C220104 via $/J assumptions (Xcimer KrF $100-120/J vs inferred DPSSL $200-500/J), but the library's internal $/J is opaque (the model doesn't surface it). If the library uses $100/J (KrF-like), C220104 would be ~$300M instead of $567.5M, dropping LCOE by ~4%. If the library uses $500/J (expensive DPSSL), C220104 would be ~$1500M, raising LCOE by ~15%. This is significant but smaller than the gain uncertainty.

One load-bearing assumption *not* explicitly called out in Section 6 (though implicit in Gap #6, optics replacement interval): **final optics lifetime at 10 Hz**. Section 2 challenge #6 says "If optics require replacement every 10^4 shots (hours of operation), replacement is frequent and costly. If optics survive 10^6 shots (weeks of operation), replacement cost is manageable." The model output shows CAS70 = $172.6M/year (annualized O&M + scheduled replacement). If optics are $10M/set (wild guess—NIF's final optics are ~$1M each for 192 beamlines, scaled down for fewer beamlines at GenF) and must be replaced every 10^4 shots (27.8 hours at 10 Hz, 90% availability), that's 315 replacements/year, $3150M/year in optics alone—18× the entire CAS70 budget in the model output. This would make the plant uneconomical. If optics survive 10^6 shots (2778 hours, ~4 months), that's 3.15 replacements/year, $31.5M/year, which fits within CAS70. The library must be assuming long optics life (or zero replacement, which is unrealistic for 10 Hz neutron flux). If the assumption is wrong, LCOE could 2-5×.

**Summary**: The two load-bearing assumptions are (1) G=120 achievable (if not, LCOE +9-20%), and (2) TBR>1 achievable (if not, concept is non-viable). Optics lifetime is a third, less-discussed but potentially catastrophic assumption (if 10^4-shot life, LCOE could 2-5×; if 10^6-shot life, manageable). The analysis surfaces (1) and (2) clearly in Sections 2 and 6; (3) is mentioned in Section 2 challenge #6 and Section 6 Gap #6 but not quantified.
