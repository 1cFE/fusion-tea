### F-1: No explicit modeling approach — LCOE sensitivity levers and testable hypotheses absent
- **Target:** Goal 4 (Modeling Approach) — no dedicated section; closest content is scattered across Sections 2 and 5
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies six technical challenges and a detailed parameter table, but never consolidates the top 2–3 parameters with highest LCOE sensitivity for this concept, does not state whether costing should use a structured framework (e.g., 1costingfe) or free-form modeling, and frames all major assumptions as open questions or risks rather than testable propositions. For example, "capsule gain Qc > 200 is extrapolated from NIF via ⅔ power-law" and "laser NOAK cost is $60–80/J" are identified as uncertainties but never reframed as hypotheses with explicit pass/fail criteria — e.g., "If NOAK laser cost exceeds $X/J, LCOE exceeds $Y/MWh and the concept is uneconomical under these plant assumptions." Without this, the analysis cannot drive model design: a reader knows what is uncertain but not what to parameterize, what branches to explore, or how the model should adjudicate the concept's viability.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short Modeling Approach section (or subsection at the end of Section 2) that: (1) names the top 2–3 LCOE sensitivity parameters for Xcimer specifically (likely: laser $/J, Qc at commercial scale, thermal efficiency); (2) states the costing framework choice with rationale — noting which cost accounts must be built from first principles (laser, target factory, FLiBe loop) vs. inherited from prior analyses; and (3) rewrites the central physical and cost assumptions as testable hypotheses with explicit pass/fail criteria, using the format: "If [parameter] achieves [threshold], then [economic outcome]; the model should determine the minimum [parameter] for cost parity with [reference concept]."
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Differentiators from conventional tokamak are not explicitly listed
- **Target:** Goal 2 (Key Differentiators) — Section 7 cross-concept notes
- **Finding:** The checklist requires that differentiators from a conventional tokamak be explicitly listed, not just implied. Section 7 compares Xcimer to indirect-drive IFE (concept 26) with three explicit bullet points and notes two shared supply chain items with concept 01, but there is no structured enumeration of what makes this concept architecturally different from the tokamak reference. Key structural differences — driver-dominant vs. magnet-dominant capex, per-shot consumables (targets, FLiBe makeup) vs. continuous plasma, pulsed vs. steady-state BOP loading, absence of plasma-facing component erosion, no plasma current drive, dramatically lower tritium inventory — are implied throughout but never collected. Cost implications of these differences are not stated. A reader building a TEA model cannot quickly identify which CAS accounts differ from the reference case and in which direction.
- **Recommendation:** Add a differentiator list (5–7 items) that explicitly compares Xcimer HDD to a conventional tokamak across major cost dimensions: driver capital, fuel cycle, plasma-facing components, BOP, availability drivers, and tritium startup inventory. For each, note whether the difference is a cost advantage, penalty, or structurally new cost category. The indirect-drive comparison bullets already present in Section 7 are the right format — apply the same structure to the tokamak comparison.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Key technical bets lack stated failure-mode cost consequences
- **Target:** Goal 5 (Risks) — Section 2, Challenges 3 and 4
- **Finding:** Challenge 2 (Qc extrapolation) states an explicit failure consequence: "if Qc plateaus below 200, the recirculating power fraction rises steeply and wall-plug gain falls below the ~10× threshold required for commercial viability." Challenges 3 and 4 do not. Challenge 3 (two-beam HDD symmetry) is flagged as undemonstrated but the failure consequence is unstated — if two-beam symmetric implosion cannot be achieved, the thick-liquid-wall chamber concept collapses (additional beam penetrations would defeat liquid-wall protection), which is a concept-level kill. Challenge 4 (FLiBe hydraulics and chamber clearing) identifies the ~1-second clearing time as the binding constraint but does not state the economic consequence if clearing time exceeds this threshold (rep rate falls below 0.25 Hz, plant output decreases, LCOE rises). These are the two largest non-laser technical bets, and their failure modes should carry the same explicit economic framing as Challenge 2.
- **Recommendation:** For Challenges 3 and 4, add one sentence each stating the failure consequence at the TEA level: what breaks architecturally or economically, which parameter is affected, and in which direction. The existing Challenge 2 failure-mode sentence is the right model for both.
- **Priority:** important
