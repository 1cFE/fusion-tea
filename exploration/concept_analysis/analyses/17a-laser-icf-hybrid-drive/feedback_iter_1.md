VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: No explicit differentiator list relative to the conventional tokamak baseline
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and overall framing
- **Finding:** The analysis is written from an IFE-centric perspective and never explicitly answers: "compared to the conventional tokamak (the TEA reference baseline), what cost categories change, disappear, or are new for this concept?" (Goal 2). Key structural differences — pulsed vs. steady-state operation, per-shot consumables vs. continuous plasma, driver energy recirculation (Q_eng recirculating power vs. plasma Q), absence of magnet systems, requirement for chamber clearing, target factory as a new cost account — are implied throughout but never collected into an explicit list. Without this, a reader building a TEA model cannot quickly identify which cost accounts differ from the reference case and in which direction.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short differentiator table or bullet list (6–8 items) that explicitly compares Xcimer HDD to a conventional tokamak across the major cost dimensions: driver capital, plasma-facing components, tritium breeding, BOP, fuel cycle, and availability drivers. For each, note whether the difference is an advantage, penalty, or structurally new cost category. This can be placed in Section 7 or as a preamble to Section 2.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: No modeling approach recommendation (1costingfe vs. free-form)
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Approach — currently absent) and Section 5
- **Finding:** The analysis contains no statement about whether standard costing tools (e.g., 1costingfe) are applicable or whether free-form modeling is required, and no rationale is offered either way (Goal 4). This matters here because the laser driver (~60–80% of direct capital) has no analogue in tokamak-based cost frameworks, per-shot consumables create a cost structure without MFE precedent, and the thermal cycle ambiguity (steam vs. He Brayton) affects BOP sizing. The analysis identifies these as challenges but stops short of recommending a modeling path.
- **Recommendation:** Add a modeling approach paragraph — either as a subsection of Section 2 or a standalone Section 2.7 — that states: (a) whether 1costingfe is applicable with modifications or whether a free-form model is required; (b) which cost accounts must be built from first principles (laser system, target factory, FLiBe loop); and (c) which can be inherited from prior analyses (tritium supply chain, BOP, structural materials). Even a preliminary judgment with explicit caveats is sufficient — the goal is to give the cost modeler a starting posture.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-3: Key sensitivities identified as challenges but not formulated as testable hypotheses
- **Target:** Section 2 (all subsections) and Section 5
- **Finding:** Section 2 correctly identifies the critical sensitivities (laser cost, Q_eng, thermal efficiency choice) but frames them as analytical challenges and data gaps rather than testable propositions the cost model must adjudicate (Goal 4). For example, the Q_eng section concludes "their product has compounded variance that makes Q_eng sensitivity analysis the central challenge in the LCOE model" — but does not state a hypothesis: what does the model need to show is true (or false) for the concept to be economically viable? Similarly, the thermal efficiency ambiguity is noted as a 36% output swing but no hypothesis is posed about which scenario leads to what LCOE range. Without testable propositions, the model has no defined pass/fail criteria.
- **Recommendation:** For the 2–3 highest-impact sensitivities (laser wall-plug efficiency, Q_eng product, thermal cycle choice), restate each as an explicit hypothesis: e.g., "Hypothesis: at 7% laser wall-plug efficiency with $80/J NOAK laser cost, LCOE exceeds [threshold]; the model should identify the minimum efficiency required for cost parity with [reference concept]." These can be added as a brief closing paragraph to each relevant Section 2 challenge item, or collected as a "Key Model Hypotheses" block at the end of Section 2.
- **Priority:** important
