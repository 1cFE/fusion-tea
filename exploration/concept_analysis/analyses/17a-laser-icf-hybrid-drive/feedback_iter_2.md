### F-1: IFE-to-IFE economic thesis not explicitly assembled
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and Section 2 (Challenges)
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly identifies coupling efficiency (90% HDD vs. 12% indirect drive) as "the coupling efficiency advantage that defines Xcimer's economic thesis" (Section 7), but never assembles the cost chain that makes this an economic advantage rather than a physics curiosity: coupling advantage → smaller required driver energy per shot for equivalent yield → lower laser capital per MWe → projected LCOE position vs. indirect drive IFE. Section 7 defers the structured IFE-to-IFE comparison until concept 26 is approved, but the economic argument for HDD over indirect drive should be stated independently — even as an order-of-magnitude estimate — so a cost modeler understands what the concept is trying to win on. As written, the analysis describes the physics differentiator and the cost uncertainty, but not the competitive case. This matters for Goal 1 (positioning within IFE family) and Goal 3 (TEA implications of differentiators).
- **Recommendation:** Add a short synthesis paragraph in Section 7 (or at the end of Section 2.7) stating the economic thesis explicitly: if HDD coupling efficiency holds at 90% vs. indirect drive's 12%, the required driver energy per shot is ~7.5× lower for equal yield, which at Xcimer's $/J target implies a laser capital of approximately $X vs. $Y for an equivalent indirect drive plant. State whether this advantage is projected to dominate BOP + target factory + FLiBe loop costs, or whether those costs erode most of the gain. This can be rough — the goal is to make the competitive thesis falsifiable, not precise.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Testable hypotheses leave two major unconstrained sensitivities without economic thresholds
- **Target:** Section 2.7 (Key Model Hypotheses)
- **Finding:** H-1 (laser efficiency × cost), H-2 (Q_eng compounding), and H-3 (thermal cycle selection) are all well-formed testable propositions with specific numerical failure thresholds. But two cost drivers identified earlier in Section 2 as "primary sensitivities" with no MFE analogue receive no corresponding hypothesis: (a) target fabrication cost per shot — Section 2.7 calls this "the unconstrained variable" that "the model must carry as a primary sensitivity," but no H-4 states a cost threshold (e.g., "if target cost exceeds $X/target at Y Hz rep rate, the target factory alone adds >$Z/MWh to LCOE"); (b) capacity factor — correctly labeled blocking in Section 6, but no scenario bracket is offered even as a placeholder. A cost modeler inheriting this analysis has three well-defined sensitivities to test and two open parameters with no economic frame. This is a Goal 4 gap (modeling recommendations) and a partial Goal 5 gap (risk quantification).
- **Recommendation:** Add H-4 for target fabrication: using the Goodin et al. criterion ($1–5/target for economic viability) and the 13.4M targets/year figure already in the analysis, calculate the LCOE contribution at $1, $10, and $100/target. State the threshold above which target factory cost dominates the LCOE delta. For capacity factor, add a scenario bracket (e.g., 0.70 vs. 0.85) with the LCOE sensitivity, even if the CF value itself is unknown — this gives the model a range to propagate rather than an unbounded parameter.
- **Priority:** minor
