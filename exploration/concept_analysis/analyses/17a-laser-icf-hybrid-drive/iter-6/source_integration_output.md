### F-1: Missing analogue O&M and availability anchor from HYLIFE-II
- **Target:** Section 6 (Data Gap Inventory), gap #6 — and Section 5 missing parameters table (O&M row)
- **Finding:** OSTI-biblio-7021072 is an ingested HYLIFE-II source that provides the only published IFE-heritage O&M and plant availability data: 6% of direct cost annually (conservative O&M), 75% plant availability (conservative), with sensitivity showing improvements to 3% O&M and 85% availability reduce LCOE by 1–2¢/kWh. The analysis correctly lists O&M as "truly-unknown, important" and states "Not published for any IFE concept; must be estimated from analogue MFE O&M data." HYLIFE-II is an IFE concept — a closer analogue than MFE data — and this source is now directly ingested. The gap classification and recommendation should be updated accordingly. Note: HYLIFE-II uses a heavy-ion driver ($570M direct), not a laser, so these figures apply to the non-driver plant systems only; the driver O&M component remains unknown.
- **Recommendation:** Update Section 6 gap #6 from "truly-unknown" to "not-yet-sourced" and add OSTI-7021072 as the analogue anchor. Add a footnote to the Section 5 O&M row noting the HYLIFE-II figures (6% direct cost / 75% availability) as an IFE heritage baseline with a caveat that driver type differs. Add OSTI-7021072 to Section 8 with its contribution scoped to non-driver O&M analogue.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Missing external authoritative assessment of direct-drive gain and implosion quality limits
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges 2 and 3) and Section 8 (Sources)
- **Finding:** OSTI-servlets-purl-2561299 (Betti 2024, a peer-reviewed IFE physics review by one of the field's leading researchers) contains two explicit assessments that the analysis currently lacks external sourcing for: (a) "it is unclear at the moment if a gain of ~100x can be achieved with a few megajoules of laser light" — directly addressing Challenge 2's Qc > 200 extrapolation, which Xcimer needs and Betti characterizes as uncertain even at 100×, let alone 200×; (b) "it is unlikely that the implosion quality of direct drive can rival that of indirect drive with current laser technology" — directly addressing Challenge 3's two-beam HDD implosion symmetry problem (conventional multi-beam direct drive is already unlikely to match NIF indirect drive; Xcimer's two-beam geometry is a harder version of that same challenge). Betti also notes a potential path forward: ultra-broadband lasers (ArF/KrF with high bandwidth) could close the gap — contextualizing Xcimer's KrF bandwidth as a partial answer to (b). The analysis frames both challenges well from internal sources but lacks any peer-reviewed physics community context. These Betti statements would change the framing from "undemonstrated" to "actively doubted by independent experts," which is a material difference for a risk assessment.
- **Recommendation:** Add Betti 2024 as a source in Section 8. In Section 2 Challenge 2 (gain extrapolation), add a sentence citing Betti's statement that gains of ~100× are "unclear" with current laser architectures, noting this is the lower bound of Xcimer's required range. In Section 2 Challenge 3 (two-beam symmetry), add a sentence citing Betti's statement that conventional multi-beam direct drive "unlikely to rival indirect drive" with current laser tech, noting that Xcimer's two-beam HDD faces this same limitation plus the additional constraint of two-beam illumination geometry. Include Betti's qualification that ultra-broadband laser operation (which KrF/ArF can provide) is the identified path to closing the gap.
- **Priority:** important
