VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: Primary modeling target (Athena pilot vs. commercial plant) is unspecified
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Approach) and testable hypotheses
- **Finding:** The analysis presents parameters and hypotheses for two distinct plant configurations — the Athena pilot (400 MWe, FLiBe blanket, FOAK laser at ~$100/J) and a future commercial plant (FLiNaK blanket, NOAK laser at $60–80/J, unspecified output scale) — without stating which is the primary TEA modeling target. Hypothesis 3 explicitly references "400 MWe Athena" while Hypothesis 1 tests NOAK laser cost at $60/J, implicitly referencing the commercial plant. These two configurations have different blanket materials, different laser cost assumptions, and different plant sizes; they are not simply two points on a learning curve. The costing framework mentions both FLiBe and FLiNaK without resolving which configuration anchors the model. This ambiguity affects Goal 4: a cost model needs a single primary design point to parameterize.
- **Recommendation:** Add a sentence at the top of the Modeling Approach subsection explicitly stating which plant configuration is the primary modeling target (Athena 400 MWe FOAK or NOAK commercial). If the intent is to model Athena as the anchor and project to NOAK via learning curve, state that explicitly and note that the FLiBe→FLiNaK transition is an architectural change rather than a cost reduction, requiring a separate parameter branch rather than a simple learning-rate multiplier.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: FLiBe→FLiNaK architectural transition has no stated TEA implication
- **Target:** Section 4 (Supply Chain) and Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes, differentiator list)
- **Finding:** Section 4 flags the FLiBe→FLiNaK transition as "a material architectural decision with significant supply-chain implications," but neither Section 5's parameter table nor Section 7's differentiator list addresses the cost implications of this change. The TEA-relevant questions — does FLiNaK cost more or less per kg than FLiBe, does the lower TBR (~1.05 vs. ~1.2) require any compensation that affects tritium startup inventory or blanket design, and does eliminating beryllium procurement meaningfully change blanket capital cost — are left open. This is a gap in Goal 3: a key architectural difference between the pilot and commercial configurations has no stated cost implication.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief entry to the Section 7 differentiator list (or an explanatory note in Section 5's missing parameters) covering the FLiBe→FLiNaK transition: state the direction and approximate magnitude of the inventory cost delta, note whether the TBR difference has any binding implication for tritium economics, and flag whether this transition should be modeled as a cost reduction (eliminating Be supply premium) or cost increase (less heritage, possible nozzle/pump redesign for different fluid properties). If the data is genuinely unavailable to resolve this, flag it as a blocking gap in Section 6.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Focused Energy not positioned as a nearest-neighbor concept
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes), Goal 1
- **Finding:** Section 7 compares Xcimer HDD to concept 26 (Laser ICF — Indirect Drive) and to a conventional tokamak, but Focused Energy — the other major private direct-drive laser IFE company targeting similar commercial timelines — appears only as a DPSSL cost reference in Section 5. As a direct-drive IFE concept, Focused Energy is architecturally closer to Xcimer than indirect drive is. The architectural differences between them (KrF/NLO vs. DPSSL driver, two-beam HDD vs. multi-beam symmetric illumination, sub-Hz high-yield vs. 10 Hz lower-yield rep rate philosophy) represent the key design-space forks within the direct-drive IFE family and have direct TEA consequences. The cross-concept section leaves the direct-drive IFE comparison underdeveloped relative to the indirect-drive and MFE comparisons.
- **Recommendation:** Add a brief paragraph in Section 7 positioning Focused Energy as the primary direct-drive nearest neighbor. Identify 2–3 architectural differences with TEA significance (driver cost $/J, rep rate and yield-per-shot economics, chamber concept), and note which concept bears more favorable cost structure under which assumptions. A single paragraph is sufficient — the goal is to place Xcimer HDD in the direct-drive IFE design space, not to fully analyze Focused Energy.
- **Priority:** minor
