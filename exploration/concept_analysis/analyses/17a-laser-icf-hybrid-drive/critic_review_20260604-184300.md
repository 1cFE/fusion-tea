## Headline issues

The installation cost may be over-estimated precisely *because* the override regime removed the equipment that the generic installation formula assumed would be there. 3. **The C220104 override's "direct" provenance label overstates the grounding — it is derived arithmetic from a published range, not a directly published dollar figure.** The whitepaper publishes $/J ranges ($60–$80/J NOAK), not a total laser system cost in $M. The analysis's emphasis on Q_eng is misplaced; a sensitivity sweep on the C220104 value (FOAK $960M vs. NOAK $560M) or on the derived multipliers (C220101 at 0.40× vs. 0.60×) would reveal the true high-leverage parameters.

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

**contract (ok):** model_setup.py uses the helper-form `run_native_and_1gw` correctly. The sensitivity sweep uses a direct `model.forward` call, which is accepted as a legal extension form. No structural misunderstanding.

**sanity (flagged — 8 accounts):** This is the substantive flag. The eight outlier accounts break into three categories:

- *Low outliers explained by thick-liquid-wall architecture* (C220101 at 0.25×, C220102 at 0.19×, C220107 at 0.00×, C220108 at 0.37×): These are all overridden, and the analysis explains why the FLiBe jet wall eliminates conventional blanket/shield/pulsed-power costs. The rationale is architecturally coherent. C220106_pump at 0.47× is borderline but not overridden and only $0.19M absolute — negligible.

- *High outlier without attribution* (C220111 at 2.04×, CAS27 at 7.67×): CAS27 is overridden to $92M (FLiBe inventory) and the 7.67× ratio is explained by the thick-liquid-wall's large molten-salt fill. This is justified. C220111 at 2.04× ($386.67M) is *not* overridden and *not* attributed in the analysis. This is the headline issue #1 above.

- *Low outlier without attribution* (CAS80 at 0.14×): Not overridden, not explained. Headline issue #2.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes ONE named plant throughout: the Xcimer Athena pilot power plant. There is no stitching of parameters from different published designs. The HYLIFE-II heritage data is used only as a reference comparison, not as a parameter source for the Athena model (except for the FLiBe inventory mass estimate, which is clearly flagged as a HYLIFE-II extrapolation). The Q_eng derivation does compound Athena-specific WPE (5%) with the whitepaper's NOAK recirculating fraction formula, but this is a single-plant derivation at pilot-demonstrator maturity, not cross-plant stitching.

One tension: the analysis notes that "eta_pin (laser wall-plug efficiency) is not a spec key — the library default governs" (model_setup.py:41-45). The library default is 10%, but Xcimer's architecture achieves only 5–7%. The analyst accepts this mismatch as "a cross-concept standardization choice." This is coherent with the framework's rules, but it means the model's back-solved `p_fus` will be inconsistent with the Q_eng = 5.5 derivation (which assumed 5% WPE). The model uses q_eng = 5.5 (which encodes 5% WPE) but the library will also apply its own eta_pin = 10% when calculating recirculating power. Depending on whether q_eng or eta_pin takes priority in the library's power balance, this could be a double-count or a wash. The analyst should verify which parameter dominates the recirculating-power calculation in the library, because if both q_eng and eta_pin independently affect the power balance, the current setup is internally contradictory.

### Override discipline (judgment)

**C220104 ($560M, "direct"):** As noted in headline #3, the provenance should be "derived." The arithmetic is simple and reproducible ($70/J × 8 MJ), but the midpoint selection is analyst judgment within a $160M range. The FOAK-to-NOAK gap ($800–960M vs. $480–640M) is larger than the override value's range uncertainty, and the analysis correctly uses NOAK to align with the model's cost_basis. The override is doing real work: generic C220104 is $1,690M, so the override cuts it by 67%. This is the highest-leverage override in the model.

**C220107 ($0, "direct"):** Correctly zeroed. The capacitor bank is architecturally internal to the laser driver. The generic value is $41M — modest but real double-counting if left in. Provenance "direct" is defensible: the claim is "this subsystem does not exist as a separate account," which is a factual architectural statement, not a derived cost estimate.

**C220101 (0.40× generic = $44.3M):** The 0.40× multiplier is pure analyst judgment. The generic value is $110.7M. The resulting $44.3M reflects that FLiBe nozzle arrays and a steel vessel are cheaper than engineered RAFM blanket modules. The direction is correct; the magnitude is a guess. Within ~10% of the library default? No — it's a 60% reduction. Doing real work.

**C220102 (0.30× generic = $23.2M):** Same pattern as C220101. The 70% reduction reflects that 50+ cm of flowing FLiBe replaces a purpose-built shield. Direction correct; magnitude guessed. The $23M result is small enough that even a 2× error is only ~$23M — not LCOE-moving.

**C220108 (0.60× generic = $88.7M):** The 0.60× multiplier attempts to capture two effects (simpler target + lower throughput) in one number. A 15× throughput reduction would suggest a much larger discount than 0.40× off, since target factory cost is partly a capital-cost problem (number of injection lines, fabrication machines). The 0.60× may be conservative. But without any cost data, this is speculation in both directions.

**CAS21 (1.25× generic = $452.3M):** The 25% premium for the 50m standoff and ~100 Argos modules is modest. Building costs scale with floor area, and doubling the laser-to-target distance might increase reactor building area substantially — but Argos modules are arrayed along the beamline, not spreading in all directions. The 1.25× seems reasonable as a placeholder.

**CAS27 ($92M absolute):** Both inputs (600 t mass from HYLIFE-II analogy, $154/kg from a tokamak study) are analyst-sourced. The mass could be 2× higher or 0.5× lower. The unit cost is from a different reactor type's salt. The result ($92M) is plausible but could range from $46M to $154M. At the 1 GWe projection, CAS27 shows $115M — the scaling from $92M at 400 MWe to $115M at 1 GWe suggests the helper applies a modest scale-up factor, which seems reasonable for a molten salt inventory that scales sublinearly with power.

**CAS70 (0.75× generic):** Eliminating first-wall replacement should indeed reduce scheduled maintenance. But laser systems have their own maintenance costs (Marx generator capacitor replacement cycles, gas purification, SBS media management), and FLiBe loop maintenance is novel and uncharacterized. The 0.75× might understate the FLiBe-specific O&M burden. However, the absolute numbers are modest ($54.5M native vs. $65.7M generic — only $11M difference), so sensitivity is low.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The native-to-1GWe scaling produces a CAS22 jump from $1,308.5M (native 400 MWe) to $3,189.5M (1 GWe). That's a 2.44× ratio for a 2.5× power ratio, implying a scaling exponent of ~0.97 — nearly linear. For reactor equipment, this is on the high side (typical scale factors are 0.6–0.8 for physical equipment). The near-linear scaling is driven by the C220104 override: $560M at 400 MWe scales to $740M at 1 GWe (1.32×), which implies the helper uses something like (1000/400)^0.3 ≈ 1.32. That's reasonable for a laser driver (more modules, not a single monolithic piece). C220111 scales from $300.1M to $386.7M (1.29×), which is also plausible.

The overall 1 GWe LCOE of $78.8/MWh positions this concept favorably relative to the comparables' median. Given that 6 of 8 overrides move costs downward, this is expected. No individual account at 1 GWe looks implausible in isolation; the concern is whether the cumulative effect of six cost-reducing overrides (all based on analyst judgment with no company cost data except C220104) creates a systematically optimistic picture.
