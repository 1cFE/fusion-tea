### F-1: F3 physics uncertainty sweep is degenerate — fusion power invariant across machine size variation
- **Target:** Model (F3 sweep in model_setup.py / model_output.txt)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The F3 physics uncertainty sweep varies R0 from 1.19 m to 1.72 m but P_fus remains fixed at 369 MW in all three cases. Because R0 drives geometry-based capital costs (vessel sizing, structural costs) but not fusion power or Q in the model, the sweep captures only a ±2.5% LCOE variation (580–609 $/MWh) — entirely from structural sizing. The sensitivity table independently confirms this: B, T_e, n_e, tau_ratio, and plasma_volume all show 0.0 elasticity. This dramatically understates the uncertainty identified as "Critical" in Section 2 ("unknown machine parameters — no physics anchor"). Q uncertainty alone spans from marginal ignition (Q_eng ~1.5) to non-burning (Q_eng < 1, LCOE → non-viable), a range the current F3 sweep cannot capture. The model's own note acknowledges the limitation but the presented ±5% LCOE range falsely reassures that physics uncertainty is bounded.
- **Recommendation:** Replace or supplement the R0-proxy sweep with a direct P_fus or Q_engineering sweep (e.g., P_fus ∈ {150, 250, 369, 500} MW or Q_eng ∈ {1.0, 1.6, 2.5, 4.0}). Hold R0 fixed at the baseline and vary the fusion power delivered to the thermal cycle. This will produce a wide LCOE range (likely 400–1,200+ $/MWh) that honestly reflects the machine-parameter uncertainty and is more useful for risk communication than the current 580–609 range.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: H1 (HTS vs. resistive copper downside) scenario not implemented in model
- **Target:** Model (sensitivity sweeps / scenario branches in model_setup.py)
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis defines H1 as a fully testable hypothesis: "IF Pranos were forced to substitute resistive copper magnets, THEN recirculating power for magnet cooling rises to 30–40% of gross electric… making the net output target infeasible." The model output shows H2 (factory learning sweep), F3 (physics proxy sweep), and an eta_th sweep, but no H1 scenario branch. The HTS-vs-copper sensitivity is particularly important for this concept because (a) HTS is confirmed as the stated technology path but field strength and tape grade are unknown, and (b) India has no domestic REBCO manufacturing, creating a genuine supply chain downside risk identified in Section 4. Without this scenario, the model does not test the concept's most India-specific technical risk.
- **Recommendation:** Add an H1 scenario branch that swaps the magnet cost structure to resistive copper: increase recirculating power (p_coils or equivalent) to reflect copper cooling loads at commercial field (≥30 MW added recirculating), reduce magnet capital (C220103 analogue), and recompute LCOE. The result should demonstrate whether — and by how much — resistive magnets push Q_engineering below 1.0 at the 50 MWe net output target, making the scenario infeasible rather than merely expensive.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Only one nearest neighbor named and compared — second comparison concept missing
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes) and header
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies 21-spherical-tokamak-hts (Tokamak Energy) as the sole nearest neighbor and compares extensively. The Reuses frontmatter lists 01-hts-compact-tokamak but this concept is not discussed as a direct comparison in the analysis body — only referenced via the 21-spherical-tokamak-hts cross-reference chain. The assessment checklist requires 2–3 named nearest neighbors. For a compact HTS tokamak at small scale, 01-hts-compact-tokamak (CFS) is the natural second: both use HTS at compact scale with D-T burning plasma and similar supply chain constraints, differing primarily in aspect ratio (ST A≈2 vs. compact conventional A≈3–4) and scale. A direct comparison would clarify which challenges are ST-geometry-specific (center stack shielding, outboard-only blanket, pulsed trade-off) versus which are shared across all compact HTS tokamaks (REBCO supply chain, 20 T+ field cost scaling, D-T tritium startup).
- **Recommendation:** Add a second nearest-neighbor comparison in Section 7 for 01-hts-compact-tokamak. The comparison should identify: (1) what cost advantages the ST aspect ratio provides (higher beta → less magnet cost per unit fusion power), (2) what penalties it adds (center stack constraint, pulsed operation risk), and (3) whether the primary Pranos differentiator is the ST topology or the 50 MWe scale — since 01-hts-compact-tokamak also targets compact scale. One paragraph of structured comparison is sufficient; it does not need to replicate the depth of the 21-spherical-tokamak-hts discussion.
- **Priority:** important
