### F-1: Model binding constraints narrative contradicts sensitivity results
- **Target:** model_output.txt — KEY BINDING CONSTRAINTS section
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The binding constraints summary states "SC coil capital cost and its lifetime are the two highest-sensitivity parameters in the model." The sensitivity sweeps directly contradict this. Plasma Q swings LCOE from net-negative (Q=3) to 68 ¢/kWh (Q=20); fusion power swings from 751 ¢/kWh (200 MW) to 50 ¢/kWh (1000 MW); thermal efficiency swings from 193 ¢/kWh (30%) to 64 ¢/kWh (46%). SC coil capital produces a much narrower range (82–115 ¢/kWh across a 5× cost span), and coil lifetime produces the narrowest variation of any parameter swept (109–90 ¢/kWh). The narrative in the binding constraints section is inconsistent with what the model actually computes. This matters because it misdirects which parameters should govern experimental milestones — the model shows confinement physics (Q) and plant scale (fusion power) as the dominant levers, not coil cost or lifetime. (Addresses Goal 4.)
- **Recommendation:** Rewrite the KEY BINDING CONSTRAINTS section to reflect the actual sensitivity ordering. Coil SC viability remains a legitimate framing constraint (the 700 MW copper reference), but the quantitative sensitivity ranking should state that Q and fusion power dominate LCOE, followed by thermal efficiency and capacity factor, with SC coil capital and lifetime as secondary levers. Also note the internal inconsistency in item 1: it states "LCOE rises above 50 ¢/kWh" if cryo load exceeds ~100 MW, but the sensitivity table shows net power goes negative at 100 MW cryo — reconcile this.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: D-T scenario branch absent despite being a stated design claim
- **Target:** model_setup.py — scenario structure
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** Deutelio's 2024 technical report explicitly claims a D-T operating mode with "3× weaker field than a tokamak," and the analysis differentiator table lists D-T as a "secondary claim." The model only models D-D. This is a material omission for Goal 3 (TEA implications): D-T has a dramatically lower ignition threshold (Q ≥ 10 is far more plausible than for D-D), ~5× higher energy per reaction, but requires a tritium breeding blanket (~$200–500M per the analysis narrative). Without a D-T scenario, the model cannot test whether PoloMac's near-term case (D-T, lower ignition barrier) is economically better or worse than its ambitious long-term case (D-D, no blanket). Given that Q is the highest-sensitivity parameter in the model, the difference between "achievable D-T Q ≈ 10" and "D-D Q ≈ 10 (extraordinary extrapolation)" represents the largest source of LCOE uncertainty in the entire analysis. (Addresses Goals 3 and 4.)
- **Recommendation:** Add a D-T scenario branch to model_setup.py that uses D-T energy partition (80% neutron / 20% charged particle), adds a tritium blanket capital cost ($200–400M range), and uses the same SC coil capital assumptions. Run the same scenario table (conservative/moderate/optimistic) as for D-D. This tests whether the blanket cost penalty exceeds the Q benefit — the central TEA tradeoff for Deutelio's concept.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: D-D scale penalty not stated as a TEA implication
- **Target:** Section 2 (TEA implications of D-D fuel choice)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly notes that D-D reactions produce ~6× less energy per reaction than D-T, but does not connect this to the plant scale required for competitive LCOE — which is the concrete TEA implication. The fusion power sensitivity shows that at 200 MW D-D fusion, net output is only ~8 MWe (economically unworkable); at 500 MW, it is 85 MWe (~$54,000/kWe); at 1000 MW, it is 212 MWe (~$19,000/kWe, approaching possible competitiveness). A 1000 MW D-D fusion plant at this geometry would require a plasma volume of roughly 8,000 m³ — already larger than any fusion device ever built (ITER is ~840 m³). This means D-D's "no blanket" capital savings may be partially or fully offset by the larger plasma volume (and associated first wall, shield, structure, and vessel costs) required to produce a viable net output. This tradeoff belongs in Section 2 alongside the blanket elimination advantage. (Addresses Goal 3.)
- **Recommendation:** Add a paragraph to Section 2 under the D-D fuel discussion stating explicitly: D-D's lower energy per reaction means that competitive LCOE requires fusion power of ~800–1000 MW or greater (per the model), which at the baseline geometry implies plasma volumes of 6,000–8,000 m³. These volumes would make first wall, shield, and structural costs scale proportionally, partially offsetting the capital savings from eliminating the tritium breeding blanket. Frame this as the core D-D economic tradeoff: no blanket, but a much larger and costlier reactor core. Note this also makes the scale sensitivity the primary mechanism by which D-D economics could reach competitiveness — which should align with the Section 5 parameter table by adding a plasma volume scaling parameter.
- **Priority:** important
