========================================================================
KEY BINDING CONSTRAINTS — Top 3 LCOE Drivers
========================================================================

  1. RECIRCULATING POWER / SC COIL VIABILITY
     The 700 MW copper coil draw (Elio 2014) is prohibitive. The model assumes
     a superconducting coil reduces this to ~15 MW cryogenic load, but no SC
     coil design has been published. Until a credible SC coil geometry, field
     target, and radiation-hardening approach is specified, the Q_eng of any
     PoloMac design cannot be determined. If the SC coil cryo load exceeds
     ~100 MW (e.g., due to large-bore HTS in high-radiation environment), the
     recirculating fraction exceeds 50% and LCOE rises above 50 ¢/kWh even
     at optimistic plasma Q.

  2. IN-VESSEL COIL CAPITAL COST AND REPLACEMENT INTERVAL
     The SC coil capital cost (sc_coil_capital_M) and its lifetime (FPY before
     replacement) are the two highest-sensitivity parameters in the model.
     A coil costing $800M with a 4 FPY replacement interval contributes
     ~20–30 ¢/kWh in annualized replacement costs alone. At $300M and 15 FPY,
     this drops to ~3 ¢/kWh. No neutron fluence limit, shielding design, or
     radiation-hard HTS coil design exists for PoloMac — these parameters are
     entirely unconstrained by available data.

  3. PLASMA Q AND FUSION POWER (CONFINEMENT PHYSICS)
     The D-D ignition problem is physics-severity-blocking. D-D requires ~6×
     higher plasma pressure at equivalent density than D-T, and achievable
     confinement time in a dipole geometry at fusion-relevant conditions is
     unknown. The model is insensitive to this only at very high Q (≥15), where
     heating power becomes a small fraction of gross electric. At Q=5, the
     heating system draws ~30% of gross electric, making LCOE highly sensitive
     to Q. This parameter cannot be resolved without plasma physics experiments
     that do not exist for PoloMac.
