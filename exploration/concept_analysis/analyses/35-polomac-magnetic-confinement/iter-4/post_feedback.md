### F-1: D-D charged particle fraction creates direct energy conversion option — not addressed
- **Target:** Section 2 (Key Differentiators table, "Power conversion" row) and Section 7 (cross-concept comparison with 19-orbital-levitated-dipole)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The model shows D-D fusion producing 66.4% charged particle power (332 MW of 500 MW), versus ~20% for D-T. The analysis classifies "Power conversion" as "Borrowed / Neutral — no cost divergence from standard thermal cycle," but this dismissal is incorrect for D-D: the high charged particle fraction is a potential differentiator that could enable direct energy conversion, analogous to 08-frc-w-direct-conversion (Helion D-He3, direct inductive capture) and claimed by 19-orbital-levitated-dipole (D-He3 dipole, direct charged-particle capture). The analysis identifies the orbital dipole as a structural nearest neighbor and draws explicit parallels on fuel cycle and in-vessel coil challenges, but never extends this to the energy conversion implication. If direct conversion applied even to a fraction of the 332 MW charged particle output, effective efficiency would significantly exceed the 30–46% thermal cycle range currently swept in the model — making the thermal efficiency sensitivity results misleading about the actual upside potential. If direct conversion is excluded as geometrically infeasible for PoloMac's dipole field topology, that reasoning should be stated.
- **Recommendation:** Add a row to the differentiator table for "D-D charged particle fraction and energy conversion pathway." Classify it as a potential advantage (or "neutral with caveat"), cite the 08-frc-w-direct-conversion and 19-orbital-levitated-dipole parallels, and explain why direct conversion is or is not modeled. If the dipole field geometry makes charged particle extraction infeasible (in contrast to an FRC's open-field geometry), say so explicitly rather than treating BOP as borrowed from standard thermal designs.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Fusion power sensitivity sweep holds geometry fixed while analysis text implies a larger device at 1000 MW
- **Target:** Section 2 narrative (D-D scale penalty discussion) and model sensitivity analysis
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis states: "At 1000 MW D-D fusion power, a plasma volume of roughly 6,000–8,000 m³ — already larger than any fusion device ever built." This frames 1000 MW as requiring a geometrically larger reactor than the 500 MW baseline (3,990 m³). However, the fusion power sensitivity sweep in the model appears to hold reactor geometry — and therefore shield cost ($804M), vessel cost ($156M), primary structure ($97.6M), and installation labor ($284.5M) — fixed at baseline values while varying only fusion power output. These volume-driven cost accounts represent ~50% of CAS22. The resulting 50 ¢/kWh at 1000 MW tests "better confinement in the same device," not "a larger device producing more power." If the commercially competitive scenario genuinely requires 6,000–8,000 m³, the shield alone would scale to ~$1.5B (2× baseline) and total capital would increase substantially, pushing LCOE well above the model's projection. The analysis uses the 50 ¢/kWh figure to frame competitive viability without acknowledging this geometry ambiguity.
- **Recommendation:** Clarify in the model commentary what the fusion power sweep assumes about geometry. If the sweep tests confinement improvement at fixed device size, state this explicitly in the model output header and in Section 2 — and note that the 6,000–8,000 m³ discussion belongs to a different, geometry-scaled scenario not currently in the model. Optionally, add a geometry-scaled variant of the fusion power sweep (where shield, vessel, and structure costs scale with plasma volume) to bound the optimistic 1000 MW projection from above.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: D-T comparison labels scenarios "equivalent" but uses different Q values at conservative tier
- **Target:** D-T scenario comparison table in model output
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The D-T comparison table header states "equivalent scenario assumptions" but the conservative tier uses Q=7 for D-T and Q=5 for D-D. At moderate and optimistic tiers the Q values are identical (Q=10 and Q=15), so the "D-T costs more than D-D" conclusion holds at the tiers where viable output exists. The asymmetry only affects the conservative case, where both scenarios go negative regardless. However, the "equivalent" label is factually incorrect and obscures the intentional physics prior (that D-T ignition is more accessible than D-D). The rationale "D-T achievable Q ≥ 10 is far more plausible than D-D Q ≥ 10" is noted in the table header, which contradicts the "equivalent assumptions" framing.
- **Recommendation:** Relabel the comparison table as "physics-adjusted scenarios (D-T conservative uses Q=7 reflecting lower ignition threshold)" and remove the "equivalent scenario assumptions" framing. A one-sentence note explaining why D-T conservative starts at Q=7 rather than Q=5 would make the comparison transparent without requiring structural changes to the model.
- **Priority:** minor
