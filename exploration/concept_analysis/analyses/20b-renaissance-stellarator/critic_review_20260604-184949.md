# Critic Review — 20b-renaissance-stellarator

## Headline issues

1. **CAS22 at 19% of comparables median is architecturally plausible but entirely untested by overrides.** The compact geometry (R0=3.8 m vs library default 5.5 m; plasma_t=0.93 m vs 1.8 m) drives CAS220103 coil cost down to $1,106M — 27% of the comparables median ($4,080M) — through the bilinear coil model (cost ∝ R0 × vessel_or × B). This is the library's honest answer for a machine this small, but the library's stellarator coil_markup of 5.87 (calibrated from NCSX modular-coil overruns for conventional 3D winding) is being applied to a laser-patterned-film manufacturing process that eliminates winding entirely. The markup is simultaneously too high (penalizing for a fabrication step that doesn't exist) and potentially too low (the novel film-deposition process has no cost data at all). The analysis correctly identifies this in §5b but the net effect is that the LCOE of 70 $/MWh is an artifact of compact geometry flowing through a cost model whose manufacturing assumptions don't match this concept's manufacturing process. This is not actionable without cost data, but should be flagged as the single most load-bearing assumption in the output.

2. **Sixteen sanity-flagged accounts all trace to one root cause — compact geometry — but the analysis never quantifies the sensitivity.** Every outlier_low flag (C220000 through CAS90) is a downstream consequence of R0=3.8 m and plasma_t=0.93 m producing a small machine with low total material volumes and proportionally lower indirect/financial accounts. The analysis discusses compactness qualitatively (§2.4, §7) but never states: "If R0 were 5.5 m (library default), LCOE would be ~X $/MWh." This single-parameter sensitivity would disambiguate how much of the 70 $/MWh result is geometry-driven vs. archetype-driven.

3. **The stellarator coil_markup (5.87) and path_factor (2.0) are structurally wrong for this concept's magnet architecture, but no override exists to correct them.** Renaissance's cylindrical HTS film magnets are not wound non-planar modular coils. The path_factor=2.0 (reflecting 3D winding path ~2× longer than a simple torus) and the 90% NCSX overrun baked into the 5.87 markup both penalize for fabrication complexity that this concept claims to eliminate. The analysis acknowledges this (§2.2, §5b C220103) but concludes "no override" because no cost data exists. The result is a model that simultaneously overcharges for conventional stellarator fabrication complexity and undercharges for the unknown risks of a novel manufacturing process. This is a known-unknowable at this stage, but the review should note that the library defaults are not neutral here — they actively misrepresent the manufacturing process.

4. **eta_th mismatch: library uses Rankine at 40% but the design specifies sCO2 Brayton-Rankine at 49–51%.** The analysis notes this discrepancy (model_setup.py comment block) but does not override eta_th because it is "ENUM-driven, Rule 6." The 10+ percentage-point difference in thermal efficiency directly affects the gross-to-net power balance, thermal plant sizing (CAS23), and heat rejection (CAS26). At 40% efficiency the library needs ~2,500 MWth to produce 1,000 MWe; at 50% it needs ~2,000 MWth. This is a ~20% reduction in thermal plant costs and a non-trivial LCOE effect. If Rule 6 prevents setting eta_th in the spec, this constraint should be documented as a modeling limitation that biases this concept's LCOE upward.

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

**dpc (ok):** P_native is coherent at 1000 MWe across the design-point table, analysis.md frontmatter, and model_setup.py. No action needed.

**contract (ok):** The model_setup.py uses the standard helper form (generic_reference + run_native_and_1gw). The three-forward structure is correct. No action needed.

**count_smell (ok):** Zero enabled overrides for a High archetype-fit grade (expected 0–4). The absence of overrides is internally consistent — Renaissance Fusion publishes no dollar-figure cost data for any subsystem. The count is within band. However, "zero overrides" combined with "16 sanity outliers" creates a tension: the library defaults are producing an answer that diverges substantially from comparables on almost every account, and no override exists to validate or correct any of them. The count_smell check says the override discipline is fine; the sanity check says the output is unusual. Both are correct — the tension is inherent in a compact machine with no published cost data.

**sanity (flagged, 16 accounts):** This is the dominant flag. All 16 outlier accounts divide into two groups:

*Group 1 — Direct geometry effect (outlier_low):* C220000, C220101, C220102, C220103, C220104, C220105, C220106 (and sub-accounts), C220111 are all low because the machine is physically small. The bilinear coil model gives C220103 ∝ R0 × vessel_or × B. With R0=3.8 (vs comparables at 7.8–18.0), the coil cost is mechanically suppressed. Blanket and shield volumes scale with surface area (~R0 × plasma_t), which is also small. This is the library doing its job — a small machine costs less material. The question is whether a machine this small can actually produce 1 GWe, which is a physics question the cost model doesn't answer.

*Group 2 — Cascade effects (outlier_low):* CAS30 (indirect at 20% of CAS20), CAS50 (capitalized financial cost, scales with overnight), CAS60 (capitalized construction cost, scales with overnight and construction time), CAS90 (annualized costs, scales with total capital) are all low because CAS22 is low. These are arithmetically correct given CAS22.

*Outlier_high:* CAS27 at 3.0× median ($15M vs $5M). The library's CAS27 default is not geometry-driven — it's a fixed special-materials charge. The 3× ratio is small in absolute terms ($10M delta) and within noise for a concept with no published materials cost data. C220106_pump at 2.6× median ($0.69M vs $0.27M) is also de minimis.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes one named plant throughout: the 1 GWe economically optimized compact liquid-wall HTS stellarator from Samulski/Prost & Volpe (Nuclear Fusion 64, 2024). However, there is mild stitching:

- The reactor geometry (R0=3.8 m, A=4.1, B=10.2 T) comes from the blanket paper's reproduction of the design-point paper's values — not from the design-point paper directly (which was not extracted).
- The power conversion system (sCO2 at 49–51%) comes from a third paper (Famà et al. 2023), which was a standalone thermodynamic optimization not specifically coupled to this reactor design.
- The p_input=5 MW value is an analyst estimate by analogy to Thea Energy Helios, not a published figure from Renaissance Fusion.

The stitching is minor — all three papers describe components of the same declared design point, and the analyst is transparent about each source. The p_input estimate is the riskiest stitch: if the NNBI system for startup is sized at, say, 50 MW installed capacity (plausible for a 2 GW fusion power machine), and if ignition is not achieved, p_input could be 10–50× higher, which would change CAS220104 from $25M to $250–1,250M.

### Override discipline (judgment)

Zero overrides means zero entries to evaluate on the provenance/value/noise criteria. The analysis's per-account walkthrough in §5b is thorough and correctly concludes "no override" for each account due to absent cost data. The walkthrough identifies C220101, C220103, CAS21, and CAS27 as accounts that *would* warrant overrides if data existed — this is a responsible articulation of the limitation.

The judgment question is whether the analyst should have attempted derived overrides where data is partially available:
- **CAS27 (special materials):** The Li-LiH + Pb pebble initial inventory could be estimated from blanket geometry (32 cm thickness × torus surface area) and commodity prices (Li ~$25/kg, LiH ~$50/kg, Pb ~$2/kg). The analysis flags this as data gap #11 (nice-to-have, derivable). A back-of-envelope estimate would likely produce a figure well below $15M, suggesting the library default is already reasonable or high. Not pursuing this is defensible given the "nice-to-have" criticality.
- **C220103 (magnets):** Any derived override here would be speculative — the laser-patterned film process has no cost basis. Not overriding is correct.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

P_native = 1000 MWe, so the two-knob projection is trivial (scale factor = 1.0, n_mod = 1). The generic, native, and 1 GWe columns are identical, confirming no overrides are active and the scaling path is degenerate. This is correct behavior.

The per-account 1 GWe values are the library's direct answer for a STELLARATOR with R0=3.8, plasma_t=0.93, B=10.2, p_input=5. The 70 $/MWh LCOE is remarkably low for a stellarator — lower than Proxima Fusion's QI-stellarator at 148 $/MWh, which uses R0=12.0 and p_input=50. The entire difference is geometry: Renaissance's machine is 3.2× smaller in major radius and uses 10× less heating power. The cost model rewards compactness linearly (bilinear coil model) and penalizes it not at all for engineering integration risk (fitting 2 GW of fusion power into a 3.8 m major radius torus with 25 MW/m² wall loading). This is a known limitation of parametric cost models — they price the steel, not the difficulty.

### Family delta vs comparables

The analysis's §7 family-delta table is well-structured but every entry in the "Magnitude" column reads "Unknown — no cost data." This is honest but leaves the reader with no quantitative guidance on whether the deltas are 5% effects or 50% effects.

The most consequential delta is **C220103 (magnets)**: Renaissance at $1,106M vs median $4,080M (0.27× ratio). This $3,000M difference is ~55% of Renaissance's total overnight cost. If the laser-patterned film process turns out to cost the same per installed ampere-meter as conventional HTS winding (eliminating the fabrication savings the concept claims), the magnet cost would not rise to the median — it would likely rise higher, because the cylindrical film architecture may have its own manufacturing challenges. The breakeven question is: at what $/kAm would Renaissance's magnets cost the same as a conventional stellarator's? The bilinear model makes this calculable but the analysis doesn't perform the calculation.

CAS21 (buildings) at $595M vs comparables median $333M is actually an *outlier_high* within the comparables (1.79× ratio), not low. This is counterintuitive for the most compact stellarator in the set. The explanation: CAS21 scales with fusion power (P_fus ≈ 2,000 MW), not with reactor volume. Renaissance's 2 GW fusion power is comparable to or higher than some comparables that have larger machines but lower power density. The building cost is driven by what's inside (thermal power to handle), not how big the reactor is. This is actually a modeling insight that the analysis misses — §7 claims CAS21 should be "advantageous (smaller reactor building)" but the model shows the opposite because building cost doesn't scale with R0.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

The two assumptions that would most change the LCOE conclusion:

1. **Compact geometry is physically realizable at 1 GWe.** If the R0=3.8 m design point cannot sustain 1 GWe net electric output (because MHD effects on the liquid wall degrade confinement, because the 25 MW/m² wall loading is unsustainable, or because ignition is not achieved at these parameters), the design would need to grow. Doubling R0 to 7.6 m would roughly double CAS22 (bilinear scaling), and cascade through CAS30/50/60/90. LCOE would approximately double. The analysis flags the liquid wall MHD concern (§2.1, data gap #3) and the ignition concern (§2.3) but does not quantify the LCOE sensitivity to either.

2. **The stellarator coil_markup and path_factor are approximately correct for this concept.** As discussed in headline issue #3, these parameters are calibrated for a fundamentally different manufacturing process. If the laser-patterned film process is 2× cheaper per ampere-meter than wound REBCO (halving the effective markup), CAS220103 drops to ~$553M and LCOE falls to ~$60 $/MWh. If the film process is 2× more expensive (novel process risk), CAS220103 rises to ~$2,212M and LCOE rises to ~$85 $/MWh. The LCOE is moderately sensitive to this parameter but not as sensitive as to geometry.

The analysis flags both concerns qualitatively (§2.1–2.4, §6 gaps #1–3) but neither is quantified as a sensitivity range in the output artifacts.
