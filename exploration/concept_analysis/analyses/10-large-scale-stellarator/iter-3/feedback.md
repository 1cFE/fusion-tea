### F-1: Cryogenic parasitic load is unquantified but data now exists to bound it
- **Target:** Section 2 (O&M Considerations / Recirculating Power) and Section 5 (Missing Parameters — "Recirculating power fraction")
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The ParaStell study (Moreno et al., 2024, Frontiers in Nuclear Engineering) reports magnet nuclear heating for WISTELL-D (quasi-isodynamic stellarator, 10.1 m major radius, 2113 MWth) of 152 kW, yielding an estimated cryogenic load of ~63.3 MWe — scaling from the ARIES-CS reference (12 kW heating → 5 MWe cryogenic load). This represents a 12.7× increase from ARIES-CS to WISTELL-D and is approximately 3% of fusion power for WISTELL-D. The analysis currently characterizes recirculating power as "dominated by ECRH power + cryogenic load" and lists it as a "derivable/important" missing parameter, but provides no quantitative anchor for the cryogenic component. The Section 5 parameter table notes "5–7% recirculating power" as the implied gap between gross (~37–40%) and net (~33.3%) efficiency, but this is attributed primarily to ECRH and pumping — not to the superconducting coil cryogenic load. For GIGA at 18 m major radius, scaling from the WISTELL-D data point suggests the cryogenic contribution could be 3–5% of fusion power or higher, which is material to the LCOE calculation. This is new quantitative information that changes the character of the recirculating power gap from "unknown" to "bounded but large."
- **Recommendation:** In Section 2 (O&M Considerations), add a paragraph on cryogenic parasitic power load as a distinct recirculating power component. Note the WISTELL-D analog (63.3 MWe for a 2113 MWth, 10.1 m machine from ParaStell 2024), explain the unfavorable scaling from ARIES-CS, and flag that GIGA at 18 m likely exceeds this. In Section 5, update the "Recirculating power fraction" missing parameter to include a reference to WISTELL-D's ~3% cryogenic fraction as a lower-bound analog, and note that the analysis's "5–7% total recirculating" estimate may be understated if cryogenic load at GIGA scale is in the 3–6% range. Add a note to hypothesis space or key hypotheses that net efficiency may be more sensitive to cryogenic load than to ECRH power in the stellarator case (the inverse of the tokamak assumption).
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: TBR/magnet shielding coupling constraint in tight LCFS-to-coil regions not addressed
- **Target:** Section 3 (Breeding Blanket in HELIAS 3D Geometry) and Section 2 (Challenge 3: Blanket Geometry Complexity)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** The ParaStell study identifies a structural design conflict specific to 3D non-planar stellarators: in regions of minimum LCFS-to-coil spacing, increasing total blanket build thickness does not improve local magnet shielding or local tritium breeding, because the available space in those regions is fixed by the coil geometry. The only way to improve magnet protection in tight-spacing regions is to reduce the breeder fraction (increasing the HTS shield fraction), which trades TBR margin for magnet protection. The analysis discusses both blanket geometry complexity (Section 2, Challenge 3 — 80 segment shapes, 20 mm gaps, stress failures in Segment 5) and TBR uncertainty (Section 3, Section 5), but treats them as independent concerns. The LCFS-to-coil spacing coupling is distinct: it means that for QI non-planar coil designs, TBR and magnet shielding cannot be simultaneously optimized in tight-spacing regions, creating a design constraint that is absent in tokamaks and in QA planar-coil stellarators. This is relevant to GIGA because GIGA's QI non-planar coil geometry (5 distinct coil shapes, varying plasma-coil gap) will have similar tight-spacing regions. The current analysis does not name this constraint.
- **Recommendation:** In Section 3 (Breeding Blanket in HELIAS 3D Geometry), under "Missing at scale," add a bullet noting that tight LCFS-to-coil spacing regions create a TBR/shielding trade-off constraint that cannot be resolved by overall blanket thickness increase — referencing the ParaStell (2024) WISTELL-D scoping study as a quantitative demonstration of this effect in a comparable QI geometry. In Section 2 (Challenge 3: Blanket Geometry Complexity), note that the 3D segment diversity challenge includes this coupling: blanket performance optimization is not separable from magnet protection in regions of minimum plasma-coil clearance. This should be framed as a risk specific to QI non-planar designs that does not apply to QA planar-coil designs (Helios) — strengthening the QI vs. QA comparison already in Section 7.
- **Priority:** important
