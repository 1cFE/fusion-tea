# Critic Review — 21-spherical-tokamak-hts

## Headline issues

1. **The 6 sanity-flagged accounts are library geometry artifacts, not concept-specific cost drivers — the analysis doesn't say this.** C220101 (5.2×), C220102 (4.4×), C220105 (4.3×), C220106_vessel (4.3×), and C220111 (2.2×) are all outlier-high relative to comparables. But the comparables are dominated by compact machines (01-hts at R0=3.3m, 28-hts at R0=2.6m, 29-NT at R0=4.55m), while ST-E1 has R0=5.0m and — crucially — elon=2.8 versus elon=1.8 for the three compact comparables. Since blanket, shield, structure, and vessel volumes scale as R0 × a² × κ, the combination of larger major radius, larger minor radius (2.17m vs 0.87–1.2m), and 55% higher elongation produces a plasma volume roughly 4–5× larger than the compact HTS comparables. The outlier ratios are almost entirely explained by geometry, not by any concept-specific cost driver or modeling error. Yet Section 7 of `analysis.md` discusses comparables deltas qualitatively (field strategy, magnet energy, shielding novelty) without ever connecting the numerical divergence to the basic fact that ST-E1 is a volumetrically large machine running the same library scaling. This is a narrative gap: the sanity flags look alarming but are mechanistically benign, and the analysis should say so.

2. **Pulsed operation's capacity-factor penalty is acknowledged but not modeled.** Section 2 (challenge #3) explicitly identifies the quasi-steady pulsed duty cycle as a 70–90% capacity factor range, the default TOKAMAK availability is 85%, and no override or spec adjustment reflects the pulsed penalty. If the true availability were 75% instead of 85%, LCOE would increase by roughly 13% (~$22/MWh at 1 GWe scale), dwarfing the sensitivity sweeps on p_input (±$5/MWh) and elongation (±$8/MWh). This is the single largest un-modeled LCOE driver acknowledged in the analysis itself.

3. **The sensitivity sweeps cover the wrong parameters relative to LCOE impact.** The two swept parameters — `p_input` (±$5/MWh range) and `elon` (±$9/MWh range) — together bound only ~$14/MWh of LCOE uncertainty. Meanwhile, the analyst's own Section 2 identifies capacity factor (potentially ±$20+/MWh), thermal cycle selection (η_th 0.33 vs 0.40, implying ~20% change in required fusion power and reactor sizing), and the 450–750 MWe P_native ambiguity (a 1.67× spread) as larger uncertainty axes. The sweeps are technically correct (they cover the lowest-confidence spec keys) but they create a false sense of bounded uncertainty by ignoring the parameters that actually dominate the LCOE error bar.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes one named plant — ST-E1 Revision D — consistently throughout. However, there is deliberate stitching across published designs for the inferred parameters:

- **Elongation (κ=2.8):** Sourced from "typical ST value" and MAST-U/pulsed-ST paper analogues, not from the Revision D design point.
- **p_input (50 MW):** Derived from the pulsed-ST paper's ST280-5T reference (R0=2.8m, A=1.9) — a substantially different machine.
- **Bootstrap fraction (~90%):** Taken from ST280-5T, not Revision D.
- **Fusion power (~1500 MW):** Scaling from the 800 MW reference at R0=2.8m using R0³ × B⁴/A⁴.

This stitching is well-documented in Section 5 (confidence tags and derivation chains) and explicitly flagged as "order-of-magnitude placeholders." The analyst has been transparent about what is known vs. inferred. The stitching matters most for `p_input` because it directly enters the cost model: the 50 MW estimate is a scaling extrapolation from a machine with less than half the major radius and a lower aspect ratio, then further inflated by an undisclosed ECRH requirement. The sensitivity sweep (40–60 MW) partially mitigates this but the range itself is an educated guess.

`plasma_t` (2.17m) deserves a note: it's labeled "medium confidence" as a deterministic derivation from R0/A, which is correct — but its LCOE impact is large because it enters the volume formula as a². The sensitivity coverage section says it "is not independently swept" because it's a derived quantity, which is sound reasoning.

### Override discipline (judgment)

With zero overrides, there are no per-entry questions to evaluate. The judgment question is instead: are there accounts where the analyst *should* have overridden but didn't?

The strongest candidate is **C220101 (blanket):** The outboard-only blanket is a known architectural distinction from the comparables (which assume full-coverage). Outboard-only coverage should reduce blanket mass by roughly 30–50% (the inboard blanket fraction depends on aspect ratio, but for A=2.3 the inboard surface area is substantial). The analyst discusses this qualitatively in Section 7 ("advantage on material cost") but does not translate it into even a directional override. A scale-factor override of 0.5–0.7 on C220101 would be defensible as an architectural adjustment even without company cost data. The same logic applies weakly to C220102 (the inboard shield is WC cermet, architecturally different from a standard shield, but the cost direction is penalty-upward, not downward — and the magnitude is unknown).

However, the analyst's stated policy — no override without published cost data — is defensible and internally consistent. It just means the model overstates blanket cost and understates shielding novelty, leaving a partially compensating pair of errors.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

At P_native=450 MWe, the 1 GWe projection requires n_mod ≈ 2.22 modules. The per-module values (the "native" column) are self-consistent: generic = native because there are zero overrides. The 1 GWe values show:

- **C220103 (magnets):** $1,170.7M at both native and 1 GWe. This is expected — magnets are a fixed per-module cost (no power scaling) and the model appears to apply a multi-unit learning factor that counteracts the module replication.
- **C220104 (heating):** $278.5M at both scales. Same mechanism as magnets.
- **CAS22 total:** $3,754.7M native → $7,618.4M at 1 GWe (ratio 2.03×). With n_mod ≈ 2.22, this implies some sub-linear scaling from multi-unit learning, which is physically reasonable.
- **CAS23 (turbine):** $118.8M → $257.9M (ratio 2.17×). BOP scales approximately linearly with power, consistent with the library.
- **CAS70 (O&M):** $160.4M → $284.3M (ratio 1.77×). O&M scales as P^0.5 per the library, so √(1000/450) ≈ 1.49 — the actual ratio is higher, suggesting an additive fixed-cost component. Plausible.

No per-account value looks architecturally implausible for a large D-T spherical tokamak. The 1 GWe LCOE of $168.4/MWh is high but within the range seen for early-stage D-T tokamak concepts.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

The two assumptions that would most change the LCOE conclusion if wrong:

1. **Capacity factor / availability at 85%.** The analysis identifies pulsed operation as challenge #3 and estimates a 70–90% range. If availability is 75% rather than 85%, LCOE increases by ~13%. This is the dominant unmodeled uncertainty and is larger than any parameter swept. The analysis acknowledges it (Section 2, gap #9) but neither models it nor flags it as the #1 sensitivity.

2. **Thermal efficiency assumption (implicit in the library default).** Section 2 challenge #5 identifies the undisclosed thermal cycle (Rankine at ~33% vs. sCO2 at ~40%). The library's TOKAMAK default thermal efficiency determines the fusion power required for 450 MWe net. If the library assumes ~35% and Tokamak Energy intends sCO2 at ~40%, the reactor is oversized in the model (higher fusion power → higher neutron wall loading → higher blanket/shield/structure costs). This would partially explain the already-high volume-driven accounts.

Both gaps are flagged in Sections 2 and 6 of `analysis.md`, but neither is highlighted as the load-bearing assumption most likely to change the LCOE headline number. The data gap table marks capacity factor as "important" rather than "blocking" — yet its LCOE impact exceeds that of several "blocking" gaps (plasma current, Q) that don't directly enter the cost model.

## What I deliberately did not say

- The comparables set's median is dominated by three compact machines with very different geometry. This makes the sanity-check outlier flags structurally inevitable for any large tokamak, which could be a concern about the *comparables selection* — but that is an upstream-table decision, out of scope for `model_critic`.

- The bootstrap fraction of ~90% is taken from ST280-5T (A=1.9, R0=2.8m), and whether this transfers to A=2.3, R0=5.0m is a physics question that could change the p_input estimate materially. This is a source-quality / analogue-transfer question that belongs in `research`-stage review.

- The Humphry-Baker & Smith shielding study (2019) was performed on a 185 MW pilot plant (R0=1.35m), and whether its findings transfer to a 5.0m machine is questionable — but this is a source-applicability concern, out of scope for `model_critic`.

- The pulsed-ST paper's C_MAG proxy (14 MJ/MW for ST vs. 33–37 MJ/MW for ARC) is presented as evidence of a magnet cost advantage in Section 7, but C_MAG is magnetic energy per unit *fusion* power, not magnetic energy per unit *net electric* power. If the ST has lower Q or higher recirculating fraction, the C_MAG advantage shrinks or inverts when normalized to net output. This is a physics interpretation question, not a modeling artifact.
