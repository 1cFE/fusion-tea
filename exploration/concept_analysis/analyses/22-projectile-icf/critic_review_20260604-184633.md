# Critic Review — 22-projectile-icf

## Headline issues

1. **C220104 driver cost is labelled "derived" but rests on a cross-design $/J figure that may not transfer** — The $2/J figure used to price the EM gun ($200M for 100 MJ) comes from the IP Group FLARE press release, which describes a pulsed-power liner-compression device, not an electromagnetic gun. Machine 3's $1.7/J is a single-shot gas gun, not an EM launcher. No published cost exists for a rep-rated electromagnetic gun at 60 km/s and 100 MJ. The analysis acknowledges the sources but does not flag the cross-technology transfer risk: pulsed-power liner $/J and gas-gun $/J may not bracket EM-gun $/J at all. A factor-of-2–5 miss on driver cost would shift native LCOE by ~$30–$80/MWh. This is the single largest load-bearing assumption in the model and it is under-flagged in the gap inventory (Gap #1 names driver energy but not driver cost uncertainty per se).

2. **C220108 target factory uses a viability ceiling as an override value, creating a circular LCOE** — The $5.6M/year target-factory override is derived by assuming the concept hits its $50/MWh LCOE target and then back-computing the maximum allowable target cost. The model then reports an LCOE of $66.8/MWh (1 GWe) that *includes* this viability-derived input. This means the LCOE output is partially pre-constrained by its own target LCOE. The analysis is transparent about this ("VIABILITY CEILING, not a cost estimate"), but the implication for the headline LCOE number is not surfaced: it is not an independent cost estimate for this account — it is a "what would the cost need to be" figure embedded in a model presented as "what does the cost look like." The gap inventory (Gap #5) captures target cost as truly-unknown but does not flag the circularity for the headline LCOE interpretation.

3. **CAS27 "direct" provenance claim is questionable — the $70M figure is from a FLARE (post-pivot) cost-comparison table, not the projectile pilot plant** — The IP Group press release's "Natural lithium per reactor: $70M" appears in a cost comparison for FLARE (333–400 MWe), not the pre-pivot 150 MWe projectile pilot. The analysis in Section 5b acknowledges the IP Group source but does not flag that a 400 MWe plant's lithium inventory would differ from a 150 MWe plant's. Section 4 estimates 200–400 tonnes ($20M–$40M) from HYLIFE scaling for the sub-Hz regime, which is substantially lower than the $70M override. The "direct" provenance label should be "derived" (the analyst selected a figure from a different design point) or the rationale should explain why the FLARE-era figure applies to the projectile pilot.

4. **C220200 heat transport ($38.8M native → $205.5M at 1 GWe) is an acknowledged structural mismatch with no override, and it may be the largest unaddressed cost error at 1 GWe** — The analysis documents that C220200 prices FLiBe mechanical pumps while the concept uses liquid lithium EM pumps, but classifies C220200 as a forbidden override target (CAS22.2 sub-rollup). At native scale ($38.8M), this is a modest concern. At 1 GWe ($205.5M), it becomes the third-largest CAS22 sub-account. The HYLIFE EM pump system (72 m³/s, 800 tonnes, 50–60% efficiency) suggests a very different cost profile than FLiBe mechanical pumps, and at 1 GWe scale with proportional flow increases the error could be significant in either direction. The analysis mentions this mismatch only in an in-line comment in `model_setup.py` (lines 244–251) and not in the gap inventory.

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

All four deterministic checks return `status: ok`. `dpc` confirms P_native coherence at 150 MWe across all three legs. `contract` validates the helper-form call shape. `count_smell` finds 6 enabled overrides consistent with the Low archetype-fit band (6–12). `sanity` shows all 38 accounts with `no_data` flags — this means no comparables data exists for any account, so the outlier-detection layer has no baseline to flag against. The `no_data` status across all accounts is a consequence of the empty comparables list and is structurally correct, but it also means the sanity check provides zero signal for this concept. The deterministic layer is clean but thin: the two checks that would provide the most value (sanity and drift) are effectively absent — sanity has no data, and drift was not triggered.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes one named plant throughout: the pre-pivot 150 MWe pilot (2022 press release). However, there is non-trivial stitching from post-pivot FLARE sources. Specifically:

- **TBR 1.8** (Section 3, Section 5): validated by TUV SUD UK for the FLARE geometry at 333 MWe, not the projectile pilot. The analysis notes "assumed same for projectile plant" (Section 5, line 169) — a reasonable assumption given the shared lithium-curtain concept, but it is an assumption, not a published value for the design point.
- **$2/J driver cost** (Section 5b, C220104): from the FLARE demonstration facility ($100M–$200M for a 50–100 MJ pulsed-power system). Applied to the projectile pilot's EM gun — a different technology.
- **$70M lithium inventory** (Section 5b, CAS27): from the FLARE cost-comparison table for a 333–400 MWe plant, applied to a 150 MWe plant.
- **"Compact enough to integrate seamlessly"** (Section 5b, CAS21 discussion): from the FLARE-era IP Group release, not a statement about the projectile pilot.

The stitching is mostly defensible — First Light's lithium-curtain architecture carries across both eras — but the analyst treats post-pivot FLARE cost data as directly applicable to the pre-pivot projectile pilot without systematically adjusting for scale (150 vs. 333–400 MWe) or technology (EM gun vs. pulsed-power liner). The `analysis.md` acknowledges the stitching piecemeal in individual overrides but does not collect it into a single coherence statement.

### Override discipline (judgment)

**C220102 (radiation shield, 15% of generic = $6.8M)**: Provenance "derived" is honest. The 15% residual for penetration shielding is a judgment call with no source — the analysis cites only the claim that neutrons don't reach the vessel wall. The 15% fraction is plausible but arbitrary. Within ~10% of zero and arguably noise-level for the total, but correctly directional.

**C220104 (driver, $200M)**: Labelled "derived," which is correct. The arithmetic (100 MJ × $2/J = $200M) is clean. The judgment concern is that $2/J comes from a pulsed-power liner device, not an EM gun. Machine 3 at $1.7/J is a single-shot gas gun, also not an EM gun. Neither source directly prices the required technology. The override is doing very real work — $200M vs. $12,591M generic — so the accuracy of this transfer assumption is critical. The analysis rationale treats $2/J as "the more conservative (later, rep-rated) estimate" but the FLARE demo is not rep-rated either; it's a single-shot demonstration facility.

**C220107 (capacitor bank, $0)**: The zero is logically sound if C220104 truly captures the entire energy-store + launcher system. The $2/J × 100 MJ framing from the IP Group source does appear to encompass the stored-energy system. Reasonable.

**C220108 (target factory, $5.6M)**: As discussed in headline #2, this is a viability ceiling, not a cost estimate. The provenance "derived" is technically correct but undersells the issue: the derivation assumes the concept's own LCOE target. The value is doing significant work ($5.6M vs. $79.1M generic). The sensitivity sweep ($1–$20/target) mentioned in the rationale is appropriate but is not reflected in the model output — only the ceiling value appears.

**C220110 (remote handling, 30% of generic = $10.1M)**: The 70/30 split claim ("~70% of conventional RH cost is driven by blanket/first-wall change-out") is attributed to "tokamak cost studies" with no specific citation. This is a reasonable analogue judgment but the 30% figure is not grounded in any published source for IFE remote handling. The override is not large enough ($10.1M) to materially affect the LCOE conclusion either way.

**CAS27 (special materials, $70M)**: Labelled "direct" provenance. As discussed in headline #3, the IP Group figure is from a FLARE cost-comparison table for a larger plant. The analysis's own scaling analysis in Section 4 estimates $20M–$40M for the 150 MWe sub-Hz plant. The gap between the analyst's own derived estimate ($20M–$40M) and the override value ($70M) is a factor of ~2–3 and is not reconciled. If $70M is conservative (deliberate choice), the analysis should say so. If the IP Group figure is taken as applicable to the pilot, the rationale should explain why a 333–400 MWe figure transfers to 150 MWe.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The native → 1 GWe scaling shows some notable patterns:

- **C220200** (heat transport): $38.8M native → $205.5M at 1 GWe, a 5.3× increase for a 6.7× power increase. This is the unoverridden structural mismatch. The scaling exponent looks reasonable for a piping/pumping system, but the base value is for FLiBe mechanical pumps, not Li EM pumps.
- **C220104** (driver): $200M native → $184.9M at 1 GWe. The override value ($200M absolute) at native scale is *higher* than the 1 GWe projection ($184.9M). This means the two-knob projection applies a scale-down factor to the override. This is worth noting: the library's scaling assumes driver cost scales sub-linearly with power, but an EM gun's cost may not follow the same exponent as a heavy-ion accelerator. No specific concern is raised — the $185M at 1 GWe is in the same range — but the inversion (native > 1 GWe for the driver) is a signature of the archetype mismatch.
- **CAS30** (indirect costs): $171.9M native → $710.5M at 1 GWe. CAS30 is computed as a fraction of direct costs. The 4.1× increase is consistent with the direct-cost scaling.
- **CAS22 total**: $453.7M native → $2,679.4M at 1 GWe. The dominant driver of this 5.9× increase is C220200 scaling ($38.8M → $205.5M) and the non-overridden accounts (C220300 instrumentation: $11.9M → $15.1M; C220500 fuel handling: $31.8M → $120M; C220700 maintenance equipment: $24.0M → $82.3M) which all scale conventionally. The overridden accounts remain flat or scale modestly.

No individual 1 GWe account value is implausible on its face given the archetype mismatch context. The 1 GWe LCOE of $66.8/MWh is driven primarily by CAS22 ($2,679M), CAS60 ($863M), CAS30 ($710.5M), and CAS90 ($401.5M).

### Family delta vs comparables

No comparables exist — all sanity-check entries show `no_data`. The Section 7 delta analysis against the HEAVY_ION archetype is the only structured reference frame. The analysis correctly identifies the pervasive archetype mismatch and articulates cost directions for each subsystem.

The key unaddressed divergence question: at 1 GWe, the non-overridden CAS22 sub-accounts (C220200, C220300, C220400, C220500, C220600, C220700) collectively contribute ~$522M. These are all scaling from a HEAVY_ION baseline that assumes multi-GeV accelerator and FLiBe-jet infrastructure. Several of these (C220500 fuel handling at $120M, C220700 maintenance equipment at $82.3M) likely overstate what a projectile ICF plant needs, but the analyst reasonably chose not to override them without data. The direction of the error is probably conservative (costs overstated), which is defensible for a low-confidence concept.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

Two assumptions would flip the LCOE conclusion most:

1. **Driver cost per joule transferability** — The entire CAS22 restructuring depends on $2/J being approximately correct for a rep-rated EM gun at 100 MJ. If the true cost is $10/J (still far below laser/accelerator $/J), the driver rises from $200M to $1,000M, native LCOE rises from $125 to ~$230/MWh, and 1 GWe LCOE rises to ~$100–110/MWh. The analysis identifies the driver as a critical gap (Gap #1) but frames it as a driver energy uncertainty rather than a driver cost uncertainty. The cost-per-joule transfer assumption is the more LCOE-sensitive variable.

2. **Target gain and yield achievability** — The model accepts the library's default `q_eng = 4.0`, which implicitly encodes a power-balance assumption. But the concept's viability requires gain >200 from projectile compression alone, which has no experimental basis beyond ~50 neutrons. First Light's own pivot to FLARE (fast ignition for high gain) implicitly concedes that pure projectile compression may not achieve the required gains. If Q_eng turns out to be 2.0 instead of 4.0 (twice the recirculating fraction), net electric output halves and LCOE doubles. The analysis identifies this clearly (Gap #2, Section 2.2) and rates it "blocking."

Both are flagged in the gap inventory but neither is identified as the single most LCOE-sensitive parameter.

## What I deliberately did not say

- **Source quality of the IP Group press release**: Several override values ($2/J, $70M lithium) depend on an investor-relations press release from First Light's largest investor. Whether this source meets the project's quality threshold for "direct" provenance is a source-quality question that belongs in research-stage review, not model_critic. I note it here because the analysis treats this source as equivalent in weight to Hawker (2020), a peer-reviewed publication, without distinguishing their evidentiary status.

- **Whether the design point should have been the FLARE 333 MWe plant instead**: Given that the projectile pilot was abandoned and many parameter values are actually drawn from FLARE-era sources, there is a question of whether the design-point selection is optimal. This is out of scope for model_critic — it is a fixed input from the upstream design-point table. But the degree of stitching from post-pivot sources into a pre-pivot design point is notable and may warrant a design-point re-evaluation at the upstream level.

- **The `installation_frac` workaround**: The spec-level correction for C220111 (lines 56–86 of `model_setup.py`) is clever and mathematically sound, but it is working around a library limitation (C220111 computed from pre-override subtotals). I did not flag this as an issue because the analyst documents it thoroughly and the output ($42.2M) is reasonable. It is worth noting as a library-level improvement candidate.
