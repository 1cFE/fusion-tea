### F-1: Hawker (2020) parameter data now available — should update Section 5 and gain-threshold framing

- **Target:** Section 5 (LCOE-Relevant Parameters) and Section 2 (Target gain challenge, Modeling Approach)
- **Finding:** The analysis explicitly states that Hawker (2020) is "Not available as an extracted source in this analysis" (Section 8, source 6) and lists net thermal efficiency as "not-yet-sourced" with the note "Hawker (2020) IFE framework may address" (Gap #6). The paper is now available as an extracted source (pmc-articles-pmc7658748.md). It contains specific LCOE parameter ranges and Pearson correlations that should populate Section 5: driver cost $2–10/J (NIF ~$9.5/J, Machine Three ~$1.7/J), plant cost $1,000–6,000/kWe, target cost threshold effect at ~$10/target, and driver lifetime as a separately ranked parameter (correlation −0.134, stronger than driver capital cost at +0.075). Additionally, the paper challenges the "200× minimum" binary framing used throughout the analysis: Hawker shows that gains ≤100 remain viable under favorable cost parameters, that gain >400 shows diminishing LCOE returns, and that the commercial threshold is a continuous gain-LCOE relationship, not a cliff edge. The analysis currently treats 200× as a physics-like hard threshold ("commercial binary threshold," "cliff edge in the parameter space") rather than as a default-parameter-dependent breakeven.
- **Recommendation:** (1) Update the Section 5 parameter table to populate the FLARE/Machine Three driver cost data point from Hawker ($1.7/J for Machine Three), and add the Hawker-derived plant cost range ($1,000–6,000/kWe) and target cost threshold ($10/target) as sourced reference ranges. Update Gap #6 status from "not-yet-sourced" to "sourced" with thermal efficiency 30–60% per Hawker's model sweep. (2) In Section 2's gain discussion and Modeling Approach, soften the binary threshold framing: note that Hawker's analysis (the paper's author is FLF's CEO) shows gain ≤100 is viable under optimistic cost scenarios and that the 200× floor is the threshold under default/central cost assumptions — the cost model should sweep gain from 10× to 1000× as a continuous sensitivity rather than treating 200× as a go/no-go gate.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Driver lifetime (reliability) is a distinct and more important LCOE lever than driver capital cost — not currently separated in the analysis

- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenge 1: Driver cost) and Section 5 (Missing Parameters table)
- **Finding:** The analysis treats the electromagnetic gun as a single "driver capital cost" challenge, framing it as "the plant's most capital-intensive novel component" (Section 2) and listing "electromagnetic gun capital cost" as the blocking missing parameter (Section 5, Gap #1). However, Hawker (2020) finds that driver lifetime (number of shots before failure/replacement) has a stronger LCOE correlation (−0.134) than driver capital cost (+0.075) across the ICF design space. For projectile ICF specifically, this distinction matters: bore erosion at 60 km/s, barrel replacement frequency, sabot lifetime, and launcher component wear under repetitive high-energy shots are a distinct engineering challenge from the one-time construction cost of Machine 4. A driver that is cheap to build but wears out after 10^5 shots (requiring replacement every ~11 days at 0.1 Hz) is far more costly over a 40-year plant life than a more expensive driver that achieves 10^9 shots. The analysis's current framing — "the EM launcher is the dominant unknown capital item" — collapses these two separate failure modes into one account, which would cause a cost model to miss the lifetime-driven cost trajectory.
- **Recommendation:** In Section 2's Challenge 1 discussion, add a paragraph separating driver capital cost from driver operational lifetime as distinct LCOE levers. Note that Hawker's model shows driver lifetime is the more important of the two. For projectile ICF, identify bore erosion rate, sabot/projectile material degradation, and capacitor/switch replacement frequency as the lifetime sub-parameters that need bounding. In Section 5's Missing Parameters table, add a new row for "Driver operational lifetime / shot count to failure" (gap type: truly-unknown, criticality: blocking) distinct from the existing "electromagnetic gun capital cost" row. In the Modeling Approach (Section 2), add driver lifetime as a third primary swept parameter alongside gain and driver capital cost.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: EM pump source (UCRL-53356) provides direct liquid Li ICF pumping analogue — partially fills Gap #8

- **Target:** Section 6, Gap #8 (Liquid lithium pump power and capital cost)
- **Finding:** OSTI source osti-servlets-purl-6360934.md is UCRL-53356 (Baker et al., LLNL, 1983), a detailed design study of electromagnetic pumps for liquid lithium circulation in the HYLIFE ICF reactor. It provides: target flow rate 8.08 m³/s, head 82.5 kPa, expected pump efficiency 50–60% for both helical-rotor (HREMP) and annular-linear induction pump (ALIP) designs, and a parametric design space for pump mass, diameter, and winding type. This is a direct engineering analogue for FLF's flowing liquid lithium curtain system — both use high-flow-rate liquid lithium circulation in an ICF reactor context. The analysis currently lists Gap #8 as "not-yet-sourced, important" with the note "Laser ICF liquid wall studies (FLiBe pumping) are partial analogues." UCRL-53356 is a better analogue (same fluid, same reactor type) and is now available. Using 50–60% pump efficiency and the HYLIFE flow parameters as a scaling baseline, a rough recirculating power fraction for FLF's design is now estimable, which is not currently done in the analysis.
- **Recommendation:** Update Gap #8 in Section 6 from "not-yet-sourced" to "partially-sourced" and cite UCRL-53356 as providing a 50–60% pump efficiency baseline for liquid Li ICF circulation. Add a note in Section 3 (Flowing Liquid Lithium Chamber maturity discussion) or Section 4 (Liquid Lithium supply chain section) that the recirculating power fraction for FLF's curtain can be rough-bounded using HYLIFE EM pump data, and provide that bound — e.g., at 8 m³/s flow and 50% efficiency the electrical pump power at HYLIFE scale is estimable, giving an order-of-magnitude check on whether lithium pumping is a trivial or significant recirculating fraction for a 333 MWe plant.
- **Priority:** minor
