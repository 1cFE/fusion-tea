VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: No IFE-internal nearest-neighbor positioning
- **Target:** Section 1 / opening framing
- **Finding:** The analysis clearly places projectile ICF within IFE (Goal 1) and provides a thorough CAS-level comparison to a conventional tokamak, but never explicitly names and compares the 2-3 nearest-neighbor concepts within IFE. Laser direct-drive ICF, laser indirect-drive ICF, and heavy-ion ICF are the natural comparators — they share the pellet implosion approach but differ in driver type, gain requirements, and driver economics. These appear only as incidental references (NIF for world-record context; "26-laser-icf-indirect-drive" for liquid wall analogue), not as a structured comparison. The checklist requires naming nearest neighbors and stating what is shared vs. differentiated within that peer group.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short nearest-neighbor comparison (before or at the start of Section 2) naming the 2-3 closest IFE approaches and explicitly stating what projectile ICF shares with them (pellet implosion physics, high gain requirement, D-T fuel cycle, pulsed chamber design) and what it uniquely changes (driver type, driver cost structure, no precision beam optics, no hohlraum required in direct-drive analogues). This positions the concept within the IFE family, not only against the tokamak reference.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Liquid Li chamber consequence-of-failure not stated
- **Target:** Section 2, Challenge 4; Section 2 hypotheses
- **Finding:** The analysis states explicit commercial consequences for two of the five key challenges: gain shortfall triggers a rep-rate cliff edge (Hypothesis 3), and driver cost sensitivity is swept as a testable proposition (Hypothesis 2). But Challenge 4 (liquid Li chamber) lists engineering uncertainties and novel costs without stating what happens commercially if the chamber proves unviable. The chamber is doubly load-bearing: it is both the first wall / structural containment and the sole tritium breeding system (TBR 1.8). If it fails — due to blast-loading dynamics, liquid metal containment cost, or pump power that consumes an unacceptable recirculating fraction — the concept loses both its "no vessel replacement" cost advantage and its high-TBR advantage simultaneously. This is not framed as a consequence or scenario branch. Goal 5 requires stating what happens if each key technical bet fails.
- **Recommendation:** Add a consequence-of-failure statement for the liquid Li chamber, structured as a scenario branch: if the flowing curtain proves unviable, (a) does a solid-wall fallback exist and at what capital cost premium, and (b) does falling back to a lower-TBR blanket push projectile ICF into the same tritium-startup-constrained position as other D-T IFE approaches? This does not require numerical estimates — a qualitative framing of the failure mode and its cascading impact on the LCOE structure is sufficient.
- **Priority:** important
