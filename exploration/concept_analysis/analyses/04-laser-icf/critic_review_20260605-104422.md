

# Critic Review — 04-laser-icf

## Headline issues

1. **CAS22 at 1 GWe is 18x below the comparable median, driven by the laser driver (C220104) not scaling with n_mod.** — The sanity check flags CAS22 at 1307.6 M$ against a comparable median of 22651.4 M$ (ratio 0.06). The dominant sub-account is C220104 (laser driver) at 288.4 M$ — identical at native, generic, and 1 GWe. This means the library's laser-driver cost is a flat constant that does not scale with plant power at all. At 1 GWe with n_mod=2, each module carries the same 288.4 M$ driver, giving a total plant driver cost of ~577 M$ — still far below what any laser ICF plant study suggests for a PW-class driver system. The analyst correctly identifies the driver as "the dominant capital cost item" and notes "no company-published cost figure exists," but the consequence is that the entire model is running on the library's unvalidated laser-driver default, which is almost certainly too low for a 30 PW CPA system. The analyst should add a prominent caveat that the LCOE is dominated by a library default for an account the analysis explicitly cannot ground.

2. **The 18 outlier flags in the sanity check are almost entirely a consequence of two structural facts — aneutronic cost elimination and the unoverridable annual-cost accounts — but neither is surfaced as a model-level caveat on the LCOE headline number.** — The CAS30–CAS90 outlier-low flags (ratios 0.05–0.13) are all downstream of the small CAS22 and the two-knob scaling arithmetic: a smaller overnight cost produces proportionally smaller IDC (CAS30), construction management (CAS50), contingency (CAS60), owner's cost (CAS70), and decommissioning (CAS90). This is mechanically correct given the inputs. However, CAS70 and CAS80 are independently overstated (DT-scale O&M and fuel costs for an aneutronic concept), which means the LCOE is simultaneously understated on capital and overstated on operations — errors that partially cancel. The analysis.md documents both framework limitations clearly, but the model_output.txt headline LCOE of 79.3 $/MWh carries no visible caveat that it is the product of partially offsetting errors.

3. **C220107 override is marked `provenance: direct` but is actually an architectural inference, not a company-published figure.** — The rationale correctly states "HB11 uses a laser driver, not a pulsed-power capacitor bank." This is a sound architectural argument for zeroing the account, but "direct" provenance means the company published this exact dollar figure. Setting a value to zero based on subsystem absence is a derived judgment (the subsystem doesn't exist, therefore its cost is zero). This should be `provenance: derived`. The distinction matters for downstream trust calibration: a "direct" tag implies a traceable published number, and auditors will look for one.

4. **C220111 (Installation & Assembly) at 75.1 M$ is 4x the comparable median (18.4 M$) with no override and no explanation in analysis.md.** — The sanity check flags this as `outlier_high`. The analysis Section 5b explicitly states "No company data. No override." But the 4x divergence from the comparable is architecturally surprising — HB11's reactor is described as "a largely empty metal sphere," which should be simpler to install than a DT laser-ICF system. If the library default is driven by the DT archetype's complex blanket/shield installation, the default is likely overstated for this concept. The analyst should either override it downward (consistent with the simplification narrative) or explain why installation cost remains high despite the simplified reactor.

## Detailed reasoning

### Deterministic-flag interpretation

**dpc (ok):** P_native is coherent at 500 MWe across all three legs. No action needed.

**contract (ok):** The module contract is satisfied via the helper form. No action needed.

**count_smell (ok):** 7 enabled overrides within the 6–12 band for Low archetype fit. The 2 disabled overrides (CAS70, CAS80) are well-documented framework limitations. No action needed.

**sanity (flagged, 18 outlier accounts):** This is the most substantive flag. The 18 outliers decompose into three clusters:

- *CAS22 sub-accounts (C220101, C220104, C220105, C220106, C220106_pump, C220106_vessel, C220107, C220110, C220111):* The low-outliers (C220101 at 0.19x, C220104 at 0.14x, C220107 at 0.0x, C220110 at 0.34x) are all either explicitly overridden (C220101, C220107, C220110) or are the un-overridden library default for the driver (C220104). The high-outliers (C220105 at 2.2x, C220106 at 5.3x, C220106_pump at 5.0x, C220106_vessel at 2.6x, C220111 at 4.1x) are all library defaults with no override. The divergence direction suggests the library's LASER_IFE archetype has a different internal cost structure than the comparable (concept 23), which is plausible since concept 23 may have its own overrides. The concerning item is C220111 — see headline issue #4.

- *CAS-level outliers (CAS22, CAS30, CAS50, CAS60, CAS70, CAS80, CAS90):* CAS22 is the aggregate of the sub-account divergences above. CAS30–CAS90 are all mechanically derived from the overnight cost and therefore inherit whatever direction CAS22 goes. The cluster of outlier-low at 0.05–0.13x is dramatic but mechanically explained.

- *CAS40 (outlier_high at 2.2x):* CAS40 at 14.1 M$ vs. median 6.3 M$ is a library default. The analysis does not discuss turbine-plant accessories separately. This is minor.

No drift block is present, confirming the recorded model_output.txt matches a live re-import.

### Spec coherence (beyond P_native)

The analysis describes one named design point — "HB11 Energy 500 MWe technoeconomic model scenario (McKenzie et al. 2023)" — but it is transparently stitching across two published designs: the 2018 patent (reactor architecture, consumable magnetic field device, direct electrostatic conversion, vessel geometry) and the 2023 paper (power-loop economics, gain requirements, LCOE boundary conditions). The analysis.md Section 5 consistency notes are commendably explicit about the inconsistencies between these two sources (G ~33,000 in the patent vs. G 100–300 in the paper; DEC in the patent vs. steam cycle on the 2025 website). This stitching is unavoidable given the data — there is no single coherent engineering design to model — and the analyst handles it well by documenting the contradictions and choosing conservative assumptions (thermal conversion, lower gain).

The `spec` dictionary in model_setup.py carries `plasma_t=4.0` (library default), which is architecturally questionable: the patent describes a sphere ≥1 m diameter (0.5 m radius), and the analyst notes the library default 4.0 m is used "pending data." A 4 m chamber radius for what's described as a compact stainless steel sphere is a significant mismatch. However, this parameter's cost impact flows primarily through C220106_vessel and the shielding accounts, which are relatively small. The analyst should note this mismatch more prominently but it is not LCOE-critical.

### Override discipline (judgment)

**C220101 (5% of generic, enabled, derived):** Sound. Architectural elimination of tritium-breeding blanket is well-grounded in the aneutronic fuel cycle. The 5% residual is a reasonable allowance for a minimal first-wall structure. Not "direct" — correctly labeled derived.

**C220102 (5% of generic, enabled, derived):** Sound. Same logic as C220101. The ~0.1% neutron fraction is well-cited. 5% residual is conservative.

**C220107 (0.0, enabled, "direct"):** The zero value is architecturally correct — no capacitor bank exists. But provenance is mislabeled. "Direct" means a company published this dollar figure. The correct provenance is "derived" (inferred from subsystem absence). See headline issue #3.

**C220108 (100.0 M$, enabled, derived):** This is the weakest override. The $100 M$ is described as "a placeholder... analogous to other IFE target factory estimates" but no specific IFE target factory cost study is cited. McKenzie's "several dollars per target" is an acceptability threshold, not a cost — the analysis correctly identifies this. The override is doing real work (generic is 183.7 M$, override is 100.0 M$, a 46% reduction), but the basis is essentially a round-number guess. The analysis is honest about this ("Highly uncertain"), which is the right posture at Low archetype fit.

**C220110 (15% of generic, enabled, derived):** Sound. The architectural argument (no rad-hardening) is well-grounded. 15% residual for mechanical handling is reasonable.

**CAS21 (50% of generic, enabled, derived):** Sound directionally. The enumeration of eliminated facilities (tritium building, hot cells, biological shielding, cryogenic prep) is specific and traceable. Whether 50% is the right fraction is unprovable without a bottom-up building estimate, but the qualitative direction is clearly correct and the analysis documents the reasoning.

**CAS27 (1.0 M$, enabled, derived):** Sound. This override is doing real work (generic shows 0.0, so the override actually *adds* $1M). The rationale is correct — commodity boron and hydrogen replace tritium startup inventory. The $1M is a placeholder but the order-of-magnitude is right.

**CAS70 (disabled):** Correctly disabled with clear rationale. The framework limitation is properly documented with a specific issue reference (`1cFE/1costingfe#31`).

**CAS80 (disabled):** Same as CAS70 — correctly disabled, properly documented.

**Override count and real work:** Of the 7 enabled overrides, all are doing real work (all change the account value by more than 10% from default). The disabled overrides are both analytically justified and properly blocked. The override discipline is solid for a Low archetype-fit concept.

### Two-knob projection (judgment)

The native-to-1GWe projection shows the expected pattern: per-module accounts (C220101–C220112, CAS21) stay constant, while n_mod-sensitive accounts (C220200, C220300, CAS23, CAS24, etc.) roughly double. This is mechanically correct.

The concerning pattern is that C220104 (laser driver) is 288.4 M$ at all three scales — generic, native, and 1 GWe. This means the library treats the laser driver as a flat-cost item that does not scale with plant power. At 500 MWe native, this gives a driver-to-net-power ratio of ~577 $/kW. At 1 GWe (n_mod=2), total driver cost is ~577 M$, giving ~577 $/kW — the same ratio because each module has its own driver. This is plausible for an IFE architecture (each module needs its own laser), but the absolute magnitude is almost certainly too low for a 30 PW CPA system. National Ignition Facility cost ~$3.5B for a much larger but lower-intensity system; scaling down to a single 30 PW beamline does not obviously get you to $288 M$. This is the library's problem, not the analyst's, but it dominates the LCOE.

### Family delta vs comparables

The family-delta discussion in Section 7 is thorough and well-structured. The key architectural divergences (driver architecture, target physics, energy conversion, scale) are all correctly identified and their cost directions assessed.

However, the sanity check reveals that HB11's CAS22 at 1 GWe (1307.6 M$) is dramatically below the comparable (concept 23's implied CAS22 from the median of 22651.4 M$). This 17x difference is not explained in the family-delta section. If concept 23 has a $2B driver override (mentioned in analysis.md §7 as "Driver cost $2B (weakly grounded)"), then much of the CAS22 gap comes from driver cost treatment: HB11 uses the library default (~288 M$) while concept 23 overrides to $2B. The family-delta section should explicitly note this — the two concepts' CAS22 values are not comparable because they reflect different override strategies for the driver, not different physics.

### Gaps and load-bearing assumptions

The two assumptions that, if wrong, would most change the LCOE conclusion:

1. **Laser driver cost (C220104 = 288.4 M$):** This is the library default, not an analyst override. If the true cost of a 30 PW CPA system is $1–3B (plausible by analogy to NIF and scaling studies), the overnight cost roughly doubles and LCOE exceeds 150 $/MWh. This gap is identified in Section 6 (gap #2, criticality: blocking) but is not flagged as the single most LCOE-sensitive assumption.

2. **Target gain physics (G = 100–300):** If the physics doesn't work, there is no reactor and LCOE is undefined. This is correctly identified as gap #1 (criticality: blocking) and is discussed extensively. However, for the *cost model's* LCOE sensitivity, the gain doesn't appear as a model parameter — it is embedded in the power balance via `q_eng` which the spec doesn't override. The LCOE is insensitive to gain *within the model* because the model takes P_native as given; the gain question is whether the plant exists at all, not what it costs.

The analysis.md Section 6 correctly identifies both as blocking gaps. The gap inventory is well-constructed and appropriately prioritized.

## What I deliberately did not say

- **Source quality of McKenzie et al. 2023:** The paper is authored by HB11's managing director and contains no independent validation. The analysis notes this ("authored by HB11 Energy's managing director and team — there is no independent validation"). Whether this source should be trusted at all for a technoeconomic analysis is a research-stage concern, not a model_critic concern. Route to research-stage review if this needs adjudication.

- **Whether the 500 MWe design point should have been selected:** The alternatives (patent single-unit, website 1 GW target) are listed in the upstream table. The selection rationale is that McKenzie 2023 is the "most specific electrical output figure traceable to a company source." This is a defensible choice but not mine to re-debate per the review scope.

- **Whether the LASER_IFE archetype is appropriate for a proton-fast-ignition concept:** HB11's physics (picosecond CPA, proton fast ignition, p-B11, kilotesla magnetic confinement) is far from the nanosecond DT-ICF the archetype was designed for. The Low archetype-fit grade captures this, but one could argue the archetype is so far off that the entire library cost structure is misleading rather than merely imprecise. I lack the data to make this argument rigorously — it would require comparing the library's internal cost-scaling functions against what a p-B11 fast-ignition system actually looks like, which no published study provides.

- **The `plasma_t=4.0` mismatch with the patent's ≥1m sphere:** I flagged this in spec coherence but did not make it a headline issue because the cost sensitivity to chamber radius in the LASER_IFE archetype is unclear without running a parameter sweep. It may matter more than I can demonstrate from the artifacts alone.
