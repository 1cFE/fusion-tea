### F-1: CAS21 civil works scenario sweep absent despite flagged undercosting
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Approach), Section 6 (Gap #15), model_setup.py
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The analysis explicitly states that CAS21 ($622M) is "likely undercosted" because a ~3 km accelerator tunnel is qualitatively different from the tokamak building geometry used to derive the per-MW scaling, and attributes the ~$60–64/MWh gap between model LCOE (~$98/MWh) and the inflation-adjusted HYLIFE-II historical reference (~$162/MWh) partly to this underestimate. Yet no CAS21 scenario sweep exists. The driver capital sweep tests 5 scenarios across a 3.6× range ($700M–$2,500M); the availability sweep tests 5 scenarios; but the CAS21 uncertainty — which could plausibly be $500M–$1,500M above baseline — is tracked only as a gap note (Gap #15). Without a quantified CAS21 sweep, the lower bound framing of the model LCOE cannot be bounded, and the historical-model gap cannot be attributed or reconciled.
- **Recommendation:** Add a CAS21 scenario sweep to model_setup.py and the model output, analogous in structure to the driver capital sweep. Cover at least three points: (1) framework default as-is (~$622M, current base case), (2) 2× scaling to represent accelerator tunnel civil works premium (~$1,244M), (3) 3× scaling as conservative upper bound (~$1,866M). Report resulting LCOE for each scenario. Add a note in Section 2 (Modeling Approach) explaining which fraction of the historical-model gap is attributable to CAS21 at each scenario point vs. driver capital.
- **Priority:** blocking

### F-2: Model operating point inconsistent with HYLIFE-II reference design
- **Target:** Model header (model_output.txt), model_setup.py
- **Category:** model
- **Finding:** The model header explicitly annotates two deviations from the HYLIFE-II reference: Driver E/shot = 7.07 MJ vs. HYLIFE-II stated 5 MJ, and Q_sci = 62.8 vs. HYLIFE-II nominal 70. At 6 Hz, the model's operating point implies fusion power of ~2,664 MW (7.07 MJ × 62.8 × 6 Hz), while HYLIFE-II achieves 940 MWe net from 350 MJ/shot × 6 Hz = ~2,100 MW fusion. The model fusion power (2,667 MW) is ~27% higher than the reference design for the same net electric output. The analysis brackets these discrepancies but does not explain whether they are (a) a framework back-calculation artifact from driving to 940 MWe net with specific eta_pin/eta_th/Q_eng inputs, (b) a deliberate modeling choice, or (c) a miscalibration. This ambiguity undermines confidence in C220104 = $1.4B as calibrated to the correct design point — the driver cost scales with beam energy, so a 7 MJ driver costs more than a 5 MJ driver, yet the same historical estimate is used.
- **Recommendation:** Add a note in model_setup.py and/or the model output explaining the source of the driver energy and target gain deviations from the HYLIFE-II reference. If the deviation is a framework back-calculation artifact, explain which input constraints drive it and whether C220104 should be adjusted proportionally to the higher beam energy (7.07 MJ vs. 5 MJ). If the deviations are intentional, state the basis. The analysis should clarify whether the model is best read as an HYLIFE-II archetype or as a self-consistent model that happens to use HYLIFE-II economic data at a different operating point.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Projectile ICF comparison is a single sentence; TEA-level differentiators are unstated
- **Target:** Section 7 (Cross-Concept Notes)
- **Category:** analysis
- **Finding:** Concept 22 (Projectile ICF, First Light Fusion / NearStar) is the most structurally similar IFE neighbor: both are non-laser IFE approaches, both have high driver efficiency relative to laser ICF, both eliminate optics in the chamber, and both face target fabrication and rep-rate chamber challenges. The analysis gives laser ICF and MagLIF substantive comparative treatment but reduces the Projectile ICF comparison to one sentence acknowledging only the shared "no optics in chamber" property. The TEA-level differentiators — EM gun driver capital structure vs. induction linac, sub-Hz vs. 5–15 Hz rep rate, solid projectile consumable vs. cryogenic DT sphere — are unaddressed. A reader comparing HIF cost models against concept 22 has no analysis guidance on which cost drivers differ and in which direction.
- **Recommendation:** Expand the Projectile ICF comparison in Section 7 to cover three TEA-level differentiators: (1) driver cost structure (EM gun is orders of magnitude simpler than a km-scale induction linac but may require per-shot replaceable components — how does this trade against HIF's reusable driver?); (2) rep rate (sub-Hz projectile vs. 5–15 Hz HIF, and the LCOE implications of lower availability from lower shot rate); (3) target economics (HIF's cryogenic DT sphere vs. concept 22's solid projectile target — which faces larger per-unit cost at commercial scale?). Three to four sentences per differentiator is sufficient.
- **Priority:** minor
