### F-1: Failure consequences not stated for two of three key technical bets
- **Target:** Section 2 (Challenges #1 and #3) and Section 5 (Key Hypotheses)
- **Finding:** The analysis identifies three key technical bets but states failure consequences for only one. H3 (rep rate) explicitly says "at 0.1 Hz... concept uncompetitive regardless of fuel cycle advantages." But Challenge #3 (compression ratio shortfall) calls the 8:1 vs. 12:1 gap a "binary risk" without stating the consequence: if 12:1 cannot be achieved in flowing liquid metal, plasma cannot reach fusion conditions — this is a concept-level physics failure, not a cost uncertainty. Challenge #1 (commercial pneumatic system feasibility) describes it as a "fundamental LCOE modeling challenge" without stating the consequence: if synchronized piston operation in the liquid metal environment proves infeasible, there is no fallback compression mechanism in the commercial design. Without explicit failure consequences, these risks cannot be captured in the TEA as scenario branches or explicit flags (Goal 5).
- **Recommendation:** For each named technical bet, add one sentence stating what happens if the bet is lost: for compression ratio, state that failure to reach 12:1 is a physics viability failure (not a cost parameter); for commercial pneumatics, state that failure would require a fundamental architecture change and eliminates the no-magnet cost advantage. Frame these alongside H3 in the hypotheses section as explicit failure modes, not just modeling challenges.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Known cost-advantage parameters absent from Section 5 parameter table
- **Target:** Section 5 (Available Parameters table) and Section 7 (TEA Implications by Differentiator)
- **Finding:** The TEA implications table (Section 7) correctly identifies two first-order cost advantages: (1) CAS22 ≈ $0 — no HTS magnet system or cryoplant — and (2) per-shot consumables ≈ $0 — liquid metal liner recycles rather than being destroyed. Both are identified as cost-relevant differentiators (Goal 3) but neither has a row in the Section 5 Available Parameters table. In a free-form parametric model anchored on this concept's cost structure, these zeroed-out accounts are explicit modeling inputs — not narrative context. Without parameter rows, the modeling recommendation (Section 5) captures only the unknowns and uncertainties, not the known structural advantages. A modeler building the TEA from Section 5 alone would not have CAS22=0 or target-cost-per-shot=0 as inputs.
- **Recommendation:** Add two rows to the Section 5 Available Parameters table: one for "HTS magnet system + cryoplant capital (CAS22)" with value "$0 — eliminated by design" and high confidence, and one for "Consumable cost per pulse (target/liner)" with value "$0 — liquid metal recycles; no destroyed targets" and high confidence. Cite the Section 7 analysis and the MagLIF contrast for each.
- **Priority:** important
