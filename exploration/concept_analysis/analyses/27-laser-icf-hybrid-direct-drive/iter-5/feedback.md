VERDICT: FINDINGS

### F-1: FOAK/NOAK base case inconsistency
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Approach)
- **Finding:** The analysis declares "Primary modeling target: Athena 400 MWe FOAK pilot" and explicitly warns that "The NOAK commercial plant has no published design-point specification and must be treated as a separate parameter branch." Yet the model base scenario is NOAK at $70/J laser. The FOAK design point ($110/J → 144.3 $/MWh) appears only as a single row in the H-1 scenario table. A reader interpreting the 115.8 $/MWh base LCOE as a FOAK Athena estimate would be misled — the base reflects optimistic NOAK assumptions that the analysis itself says cannot be grounded in a published design-point spec.
- **Recommendation:** Reconcile the stated primary target with the model base case. Either: (a) make FOAK ($110/J) the model base and NOAK the aspirational scenario, consistent with the Athena anchor language; or (b) revise the Modeling Approach to explicitly state that NOAK is the base because the TEA goal is commercial viability assessment, and designate FOAK as the near-term reference branch. The current text says one thing and the model does another without explanation.
- **Priority:** important

### F-2: Total plant capex multiplier understated relative to model
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Approach, Top LCOE Sensitivity Parameters item 1) and model output
- **Finding:** The analysis states "Total plant capex is likely 2–3× the laser cost after chamber, BOP, and indirect costs." The model shows all-in capital of $3,219M against a laser cost of $700M — approximately 4.6× (or ~3.6× on an overnight basis excluding IDC and financing). This is a material discrepancy: the 2–3× framing implies laser improvements translate more directly into LCOE improvements than the model's cost structure actually supports. At 3.6–4.6×, a 10% laser cost reduction produces only a ~2–3% LCOE improvement, not the ~4–5% implied by the 2–3× framing. This also affects the breakeven $/J calculation for LCOE parity with new fission — a hypothesis the analysis says the model should determine.
- **Recommendation:** Revise the multiplier statement in the Modeling Approach to match the model (approximately 3.5–4.5× depending on what cost components are included), or provide a derivation that reconciles the 2–3× claim with the CAS account totals. If the model's overhead structure is judged conservative (e.g., because Xcimer claims a simpler balance of plant), explain why and what the intended multiplier should be.
- **Priority:** important

### F-3: Target fabrication treatment left structurally unresolved
- **Target:** Section 2 (Modeling Approach, LCOE floor note) and Section 6 (gap table, row 4)
- **Finding:** The analysis correctly identifies target fabrication as a blocking gap, notes the base LCOE excludes it entirely, and declares the H-4 addendum table "required." However, it does not specify whether this off-model treatment is a permanent architectural choice or a placeholder pending cost data. At the Goodin threshold ($2.50/target), target fabrication contributes ~$11/MWh — approximately 9% of the realistic central LCOE. A cost item of this size needs a defined CAS home: CAS80 (fuel cost analogue, since targets are per-shot consumables analogous to fuel), a new dedicated account, or an explicitly permanent addendum outside the cost model. Without this decision, any future pass that incorporates target cost data has no designated place to insert it, and the base LCOE will continue to be presented as a lower bound with no clear path to becoming a realistic central estimate.
- **Recommendation:** State explicitly in the Modeling Approach whether target fabrication is: (a) a CAS80 fuel-cost analogue to be incorporated into the model when cost data becomes available, or (b) a permanent off-model addendum because its cost structure (per-shot, not per-kWh) doesn't map cleanly to CAS80 recurring fuel cost. If (b), the analysis should state that the "realistic central LCOE" is always the base plus H-4 at the Goodin threshold, and this addendum is not a temporary gap but a structural feature of IFE cost modeling with no MFE equivalent.
- **Priority:** important
