Background

What Phase 1 Established

Before building cost models, we needed to understand the structure of the design space. Phase 1 tested whether morphological analysis could explain ~38 active fusion concepts. The answer was no — but the failure was deeply informative.

Phase 1a

Building the Differentiation Table

38 concepts × 12 design columns. Each cell: a controlled-vocabulary value, N/A (structurally inapplicable), or TBD (unknown).

Result: One column — "Confinement Concept" — had 29 unique values for 38 rows. It was nearly a unique identifier, doing all the descriptive work. The table was a lookup, not an analysis.
Phase 1b v2

Restructuring Reveals Context-Dependence

Decomposed the overloaded "Confinement Concept" column into an 8-column hierarchy. Resulting table: 38 × 18 columns.

Deeper questions → narrower applicability → more N/A. Every N/A traces to an upstream design choice. This is the empirical signature of context-dependence.

Verdict

Classification Scheme, Not Design Space

The table correctly categorizes by physics approach but cannot explain within-family divergence, cannot generate viable concepts, and has only ~2 effective degrees of freedom. Structure is a shallow tree, not a Cartesian grid.

Motivation

The Problem Phase 2 Addresses

The table records WHAT each concept chose. Phase 2 asks: can we build a representation that captures WHY?

What the Table Says

"CFS builds a compact tokamak with HTS magnets and D-T fuel."

"TAE pursues p-B11 fuel in an FRC."

What the Reasoning Reveals

"CFS bets on high-B field (HTS-enabled) → compact device → economically competitive — but creates cascading challenges: blanket space, disruption severity, neutron flux density."

"TAE's fuel choice eliminates the entire neutron/tritium problem set while creating an extreme temperature challenge no other concept faces."

Four Tests for the Reasoning Tree

Test 1

Negative Space Explanation

Explain why certain combos don't exist (no p-B11 tokamak, no steady-state IFE)

Test 2

Constraint Validation

Do first-principles constraints hold against 38 real concepts? Target: >70% validated

Test 3

Divergence Depth

Does the tree provide richer reasoning than category labels at branch points?

Test 4

Concept Family Recovery

Do tree paths arrive at recognizable families (tokamak, stellarator, laser ICF)?

Framework

The Six Requirement Lenses

Every fusion power plant must satisfy six universal requirements. These are not tree nodes — they are lenses applied at every decision point, producing context-dependent analysis.

⛽
R1

Fuel the Reaction

D-T → where does tritium come from? p-B11 → commercially available (trivial). The fuel choice cascades.

🔥
R2

Achieve Fusion Conditions

Confine, heat, stabilize. How you do each depends on everything upstream.

⚡
R3

Produce Net Energy

Gain, driver efficiency, recirculating power. The economics hinge here.

🔋
R4

Extract Usable Energy

Neutrons vs. charged particles? Thermal cycle vs. direct conversion? Geometry-dependent.

☢️
R5

Manage Nuclear Environment

Shielding, activation, waste classification, tritium safety — all concept-dependent.

🏗️
R6

Maintain Structural Integrity

Neutron damage, thermal loads, cyclic stress. Same requirement, completely different challenges per concept.

Algorithm

How the Reasoning Tree Works

A three-step loop executed one tree level at a time: expand, validate, review. Each node sees only its own path from root — no cross-branch memory.

1

Expand

LLM receives accumulated context + R1–R6 lenses + current question. Produces options, constraints, negative space, and new downstream questions.

2

Validate

Extract constraints into formal format. Map to table columns. Check against 38 real concepts. Check cross-branch consistency.

3

Review

Render tree + constraints as markdown. Human reviews physics, constraint quality, and selects branches for next expansion.

render.py

L0 Expansion

Root: How Should We Confine the Reaction?

No prior context. The LLM reasons from scratch about fundamental confinement approaches, producing 5 options, 5 negative-space entries, and 22 constraints.

Options Generated

Closed Magnetic Topology

MFE

Eliminate end losses via toroidal geometry. Trade-off: requires rotational transform — either from plasma current (disruption risk) or 3D coil complexity.

Constraints: 6 Questions: 6

Open Magnetic (Mirror)

MFE

Accept end losses for geometric simplicity. Compensate with magnetic plugging and/or direct energy conversion of escaping ions at >80% efficiency.

Constraints: 4 Questions: 5

Inertial Confinement

IFE

Invert the Lawson trade-off — extreme transient density (~1000× compression) instead of sustained confinement. Fundamentally pulsed architecture.

Constraints: 5 Questions: 6

Magnetized Target (MIF)

MIF

Embed magnetic field in target to reduce compression ratio. Enables efficient mechanical drivers at intermediate density between MFE and IFE.

Constraints: 4 Questions: 5

Electrostatic Potential Well

Exotic

Use electric fields to confine ions. Fundamentally limited by Earnshaw's theorem — Coulomb scattering cross-section exceeds fusion cross-section by ~10⁴.

Constraints: 3 Questions: 3
Negative Space: 5 Non-Viable Approaches L0
✕
Gravitational Confinement

Minimum mass for self-confinement exceeds ~10²⁹ kg (brown dwarf scale). The gravitational-to-electromagnetic force ratio is ~10⁻³⁶ — no terrestrial device can produce the required gravitational potential.

✕
Direct Material Containment

Plasma at 10⁸ K vs. wall materials at ~4000 K. The energy density gradient is insurmountable — no material survives direct contact with fusion plasma.

✕
Beam-Target at Sub-Thermal Energies

Coulomb scattering cross-section exceeds fusion cross-section by ~10⁴ at relevant energies. Net energy is impossible from this ratio alone — beam particles scatter before fusing.

✕
Chemical/Molecular Confinement

Fusion temperatures (~10 keV) exceed chemical bond energies (~eV) by four orders of magnitude. All molecular structure dissociates before fusion conditions are reached.

✕
Purely Radiative Confinement

Radiation pressure at fusion temperatures is ~10¹³ Pa, but producing this externally would consume more power than fusion produces — a fundamental R3 (net energy) violation.

Concept Family Recovery

Concepts emerged through reasoning about physics trade-offs, not by naming known devices:

"Plasma-current-driven transform → simple axisymmetric coils, disruption risk, current-drive power" → Tokamak family
"External 3D coils → disruption-free, complex coil geometry, fixed transform profile" → Stellarator family
"Hybrid → larger optimization space, graceful degradation vs. catastrophic disruption" → Advanced / quasi-symmetric
Negative Space: 4 Non-Viable Approaches L1

These are non-viable specifically because we chose closed magnetic topology — demonstrating context-dependent negative space:

✕
No Rotational Transform at All

Charge separation from grad-B drifts destroys confinement in microseconds. Direct R2 violation — the topology demands transform.

✕
Pulsed Inductive Drive Only

Finite flux swing limits pulse duration. For power plants, this produces unacceptable duty cycle — fundamentally incompatible with steady electricity generation.

✕
Open Field Lines Within Closed Geometry

Field lines terminating on material surfaces defeats the purpose of closing the geometry — you get mirror-like end losses without mirror-like geometric simplicity.

✕
Passive (Uncontrolled) Plasma Current

Resistive decay and uncontrolled profile evolution lead to instability. R2 and R6 demand active control of the current profile.

Notable L1 Constraints 4 highlighted
FORCE R6 — Plasma-current option

Disruption risk is non-zero

"MHD theory shows that no known control scheme can guarantee disruption probability exactly zero over plant lifetime."

ELIMINATE R2 — External-coils option

Disruption risk eliminated by construction

"With no net current, the MHD free energy source for disruptions does not exist." — The same variable takes opposite values depending on the transform choice. This IS context-dependence.

FORCE R4 — External-coils option

Blanket coverage reduced by external coils

"Physical coil conductors, casings, and structural supports occupy radial and poloidal space around the plasma vessel." — Connects rotational-transform choice to a tritium-breeding consequence.

FORCE R2 — Hybrid option

Disruption character altered

"When external transform is present, losing plasma current does not immediately destroy confinement — the plasma settles to a lower-transform state." — A spectrum, not binary.

Insights

Key Early Observations

The Unmappability Gap Is Real and Informative

At L0, the LLM reasons about field topology, loss cones, and compression ratios. The table talks about Confinement Family and Magnet Type. These are different levels of abstraction. The reasoning tree exposes physics-level structure the classification scheme compressed away.

Requirement Lenses Produce Cross-Cutting Analysis

At every node, the LLM examines all six requirements against each option. This reveals trade-off structures the flat table's column-per-dimension format cannot represent — how R2 trades against R3 differently for mirrors (plug power) vs. tokamaks (current-drive power) vs. IFE (driver efficiency).

Negative Space Is the Strongest Early Signal

The explanations for why certain approaches don't work are precise, physics-grounded, and trace to specific requirement-context interactions. This is the explanatory power the flat table lacked entirely.

Open Questions

Design Issues for Deeper Expansion

Two design issues surfaced during early execution that need resolution before investing heavily in deeper expansion.

How should the "next question" be determined?

Currently, the first downstream question from each option automatically becomes the child node's question. The rest are discarded. The tree's entire branching structure is determined by the LLM's ordering within a single call — implicitly and invisibly.

A. Human selection after each expansion — most controlled, but slowest
B. Expand all questions as siblings — explores more, but multiplies LLM calls
C. LLM-ranked selection — second prompt to rank by architectural discrimination
D. Accept current approach — if the constraint registry matters more than tree shape

How should constraints be validated?

The current validation pipeline uses a hand-built vocabulary map (~90 entries) to translate LLM variables into table lookups. At L0, all 22 constraints are unmappable because the LLM reasons at the physics level, not the engineering-classification level.

A. Structured ontology — force LLM to use table vocabulary (increases mappable rate, may lose physics insight)
B. LLM-based qualitative assessment — flexible but introduces assessor bias
C. Cross-branch consistency — compare independently expanded branches (doesn't need column mapping)
D. Accept table validation may not be practical — value the constraint registry as a reasoning artifact
