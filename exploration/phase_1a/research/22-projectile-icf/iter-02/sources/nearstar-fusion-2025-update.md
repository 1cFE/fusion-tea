## Recent Developments

- **February 2025**: Virginia Venture Partners and Ecosphere Ventures invested in NearStar Fusion
- **2025 (planned)**: Will publish experimental results and detailed concept paper
- **Roadmap**: Break-even experiments in ~5 years; prototype power plant in ~10 years

## Technical Specifications (Confirmed)

### Driver
- **Type**: Hypervelocity plasma armature railgun
- **Projectile mass**: 50 grams
- **Velocity**: 10 km/s (~30× speed of sound)
- **Kinetic energy**: >1 MJ per shot
- **Repetition rate**: 1 Hz
- **Construction**: Commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) technologies

### Fuel
- **Primary**: Deuterium-Deuterium (D-D) — explicitly avoiding tritium
- **Rationale**: Eliminates tritium supply chains, breeding requirements, and first wall challenges
- **D-T**: Listed only as backup option

### Target Design
- Magnetized fuel targets (builds on Sandia Z-Machine metallic fuel target liner approach)
- "MTIF" = Magnetized Target Impact Fusion

### Power Plant
- **First wall**: Molten lead (minimizes neutron embrittlement, enables conventional materials)
- **Design philosophy**: Linear modularity, mass production, factory assembly
- **Scalability**: 50 MW to 1 GW+
- **Installation**: On-site in months, not years
- **Positioning**: "Retrofit the heat source in traditional hydrocarbon power plants"

## Classification Notes

NearStar's MTIF approach differs from pure Projectile ICF in key ways:
1. **Magnetized fuel** — fuel is magnetized during compression → closer to MIF than IFE
2. **D-D fuel preference** — not D-T
3. **Railgun driver** — related to but distinct from First Light's electromagnetic gun
4. **Z-Machine heritage** — explicitly builds on Sandia pulsed power liner implosion work

This concept may warrant its own row in the differentiation table (e.g., "Magnetized Target Impact Fusion") rather than inclusion under "Projectile ICF (D-T)".
