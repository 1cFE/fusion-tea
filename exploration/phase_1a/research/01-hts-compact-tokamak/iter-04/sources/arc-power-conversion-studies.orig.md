# ARC Power Conversion System Studies

## Source 1: Colliva et al. (2024)
**Title**: "Analysis of Power Conversion System Options for ARC-like Tokamak Fusion Reactor Balance of Plant"
**Published**: MDPI Sustainability, Vol 16, Issue 17, 7480 (2024)
**URL**: https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/17/7480
**Also**: SSRN preprint https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4482183

### Key Findings
- Studied three cycles for ARC-like reactor: supercritical steam Rankine, supercritical CO2 Brayton, supercritical helium Brayton
- Used GE GateCycle software for modeling
- **Rankine cycle**: Highest net efficiency, least mass flow (344 kg/s), smallest secondary heat exchanger, most commercially available and technologically mature
- **Brayton cycles (CO2, He)**: More compact configurations, but layout complexity and thermomechanical stress issues
- **Conclusion**: "Supercritical steam design resulted as the most promising solution among the investigated options"
- Rankine "currently meets the ARC design requirements"

## Source 2: Segantin et al. (2020)
**Title**: "Exploration of power conversion thermodynamic cycles for ARC fusion reactor"
**Published**: Fusion Engineering and Design, 2020
**URL**: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920379620301939

### Key Findings
- Earlier study of thermodynamic cycles for ARC
- Baseline ARC paper (Sorbom 2015) used Rankine at ~30% efficiency
- sCO2 Brayton could offer higher efficiency (up to 40%+)
- Both studies treat Rankine as the baseline/reference case

## Relevance to Differentiation Table
CFS has not publicly committed to a specific power conversion cycle. However, all published analyses use Rankine (steam) as the ARC baseline. `Thermal (steam)` remains the best-supported value with medium confidence — no CFS-authored statement specifies the commercial plant cycle.
