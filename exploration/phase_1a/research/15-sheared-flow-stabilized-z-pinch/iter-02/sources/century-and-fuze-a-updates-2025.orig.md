## Century Platform Progress

- Commissioned June 2024
- First vertical, liquid metal-lined and -cooled, high-repetition-rate Z-pinch plasma system
- Uses liquid bismuth (not LiPb — Century is an engineering demo, not a DT reactor)
- Initial operations: single shots every 10 seconds, ~1.4 kW average power
- By Feb 2025: DOE certified 1,080 consecutive shots over 3 hours without failure (DOE Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program)
- By Sept 2025: 39 kW average input power for 100+ shot campaigns, 0.2 Hz (one shot every 5 seconds)
- Target progression: 100 kW → 1 MW → 10 MW (commercial scale)
- Plasma current: up to 500 kA per pulse

## Century Paper (FST 2025)

- Title: "Century: Zap Energy's 100-kW-Scale Repetitive Sheared-Flow-Stabilized Z-Pinch System with Liquid Metal Cooling"
- Published in Fusion Science and Technology, 2025
- Describes integrated engineering test of high average power repetitive Z-pinch with liquid metal

## FuZE-A (Fifth Device)

- Announced late 2025 for commissioning ~Dec 2025 / Jan 2026
- Fifth SFS Z-pinch device overall
- Purpose: develop advanced accelerators for high-performance plasma stabilization
- Will operate alongside FuZE-3 and FuZE-Q

## APS DPP 2025 Presentation

- Presenter: Benjamin J. Levitt, Zap Energy Inc.
- Title: "Progress Towards Commercial Fusion Based on Zap Energy's Sheared-Flow-Stabilized Z-Pinch Concept"
- Key quote: SFS physics offers "a path toward pulsed, quasi-steady-state, Z pinches, with enhanced stability properties"
- Century integrates: repetitive pulsed power, high-duty-cycle cathodes, liquid metal wall systems

## Device Timeline (all SFS Z-pinch)

1. ZaP (University of Washington) — original research device
2. FuZE — first Zap Energy device
3. FuZE-Q — larger device, ~1 MJ capacitor bank, up to 1.5 MA
4. FuZE-3 — three-electrode design, gigapascal pressures achieved
5. FuZE-A — advanced accelerator development (commissioning ~late 2025)
- Century — parallel engineering integration platform (liquid metal, rep-rate)
