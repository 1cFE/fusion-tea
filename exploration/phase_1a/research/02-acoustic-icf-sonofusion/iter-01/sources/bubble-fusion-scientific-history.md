# Bubble Fusion / Sonofusion — Scientific History
**Sources**: Wikipedia (Bubble fusion), various search results
**Accessed**: 2026-03-08

## Core Concept
- Acoustic cavitation: ultrasonic vibrations create, expand, and violently collapse gas bubbles in liquid
- Hypothesis: collapse achieves temperatures and densities sufficient for thermonuclear fusion
- Uses deuterated liquids (deuterated acetone, heavy water)
- D-D reactions would produce 2.45 MeV neutrons and tritium as signatures

## Taleyarkhan Claims (2002)
- Published in Science (March 8, 2002)
- Rusi P. Taleyarkhan et al. at Oak Ridge National Laboratory
- Deuterated acetone + acoustic cavitation
- Claimed tritium production and neutron output consistent with D-D fusion
- Bubble clusters nucleated by neutrons at nanoscale, grow ~100,000× to mm scale before implosive collapse
- Simulations suggested 10⁶–10⁷ K compressed conditions

## Failed Replications
- Putterman & Suslick (2005, BBC Horizon): no evidence of fusion with more sophisticated detection
- Putterman found no neutrons above background — at least 100,000× less than claimed
- University of Göttingen: no replication
- University of Illinois: no replication
- Oak Ridge (independent team): no replication
- Office of Naval Research funded study: no replication

## Misconduct Finding
- 2008: Purdue review board found Taleyarkhan guilty of "falsification of the research record"
- Added an author who hadn't participated to assert "independent confirmation"
- 2009: Office of Naval Research debarred him for 28 months from federal funding

## Current Scientific Status
- Sonoluminescence itself is well-established (confirmed, reproducible)
- Temperatures achieved: 7,000–16,000 K (Flannigan & Suslick 2010)
- Densities: up to 10²¹ cm⁻³ (comparable to laser ICF)
- BUT: these conditions are far below thermonuclear fusion requirements (~10⁸ K / ~10 keV)
- Gap from sonoluminescence to fusion: approximately 4 orders of magnitude in temperature
- No credible, independently replicated evidence of fusion from acoustic cavitation exists

## Other Companies (Historical)
- Impulse Devices, Inc.: built sonofusion research reactors (~$250K, 1-foot stainless steel sphere with heavy water)
- NanoSpire, Inc. (Mark LeClair): claimed cavitation fusion — extraordinary claims, no independent verification
