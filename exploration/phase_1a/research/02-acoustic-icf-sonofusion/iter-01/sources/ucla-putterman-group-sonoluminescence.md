# UCLA Putterman Research Group — Sonoluminescence
**URL**: http://acoustics-research.physics.ucla.edu/sonoluminescence/
**Accessed**: 2026-03-08

## Key Technical Facts
- Sound wave energy concentrates by 12 orders of magnitude to create light flashes < 50 picoseconds
- Bubble interiors reach "over twice as hot as the surface of the sun" (>11,600 K)
- Multi-bubble experiments produce emissions "more ultraviolet than a 10,000 K blackbody"
- Dense plasma with charge densities exceeding 10²¹ free charges/cm³
- Hot spots range from 10 nm to 100 μm
- Flash rates vary from single events to 10 million/second
- One setup: 40,000 light flashes/second at 40 kHz
- Group designed neutron detector (20% efficiency, nanosecond timing) to search for fusion neutrons

## Fusion Relevance
- Key question: can ~30 atm sound wave implode a bubble with enough force for fusion/neutrons?
- Taleyarkhan claimed fusion; independently unconfirmed
- Putterman's own neutron measurements found no neutrons above background — fusion events at least 100,000x less than Taleyarkhan claimed

## Pyroelectric/Crystallic Fusion (Related Work)
- Pyroelectric crystal (LiTaO₃) heated from −34°C to 7°C produces >100,000 V
- Tungsten tip focuses field to ~25 GV/m
- Ionizes and accelerates D ions to ~1% speed of light
- Fusion confirmed with neutron detection (Nature 2005)
- This is beam-target fusion, NOT thermonuclear — net energy gain not possible

## Key Publications
- Flannigan & Suslick, Nature Physics 6, 598–601 (2010): electron density >10²¹ cm⁻³, temperatures 7,000–16,000 K
- Nature 2005: pyroelectric crystal fusion demonstration
