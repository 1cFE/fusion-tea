# OpenStar Technologies Prototype Roadmap

**Sources**: Multiple news articles, company website, Wikipedia

## Device Naming
- **Marsden**: Vacuum chamber (5.2 m diameter)
- **Junior**: First-generation core magnet (550 kg, 5.63 T design, REBCO HTS)
- **Tahi**: Next-generation prototype device (20 T target, 4x Junior field strength)
- **Maui**: Third-generation model, target neutron production and revenue generation
- **Tama Nui**: Fourth-generation, target 50-200 MW electricity

## Company Info
- Founded: 2021, Wellington, New Zealand
- Founder/CEO: Ratu Mataira
- Chief Science Officer: Darren Garnier (led MIT LDX program)
- CTO: Rod Badcock (superconducting systems expert)

## Timeline
- Late 2024: First plasma with Junior (mechanically supported, He-4 gas)
- Feb 2026: Levitated plasma confinement demonstration
- ~2028: Tahi operational (supported by NZD 35M NZ government funding)
- ~2030: Maui class reactors (target market entry)
- 2030s: Grid electricity target

## Funding
- Total raised by 2024: NZD 20M
- Feb 2026: NZD 35M from NZ Regional Development Fund
- Estimated need: $500M-$1B for full development

## Key Milestones
- First commercial company to create/confine plasma with levitated dipole
- 170 kJ stored energy via flux pump (record)
- Plasma at ~300,000°C for 20 seconds
- Flux pump needs only 10 W to maintain magnet indefinitely

## Lab Experiments (Heritage)
- LDX (MIT/Columbia, 2004-2011): First levitated dipole experiment, DOE funded
  - High-beta (20%) quasi-steady discharges >20 seconds
  - Inward turbulent pinch observed (Nature Physics)
  - ECRH heating at 2.45-28 GHz
  - Ended when DOE redirected funding to tokamaks
- RT-1 (University of Tokyo): Similar design, 1st-gen HTS (Bi-2223) magnet
  - Also observed peaked density profiles
  - Compact design explored for space applications
