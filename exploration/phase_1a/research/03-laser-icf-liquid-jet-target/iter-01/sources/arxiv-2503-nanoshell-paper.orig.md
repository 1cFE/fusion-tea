# Fusion in a Nanoshell: Harnessing Plasmonic Fields for Nuclear Reactions
**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15531
**Authors**: Dmitri E. Kharzeev (Stony Brook/BNL), Jacob Levitt (Cortex Fusion Systems), Carlos Trallero-Herrero (UConn)
**Submitted**: 2025-02-27, revised 2025-04-04
**IP notice**: "Systems, methods, and underlying principles...are the intellectual property of Cortex Fusion Systems, Inc."

## Core Concept
High-intensity laser irradiation of D2O-filled metal nanoshells to induce D-D fusion via plasmonic field enhancement.

## Target Design
- Hollow metallic spherical nanoshells (gold specified)
- Inner radius R1, outer radius R2 (thin-shell regime: thickness ≤ skin depth)
- Typical radius: ~100 nm or larger
- Filled with liquid D2O (heavy water)

## Laser Specifications
- Peak pulse intensity: ~1 atomic unit sufficient (modest by fusion standards)
- External electric field: ~10^9 V/cm
- Plasmonic-enhanced internal field: ~10^11 V/cm (orders of magnitude amplification)
- Wavelength: ~1 μm
- Pulse duration: ~3 femtoseconds
- Target repetition frequency for reactor: 1 MHz

## Physics Mechanism
- Plasmonic field enhancement inside nanoshell accelerates deuterons
- Characteristic deuteron momentum: ~10 MeV
- Effective kinetic energy: equivalent to thermonuclear plasma at ~25 keV (~10^8 K)
- Deuteron mean free path for fusion: ~cm (exceeds nanoshell size → most deuterons escape without fusing)
- Deuteron scattering mean free path: ~10 nm (smaller than nanoshell → multiple collisions)
- NOT implosion/compression — electrostatic acceleration by oscillating plasmonic fields

## Fusion Parameters
- **Fuel**: D-D (explicitly, not D-T)
- **Fusion rate per nanoshell**: ~10^7 s^-1
- **Power per nanoshell**: ~1 μW
- **Energy per D-D fusion**: 3333 MeV (note: this seems high — standard D-D is 3.27 MeV for D+D→He3+n or 4.03 MeV for D+D→T+p; paper may be calculating something differently or including secondary reactions)

## Projected Reactor Parameters
- 1 MHz rep rate, 1 million nanoshells ignited per pulse
- Fusion power: ~1 MW
- Neutron flux: ~10^19 n/s ("exceeds current devices by nine orders of magnitude")
- Q-factor: ~100 (with 30% conversion efficiency, 3 kW laser consumption)
- Relativistic electron temperature: ~3 MeV
- Oxygen ion temperature (from D2O): ~200 keV

## What's NOT Addressed
- Energy capture/conversion method
- Neutron management (beyond flux calculation)
- Tritium handling (D-D doesn't need external tritium)
- Practical engineering of nanoshell delivery
- "Many practical challenges exist" — acknowledged but not detailed

## Key Caveat
The paper's claim of 3333 MeV per D-D fusion event is anomalous — standard D-D fusion releases ~3-4 MeV. This may indicate they're counting energy from a chain of secondary reactions or there's a different calculation basis. Needs verification.
