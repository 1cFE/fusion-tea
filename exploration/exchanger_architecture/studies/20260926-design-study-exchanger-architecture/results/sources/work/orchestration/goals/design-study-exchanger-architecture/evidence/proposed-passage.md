We used the expanded library to compare two connections for the same three heat exchangers: all in series, or a blanket-helium exchanger followed by parallel PbLi and divertor branches. We supplied a common heat-source scenario and kept the purchased equipment fixed. Adjusting the branch split removed a failure caused by the original fixed split. At a supplied fusion load of 2,300 MW, the network removed all heat with 1,400 kg/s of cycle helium, while series needed the next tested flow, 1,500 kg/s. The resulting reduction in compressor work raised net electricity from 732 to 800 MW. With identical represented costs, conditional LCOE fell; under a zero-tritium-price bookkeeping sensitivity, the difference was about 5.6 USD2004/MWh. The result also exposed the limit of the comparison: primary return requirements and practical exchanger approaches were not qualified, and network pressure losses could change the preference. The study therefore identifies a conditional operating advantage and the cost/power allowance available to pay for it, rather than selecting a finished plant design.
