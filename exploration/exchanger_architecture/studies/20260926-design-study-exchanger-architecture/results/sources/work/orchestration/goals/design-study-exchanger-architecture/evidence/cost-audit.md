# Exchanger architecture: bounded cost audit

2026-09-26. [AGENT] Audit of existing code and stored evidence, not a review of future implementation. Only this file was changed. No native study was executed, no external source fetched, and no quarantined material read. Numerical extraction and formula arithmetic used `.codex-test/run python`.

## Findings that should shape the comparison

1. **Changing connections has no direct cost in the current model.** The three exchanger purchases depend on selected area; pump purchases depend on selected flow capacity; machines depend on selected ratings. Neither network mode nor split enters a purchase. Piping and secondary transport are fixed allowances. The model therefore cannot establish that two physical layouts cost the same. It merely assigns them the same represented inventory. Evidence: `models/designs/aries_cs_integrated/plant.sysml:720`, `:1006`, `:1025`; `exploration/aries_integrated/native_completions/equipment/selected_inventory_purchase_impl.py:6`.
2. **At identical source and cycle settings, complete heat removal implies equal modeled electricity.** The closure solves `C × (T_turbine − T_heater) = accepted_heat`; the recuperator and turbine relationships depend on the common cycle settings. When both arrangements accept the same total duty, the resulting turbine temperature, machine work and net power coincide within numerical accuracy. Split changes the ability to transfer each branch's heat. It does not independently improve conversion efficiency after all heat is accepted. Evidence: `exploration/aries_integrated/native_completions/network_heat_driven_closure_impl.py:65`. Optimizing other permitted settings can still yield different best passing outputs because topology changes the feasible region.
3. **The prior ranking reversal has no economic winner.** The B3 series/network pair at density 5.0e20 m⁻³ and hollowness 0.66 gives 423.107 MW and 1,119.408 USD2004/MWh in both arrangements. At 5.5e20, series passes while network leaves 45.224 MW unremoved. At 5.75e20 both fail. Those latter finite prices cannot enter a recommendation. Evidence: `exploration/aries_integrated/studies/20260926-aries-design-choice-interactions/results/interactions.md`, B3.
4. **Heating is accounted for consistently as a supplied assumption, but its adequacy for the plasma is unestablished.** The baseline deposits a supplied 20 MW auxiliary heat and charges 40 MW electricity at efficiency 0.5. The calculated plasma supplies fusion power to fuel and deposition, but does not supply its own heating requirement to this electrical input. Keep this distinction in the scenario name and resolve it before claiming a self-consistent calculated-plasma plant. Evidence: `models/designs/aries_cs_integrated/plant.sysml:34`, `:389`; `exploration/aries_integrated/native_completions/plant_electrical_balance_impl.py:19`.

## Finance, fuel and energy accounting

The existing real-currency convention is USD2004, 40 calendar operating years, availability 0.85, discount rate 5%, six construction years with midpoint financing, and no inflation escalation. Overnight equals direct × 1.49: indirect 20% of direct, contingency 20% of direct plus indirect, owner 5% of direct. The baseline finances to 5,035.910 MUSD2004. O&M is 70 MUSD/year and consumables 5 MUSD/year. Replacement life is 5 full-power years, giving six dated events of 72.23135 MUSD at this availability/life. The annual reserve is reported separately and is not added to the dated-event lifecycle total. Other overhaul is 5% of overnight at year 20, gross terminal expense 10% at year 40 and salvage 2% at year 40. Evidence: `plant.sysml:1965`, `:2008`, `:2051`, `:2108`; `native_completions/lifecycle/lifecycle_cashflow_accounts_impl.py:9`.

Tritium burn and permanent exhaust losses scale with source fusion and availability. Stock decay runs over calendar time. Annual purchase is `max(burn + loss + decay − new_usable_feed, 0) × price`; new feed excludes the already recycled exhaust. Baseline burn fraction 0.05, exhaust recovery 0.99, no new feed and price 30 MUSD/kg give 104.667707 kg/year external tritium and 3,140.031 MUSD/year purchase. Initial stock uses the same price, so changing it changes both capital and annual cost. Deuterium is priced separately at 1,000 USD/kg. All breeding/supply support remains unqualified. Evidence: `plant.sysml:110`, `:1595`; `native_completions/equipment/annual_selected_fuel_impl.py:6`.

At an identical source load and fuel convention the annual fuel amounts are identical across topology; their per-MWh contributions change only through net electricity. Thus show total conditional LCOE and nonfuel LCOE together. Baseline total is 1,119.408 USD/MWh, of which tritium is 996.692; the residual after tritium is 122.716 and still contains the small deuterium expense. Do not describe this residual as strictly nonfuel without also removing deuterium. Do not make the arbitrary 100 kg/year supply threshold the primary comparison.

Electrical accounting subtracts compressor shaft work before generator conversion, then primary pump electricity, heating electricity, cryogenic load, fuel processing, controls and other loads. Recovered pump heat enters source duty; only its unrecovered remainder appears as pump loss in the whole-plant ledger. Added pumping therefore cannot always be represented by subtracting its full electric demand from net: recoverable heat can change accepted duty and the thermal boundary. A pure extra-electric-load allowance is valid only when explicitly dissipated outside the heat supply. Evidence: `native_completions/plant_electrical_balance_impl.py:14`; `native_completions/integrated_plant_ledger_impl.py:35`.

## Paired economics and break-even allowances

[AGENT derivation] Define `h = 8760 × availability`, net power `P_i`, annualized cost `A_i = CRF × PV_total_cost_i`, and `L_i = A_i/(h P_i)`, with costs in USD2004 and power in MW. For a common source pair S (series) and N (network), `ΔL = A_N/(h P_N) − A_S/(h P_S)`. Decompose each `A` into capital, nonfuel operation, fuel, replacements, overhaul and terminal/salvage. If numerator terms are equal, `ΔL = (A/h) × (1/P_N − 1/P_S)`. If both power and numerator are equal, the difference is zero.

Let an unrepresented network addition cost `B` USD/year on an annualized basis and consume `w` MW of net output, with no recovered heat or other performance change. Then `L_N,adjusted = (A_N+B)/(h(P_N−w))`, requiring `P_N−w > 0`. The exact equal-price boundary is:

`B_max(w) = L_S × h × (P_N−w) − A_N`.

Equivalently, `w_max(B) = P_N − (A_N+B)/(h L_S)`. Negative allowance means the network already loses that passing pair; zero means it has no room for a positive differential burden. Report signed values and never reinterpret a negative allowance as zero-cost feasibility. The baseline equal-output pair has zero positive cost/power allowance. A design feasible only in one arrangement has no passing paired LCOE comparator at that same load; report range extension separately.

For a direct purchased increment `D`, use existing cost conventions rather than dividing purchase price by lifetime. At current finance the annualized increment is `g_D D`, where `g_D = 1.49 × CRF × [(1+r)^(construction_years/2) + overhaul_fraction/(1+r)^20 + (terminal_fraction−salvage_fraction)/(1+r)^40] = 0.1031448485/year`. This includes the current proportional overhaul/terminal treatment and assumes no extra component-specific replacement. The corresponding overnight coefficient is 0.0692247305/year. Additional annual O&M is added directly. Thus `D_max = (B_max − extra_annual_OM − annualized_extra_replacements)/g_D`. State whether plotted capital is direct or overnight. With 0.85 availability, one USD/MWh of paired advantage permits 7,446 USD/year per MW of network output before any extra power burden.

Missing differentials are branch piping/manifolds, splitter/mixer hardware, valves/control, branch pressure losses, topology-dependent maintenance and installation. Recuperator purchase/rating and cycle-side hardware support are also incomplete, but holding recuperation and common machine hardware fixed avoids assigning an unsupported recuperator advantage. No existing detailed law supports these differentials. Use the bounded allowance equations, or explicit supplied increments to existing allowances with scope stated; do not call those increments an engineering estimate. Reusing the 891 MW case's broad cost bounds as a new architecture estimate would be unjustified.

## Exposed knobs, minimum sensitivities and release checks

Price factors on fixed allowances can carry an explicit bounded increment, but the chosen baseline scope must stay identifiable.

Minimum useful sensitivity set, after the screen chooses representative common passing and boundary cases:

- Conductance: ±20% assumed U at fixed purchased area, declared as an assumption bracket, with the limiting branch identified. Separate priced area alternatives from this uncertainty.
- Temperature approach: explicit minimum terminal approaches such as 0, 5 and 10 K as declared study screens, subject to the thermal audit's source-consistency finding. These are requirements applied to computed terminal differences, not a physically independent blanket-temperature redesign.
- Pumping: cubic versus fixed-power assumptions at identical selected capacity; a bounded extra dissipative electrical load up to the exact break-even value. Use a separate coupled native case if claiming recoverable pump heat. A network pressure-loss sensitivity must rerun closure with the resulting expansion conditions.
- Architecture cost: plot the two-dimensional direct-capital/extra-power break-even line; include zero and the exact crossing. Recompute at 3% and 8% real discount to expose finance dependence of the capital allowance.
- Fuel: paired no-credit purchase convention and a clearly conditional low-cost fuel case, such as a stated lower tritium price, with initial stock repriced consistently. Report nonfuel contributions so a denomination effect cannot masquerade as a connection benefit. If using the historical feed100 scenario, keep it a secondary scenario and retain identical feed across the pair.

Before ranking, require: both cases executed; finite positive net; branch heat-removal and energy residual checks; all selected equipment/stock screens; defined exchanger states and declared terminal approaches; required primary return conditions; identical source/fuel/finance inputs and permitted operating freedom; unchanged selected inventories in a connection-only pair; component LCOE sum and annual/PV reconciliation; no reserve/event or IDC double count; separate failed cases and scientific support flags. Native lifecycle code deliberately refuses nonpositive net electricity, so preserve such execution failures instead of dropping them from the candidate ledger. The source-finance branch supplies 1,000 MW and is a diagnostic only; never use it as predicted electricity for this study.
