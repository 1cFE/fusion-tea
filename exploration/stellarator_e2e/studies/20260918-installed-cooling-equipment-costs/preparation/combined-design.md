## Implementation refinements

Native generation requires separate cost-selection and energy-addition producers. The retained legacy cost reads net power, so placing that input on the energy producer creates a dependency cycle. Splitting the two producers preserves the released equations. The optional delivered-shipping input precedes skipped default formals, following the native generator's established binding order.

BNL target pumping duty uses primary shaft/fluid work per machine, before motor/drive losses. The source denominator is50hp pumping duty rather than the140hp motor rating. At the entering unity drive efficiency, shaft and electrical numbers coincide; nonunity drive verification must distinguish them. This target mapping is a declared engineering transfer, not a new source claim. Physical inventory-volume outputs exclude the purchased reserve; inventory mass and standard helium purchase volume include it. Exchanger area and metal outputs are per exchanger; pipe and inventory outputs are plant totals.

Independent implementation review requires a scenario guard: cost and secondary-energy selectors must each be finite and exactly0 or1; either selected effect requires equipment_enabled=true. Otherwise execution raises a deliberate domain error. This prevents disabled zero equipment outputs from replacing the existing allowance. Both cost and energy consumers read the guard's validated outputs; generic false/0/0 remains valid.

The conceptual salt pressure schedule assumes0.20MPa absolute at cold-pump suction. At40m head and1882.09kg/m³, the calculated pump rise is0.73827MPa and discharge is about0.93827MPa absolute. This pressure schedule is an AGENT operating assumption, not a priced pressure-class qualification. It stays below the NETL150psig category threshold, although the adopted pump-setting allowance is the separate ORNL analogy. Primary helium remains8MPa; the exchanger's tube-to-shell pressure difference is therefore severalMPa. The selected tube/shell thicknesses remain cost geometry with explicit sensitivities, not a stress-code result. No source-certified allowable stress, corrosion allowance, design pressure or weld efficiency is inferred from those thicknesses. Changing salt pressure would require revisiting applicability rather than silently retaining a qualified price claim.
