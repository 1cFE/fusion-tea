1. The Question

Fusion power has 36+ competing approaches at various stages of maturity. How do you compare their economics?

Decision-makers — investors, policymakers, researchers — need structured comparisons, not just headline LCOE numbers. But comparing fusion economics across fundamentally different approaches is hard: different confinement mechanisms, different cost structures, different data quality, different estimation maturity.

This project builds the analytical infrastructure for that comparison: a taxonomy of the concept space, reusable cost modeling patterns, and traceable parameter chains from source literature to model outputs. The models are built in SysML v2 using agent-assisted tooling.

Research Questions

These five questions drive all downstream work — taxonomy, source selection, modeling targets, and comparison methodology.

RQ-1
What are the dominant cost drivers for fusion power, and how do they differ across confinement approaches?
Rationale

CAS22 (reactor plant equipment) is ~60% of cost for magnetic confinement tokamaks, but inertial, magnetized target, and exotic approaches have fundamentally different power core structures, drivers, and balance-of-plant requirements. Understanding where the money goes for each approach is the foundation of any meaningful comparison.

RQ-2
What LCOE range is credible for each major fusion approach, and what assumptions drive those estimates?
Rationale

Published LCOE figures vary enormously (30–200+ $/MWh) and depend critically on assumptions about plant availability, magnet or driver cost, neutron economy, rep rate, and thermal conversion efficiency. We need to decompose LCOE into its constituent assumptions — not cite headline numbers.

RQ-3
Which cost structures are shared across fusion concepts, and where do they fundamentally diverge?
Rationale

This directly informs modeling architecture. Shared structures (e.g., thermal power conversion, buildings, site work) become reusable library components. Divergent structures (e.g., magnet systems vs. laser drivers vs. pulsed power) become concept-specific designs. The answer determines how much modeling reuse is achievable.

RQ-4
What is the state of cost estimation maturity across fusion approaches?
Rationale

An ARIES tokamak study with AACE Class 3–4 estimates is fundamentally different from a startup whitepaper with Class 5 estimates. Understanding estimation maturity for each concept frames how much confidence to place in any comparison and where the biggest knowledge gaps lie.

RQ-5
Which cost and performance parameters exhibit both high sensitivity and high uncertainty?
Rationale

A parameter that moves LCOE by 2x if wrong AND is poorly constrained by available data is more important than one that's well-known or low-impact. This is the “where should attention focus” question — identifying the highest-leverage unknowns across the fusion landscape.

Scope

In Scope

  • Magnetic confinement (tokamaks, stellarators, mirrors)
  • Inertial confinement (laser, heavy-ion)
  • Magnetized target fusion
  • Exotic concepts
  • ~36+ concepts in taxonomy, ~13 for deeper modeling

  • Fission-fusion hybrids
  • Concepts with zero public technical data
  • Detailed engineering design (we model economics)

Project: Fusion TEA (Techno-Economic Analysis)
Purpose: Investigate the economics of nuclear fusion across fundamentally different approaches
Status: Active — Investigation scoping (DEMO epic)

What We're Doing

We are building a comparative techno-economic analysis of fusion power across the full landscape of approaches — magnetic confinement, inertial confinement, magnetized target, and exotic concepts. The goal is not to model one reactor design in detail, but to understand how fusion economics work across approaches that differ in confinement mechanism, fuel cycle, energy conversion, and engineering complexity.

The analysis is built in SysML v2 using the agentic-mbse toolchain. Models are validated against reference implementations (PyFECONS) where overlap exists, and all cost parameters trace to domain literature.

Why This Matters

Fusion has dozens of competing approaches at various stages of maturity. Decision-makers — investors, policymakers, researchers — need structured comparisons, not just headline LCOE numbers. This project builds the analytical infrastructure to produce those comparisons: a taxonomy of the concept space, reusable cost modeling patterns, and traceable parameter chains from source literature to model outputs.

Modeling Requirements

Project-specific rules that all modeling work must follow. These extend the standard rules in MODELING_GUIDE.md with requirements derived from the investigation scope (OVERVIEW.md).

Modeling Requirements

These define the patterns that all SysML v2 models in this project MUST follow.

MR-1: CAS Hierarchy as Primary Cost Decomposition

All models MUST use the CAS (Cost Account Structure) hierarchy as the primary cost decomposition.

  • Rationale: CAS is the standard used by ARPA-E, the ARIES program, and industry costing tools. Common decomposition enables cross-concept comparison.
  • Enforcement: Model review — every cost-bearing element must map to a CAS category.

MR-2: Standard Costed Component Interface

All cost-bearing components MUST implement a standard interface with at minimum: capital_cost.

  • Rationale: Uniform interface enables automated cost rollup and cross-concept comparison.
  • Enforcement: Model validation — every cost-contributing part must specialize the interface.

MR-3: Library Concept-Agnostic, Designs Concept-Specific

Library definitions (models/library/) MUST be concept-agnostic. Concept-specific values MUST live in models/designs/{concept}/.

  • Rationale: ~60% of definitions are shared across concepts. Clean separation maximizes reuse.
  • Enforcement: No reactor-type-specific values in library/. Designs import from library and specialize.

MR-4: Traceable Citations on All Quantitative Values

All quantitative values MUST carry structured citations that resolve to source files.

FieldRequiredDescription
SourceMUSTRepo-relative file path to cited artifact
RefSHOULDLocation within source (line numbers or section heading)
BasisSHOULDWhat was derived and how
  • Rationale: LLM agents perform significant research and modeling. Machine-checkable citations prevent hallucination.
  • Enforcement: scripts/trace_audit.py (planned) walks citation chains and reports gaps.

MR-5: Standard Output Schema for Cross-Concept Comparison

Models SHOULD produce standard outputs enabling comparison along the 7 axes.

  • Status: Intent defined. Specific fields TBD — informed by taxonomy and concept analysis.

MR-6: Modeling Patterns Defined Before Production Models

Documented modeling patterns SHOULD be defined and validated BEFORE building production models.

  • Rationale: Pattern validation prevents rework. Discovering a broken pattern after 5 concept models is expensive.

Process Requirements

PR-1: Taxonomy Before Modeling

The investigation MUST begin with taxonomy development before selecting modeling targets.

PR-2: Concept Analysis Identifies Shared vs. Divergent Structure

The taxonomy stage MUST include a concept analysis phase.

PR-3: Documented Patterns Before Production Models

The process MUST produce documented modeling patterns BEFORE production modeling begins.

PR-4: Iterative Process with Feedback Loops

The process SHOULD accommodate iteration — research reveals taxonomy gaps, concept analysis reveals literature gaps, modeling reveals pattern gaps.

PR-5: Committed Artifacts at Every Phase

Each phase MUST produce committed artifacts. Knowledge transforms are visible — a reader can see what went in and what came out at every step.

  • Source ingestion: extracted documents in knowledge/sources/
  • Domain research: DI-XXX entries in KNOWLEDGE.md
  • Taxonomy: classification artifact committed to repo
  • Concept analysis: comparison artifact committed to repo
  • Model construction: SysML files passing validation with citations per MR-4
  • Analysis: output data and visualizations committed
